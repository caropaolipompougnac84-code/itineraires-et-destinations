Lesson 4. Listening to Campaigners

Goal: I can say who is speaking and what they want.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 4 of 8. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Pause when necessary: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Read the goal and find your place. 0 to 4
  2. Read the short text and word meanings. 4 to 12
  3. Complete reading and vocabulary work. 12 to 20
  4. Complete listening or the labelled no-video route. 20 to 30
  5. Complete language practice. 30 to 37
  6. Prepare and say your answer. 37 to 44
  7. Keep the written record. 44 to 49
  8. Check answers and note homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the short written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework at the end of this page, with its worked example and self-check.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: name a speaker and concern.
  • 46 to 49: keep the written record.
  • 49 to 51: note homework.
  • 51 to 52: distinguish could rise from recorded.
Short text

Muskrat Falls and Site C are different dam projects.

At Muskrat Falls, Billy Gauthier protested in 2016.

He wanted to protect water and traditional activities.

At Site C, Roland Willson criticised the project.

He proposed other energy choices.

A prediction says what may happen.

A measurement reports what was observed.

Building your vocabulary

  • protect: keep safe;
  • protest: publicly show disagreement;
  • alternative: another choice;
  • prediction: what someone thinks may happen;
  • measurement: an observed amount or result.

Exercise 2

Match words

Worked example.
protect → keep safe.

Match:

  1. protest;
  2. prediction;
  3. measurement.
  1. an observed result;
  2. publicly show disagreement;
  3. what someone thinks may happen.

Self-check

1b, 2c, 3a.

Exercise 3

Listen to Billy Gauthier

Listening document. Emotional hunger striker protesting Muskrat Falls. CBC News NL · YouTube, 18 October 2016 · 2 min 40. Listen first without reading. If the video does not work, use the support account and write “Reading-support route”. Open on YouTube.

This report discusses a hunger strike. You may use the support account instead.

Worked example of a note.
Speaker: Billy Gauthier.

Listen for:

  1. What activity does his daughter enjoy? .
  2. Does he talk about protecting future generations? .
Support account

Gauthier links his protest to fishing and future generations. His daughter enjoys fishing.

Use it only if you cannot listen. Label your answer “Reading-support route”.

Self-check

Fishing; yes.

Exercise 5

Choose careful words

Worked example.
“Pollution could increase.”
This is a prediction.

Now label:

  1. “The test recorded a higher level.” .
  2. “Levels might rise.” .

Complete:

  1. Campaigners want to water.
    Choose: protect / protecting.
  2. Authorities should the concern.
    Choose: explain / to explain.

Self-check

Measurement; prediction; protect; explain.

Speaking: your route

Worked example.
Gauthier wants to protect fishing. Willson proposes other energy choices.

Route S · SEGPA

Use the Route C sentences below. Say the first sentence aloud. Then say the second one. You may read them from the page, one at a time.

Route C · CAP or CFA

Say:

Billy Gauthier wants to protect water.
Roland Willson proposes other energy choices.

Route P · Bac pro or CFA

Prepare a safety-meeting message:

The reported concern is…
We need evidence about…
A possible alternative is…

Route G · Lycée général, LVA

Explain one similarity and one difference between the reports. Identify testimony and uncertainty.

Route G · Lycée général, LVB

Give three sentences naming the speakers and concerns.

Self-check

Do not say that every Indigenous person has the same view.

Written record

I identify the speaker and the concern. A prediction is not the same as a measured result.

Homework

Worked example.
Prediction: levels could rise.

Write one prediction and one measurement sentence.

Self-check

Prediction: pollution might increase.
Measurement: the test recorded a higher level.
