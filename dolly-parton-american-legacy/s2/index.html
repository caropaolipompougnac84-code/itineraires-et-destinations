Lesson 2. Rags to rhinestones

Debatable question: Can you be proud of where you come from and still reinvent yourself completely?
By the end of this lesson, I can: tell the story of a childhood, talk about past habits, describe an outfit precisely.

For the teacher: timing (52 minutes)
Step Time Activity
1 0-4 Recap and flashcard duel; “Guess who” (homework)
2 4-7 The question of the day
3 7-17 Document 1 (reading) + Document 2 (song, 3 min)
4 17-24 Expanding your vocabulary: the Dolly look
5 24-30 The teacher’s point: past simple, used to, order of adjectives
6 30-38 Exercises
7 38-47 Speaking task: “Then and now” chain story + opinion debate
8 47-50 Written record
9 50-52 Homework

Stop point (minute 47). The story of the coat has been told by the class and every student has said one sentence.

Emergency exit (8 minutes). Play only the song (3:04) with the gap task, then a “one sentence each” chain story of the coat in the past simple. Dictate the written record.

Step 1. Recap

  1. Give three colours from the Warhol portrait.
  2. Complete: She looks ... a film star.
  3. Give the comparative of bright and colourful.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 platinum blonde, lilac, scarlet, turquoise, pale peach (any three) · 2 like · 3 brighter, more colourful.

Two students read their “Guess who” description; the class guesses.

Step 2. The question of the day

Write on the board: “It costs a lot of money to look this cheap.” (Dolly Parton). Ask: What is surprising in this sentence? Can a person who grew up poor be proud of it and still wear rhinestones?

Transition. To answer, we need her story: where did she come from?

Step 3. Documents

Document 1. Her childhood (reading)

Dolly Rebecca Parton was born on 19 January 1946 in Locust Ridge, in the Great Smoky Mountains of East Tennessee. She was the fourth of twelve children. Her parents were farmers and the family lived in a one-room cabin. Her father could not read. Her mother sang old ballads and church songs, and music was everywhere in the family. In 1964, the day after she finished high school, she left for Nashville to become a singer.

Reading task. Find: her date of birth · the number of children in the family · the type of house · the city where she went in 1964.

Answer key

Answer key: 19 January 1946 · twelve · a one-room cabin · Nashville.

Document 2. Coat of Many Colors (song, 1971)

Watch or listen: Dolly Parton, Coat Of Many Colors (Official Audio), Dolly Parton channel: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KFbYFHaigSk
Play from 0:00 to 3:04 (whole track, 3 minutes 4 seconds).

Exact link, to keep unchanged: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KFbYFHaigSk

Before listening (summary to read). One autumn, her mother had no money for a coat, so she made one with scraps of fabric. As she sewed, she told Dolly the Bible story of Joseph and his coat of many colours. Dolly was proud of her coat, but at school the other children laughed at her. Years later, Dolly wrote a song about that day. It reached number 4 in the American country charts in 1971, and Dolly always said it was her favourite song.

Task during listening (first time). Tick the words you hear: rags · colours · mother · money · school · city · proud · rich · happy · coat.

Answer key

Answer key: you hear rags, colours, mother, money, school, proud, rich, happy, coat. You do not hear “city”.

Task during listening (second time, from 1:30 to 3:04). Answer: How did the children react? How did Dolly feel? What does she say about being “rich”?

Answer key

Answer key: They laughed at her. She felt hurt but she was still proud of her coat. She says that one is only poor if one chooses to be: she felt rich because of the love in it.
(Teacher’s note: the lyrics are protected by copyright; do not hand out the full lyrics. Use the summary above and short quotations only.)

Document 3. Dolly on her look

Quotation Source
“It costs a lot of money to look this cheap.” Dolly Parton; she confirmed this famous line to USA Today (21 February 2024)
“I’m not offended by all the dumb blonde jokes because I know I’m not dumb, and I’m not blonde either.” quoted by the BBC and The Guardian (2026)
“I look so totally artificial but I am so totally real.” CBS News, 2003

She described her style as “a country girl’s idea of glam” (quoted by People).

Reading task. In each quotation, find the contrast.

Answer key

Answer key: money / cheap · blonde jokes / not dumb, not blonde · artificial / real.

Step 4. Expanding your vocabulary: the Dolly look

A. Theme vocabulary

Field Words
Hair and wigs a wig, a hairpiece, big hair, curls, waves, bouffant, volume, platinum, bleached, teased, an updo
Bling rhinestones, sequins, glitter, sparkle, beads, studs, jewels, hoop earrings
Make-up foundation, blush, lipstick, eyeshadow, eyeliner, false lashes, nail polish, long nails
Clothes a rhinestone suit, a jumpsuit, a gown, fringe, high heels, cowboy boots, patchwork, rags, tight, flashy
Colours and shades hot pink, baby blue, gold, silver, ivory, pastel, bright, pale, shiny, sparkly
Opinion glamorous, over the top, eye-catching, tacky, elegant, bold, timeless
Story to grow up, a cabin, a farmer, poor, scraps of fabric, to sew, to laugh at someone, to be proud of

B. Words to tell a story and argue

Function Words
Order events first, then, after that, one day, years later, finally
Contrast but, although, even if, in spite of + noun
Cause and consequence because, so, that’s why, as a result
Opinion with nuance I think her look is a bit over the top, but... · It may look tacky, yet...

Exercises on vocabulary

Exercise B1. Sort the words into the right field: sequins, wig, blush, gown, curls, glitter, eyeliner, cowboy boots.

Answer key

Answer key: Hair: wig, curls · Bling: sequins, glitter · Make-up: blush, eyeliner · Clothes: gown, cowboy boots.

Exercise B2. Fill in the gaps with: proud · scraps · laughed · grew up · sewed
Dolly (1) __ in a cabin. Her mother (2) _ a coat with (3) of fabric. The children (4) at her, but she was (5) ___ of her coat.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 grew up · 2 sewed · 3 scraps · 4 laughed · 5 proud.

Exercise B3. Opposites. Match: 1 tacky · 2 poor · 3 artificial · 4 pale
a. real · b. elegant · c. bright · d. rich

Answer key

Answer key: 1b, 2d, 3a, 4c.

Flashcards

Front Back
rags (blue) old torn clothes, haillons · The children said her coat was made of rags.
although (green) bien que · Although she grew up poor, she became a star. · al-THOUGH
used to + base verb (red) habit in the past · She used to sing with her mother.

Step 5. The teacher’s point

Teacher script. Tell the story of the coat with the class, one sentence each. Write regular and irregular verbs in two columns. Then show how used to describes a habit, not a single event. End with the adjective order line and build one outfit description together.

Point Form Examples
Past simple, regular verb + -ed live → lived, laugh → laughed, use → used
Past simple, irregular to learn be → was/were, grow → grew, make → made, wear → wore, write → wrote, sing → sang, leave → left
Negative and question didn’t + base verb; Did + subject + base verb? She didn’t have a coat. Did the children laugh? Yes, they did.
used to + base verb a past habit or state She used to sing with her mother. She didn’t use to wear wigs as a child.
Order of adjectives opinion → size → age → shape → colour → material + noun a beautiful long pink satin dress; big blonde curls

Quick check. Put in order: dress / pink / a / sparkly / long → a long sparkly pink dress (a sparkly long pink dress is also accepted: sparkly can be read as an opinion).

Step 6. Exercises

Exercise 1. Find the past forms of these verbs in Documents 1 and 2: be, live, sing, make, tell, laugh, write, leave.

Answer key

Answer key: was, lived, sang, made, told, laughed, wrote, left.

Exercise 2. Put the story in order.
a. The children laughed at her. b. Her mother made a coat with scraps of fabric. c. Years later, she wrote a song about it. d. Dolly wore her coat to school. e. The family had no money for a coat.

Answer key

Answer key: e, b, d, a, c.

Exercise 3. used to.
1. The family (live) ... in a one-room cabin. 2. Her mother (sing) ... old ballads. 3. The children (not / have) ... new clothes. 4. Dolly (dream) ... of becoming a star.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 used to live · 2 used to sing · 3 didn’t use to have · 4 used to dream.

Exercise 4. Describe the outfit (adjective order).
1. a (pink / sparkly / long) gown 2. a (white / beautiful / fringe) jacket 3. (big / blonde / curly) hair 4. (red / high / shiny) heels

Answer key

Answer key: 1 a long sparkly pink gown (or a sparkly long pink gown) · 2 a beautiful white fringe jacket · 3 big curly blonde hair · 4 shiny high red heels.

Exercise 5. Transform into questions in the past simple.
1. She grew up in Tennessee. (Where...?) 2. Her mother made the coat. (Who...?) 3. She left for Nashville in 1964. (When...?)

Answer key

Answer key: 1 Where did she grow up? 2 Who made the coat? (subject question: no did) 3 When did she leave for Nashville?

Exercise 6. Short production (8 sentences). Four sentences about Dolly as a child (past simple and used to), four about her stage look. Give your opinion with one adjective from the Opinion line and one contrast word.
Model answer: As a child, Dolly lived in a cabin. She used to wear clothes made by her mother. One autumn, her mother sewed a coat with scraps of fabric. The children laughed at her. Later, she wore big platinum wigs and sparkly rhinestone suits. Although her look was over the top, it was glamorous. She never hid where she came from. I think she was proud of her roots and free to reinvent herself.

Help box. Frame: When she was a child, she lived... She used to... Later, she wore a ... ... ... (opinion, colour, material) ...
Going further. Explain the joke in 60 words: It costs a lot of money to look this cheap. Start: I think she means that... This joke shows that...
Possible answer: I think she means that her look seems cheap and flashy, but in fact wigs, rhinestones and stage make-up are very expensive. The joke shows that she was not ashamed of her style: she laughed at it first, so the critics could not hurt her.

Step 7. Speaking task: “Then and now”

  1. Chain story (whole class, 4 min). Each student says one sentence of Dolly’s childhood in the past simple; the next student must start with a connector (then, after that, one day, years later).
  2. Mini-debate in pairs (5 min). Student A: “She was proud of where she came from.” Student B: “She reinvented herself completely.” Each student speaks for one minute with two arguments, then they agree on a nuanced sentence with although.

Possible nuanced sentence: Although she reinvented her look, she stayed proud of her roots: her favourite song is about a coat made of rags.

Step 8. Written record

Lesson 2. Rags to rhinestones
Dolly grew up poor in a one-room cabin in Tennessee. Her mother made her a coat of many colours with scraps of fabric; the children laughed at her. In 1971, Dolly turned this memory into a song. Later, she built a famous look: big platinum wigs, rhinestones, long nails.
Grammar. Past simple for finished events (she grew up, she wrote). used to + verb for past habits. Order of adjectives: opinion, size, age, shape, colour, material + noun.
My answer: Although she ..., she ...

Step 9. Homework

  1. Make six flashcards (two from the Hair line, two from Bling, one green, one red). Revise last lesson’s cards.
  2. Present one stage outfit of Dolly in 60 words (adjective order + past simple), with a photo from an official website (link only).
  3. Help box: record a 45-second oral description of your outfit.
