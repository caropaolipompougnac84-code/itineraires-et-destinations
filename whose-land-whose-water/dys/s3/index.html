Lesson 3. A Dam Changes a Place

Goal: I can name a benefit and a loss.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 3 of 8. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Pause when necessary: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Read the goal and find your place. 0 to 4
  2. Read the short text and word meanings. 4 to 12
  3. Complete reading and vocabulary work. 12 to 20
  4. Complete listening or the labelled no-video route. 20 to 30
  5. Complete language practice. 30 to 37
  6. Prepare and say your answer. 37 to 44
  7. Keep the written record. 44 to 49
  8. Check answers and note homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the short written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework at the end of this page, with its worked example and self-check.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: say one benefit and one loss.
  • 46 to 49: keep the written record.
  • 49 to 51: note homework.
  • 51 to 52: check used to hunt.
Short text

A dam holds back river water.

A reservoir stores the water.

The James Bay project produced electricity.

It also changed land and community life.

The 1975 agreement brought compensation and institutions.

Some places and activities were lost or changed.

People can recognise a benefit and still explain a loss.

Building your vocabulary

  • dam: a structure holding back water;
  • reservoir: an artificial lake;
  • benefit: something helpful;
  • loss: something no longer available;
  • agreement: a decision accepted by parties.
Exercise 1

Complete the sentences

Worked example.
A dam holds back .
Answer: water.

Now complete:

  1. A reservoir stores .
  2. The project produced .
  3. The agreement was signed in .

Self-check

Water; electricity; 1975.

Exercise 2

Sort words

Worked example.
Improved public services → possible benefit.

Now sort:

  1. Compensation: benefit / loss.
  2. Flooded hunting grounds: benefit / loss.
  3. New institutions: benefit / loss.

Self-check

Benefit; loss; benefit.

A benefit does not cancel a loss.

Exercise 3

Listen to the main report

Listening document. How one deal led to the biggest project in Quebec history, but left a conflicted legacy. CBC News · YouTube, 11 November 2025 · 4 min 02. Listen first without reading. If the video does not work, use the support account and write “Reading-support route”. Open on YouTube.

Use 0:00 to 2:08 first.

Worked example of a note.
Project: James Bay.

Now listen for:

  1. Announcement year: .
  2. Peoples involved in opposition: .

Then use 2:08 to the end:

  1. One benefit: .
  2. One cost: .
Support account

The project was announced in 1971. Cree and Inuit opposition included legal action. Negotiations brought compensation and greater autonomy in some areas. The project also transformed land.

Use it only if you cannot listen. Label your answer “Reading-support route”.

Self-check

1971; Cree and Inuit; compensation or greater autonomy; changed or flooded land.

Exercise 4

Compare another report

Listening document. 50 years since James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement, mixed reaction to historic signing persists. CBC News North · YouTube, 11 November 2025 · 2 min 49. Listen first without reading. If the video does not work, use the support account and write “Reading-support route”. Open on YouTube.

Worked example.
One speaker mentions a benefit. Another speaker stresses a concern.

Listen and write:

  1. One benefit or positive assessment I heard: .
  2. One concern I heard: .

Self-check

Your notes must come from the report. If you cannot identify a detail, write “I need another listening”, not an invented answer. The support text above permits a reading-route answer about benefits and losses, but not a claim to have heard a particular speaker.

Exercise 5

Talk about the past

Worked example.
Families travelled along the river in the past.
Families used to travel along the river.

Complete:

  1. Families used to on the land.
    Choose: hunt / hunted.
  2. The agreement signed in 1975.
    Choose: was / were.

Self-check

Hunt; was.

Speaking: your route

Worked example.
The project produced electricity, but it also changed land.

Route S · SEGPA

Use the Route C sentences below. Say the first sentence aloud. Then say the second one. You may read them from the page, one at a time.

Route C · CAP or CFA

Say:

The dam produces electricity.
It also changes the land.

Route P · Bac pro or CFA

Prepare a project briefing:

Main benefit:…
Main risk or loss:…
Question before approval:…

Route G · Lycée général, LVA

Give a five-sentence explanation including a benefit, a loss and a source.

Route G · Lycée général, LVB

Give three linked sentences with but and because.

Self-check

Include both an outcome presented as useful and an outcome involving loss or change.

Written record

A development project can bring benefits and losses. I identify both and say whose view I am reporting.

Homework

Worked example.
Benefit: electricity. Loss: changed hunting grounds.

Write one benefit and one loss from this lesson.

Self-check

Use examples supported by the text.
