Documents

Key document

Election Day is the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November. On that day, citizens vote for a president in their state. But the president is not elected directly: the votes are counted state by state, and each state has a number of electors. A state has as many electors as members of Congress: its representatives plus its two senators. Washington, D.C. has three. In total there are 538 electors. To win, a candidate needs at least 270, more than half. In 48 states and Washington, D.C., the winner of the state gets all its electors. Maine and Nebraska share theirs. The electors vote in December, Congress counts the votes in early January, and the new president is sworn in on 20 January.

Read the source: USAGov, Electoral College · National Archives, About the Electoral College

Grammar to say it: the passive voice (4), by + agent

Why we need it

To explain who chooses the president, we need the passive with by: The president is elected by the Electoral College. The votes are counted by Congress.

Grammar point: board explanation

Teacher script
  1. Point to the diagram. Ask: Who elects the president? Write: The president is elected by the Electoral College.
  2. Explain: by introduces the agent, the person or group that acts.
  3. Ask: Do we need by in Senators are elected for six years? Explain why not: the agent is obvious or unknown.
  4. Contrast by (who) and with (what tool): The ballot is marked with a pen.
  5. Review prepositions of place: in a state, at the polling station, on the ballot.
  6. Students copy, then do the quick check before the exercises.

The passive voice (4): by + agent; prepositions by and with

Rule
by + agent (who does the action). Leave out the agent when it is obvious, unknown or not important.
Form
The president is elected by the Electoral College. The votes are counted by Congress. The bill was signed by the president. No agent: Senators are elected for six years.
Use
Keep by when the agent is new or important information.
Examples from the lesson
  • The president is elected by the Electoral College.
  • The electoral votes are counted by Congress in early January.
  • Senators are elected for six years.
Common mistakes
  • The president is elected from the electors. → by the electors.
  • The ballots are counted by someone. → The ballots are counted (no useful agent).
  • The ballot is marked by a pen. → with a pen (a tool).
French and English
par → by (agent); avec → with (tool). Le président est élu par les grands électeurs = The president is elected by the electors.
Also on the board: Prepositions of place
in a state, in the House, in Washington; at the polling station, at the Capitol; on the ballot, on Capitol Hill, on the map.

Figures of speech: metonymy

Learn

Metonymy uses a place or an object to speak about the people linked to it. The White House announced means the president and his team. Capitol Hill means Congress. Washington often means the federal government.

Finer grammar: passive with modals and the perfectGoing further

Rule

Modal passive: The result must be certified. Perfect passive: The votes have been counted. Modal perfect passive: The result could have been contested.

  1. The result / must / certify / by each state.
  2. All the votes / have / count. (present perfect)

Text analysis: a compromise of 1787Going further

The Electoral College was designed at the Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia in 1787. The delegates disagreed: some wanted Congress to choose the president, others wanted a direct vote of the people. The Electoral College was a compromise between the two ideas. Each state was given as many electors as it had members of Congress. Critics say the system gives too much weight to a few swing states; supporters reply that it forces candidates to campaign across many different states.

  1. Find two verbs in the passive.
  2. What were the two ideas of the delegates?
  3. Find a word meaning an agreement where both sides accept less.
  4. Find the critics' argument and the supporters' argument.
  5. Which linking punctuation separates the two opinions?
Answer key
  1. was designed, was given.
  2. Congress chooses the president, or the people vote directly.
  3. a compromise.
  4. Critics: too much weight to swing states. Supporters: candidates must campaign in many states.
  5. The semicolon.
