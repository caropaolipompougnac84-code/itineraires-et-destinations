Lesson 1. What Can I See?

Goal: I can describe an image without guessing too much.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 1 of 8. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Pause when necessary: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Read the goal and find your place. 0 to 4
  2. Read the short text and word meanings. 4 to 12
  3. Complete reading and vocabulary work. 12 to 20
  4. Complete listening or the labelled no-video route. 20 to 30
  5. Complete language practice. 30 to 37
  6. Prepare and say your answer. 37 to 44
  7. Keep the written record. 44 to 49
  8. Check answers and note homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the short written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework at the end of this page, with its worked example and self-check.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: say one observation and one cautious sentence.
  • 46 to 49: keep the written record.
  • 49 to 51: note homework.
  • 51 to 52: check might show.
Short text

Canada has First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples.

These are different peoples.

Jim Logan’s painting shows a boy and a dog in a village.

Norval Morrisseau’s painting shows a loon, a fish and a sun.

Lines connect the elements.

We can describe the images.

We can also suggest meanings.

A suggestion is not a proven fact.

Image descriptions

Logan:

A boy in a red jacket is shown with a blue dog. There is yellow grass. Buildings appear behind them. The colours are bright.

Morrisseau:

A loon, a fish and a sun appear in bright colours. Dark outlines surround the forms. Connecting lines link elements of the image.

These descriptions provide access to the activity. They do not replace every detail of the paintings.

Building your vocabulary

  • see: notice with your eyes;
  • might: perhaps;
  • line: a long thin mark;
  • connect: join;
  • alone: without another person.
Exercise 1

Find information

Worked example.
Question: Which painting shows a dog?
Answer: Logan’s painting.

Now answer:

  1. Which painting shows a fish? .
  2. What connects elements in Morrisseau’s painting? .
  3. Are First Nations, Inuit and Métis one single people? .

Self-check

  1. Morrisseau’s painting.
  2. Lines.
  3. No.
Exercise 2

Match words

Worked example.
connect → join.

Now match:

  1. might;
  2. alone;
  3. see.
  1. notice with your eyes;
  2. perhaps;
  3. without another person.

Self-check

1b, 2c, 3a.

Exercise 3

Use careful language

Worked example.
The boy / feel lonely.
The boy might feel lonely.

Now complete:

  1. The lines might relationships.
    Choose: show / to show.
  2. The boy might alone.
    Choose: feel / feels.
  3. I can a dog.
    Choose: see / seeing.

Self-check

Show; feel; see.

Exercise 4

Fact or suggestion?

Worked example.
“The dog is blue in the painting.”
This is an observation.

Now label:

  1. “The boy might feel sad.” .
  2. “There are connecting lines.” .
  3. “The village must be a reserve.” .

Self-check

  1. Suggestion.
  2. Observation.
  3. Unsupported claim. The image alone does not establish this.

Speaking: your route

Worked example for speaking.
I can see a bird. The lines might show a connection.

Route S · SEGPA

Use the Route C sentences below. Say the first sentence aloud. Then say the second one. You may read them from the page, one at a time.

Route C · CAP or CFA

Say:

I can see a boy and a dog.
The boy might feel alone.

Route P · Bac pro or CFA

Imagine that you are preparing an exhibition label.

Say:

This painting shows…
One possible meaning is…
Visitors should not assume…

Route G · Lycée général, LVA

Say four sentences. Include an observation, an interpretation, a comparison and a limit.

Route G · Lycée général, LVB

Say three sentences:

I can see…
This might mean…
I cannot prove…

Self-check

You should describe something visible and use might for an uncertain meaning.

Written record

I describe what I see. I use “might” for a possible meaning. I do not turn a guess into a fact.

Homework

Worked example.
Front: might.
Back: perhaps.

Make two cards: connect and alone.

Self-check

Connect = join; alone = without another person.
