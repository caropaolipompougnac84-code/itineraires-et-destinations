Lesson 1. Dignified power

How does a Queen who reigns but does not rule exercise power through her image?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 8 lessons.

  1. Recap (marked /3) 5 min
  2. You already know, then the stamp hook 10 min
  3. de László and steps 1 and 2 of the method 14 min
  4. Speaking: “A face on a stamp is a political act” 10 min
  5. Written record and exit ticket 8 min
  6. Homework given, last questions 5 min
  • Written record. A short definition of dignified power through image, and steps 1 and 2 applied to the 1933 portrait.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: one sentence agreeing or disagreeing with the stamp motion, or one concession.
  • Homework for next lesson. Find one official photograph of Elizabeth II on the Royal Collection Trust website (give the link, do not copy the picture) and apply steps 1 and 2.

Stopping point. End of this lesson.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson: dictate a short written record (two sentences); a quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each); write the homework on the board.

You already know (if you have studied The King Reigns, lesson 9 and Who Rules Britain?): Bagehot’s two parts of the constitution, dignified and efficient; the King reigns but does not rule; the sovereign’s three rights (to be consulted, to encourage, to warn). You may already have sorted ceremonies and Cabinet government into two columns, and read Bagehot’s own words, including the idea that royalty lives on mystery.

What is new here. We do not reread that extract, and we do not repeat the two-column sort. We start from the distinction you already have and ask how Elizabeth II used a face (on stamps, coins, portraits and screens) as the main instrument of dignified power.

Recap · marked /3
  1. In one sentence, what is the difference between reigning and ruling?
  2. Which part of the constitution, in Bagehot’s vocabulary, wins reverence?
  3. Name one official image of a monarch that you have already seen in another lesson.

Answer key

  1. To reign is to be the official head of state; to rule is to take the decisions of government.
  2. The dignified part (the Crown, the ceremonies, the face that is seen).
  3. Accept any accurate example from The King Reigns or Who Rules Britain? (a coronation, a King’s Speech, a portrait already studied).

Hook. Show a British stamp or coin with the Queen’s profile (the Machin profile, used on stamps from 1967). Why put a face on money? A face on stamps and coins makes the State personal and familiar. It creates loyalty without any political choice.

Queen from 6 February 1952 to 8 September 2022: the longest reign in British history. Platinum Jubilee in 2022.

Speaking (2 minutes). “A face on a stamp is a political act.” Agree or disagree. Use one concession.

Answer key · model points

A face on stamps and coins makes the State personal and familiar; it creates loyalty without any political choice. Bagehot thought people obey because they respect a symbol, not because they understand institutions. Counter-argument: in a modern democracy, legitimacy should come from elections, not reverence. A good answer uses a concession (Admittedly…, nevertheless…).

Language for argument

Use these structures in your speaking and writing (not as isolated drills). Practise them in every production task.

  • Concession. Admittedly…; It is true that…; nevertheless…; even so…
  • Hedging. It could be argued that…; appears to; may; seems to.
  • Hypothesis. If the monarchy had remained invisible, …; If she had spoken sooner, …
Exit ticket
  1. In your own words: how can an image do “dignified” work?
  2. Write one concession about the face on the stamp.

Homework

Find one official photograph of Elizabeth II on the Royal Collection Trust website (do not copy the picture). Apply steps 1 and 2 of the method in a short paragraph, and give the link.
