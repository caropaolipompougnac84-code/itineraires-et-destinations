Lesson 6. Living in Australia

How do you share a house, stay safe at the beach, and plan longer journeys?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 8 lessons.

  1. Recap (marked /3) 5 min
  2. Finding a place to live and a rent calculation 12 min
  3. Beach safety, a barbie, and dos and don’ts 16 min
  4. The Outback and Cairns 12 min
  5. Task, written record and homework 7 min
  • Written record. Four weeks at AUD 300 is AUD 1,200, but a calendar month is not always four weeks; swim between the red and yellow flags; inspect a room before you pay.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: one house rule, or one beach-safety rule, or one thing to check before an Outback journey.
  • Homework for next lesson. Write three house rules and two beach-safety rules. Add flashcards: bond, barbie, flags.

Stopping point. End of this lesson.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson: dictate a short written record (two sentences); a quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each); write the homework on the board.

Recap · marked /3
  1. What is specified work, in one sentence?
  2. How long is the usual requirement for a second subclass 417 visa?
  3. What is a White Card for?

Answer key

  1. Eligible work, in the right industry and postcode, counted under Home Affairs rules; not any farm job.
  2. Three months, normally at least 88 calendar days of eligible full-time-equivalent work. Recheck the official page.
  3. General construction induction training, required for construction work.

Find a place to live

Compare flatsharing options and inspect accommodation before paying. Check the agreement, bond, bills, house rules and the identity of the person offering the room. Do not pay a rental deposit to an unverified person.

Advertisements may quote rent per week. For this classroom example only, a room costs AUD 300 per week: four weeks cost AUD 1,200, but a calendar month is not always four weeks. Ask whether utilities are included and what payment schedule the agreement requires. This is a calculation exercise, not a current rental listing.

Beach safety

At a patrolled beach, swim between the red and yellow flags. Follow lifeguard instructions, check local warnings and protect yourself from the sun. Conditions differ between locations.

Meet people at a barbie

Dos

  • Greet people politely: “How’s it going?”
  • Listen and use local expressions naturally.
  • Respect wildlife and local guidance.
  • Ask when you do not understand.
  • Keep agreements about shared chores.

Don’ts

  • Do not assume everyone wants to be called “mate”.
  • Do not pressure people to discuss personal finances or politics.
  • Do not assume a tip is compulsory.
  • Do not pay a rental deposit to an unverified person.
  • Do not swim outside the patrolled area against advice.

Plan an Outback journey

Distances can be large. Check fuel, water, communication, weather and road conditions. Tell someone your route and arrival plan.

Discover Cairns

Your task. Calculate four weeks of rent at AUD 300 per week. Write three house rules and two beach-safety rules.

Answer key

AUD 1,200 for four weeks. Possible rules: clean shared areas, discuss guests, respect quiet hours; swim between the flags and follow lifeguard instructions.

Exit ticket
  1. How much is four weeks of rent at AUD 300 a week?
  2. Where should you swim at a patrolled beach?

Answer key

  1. AUD 1,200. A calendar month is not always four weeks.
  2. Between the red and yellow flags, and follow lifeguard instructions.

Homework

Write three house rules and two beach-safety rules in your notebook. Add flashcards: bond, barbie, red and yellow flags.
