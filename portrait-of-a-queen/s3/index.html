Lesson 3. Crowned on camera, 2 June 1953

The Crown, Season 1, Episode 5, "Smoke and Mirrors", and the real coronation.

1The teacher’s briefing8 min

For the teacher

To recount a ceremony in which what matters is what is done to the Queen (she is crowned, anointed, filmed), students need the passive, in every tense. Start from “She was crowned in Westminster Abbey”, have the class decline the passive in a table, then introduce the impersonal passive (It is said that… / She is said to have…).

Starting point

On 2 June 1953 Elizabeth II was crowned in Westminster Abbey. The ceremony was broadcast live, but the anointing was not filmed.

The passive: be + past participle, in every tense

TenseExample
present simpleThe crown is kept in the Tower of London.
present continuousThe coronation is being filmed.
present perfectThe dress has been shown in several exhibitions.
past simpleShe was crowned in 1953.
past continuousThe abbey was being prepared for months.
past perfectThe ceremony had been rehearsed many times.
futureThe day will be remembered.
future perfectBy noon the crown will have been placed on her head.
modalThe anointing must not be filmed. It could have been televised.

Impersonal passive (formal, C1)

It is said that 27 million people watched. / The coronation is said to have been watched by 27 million people in the UK.

Help: passive = be (in the right tense) + past participle: is crowned, was crowned, has been crowned.

2The document8 min

Official video: The Crown | Official Trailer | Netflix (Netflix, YouTube)
No official Netflix clip of the coronation scene is available: watch the official Season 1 trailer, then study the lines below from Season 1, Episode 5, "Smoke and Mirrors" (2016).
Philip: Are you my wife or my queen?
Elizabeth: I am both. And a strong man would be able to kneel to both.The Crown, S1E5 "Smoke and Mirrors", Netflix, 2016 (as quoted by Harper's Bazaar). Short quotation for study.

The real day. Elizabeth II was crowned in Westminster Abbey on 2 June 1953. Her coronation was the first to be broadcast live on television; according to the Royal Collection Trust, 27 million people in the United Kingdom watched. The most sacred moment, the anointing with holy oil, was not shown: it took place under a canopy, away from the cameras. In the series, Philip, who chairs the coronation committee, pushes for television and resists kneeling to his wife; in reality he was the first layman to pay homage to the new Queen.

Little-known facts.
  • The Queen chose Norman Hartnell, who had made her wedding dress, and she shaped the design herself. She picked the eighth of his eight designs, asked for coloured silks to enrich the traditional white and gold, and insisted that the emblems of the United Kingdom (Tudor rose, thistle, shamrock, leek) be joined by those of the Dominions: the wattle of Australia, the silver fern of New Zealand, the maple leaf of Canada, the lotus of India and Ceylon, wheat, cotton and jute for Pakistan, the protea of South Africa. Hartnell added an embroidered four-leaf clover for luck, placed on the skirt where her left hand could touch it. Source: Royal Collection Trust.
  • In the BBC documentary The Coronation (2018), the Queen called the ride in the Gold State Coach "horrible": "It's only sprung on leather, not very comfortable." Of the Imperial State Crown she said: "You can't look down to read the speech, you have to take the speech up. Because if you did, your neck would break, it would fall off." Source: The Guardian, 12 January 2018.
The photographs. Cecil Beaton took the official coronation portraits. They are protected by copyright, so we do not reproduce them: see them on the Royal Collection Trust website (search "Cecil Beaton coronation").
What the coronation says about the monarchy. In her coronation oath the Queen promised to govern "according to their respective laws and customs": in a constitutional monarchy the sovereign reigns but does not rule. For her role as head of state and for the Crown's property, open the Monarchy and property page.

Understanding the document

  1. What was new about this coronation?
  2. Which moment was hidden from the cameras, and why?
  3. What does the exchange between Philip and Elizabeth reveal?
  4. Why is the four-leaf clover a touching detail?
  5. How does the real Queen's humour in 2018 contrast with the solemn image of 1953?
Answers
  1. It was the first to be broadcast live on television.
  2. The anointing with holy oil, the most sacred part, under a canopy.
  3. The tension between private marriage and public role: as Queen she ranks above her husband.
  4. It is a private, secret wish for luck hidden in a public, symbolic garment.
  5. She describes the coach as "horrible" and jokes about breaking her neck: the myth meets everyday discomfort.

4Grammar in practice

From recognition to open production.

RecognitionExercise 3. Active or passive? Which tense?

Identify.

  1. The anointing was not filmed.
  2. The crown is kept in the Tower.
  3. Hartnell had designed eight dresses.
  4. The dress has been displayed many times.
  5. The abbey was being decorated.
  6. The anthem will be sung.
Answers
  1. passive, past simple
  2. passive, present simple
  3. active, past perfect
  4. passive, present perfect
  5. passive, past continuous
  6. passive, future

Controlled practiceExercise 4. Put into the passive

Keep the same tense.

  1. Hartnell designed the dress.
  2. They are filming the procession.
  3. The Archbishop had rehearsed the service.
  4. Millions will remember this day.
  5. Someone must polish the coach.
  6. They have kept the dress in the Royal Collection.
Answers
  1. The dress was designed by Hartnell.
  2. The procession is being filmed.
  3. The service had been rehearsed by the Archbishop.
  4. This day will be remembered by millions.
  5. The coach must be polished.
  6. The dress has been kept in the Royal Collection.

TransformationExercise 5. Impersonal passive

Rewrite in two ways (It is said that... / X is said to...).

  1. People say the coach weighs about four tonnes.
  2. People believe Hartnell added a clover for luck.
  3. People report that 27 million watched.
  4. Experts think the crown is very heavy.
Answers
  1. It is said that the coach weighs about four tonnes. The coach is said to weigh about four tonnes.
  2. It is believed that Hartnell added... Hartnell is believed to have added a clover for luck.
  3. It is reported that 27 million watched. 27 million people are reported to have watched.
  4. It is thought that the crown is very heavy. The crown is thought to be very heavy.

5Figures of speech

  • Chiasmus and parallelism: "Are you my wife or my queen?" "I am both." Two roles, one answer.
  • Metonymy: the Crown for the monarchy and the state; the throne for royal power.
  • Symbol: the orb (the world under God), the four-leaf clover (luck).

Exercise 7. Metonymy or symbol?

Explain.

  1. "The Crown has decided to..."
  2. The orb with its cross.
  3. "Downing Street said..."
  4. A white dress for purity.
Answers
  1. metonymy (the monarchy, the institution)
  2. symbol (Christian rule over the world)
  3. metonymy (the Prime Minister's office)
  4. symbol

6Idioms

to roll out the red carpet
to welcome somebody with great ceremony.
From the red carpets laid for important guests.
London rolled out the red carpet for the Commonwealth leaders.
pomp and circumstance
formal, splendid ceremony.
From Shakespeare, Othello ("pride, pomp, and circumstance of glorious war"), later the title of Elgar's marches.
The coronation was all pomp and circumstance.
to stand on ceremony
to insist on formal behaviour.
Usually negative: Don't stand on ceremony (relax).
Please, don't stand on ceremony: call me Liz.
to crown it all
on top of everything else (good or bad).
Figurative use of the final act of a coronation.
It rained, the coach was uncomfortable and, to crown it all, the crown weighed over two kilos.

Exercise 8. Idioms in context

Complete.

  1. The hotel ______ for the celebrity.
  2. We missed the train, lost our bags and, ______, it started snowing.
  3. Relax, you don't have to ______ here.
  4. The royal wedding was all ______.
Answers
  1. rolled out the red carpet
  2. to crown it all
  3. stand on ceremony
  4. pomp and circumstance

7Expression task7 min

Two voices. Pair work: A is a BBC commentator in 1953 (enthusiastic, passive voice); B is the Queen in 2018 remembering the day (ironic, active voice). Prepare and perform a 2-minute dialogue.

Exit ticket · formative check
  1. Passive, present perfect: They have shown the dress.
  2. Impersonal passive: People say she was nervous.
  3. Name one secret detail of the coronation dress.
Answers
  1. The dress has been shown.
  2. She is said to have been nervous.
  3. A four-leaf clover where her left hand would rest.
