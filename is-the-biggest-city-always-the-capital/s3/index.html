Lesson 3. A capital built for a deal

Explain why Washington, D.C. was built, with the passive, and recall the Juneau mystery without repeating it.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 7 lessons, plus a final-task assessment.

  1. Recap of lesson 2 4 min
  2. Read: a capital built for a deal, then check you understand 16 min
  3. America Faceted: what holds the states together 8 min
  4. Juneau: a short recall, then two new questions 10 min
  5. Grammar: the passive 14 min
  • Written record. Washington, D.C. was chosen after a deal in 1790: the North got the war debts taken over, the South got a capital on the Potomac. It is a federal district, not a state capital. The site was chosen by George Washington in 1791.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student answers one question on the 1790 deal, or puts one sentence into the passive.
  • Homework for next lesson. Write a short paragraph (80 to 100 words): “Why was Washington, D.C. built?” Use at least three verbs in the passive and two connectors (as a result, however, whereas).

Stopping point. After America Faceted, keep the passive exercise even if the Juneau recall is reduced to the fact and one question.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson inside the 52 minutes (this is not 52 plus 8). Dictate a short written record (two sentences). A quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each). Write the homework on the board.

You already know the three-step Juneau mystery if you have studied The Capital Trap, lesson 4. This lesson does not repeat that photo sequence. After the new document on Washington, D.C., a short recall is enough, with two questions that go further: why Juneau is still the capital, and the move from Sitka in 1906.

Recap · 4 min

  1. What is the capital of Texas? And the biggest city?
  2. Complete: New York City is almost ninety times ___ big ___ Albany.
  3. How do you pronounce the end of the verb “to relocate”?

Answer key

  1. Austin is the capital; Houston is the biggest city.
  2. as… as
  3. /eɪt/

Read: a capital built for a deal · 16 min

After independence, the young United States had no permanent capital. Congress met in several cities, including New York and Philadelphia.

In 1790, northern and southern leaders made a deal. The national government would take over the war debts of the states, as the North wanted, and the new capital would be built in the South, on the Potomac River, as the South wanted. The Residence Act of July 1790 gave President George Washington the task of choosing the exact site. He chose it in 1791, and the new city was named after him.

Until the new city was ready, Philadelphia served as the temporary capital, from 1790 to 1800. Congress met in Washington for the first time in November 1800. Today Washington, D.C. is not part of any state: it is a federal district. With about 690,000 inhabitants (2020 census), it is much smaller than New York City.

Check you understand

  1. Where did Congress meet before Washington, D.C. existed?
  2. What did the North get from the deal of 1790? And the South?
  3. Who chose the exact site of the city?
  4. Why is Washington, D.C. not a state capital?
  5. Find two verbs in the passive. Why does the author use the passive?

Answer key

  1. In several cities, including New York and Philadelphia.
  2. The North got the national government to take over the war debts of the states; the South got the capital on the Potomac River.
  3. President George Washington.
  4. Because it is not part of any state: it is a federal district, the capital of the whole country.
  5. “would be built”, “was named”. The passive puts the focus on the city and the result, not on the people who acted.

America Faceted · 8 min

The United States is a country of many facets. It is made up of 50 states, and each one has its own constitution, its own laws and its own capital. Very often, that capital is not the biggest city: Albany, not New York City, is the capital of New York State, and Sacramento, not Los Angeles, is the capital of California. So what holds such a fragmented country together? The answer is a short but carefully crafted text, the Constitution, written in Philadelphia in 1787. It is the oldest written national constitution still in use. It is built on federalism: power is shared between the federal government in Washington and the governments of the states.

Expanding your vocabulary

  • fragmented: divided into many separate parts
  • federalism: a system that shares power between a national government and states
  • to hold together: to keep separate parts united
  1. Name three things that each state has.
  2. Give one example of a state capital that is not the biggest city of its state.
  3. What holds the country together, and how does federalism work?

Answer key

  1. Its own constitution, its own laws and its own capital.
  2. Albany (not New York City) in New York State, or Sacramento (not Los Angeles) in California.
  3. The Constitution of 1787, the oldest written national constitution still in use. Federalism shares power between the federal government in Washington and the governments of the states.

Juneau: a short recall · 10 min

The fact: Juneau cannot be reached by road from the rest of North America. You go there by plane or by boat. Anchorage is much bigger.

  1. Imagine two reasons why Juneau is still the capital.
  2. The capital of Alaska was moved from Sitka to Juneau in 1906. Would you like to live in a capital without a road to the outside world?

Answer key

  1. Open answers: tradition, the cost of moving the government, the history of the gold rush.
  2. Open answer.

Grammar: the passive · 14 min

Rewrite the sentences in the passive, then say why the passive is useful in history texts (see also the language page).

  1. George Washington chose the site in 1791.
  2. Congress moved the government to Washington in 1800.
  3. Architects planned the city on the Potomac.

Answer key

  1. The site was chosen (by George Washington) in 1791.
  2. The government was moved to Washington in 1800.
  3. The city was planned on the Potomac.

The passive puts the focus on the place or the event, not on the person.

Written record. Washington, D.C. was chosen after a deal in 1790: the North got the war debts taken over, the South got a capital on the Potomac. It is a federal district, not a state capital. The site was chosen by George Washington in 1791.
