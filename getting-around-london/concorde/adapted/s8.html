Step 2

Today's language goal

To report what a documentary suggests without claiming more than it proves, we need hedging: probably, may have, is believed to, seems to.

Part A1. Listening (20 minutes): « The Mitrokhin Archive »

Document. Extract from the documentary, 34:55 to 38:25 (3 minutes 30), not studied in class. You will hear it three times, with one minute between listenings. Take notes as you wish.

Task. Write an account in French of what you understood: who, what, where, when, how, why. Then explain how these revelations shed light on the supersonic race.

Open this extract on YouTube if it does not play here. PBS America, Concorde: The Untold Story, Part 1 (2023), English subtitles available.

Expected points by level (students write in French)

A1: isolated words: Russian, 1991, British Embassy, suitcase, KGB, Concorde, Moscow.

A2: in 1991, a Russian, Vasili Mitrokhin, turns up at the British Embassy in Riga, Latvia, with a suitcase of secret KGB documents.

B1: he claims to be a defector and a KGB archivist; under the clothes and food (« some sausages ») there are top secret documents; he says he has tens of thousands more pages hidden around his cottage near Moscow; MI6 sends agents to check: it is true. Concorde was a target of Soviet espionage.

B2: he copied the archive and smuggled handwritten notes in his shoes and clothes, put them in milk churns and buried them in his garden; the archive is « absolutely explosive », with operations still live when the Soviet Union collapsed; it is held at Churchill College, Cambridge, with restricted access; a Soviet government body collected industrial espionage, and Soviet industry could request information on Western systems; « One specific target was Concorde »; the KGB networks probably informed Moscow of Concorde's delays: « The Russians had the dates for Concorde [...] they just made sure they did it first ».

C1: the implicit meaning: espionage may explain why the Tu-144 was ordered into the air before the end of 1968; the narrator is careful (« probably »); the irony of a « technological » victory partly built on stolen information; the responsibility of states in the race.

Step 3

Part A2. Reading (32 minutes): quiet supersonic flight

One year ago, NASA's Quesst mission made history with the first flight of the X-59 quiet supersonic research aircraft. Since then, the X-59 has completed 40 additional flights, marking a year of steady progress for the program. While its first flight on Oct. 28, 2025, focused on low, slow flights to conduct systems checks, the X-59 has since reached a maximum altitude of more than 56,000 feet and speeds up to Mach 1.53. The X-59 has spent the past year expanding its flight envelope, flying at the altitudes, speeds, and conditions that will be utilized for all aspects of the Quesst mission. The aircraft's team will continue that work in the coming months, but also transition toward a new phase of testing: acoustic validation, which will focus on analyzing the sounds the X-59 makes. The X-59 is shaped so that when it flies at its intended quiet supersonic altitude of 55,000 feet and speed of Mach 1.4, people on the ground won't hear a loud sonic boom; they'll hear a thump projected to sound like a car door closing from about 20 feet away. During future flight tests, NASA will study and record those sonic thumps. [...] This speed and altitude are the base conditions for the X-59 when it will eventually fly over several U.S. communities enabling NASA to gather data about how people may perceive its quiet thump. NASA will share this data with U.S. and international regulators to help establish new data-driven noise standards to enable a future viable market for supersonic commercial flight over land. NASA, Quesst mission blog, « NASA Celebrates a Year of Progress Since First X-59 Flight », 28 October 2026 (paragraphs 1 to 4), and « NASA's X-59 Aircraft Flies Supersonic for First Time », 5 June 2026 (last paragraph). Public domain (NASA). One punctuation mark adapted in paragraph 4.

Glossary. a thump: un bruit sourd; the flight envelope: le domaine de vol; regulators: les autorités de régulation; data-driven: fondé sur des données.

A. Account (in French). Present the project, its results, its aim and what is at stake, and link it to the story of Concorde.

B. Answer in English.

  1. Give the date of the X-59's first flight and the number of flights completed since then.
  2. True or false? Justify with a quote: the X-59 was designed to fly faster than Concorde.
  3. What will people on the ground hear instead of a sonic boom? Quote the comparison.
  4. Why will NASA fly the X-59 over U.S. communities?
  5. Explain « a future viable market for supersonic commercial flight over land ». Why was this not possible for Concorde?

Expected points for the account

A, B1: NASA's quiet supersonic research aircraft (Quesst mission); first flight on 28 October 2025; 40 more flights in a year; up to Mach 1.53 and more than 56,000 feet. B2: next phase, acoustic validation; a shape designed to replace the boom with a thump; flights over U.S. communities to measure how people perceive the noise; data shared with regulators to set new noise standards and open a market for supersonic flight over land. C1: the innovation answers Concorde's main limit (the sonic boom kept it to ocean routes); an approach based on measurement and public acceptance rather than national prestige, unlike the race of the 1960s.

Answers B

  1. 28 October 2025; 40 additional flights.
  2. False: « speeds up to Mach 1.53 », intended speed « Mach 1.4 »: slower than Concorde (Mach 2).
  3. « a thump projected to sound like a car door closing from about 20 feet away »
  4. « to gather data about how people may perceive its quiet thump »
  5. A profitable market for supersonic passenger flights over land, once noise standards allow it. Concorde's sonic boom was too loud: it could only fly supersonic over the ocean.

Grammar lesson: hedging

To report what a documentary suggests without claiming more than it proves, we need hedging
On the board, to copy

Rule

Not sure? Say probably or may have.

Form

  • adverb: The KGB networks probably informed Moscow of Concorde's delays.
  • may have + past participle: Espionage may have helped the Tu-144 fly first.
  • is believed to: The archive is believed to contain ...
  • seems to have + past participle: The Russians seem to have known Concorde's dates.

Examples from the lesson

  • “The KGB networks probably informed Moscow of Concorde's delays.”
  • “Espionage may have helped the Tu-144 fly first.”
  • “It is believed that the KGB knew the dates.”

Common mistakes

  • Not The KGB stole Concorde., but The KGB probably obtained information on Concorde.
  • Not may helped, but may have helped

Quick check (2 minutes, hands up or mini whiteboards)Which sentence is careful?

  1. The KGB stole Concorde.
  2. Espionage may have helped the Tu-144.

Answer key

Sentence 2 (may have).

Exercises

EntryRecognise. Underline the hedging words.

  1. The Russians seem to have known Concorde's dates.
  2. Espionage probably played a part in the race.

Answer key

1. seem to have known. 2. probably.

CoreTransform. Make these claims careful.

  1. Espionage explained the Soviet victory.
  2. The Russians knew Concorde's dates.

Answer key

1. Espionage may have explained part of the Soviet victory. 2. The Russians seem to have known Concorde's dates.

StretchProduce. In thirty seconds, give one fact, one hypothesis and one careful conclusion about Concorde and Soviet espionage.

Answer key

For example: The Tu-144 flew before Concorde, on 31 December 1968. Soviet spies may have helped. It is likely that espionage saved time, but it cannot explain everything.

Sentence starters:

  • We know that ...

  • Espionage may have ...

Model answer

We know that the Tu-144 flew on 31 December 1968, before Concorde. The KGB probably informed Moscow of Concorde's delays, and espionage may have helped the Soviets fly first. But we cannot be sure how much it changed: the archive shows the intention, not every result.
