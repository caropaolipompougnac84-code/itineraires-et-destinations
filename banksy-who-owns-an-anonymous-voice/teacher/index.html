Teacher's guide

This guide is for the teacher. Students use the lesson pages, the student pack and, if they need it, the dys version. The sequence was designed by Madame Carole Paoli Pompougnac and laid out for the site by Grok.

1. What this sequence will not do

  • It will not present Robin Gunningham, or the later name David Jones, as a confirmed civil identity. Both names are press claims. The accurate classroom sentence is on the presentation page and in Lesson 2.
  • It will not ask students to find a private address, to identify relatives, to contact a private person, or to "solve" the artist's identity.
  • It will not flatten a documented Reuters investigation, published on 13 March 2026, into "just a rumour". Students learn to say according to, and to keep a well-supported claim in the category of a claim.
  • It will not treat a High Court decision about one physical wall, in 2015, as a universal rule about every mural.
  • It will not treat a sale price as proof of the artist's intention.

The original request for this sequence mentioned a Reuters investigation in 2025. The investigation located for the sequence was filed on 13 March 2026. The date is corrected throughout.

2. Photographs and rights

A Creative Commons or public-domain licence for a photograph does not remove rights in the artwork that was photographed. Pest Control is Banksy's official contact and the body that issues certificates of authenticity for eligible works. It does not, according to its own FAQ, certify illegally executed works, and it does not treat the removal of a street mural as a normal way to obtain a certificate.

Every photograph on the site is credited in its caption (photographer, licence, Wikimedia Commons page). The photographs were downloaded into the page folder. No photograph already used elsewhere on itineraires-destinations.fr was reused here. Dismaland was a multi-artist project: captions warn teachers and students not to attribute every object to Banksy. The Girl with Balloon photograph is a reconstructed mural in a Brussels exhibition, not the original 2002 London wall; the caption says so. The Irpin gymnast is not the Borodianka handstand gymnast. Interpretations of the judo mural that name a political leader remain interpretations.

Pest Control is Banksy's official contact and the body that authenticates eligible works. Its FAQ states that it does not issue certificates for illegally executed works, and reporting about the organisation describes a policy against authenticating street pieces removed from their intended locations. Mention this gently, as a fact about authentication, not as a moral lecture.

3. The two pathways

Every lesson uses the same images, the same videos and the same questions. The A2 pathway offers frames, shorter outputs and more explicit support. The B1 pathway asks for connected explanation, attribution, qualification and a response to an opposing view. The diagnostic in Lesson 1 is not marked; it is used to place students. A student can move from one pathway to the other later. Première classes can take the B1 pathway as the main track, and still use the A2 frames as a help box.

The 2019 programme identifies B1+ as the examination expectation for LVA in the vocational baccalaureate, A2+ for LVB, and A2 for the relevant CAP assessment. The two pathways are teaching pathways, not a claim about every student's final examination target.

3b. Ancrage dans le BO: activités langagières, descripteurs and evaluation

The sequence is written for the programme de langues vivantes of the voie professionnelle, BO spécial n° 5 du 11 avril 2019 (arrêté du 3 avril 2019). It does not use the numbered cultural axes of the lycée général et technologique. Each lesson page names the activités langagières it practises, and the CECRL descriptors visés, A2 and B1. The presentation page gives the full tables. The mapping below is for the teacher.

Evaluation logic (CCF / épreuve)

For the baccalauréat professionnel, candidates in établissements publics or privés sous contrat, and apprentices in habilitated CFA, are assessed by CCF. Other candidates take a terminal examination. Each of the five activités langagières (compréhension de l'oral, compréhension de l'écrit, expression écrite, expression orale en continu, interaction orale) counts for one fifth of the mark. Médiation is in the 2019 programme, from the CECRL Companion Volume; it is taught here, especially in Lessons 2 and 6, but it is not a sixth scored CCF activity.

Situation A (1 hour): oral comprehension (document of at most one minute thirty, three listenings, compte rendu in French); written comprehension (unknown authentic document, at most fifteen lines, compte rendu in French); written production (choice of two subjects, in English, at least 100 to 120 words). Situation B (10 minutes): at most five minutes of continuous presentation, on a piece of work, a project, a product, a service or a professional experience, then at most five minutes of interview. The expected level at the end of the cycle is B1+ (LVA) and A2+ (LVB).

This sequence is for Seconde. It trains those activities and uses grids aligned with official-style criteria: realisation of the task, organisation, lexical range and accuracy, linguistic accuracy, ease (continuous speaking), and, for interaction, understanding the partner, reacting, and keeping the exchange going. The mid-point and final orals approximate Situation B (a mediator presenting a work, as one would present a project or a service). The final written label trains expression écrite. Listenings and source texts train comprehension, as classroom practice, not as a full mock paper under exact examination timing. After each oral, the student notes what was missed and produces an improved version.

The visual slogan, as a teaching thread

Banksy's works are treated throughout as speech acts: slogans in the form of drawings. In every lesson, students answer four questions: what does the image say, to whom, in what tone, and with which visual devices (stencil, written words, official look, placement, contrast, a real object, a contradiction between words and place). A lexical bank is introduced in Lesson 1 (visual slogan, to denounce, to mock, irony, tone, target, placement) and is recycled later. The final task begins with this reading, before identity and ownership. Do not let the four questions become a mechanical form: at B1, students must justify the tone and the device from what is visible.

4. Timing

Each lesson lasts 55 minutes and is planned over 52 minutes, with three minutes for arrival and transitions. A checkpoint at minute 44 tells you what every student must already have done. The eight-minute essential closing route replaces the last eight minutes when the class needs more time. It does not add eight minutes. Every lesson ends with a written record, an oral contribution from every student, and homework.

  • Shredding film, banksyfilm: youtube.com/watch?v=vxkwRNIZgdY, 00:00 to 02:00, shown twice at most. Preview it, and check local access. It is the artist's presentation of the event, not an independent investigation.

Teacher-written listening texts are original teaching materials. Tell students so, and do not present them as quotations from Banksy, Reuters or Pest Control. They are based on the referenced issues.

6. Assessments

Diagnostic, Lesson 1: not marked, used to place students on a pathway. Mid-point oral, Lesson 3: Situation B practice (continuous speaking then a partner's objection), A2 and B1 grids aligned with official-style descriptors, coefficient 1. Summative, Lesson 6: written label and recommendation (expression écrite, Situation A practice) plus oral continuous speaking and interaction (Situation B practice), A2 and B1 grids aligned with official-style descriptors, coefficient 2. Every grid has a language criterion. After the oral, each student notes what was missed and writes an improved version. For Lesson 6, record pairs simultaneously if you cannot hear thirty presentations live. None of these classroom assessments is the CCF itself.

Do not require public social-media accounts or public posting. School-approved recording only, submitted privately.

7. Legal and civic caution, in brief

The Creative Foundation v Dreamland Leisure Ltd, 11 September 2015, concerned a physical section of wall bearing Art Buff. The EUIPO decision of 25 October 2022 (case R 1246/2021-5), about a trademark in a monkey image, addresses trademark law and rejects the idea that a pseudonym necessarily makes copyright unenforceable. It does not settle every copyright question, and it does not say that all Banksy trademarks are valid. Mention it only if a confident B1 group asks; do not turn it into a general rule.

8. Homework and flashcards

Homework is given at the end of every lesson. For more fragile groups, prefer short oral tasks (a recorded sentence, a rehearsed turn) and flashcard revision. Students make their own cards in every lesson. Colour code, if you use one: navy for theme vocabulary, gold for words to argue, a third mark (for example a simple box) for grammar structures. Avoid a green code if you are working on a navy English sequence and want the page to stay consistent.

9. The dys version

The dys version can be used alone, including after an absence, without an AESH. Texts are shorter, consignes go step by step, a worked example precedes each exercise, and corrigés are there for self-assessment. The ambition of the questions is the same. Italics are avoided; important words are in bold.

10. What was hedged or corrected from the raw research

  • The Reuters date is 13 March 2026, not 2025.
  • The Irpin gymnast with a ribbon is not the Borodianka handstand gymnast. Some summaries confuse the two.
  • The judo figure is not identified as a named political leader in this sequence.
  • The 2021 sale of Love is in the Bin is given as a reported price of about 18.6 million pounds, as a market event, not as a window into intention.
  • Girl with Balloon, on this page, is an exhibition reconstruction. The original London mural of 2002 is described, not passed off as the photograph.
