Lesson 3. Who Is Represented?

Goal: I can compare Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 3 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Split the lesson into two blocks: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Say one sentence from the previous lesson (Lesson 1: what you already know about elections) 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Read the goal and the word box 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Read or listen to your route’s text 8 min (minutes 8 to 16)
  4. Exercise 1, then check it 8 min (minutes 16 to 24)
  5. Pause: look away from the text or move 2 min (minutes 24 to 26)
  6. Exercise 2, then check it 8 min (minutes 26 to 34)
  7. Exercise 3, then check it 6 min (minutes 34 to 40)
  8. Say your final answer aloud with the speaking frame 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  9. Written record 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  10. Progress record 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  11. Read and write down the homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. One sentence for your route (see below), copied or typed.
  • Participation. Say at least one answer aloud. In class, use a quiet voice; at home, say it to yourself. You do not need to record yourself.
  • Homework for next lesson. Make one comparison card. Front: “One similarity?” Back: your accurate answer. Then make one difference card.

Stopping point. Minute 44: stop the main exercises. Keep the written record and the homework, even if an exercise is unfinished.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: say the emergency sentence of the lesson aloud.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: complete the short written record.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: write down the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: check one answer with the key. Do not rush through all the unfinished exercises.

The comparison is based on the official page of Puerto Rico’s Resident Commissioner and on the Twenty-third Amendment of the Constitution.

Word box

WordMeaning
territoryan area with a special legal link to a country
representativesomeone who speaks or acts for a group
senatora member of the Senate
presidential electora person who casts an electoral vote for president
whereasa word used to contrast two facts

Choose your route: Route S · SEGPA · Route C · CAP · Route P · Bac pro · CFA. Do only your route.

Route S · SEGPA

Short text

Puerto Rico is a US territory.

It has a representative in the House.

It has no senators.

Washington, D.C. has a representative too.

It also has no senators.

The two places are not states.

Exercise S1

True or false

Worked example. “Puerto Rico is a US territory.” True.

Now you.
1. Puerto Rico has senators.
2. Washington, D.C. is a state.

Self-check

1. False.
2. False.

Exercise S2

Complete

Worked example. “Puerto Rico has a representative.”

Now you.
Complete: “Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. have no .”

Self-check

senators.

Exercise S3

Compare

Worked example. “Both places have representatives.”

Now you.
Write one similarity. Say your answer aloud.

Self-check

Possible answers: Both places have representatives. Neither place has senators. Neither place is a state.

Route C · CAP

Short text

Puerto Rico elects a Resident Commissioner.

This representative can introduce bills and vote in committees.

However, the representative cannot vote on the final approval of a bill in the full House.

Washington, D.C. has a House delegate.

Neither place has senators.

Washington, D.C. has presidential electors.

Puerto Rico does not.

Exercise C1

Match

Worked example. “Resident Commissioner” goes with Puerto Rico.

Now you.
Match with Puerto Rico or Washington, D.C.:
1. House delegate.
2. No presidential electors.

Self-check

1. Washington, D.C.
2. Puerto Rico.

Exercise C2

Complete the contrast

Worked example. “Both places have House representation.”

Now you.
Complete: “Washington, D.C. has presidential electors, whereas .”

Self-check

“Washington, D.C. has presidential electors, whereas Puerto Rico does not.”

Exercise C3

Correct

Worked example. “Puerto Rico has no representation.” Correction: “Puerto Rico has a Resident Commissioner.”

Now you.
Correct: “The Resident Commissioner has no powers.”

Self-check

“The Resident Commissioner has powers, including introducing bills and voting in committees.”

Route P · Bac pro

Short text

Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. have federal representation, but not the same arrangements as states.

Neither has senators.

Puerto Rico’s Resident Commissioner has responsibilities in the House, including committee work.

However, the Resident Commissioner cannot vote on the final approval of a bill in the full House.

Washington, D.C. has presidential electors.

Puerto Rico does not.

These examples show why “representation” needs a precise explanation.

Exercise P1

Explain the difference

Worked example. “No representative” and “a representative with limited voting powers” do not mean the same thing.

Now you.
Explain why “Puerto Rico has no representation” is misleading.

Self-check

It ignores the Resident Commissioner and the powers of that office.

Exercise P2

Concession

Worked example. “Although the office has powers, it also has limits.”

Now you.
Complete: “Although Puerto Rico has a Resident Commissioner, .”

Self-check

Possible answer: “Although Puerto Rico has a Resident Commissioner, the office cannot vote on the final approval of a bill in the full House.”

Exercise P3

Write a comparison

Worked example. Opening: “The two places share some features, but their arrangements are not identical.”

Now you.
Write 60 to 80 words. Include whereas and one similarity.

Self-check

Mention: no senators, House representation, and the difference in presidential electors.

CFA pathway

CAP apprentice: use Route C. Bac pro apprentice: use Route P. Do Exercises 1 and 2 of your route, then do this task instead of Exercise 3. Keep the same written record and homework.

CFA task

Use precise information

Worked example. “A person can have a role with specific limits.”

Now you.
Correct: “Puerto Rico has nobody in Congress.”

Self-check

“Puerto Rico has a Resident Commissioner with specific powers and limits.”

Written record

S: “Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. have no senators.”

C: “They have representation, but their arrangements differ.”

P: “Representation is not simply present or absent. Its powers and limits matter.”

Speaking frame. “Both places , whereas .”

Emergency sentence. “Puerto Rico has representation, but the office has limits.”

Homework

Make one comparison card. Front: “One similarity?” Back: your accurate answer. Then make one difference card.
