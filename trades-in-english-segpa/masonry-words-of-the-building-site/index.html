Bricklaying: Building Site Vocabulary

Level: SEGPA, champ professionnel Habitat, 4e and 3e, masonry and building trades (gros œuvre)

Lesson: Expanding your vocabulary

You are in SEGPA, in 4e or 3e, in the champ professionnel Habitat. You are discovering masonry and the building trades. In this lesson you will use words for machines and tools on a building site (un chantier). You must put the words into sentences and into speech. Do not only copy them from the list.

2. On the building site: complete the dialogue

Read the dialogue. Fill each gap with one word or expression from the list. You may need a plural.

Mason: Good morning. Put on your (1) before you enter the site.

Apprentice: I have it on. Where do we start the wall?

Mason: Look at the (2) first. The wall starts here. Use the (3) to check that the first row is straight.

Apprentice: The sand is over there.

Mason: Yes. The (4) will bring more sand this morning. The (5) will lift the pallets. Keep away from the (6): it is digging the trench.

Apprentice: And the big machine with a blade at the front?

Mason: That is the (7). It is pushing the earth. The (8) will lift the beams up to the first floor.

Apprentice: Do I go up on the (9) to help with the high wall?

Mason: Not today. For the window, use the (10). Keep both feet on the steps. The (11) will arrive at ten with the mix for the floor.

3. Now speak: a short role-play

Work in pairs. One student is the mason. The other student is the apprentice. You are on a building site in the morning. You must use at least eight words from the word bank. Speak in full sentences. Then swap roles.

Before you start, write six lines you plan to say. Do not read a list of words: put each word into a sentence the other person can understand.

Word check. Tick each word when you use it in a sentence. You need at least eight.

4. The spirale de Gorse

Spirale de Gorse · Version courte, en quatre tours

Do a short spiral in four tours. First read the example. Then do the same with another word from the list.

Example: the crane

Tour 1. I notice. I see a tall yellow machine with a long arm and a hook.

Tour 2. I identify. This is a crane [grue]. It stands on the site and lifts heavy loads.

Tour 3. I understand. The crane is useful because blocks and beams are too heavy to carry by hand. People must stay away from the hook.

Tour 4. I find the meaning. On a building site, the crane helps the team to lift and place materials in safety.

Now you. Choose one other word from the list, for example scaffolding or hard hat. Write one or two short sentences for each tour.

Tour 1. I notice.

Tour 2. I identify.

Tour 3. I understand.

Tour 4. I find the meaning.

To finish Tour 4, you can use: The point is… / I keep to the text: … / I stay focused on… / Beyond this, other questions may arise and open up further exploration.

Task 4. The spirale de Gorse (model for scaffolding)

Tour 1. I notice. I see a metal structure with floors and a ladder, in front of a wall.

Tour 2. I identify. This is scaffolding [échafaudage]. Workers stand on it to work high up.

Tour 3. I understand. The scaffolding is useful because the wall is too high to reach from the ground. A hard hat is necessary on it.

Tour 4. I find the meaning. On a building site, scaffolding lets the team work in height with more safety.

A clear spiral on hard hat, excavator or another word from the list is also accepted.
