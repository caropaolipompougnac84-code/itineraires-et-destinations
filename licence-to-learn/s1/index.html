Lesson 1. The new James Bond: a convincing choice?

Activities in this lesson: CECompréhension écrite · POProduction orale · PEProduction écrite

Students can explain the official announcement; say whether the choice is convincing; and say what kind of Bond this actor could create, without turning the lesson into a biography.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Six lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure. Lesson 6 is the final-task interview.

  1. Pair starter: is this a convincing choice, and what kind of Bond could he create? 0 to 5
  2. Read Document A; complete the news grid 5 to 12
  3. Read the profiles; complete the candidate grid 12 to 21
  4. Grammar workshop and correction 21 to 31
  5. Prepare an evidence-based view of the choice 31 to 35
  6. Paired debate: is the choice convincing, and what kind of Bond could he create? 35 to 44
  7. Every student gives a final qualified view of the choice 44 to 47
  8. Set homework 47 to 48
  9. Complete the written record 48 to 52
  • Written record. Complete the five-sentence written record on the official announcement, earlier reporting, and the classroom debate.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student speaks for 30 to 40 seconds, then answers a partner question.
  • Homework for next lesson. three flashcards (successor, announcement, convincing) and 70 to 90 words: is this choice convincing, and what kind of Bond could he create?

Stopping point. Stopping point: minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, this eight-minute ending replaces unfinished extension work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 47: each student says whether the choice is convincing, and what kind of Bond he could create;
  • 47 to 48: homework;
  • 48 to 52: complete the written record.

CEStudent document A. The official announcement

Original classroom adaptation of current reporting:

On 16 November 2026, Amazon MGM Studios announced that Robert Pattinson will be the new James Bond. The classroom debate is whether this choice is convincing, and what kind of Bond he could create. His biography is studied in A Life in Roles.

Before this announcement, Callum Turner, Jack Lowden and Josh O’Connor had all appeared in reporting about the casting process. More recent coverage had also linked other actors to the role. A reported screen test was not an official casting announcement.

The official Bond website has announced Denis Villeneuve as director and Steven Knight as writer of the next film.

These facts were checked on 25 November 2026.

Teacher update rule. Date the news box on the day it is used. Do not use betting odds as evidence of a casting decision.

POCEStudent document B. Three actors discussed in the reporting

These short profiles describe career experience, not a confirmed shortlist.

Jack Lowden

Jack Lowden trained at the Royal Conservatoire of Scotland. He developed his career on stage and won an Olivier Award for his performance in Ghosts. He played a fighter pilot in Dunkirk and River Cartwright in Slow Horses. His work gives students examples of theatre, historical drama and screen espionage.

Callum Turner

Callum Turner began his career as a model before moving into acting. He played Theseus Scamander in the Fantastic Beasts films and appeared in The Capture. He also played a rower in The Boys in the Boat and a pilot in Masters of the Air. His career includes thrillers, fantasy and historical drama.

Josh O’Connor

Josh O’Connor trained at Bristol Old Vic Theatre School. He played Prince Charles in The Crown and won an Emmy for the role. His films include God’s Own Country and Challengers. Reports have linked him to Bond, but recent claims about his position in the casting process conflict.

Building your vocabulary and grammar workshop

Exercise 1. Finished past events

Complete in the past simple. Verbs: begin, play, train, win.

  1. Lowden a fighter pilot.
  2. Turner his career as a model.
  3. O’Connor an Emmy.
  4. O’Connor at Bristol Old Vic Theatre School.

Answer key

  1. played
  2. began
  3. won
  4. trained

POExercise 2. Past simple or present perfect?

Choose the correct form.

  1. Turner played / has played a rower in The Boys in the Boat in 2023.
  2. Several actors were linked / have been linked to Bond before the official announcement.
  3. O’Connor won / has won his Emmy in 2021.

Answer key

  1. played
  2. were linked
  3. won

Rule. Use the past simple with a finished past date, or for a situation that ended before a later event. Use the present perfect to connect an earlier event with a situation that is still open.

Exercise 3. Possibility is not certainty

Rewrite using might or could.

  1. Perhaps Pattinson will create a thoughtful Bond.
  2. Perhaps this actor will create a more vulnerable Bond.
  3. Perhaps this choice will convince the public.

Possible answers

  1. Pattinson might create a thoughtful Bond.
  2. This actor could create a more vulnerable Bond.
  3. This choice might convince the public.

Exercise 4. Compare responsibly

Complete.

  1. This argument is convincing than an argument based only on appearance.
  2. A confirmed announcement is reliable than a rumour.
  3. An actor’s previous role does not prove that they are the choice.

Answer key

  1. more
  2. more
  3. best

POProtected oral intervention

Each student speaks for 30 to 40 seconds.

On 16 November 2026, Amazon MGM Studios announced that Robert Pattinson will be the new James Bond.

I find this choice because .

He could create a Bond who .

His biography is studied in A Life in Roles.

However, my view is an opinion, not a fact.

Partner question: which detail supports your opinion?

Debate rule: discuss whether the choice is convincing, and what kind of Bond he could create. Do not turn the lesson into a biography, and do not rank people by attractiveness or repeat stereotypes as facts.

Homework

Compulsory:

  1. Make three flashcards: successor, announcement, convincing.
  2. PEWrite 70 to 90 words: is this choice convincing, and what kind of Bond could he create?
  3. Include one past-simple sentence, one comparison and one might/could sentence.

Extension: compare two headlines and explain which one expresses uncertainty more clearly.
