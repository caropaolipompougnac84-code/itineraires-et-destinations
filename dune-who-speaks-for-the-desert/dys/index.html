Dys version

This version can be used on its own, and after an absence. The font is a sans-serif. Headings are navy. There is one instruction on each line. Examples come before exercises. Keys are in a fold. Italics are not used; important words are in bold.

After an absence: read the written trace of each missed lesson (or the recap on the presentation page), make the two model flashcards of the lesson, do the exercise below, and check with the key.

Lesson 1. A Planet Called Dune

Frank Herbert wrote Dune in 1965.

Dune is a desert planet.

Water is very rare.

Step 1. Read the text.

Step 2. Underline the word water.

Step 3. Complete the sentence.

Example: On Arrakis, people must save water.

Exercise: On Arrakis, people must ________ (wear a suit / protect children / recycle water).

Key, Lesson 1

Any of the three is correct.

\n

Lesson 2. The Spice

The spice is a substance which exists only on Arrakis.

Everybody needs it.

Example: The Fremen are a people who live in the desert.

Exercise: The spice is a resource ________ the empire needs. (who / which)

Key, Lesson 2

which.

\n

Lesson 3. Whose Planet?

Strangers put a legend on Arrakis, long ago.

The Fremen believe it.

Example: Although Jessica is a stranger, the Fremen help her.

Exercise: Although the legend is false, ________.

Key, Lesson 3

the Fremen believe it / it gives power to Paul.

\n

Lesson 4. Making a Messiah

The prophecy was written by strangers.

Paul fulfils it.

Example: The legend was planted.

Exercise: People told the story. The story ________ told.

Key, Lesson 4

was.

\n

Lesson 5. Mid-sequence assessment

Use the student sheet of Lesson 5.

If you need a shorter writing task, answer in 80 words, with one example and one quotation.

Lesson 6. The Ecologist's Warning

Kynes is a scientist.

He wants to make the desert green.

He says a hero is dangerous.

Example: If the spice disappears, the Guild will stop.

Exercise: If the Fremen follow a hero, they ________ (lose / will lose) their voice.

Key, Lesson 6

will lose (or might lose).

\n

Lesson 7. The Hero Trap

Paul is "less than a god, more than a man".

Example: Paul is more powerful than the Emperor.

Exercise: A legend is ________ dangerous ________ an army.

Key, Lesson 7

more ... than (or as ... as).

\n

Final task, Lesson 8. Seven boxes

Use the seven boxes of the guided B1 model. Write one sentence in each box. Tick the box when it is done.

  1. Who are you? ☐
  2. What is positive? (use a passive) ☐
  3. Problem one. (use a passive) ☐
  4. Problem two. (use although) ☐
  5. One quotation. ☐
  6. A consequence. (use if) ☐
  7. Your decision. ☐

Self-assessment. Tick:

  • I said who I am. ☐
  • I gave one good point. ☐
  • I gave one problem. ☐
  • I used one quotation. ☐
  • I gave my decision. ☐
