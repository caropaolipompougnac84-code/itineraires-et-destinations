A Day in London · page enseignant

An everyday-life English game, from A1 to B1 · vocational lycée, CAP, years 6 to 9, also offered in general lycée.

Back to the sequence · Open the game · Gap-fill worksheets · Road map (9 lessons of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes)

The principle

The student follows Leo, 19, who comes from France and is going to London for his work placement. He buys his ticket on his own at the station in his town, takes the train and then the Eurostar, and his London friend Amy meets him on arrival; she then guides him and comes back in the dialogues. An introduction page presents the story and the rules. The student then moves Leo on the map and completes 9 missions, in the order of the story: catching the train (then discovering the Tube and the Oyster card with Amy in London), doing the shopping, booking a flight, making and then moving a doctor’s appointment, going to the chemist’s, asking the way, ordering at the café, phoning the work placement.

This is not a simple quiz. Each mission asks students to think and to take part. The student first makes their own choices (a menu, a destination, symptoms, a diary, a diet, a work-placement location), which the game uses again in the dialogue and the mini-game. They have to choose under constraints (a budget, a timetable, needs), justify, produce their own dialogue, then recall the words from memory.

The videos

Each mission begins with its episode of the video series A Day in London: 9 episodes of about 2 minutes, one per mission and in the order of the map (1 station and Tube, 2 supermarket, 3 travel agency, 4 call to the doctor’s surgery, 5 a change of plans at home, 6 chemist’s, 7 asking the way at Covent Garden, 8 café, 9 call to the work placement). Leo and Amy act out the situation; the narrator then has the key sentences repeated, with a pause to speak out loud. Each episode also exists in a vertical format for the phone. The Videos section of the game gathers them together. Video icons: Twemoji (CC BY 4.0).

How a mission unfolds (inside a 52-minute lesson)

On the road map of the sequence, a mission occupies the heart of the lesson (22 to 25 minutes), framed by the video, a speaking time in which everyone speaks at least once, the written record and the homework for the next lesson. The emergency exit (8 minutes, inside the 52 minutes) takes up these three outcomes if time runs short at the stopping point.

  • Your turn to choose: the situation and the personal choices, with the aims (« I can… »).
  • The words: 12 to 15 illustrated cards to turn over, with a natural British voice (the word and an example sentence); some cards also give the American variant.
  • Branching dialogue: the student chooses their reply. Each wrong answer is explained without judgement (politeness, false friends, grammar, consistency with their choices) and they try again.
  • Mini-game: drag and drop (mouse or finger), reading a board, a map, a diary. The result depends on the student’s choices.
  • Your turn: a personal written or spoken production (recorded in the browser or on a phone), with a model built from their choices and a checklist.
  • What I have learnt: 5 words or expressions to recall from memory, then a sentence « next time, I will say… ».

Points, stars (1 to 3, always at least one) and badges reward the effort. The notebook gathers the student’s words, choices and texts, and offers spaced revision (1, 3, 7, 14 days). The bristol cards of the sequence make it possible to revise the same words without a screen.

The 9 missions

The detail (aims, vocabulary, mini-game, production) is taken up here to prepare the class. The minute-by-minute plan is on the sequence page.

  1. Catch the right train (A1 towards A2, 16 words). Read a departures board, work out an arrival time, buy a single or a return, ask for the platform. In London, choose with Amy between an Oyster card and a paper ticket. Production: a conversation at the ticket office with their train, their Eurostar and their ticket.
  2. Shopping on a budget (A1 towards A2, 15 words). Shop with a budget and quantity words (a jar of, a loaf of…), pay, check the change, speak politely to the cashier. Mini-game: a basket according to a menu, a number of people and £12. Production: a conversation at the checkout.
  3. Compare flights and book (A2 towards B1, 15 words). Compare prices, times and stopovers, justify with because and comparatives, book. Mini-game: 3 flights. Production: a booking with their destination and their flight.
  4. Make a doctor's appointment (A1 towards A2, 14 words). Parts of the body and symptoms, phoning, for / since. Mini-game: 7 symptoms on the body. Production: a call to the surgery.
  5. Change of plans (A2 towards B1, 13 words). Apologise, explain, read a diary, suggest a new time. Production: a call to move the appointment.
  6. At the chemist's (A2, 14 words). Explain a small problem, ask for advice, reformulate a dosage (twice a day, every four hours…). Mini-game: 7 products and 7 problems. Production: a conversation at the chemist’s.
  7. Ask the way (A1 towards A2, 14 words). Ask politely, ask someone to repeat, give directions (turn left, opposite, next to…). Mini-game: a map, 3 routes. Production: give the way to the hotel, then ask a classmate the way.
  8. Order at a café (A1 towards A2, 13 words). Read a menu, choose according to a diet and a budget of £10, order, talk about allergies. Production: their order.
  9. Phone your placement (A2 towards B1, 13 words). Start and end a professional call, explain a delay or an absence, ask practical questions. Mini-game: put 6 lines back in order. Production: their call.

127 words and expressions in all, recorded with natural neural voices (British English by default, a few American variants).

Differentiation, class work and assessment

A1: more support (totals and times shown, route text visible, first letter in the recall, model open, 12 words minimum). A2 towards B1: more challenge (trap replies, mental arithmetic, listening without the text, because / comparatives / future, 25 words minimum). Traduction button: instructions in French, dialogues in English with the translation underneath.

In class: a collective vote on the replies, or pairs on a phone / in the computer room, then a role play in front of the class. At home: one mission per lesson; the student copies their text or downloads their recording for the school’s digital workspace (ENT). The gap-fill worksheets are printed or filled in on the screen (a check button). The production of each mission can be used as an assessed role play (being understood, politeness, accurate personal information, words from the mission).

The role plays that take the work further (a station announcement, a leaflet, a booking email, a health record, a voicemail, notices, a city guide, the canteen menu, a call to the tutor) are set out on the sequence page.

Data and privacy

No account, no advertising. The first name, the choices, the texts and the progress stay only on the student’s device (the browser’s local storage). The audio recordings do not leave the device: the student can download them if they want to upload them to the ENT. On a shared computer, the Recommencer button in the notebook erases the progress. Voices: Kokoro-82M (Apache 2.0 licence), audio files generated in advance, with no in-browser speech synthesis.
