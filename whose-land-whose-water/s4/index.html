Lesson 4. Dams Today: Muskrat Falls and Site C

Goal: I can report a campaigner’s concern without confusing a prediction, a quotation and a measured outcome.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 4 of 8. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure.

  1. Recall benefit and loss. 0 to 4
  2. Read the two case introductions. 4 to 9
  3. Muskrat Falls video: two listenings and notes. 9 to 19
  4. Site C video: two listenings and notes. 19 to 29
  5. Compare the cases. 29 to 35
  6. Grammar and vocabulary practice. 35 to 40
  7. Protected news report. 40 to 44
  8. Written record. 44 to 49
  9. Homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student presents a 35-second news report without interruption.
  • Homework for next lesson. 100 to 120 words: why can the expression “clean energy” require explanation? Two flashcards.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: each student names one concern and its speaker.
  • 46 to 49: copy the first three written-record sentences.
  • 49 to 51: record homework.
  • 51 to 52: classify “could increase”.

Student text

Read

At Muskrat Falls in Labrador, campaigners raised concerns about the effects of flooding on water, fish and traditional activities. In 2016, Inuk artist Billy Gauthier took part in a hunger strike.

At Site C in British Columbia, West Moberly leadership challenged the project and proposed alternatives. Supporters argued that the dam would provide electricity.

These cases involve different peoples and territories. Inuit and Innu are not interchangeable names. A campaigner’s prediction about contamination is also not the same as a later measurement.

Building your vocabulary

  • downstream: further along a river’s direction of flow;
  • campaigner: someone working publicly for change;
  • methylmercury: a harmful form of mercury;
  • alternative: another possible choice;
  • prediction: a statement about what may happen;
  • measured outcome: a result established through observation or measurement.

Listening documents

Listening protocol. 1. Read the listening purpose. 2. Listen once for the main message. 3. Note keywords, not complete sentences. 4. Listen again for the named details. 5. Prepare a short restitution. 6. Only then consult the support text and the answer key. Label your notes: H I heard it; R I read it afterwards; I I inferred it; ? I am unsure.

Grammar exercise

Complete with rise, to rise, protect or to protect.

  1. Flooding can cause methylmercury levels .
  2. Environmental change can make health risks .
  3. Campaigners want the authorities traditional activities.
  4. Authorities should people’s health.

Protected spoken turn

Each student presents a 35-second news report. The partner must not interrupt.

Today’s report concerns…
According to the interview…
The campaigner asks for…
We still need evidence about…

  • □ I gave a complete spoken answer.
  • □ I listened without interrupting.
  • □ I asked or answered a question.

Answer key

A: Billy Gauthier; hunger strike; better clearing/protection before flooding; fishing; the ability of future generations to continue traditional activities.

B: Chief Roland Willson, West Moberly; his account of a commitment to reconsider the project after a review; continuation of Site C; wind, solar or geothermal energy. Attribute the promise to his testimony rather than treating the interview as an independent finding about the review.

Grammar: to rise; rise; to protect; protect.

Extension: “Although a project supplies electricity, it may also damage valued places.”

Vocabulary: prediction; measurement; opinion.

Written record

The Muskrat Falls and Site C reports present concerns about health, territory and decision-making. Campaigners may support some forms of development while opposing a particular project or method. We distinguish testimony, predictions and measured outcomes. We also identify the nation or community concerned.

Homework

Write 100 to 120 words:

Why can the expression “clean energy” require explanation?

Use one example, one attributed viewpoint and one sentence beginning “Although”.

Flashcards. Make two cards from today’s word box, for example prediction, measured outcome: the word on the front, the meaning and one example sentence on the back. See the flashcard method.
