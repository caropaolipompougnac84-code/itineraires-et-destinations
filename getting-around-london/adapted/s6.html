Lesson 6: Mind the gap: British humour underground

Lesson outline

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes.

  • 8 min · Warm-up
  • 14 min · Documents
  • 12 min · Activities
  • 8 min · Role play : everyone speaks at least once
  • 10 min · Gap-fill et trace écrite
  • Written record. Two or three key sentences from the lesson.
  • Participation. everyone speaks at least once: one sentence each.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework already given on the page.

Stopping point. End of this lesson. The homework is already given lower down on the page.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, we condense: we dictate a short written record (two or three key sentences from the lesson) ; a round of the class, one sentence each, so that everyone has spoken at least once; the homework, already given on the page, is written on the board.

Question: What does the Tube tell us about British humour?
Step 1

Goals

  • Analyse humour
  • Write a whiteboard message
Step 2

Warm-up

What does Mind the gap mean? Where would you hear it?

Step 3

Read

Mind the gap.Recorded warning on the London Underground, introduced around 1968.

The phrase had to be short because digital storage was expensive, and a short warning was easier to paint on the platform. Many stations also have a whiteboard where staff write a joke, a poem or a thought of the day. Station names are full of stories: Baker Street is the home of Sherlock Holmes, Paddington gave its name to a bear from darkest Peru, and King's Cross railway station has a Platform 9¾ for Harry Potter fans.

Sources: Wikipedia, Mind the gap; London Underground.

Step 4

Tasks

Task a. Explain why Mind the gap became a symbol of London.
Task b. Find the story behind one station name.
Task c. Write a funny or poetic whiteboard message for a station.
Task d. Compare British humour in the station with the humour of the ad in lesson 1.
Step 5

Sentence starters

  • Mind the gap means...
  • This station is famous for...
  • My message for the whiteboard is...
  • British humour often relies on...
Step 6

Role play

A station worker and a grumpy passenger: the worker answers every complaint with a joke.
