Lesson 7. The Cross-Border Radio Discussion

What can elections change, and what should responsible reporting avoid claiming?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 7 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55 stay free for collecting work, moving and the change of room.

  1. Paired opening: each student gives a sourced fact and its limit 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. The task and the assessment criteria 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Accuracy audit, Exercise 7A 6 min (minutes 8 to 14)
  4. Rehearse openings; check speaking distribution 6 min (minutes 14 to 20)
  5. Eight-minute radio discussion in simultaneous groups 8 min (minutes 20 to 28)
  6. Peer feedback and individual clarification questions 6 min (minutes 28 to 34)
  7. Repeat a selected section with improved interaction 6 min (minutes 34 to 40)
  8. Complete and hand in the individual briefing note 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  9. Written self-assessment 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  10. Exit ticket: one claim improved through qualification 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  11. Homework for the next English lesson 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. The five-line self-assessment: one fact I explained accurately, one source I used, one question I answered, one claim I qualified, my next language target.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks: each group member has a protected opening, at least one question or answer in the discussion and one qualification in the final round.
  • Homework for next lesson. Revise one paragraph of the briefing note and explain the revision in two sentences; review six flashcards; write one rule for checking claims on another topic.

Stopping point. Minute 44, after the briefing notes have been handed in.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 3: each student gives a 30-second role contribution in their group.
  2. Minutes 3 to 5: complete the written self-assessment.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: hand in the briefing note and record the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: the teacher checks the participation roster. A shortened performance is formative unless enough individual evidence has been collected.

Objectives

Combine cultural analysis, institutional knowledge and source-based reasoning in an interactive discussion.

Language: turn-taking, concession, clarification and synthesis.

Final task

In groups of four, produce an eight-minute radio discussion in English for a Canadian student audience: The Verdict of the People: What Can US Elections Change at Home and Across Borders?

Your audience knows little about US institutions. Do not read a complete script: use keyword cards and source notes. Use Bingham’s painting as an opening cultural reference, verified election information with its date and status, accurate institutional explanations, Puerto Rico as an example of unequal federal representation and a carefully qualified discussion of cross-border consequences.

Common B1+ route, for every student: give two accurate facts relevant to your role; identify at least one source; explain why one fact matters; ask or answer one question; state one limit.

Towards B2: an unprepared follow-up; a response to a counterargument; a comparison between two sources; a reformulation that corrects another speaker’s misunderstanding.

RoleResponsibilities
HostExplain the painting’s relevance; ask at least two real questions; reformulate one answer; distinguish one established finding from one hypothesis; summarise. The host is not only a timekeeper.
Washington reporterPresent verified election information and explain the powers of Congress.
Canadian or Mexican business reporterExplain one cross-border issue, how it could lead to a consequence, and a limit.
Puerto Rico reporterExplain representation and prevent a misleading generalisation.

Exercise 7A

Accuracy audit

Correct these statements before the discussion.

  1. Midterms directly replace the president.
  2. The Senate alone approves all ordinary legislation.
  3. Puerto Rico has no representative.
  4. A media call is the same as certification.
  5. The painting is a neutral photograph of an election.
  6. A congressional result automatically decides the next trade policy.

Answer key

  1. They elect Congress; they do not directly elect a new president.
  2. Ordinary legislation generally needs both chambers and presentation to the president.
  3. It has a Resident Commissioner with specific powers and limits.
  4. A media call and legal certification are different acts.
  5. It is an artistic representation that needs interpretation.
  6. It may affect the political context, but the policy outcome needs further evidence.
Exercise 7B

Choose a useful connector

Use: however, nevertheless, moreover, therefore.

  1. The two places share some features; , their presidential-elector arrangements differ.
  2. The report is recent. , its categories must still be checked.
  3. The Senate considers nominations. , it has responsibilities concerning treaties.
  4. We do not have certification evidence. , we should not describe the result as certified.

Answer key

  1. however.
  2. Nevertheless.
  3. Moreover.
  4. Therefore.

Accept alternatives that give the intended logical link.

Exercise 7C

Repair the interaction

Rewrite each response politely and usefully.

  1. “That is stupid.”
  2. “You are wrong about everything.”
  3. “I do not know, so I will guess.”
  4. “That proves what everybody thinks.”

Answer key

  1. “I am not convinced by that argument. What evidence supports it?”
  2. “I think we are using different categories. Which figure are you referring to?”
  3. “I cannot establish that from our sources.”
  4. “That identifies an outcome, but it does not establish every voter’s motivation.”
Exercise 7D

Individual briefing note

Common B1+ route, 140 to 170 words, written for your role: introduce your role’s question; explain two sourced facts; give one implication; state one limit; connect briefly to the driving question of the sequence. Use your own words and list your sources separately.

Towards B2: integrate a counterpoint or compare two interpretations.

Answer key

No single model answer should supply election results. Check that each factual statement matches the student’s source log and that the cross-border consequence is presented as a hypothesis, not an established outcome. The word range is a classroom planning choice, not a CEFR threshold.

Expanding your vocabulary

FunctionUseful language
Introduce evidence“According to the source, updated on…”
Clarify“Do you mean the outgoing or the incoming Congress?”
Distinguish“That is a projection rather than a certified result.”
Concede“That is a reasonable point; however…”
Identify uncertainty“Our sources do not establish that.”
Summarise“We agree on the rule, but the consequence is still uncertain.”

Quick exercise. Which expression would you use when a speaker mixes up the Congress elected in November 2026 and the one sitting before January 2027?

Answer key

“Do you mean the outgoing or the incoming Congress?”
