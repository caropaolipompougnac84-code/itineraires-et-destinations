Lesson 8. Final Task: A Citizens’ Hearing and an Open Letter

A mock parliamentary committee, and an open letter.

Timing note. The source dossier gave 30 minutes to the hearing and 20 minutes to the open letter. In a large class, three minutes per group plus questions may need to be organised separately, or over two sittings. The road map below keeps a shorter hearing inside 52 minutes and finishes the letter for homework. The assessment grids still cover both tasks.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. This is the final task of the eight lessons.

  1. Recap: the four dossiers 0 to 4
  2. Choose a dossier and prepare four parts 4 to 20
  3. A shorter hearing (about two minutes per group, then questions) 20 to 40
  4. Start the open letter 40 to 44
  5. Written record 44 to 48
  6. Homework: finish the letter and a short self-assessment 48 to 52
  • Written record. The four-part plan of your dossier, and the first sentences of the open letter.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: as a witness, as an MP asking a question, or as the chair.
  • Homework. Finish the open letter (200 to 250 words). Give yourself a mark on each criterion of the grid and write one sentence: what I will remember from this unit.

Stopping point. Minute 44, after a first draft opening of the letter.

Emergency exit · 8 min. See the box at the end of the lesson.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

The four dossiers of this sequence.

  1. Name the four dossiers we studied.
  2. Give one figure with its source for one of them.

Answer key

  • The White Paper (1969); the dams (James Bay, Muskrat Falls, Site C); the pipelines (Coastal GasLink, Trans Mountain); water and the Calls to Action.
  • For example: 39 long-term drinking water advisories were still in effect on 21 October 2026 (Indigenous Services Canada).

4 to 20 min

The scenario and the four parts

Your class becomes a mock parliamentary committee. Each group is a team of witnesses and presents one dossier to the committee. The other students are the Members of Parliament (MPs): they listen and ask questions. One student chairs the hearing, one keeps the time.

  • Dossier A. The White Paper, 1969, and the Red Paper.
  • Dossier B. The dams: James Bay, Muskrat Falls, Site C.
  • Dossier C. The pipelines: Coastal GasLink and Trans Mountain.
  • Dossier D. Drinking water and the 94 Calls to Action.

Prepare four parts:

  1. The promise: who promised what, and when? (reported speech)
  2. What was done: facts and figures, each with its source (the passive).
  3. What was not done: facts and figures, each with its source (failed to, should have).
  4. Our recommendation: what should happen now? (ought to, have to)

Respect every voice: if a community is divided, say so, and present both sides.

20 to 44 min

The hearing, then the opening of the letter

About two minutes per group in this official timing, then questions from the MPs. If the class is large, the teacher may organise a fuller hearing separately; the grid on the assessment page still applies.

Useful language: Madam Chair, members of the committee, we are here to talk about… In 2015, the government promised that it would… Since then, … have been lifted. However, … We recommend that the government ought to… Could the witness explain why…? According to which source…?

Open letter (started in class, finished for homework). Individually, write an open letter of 200 to 250 words to an MP (imaginary). Choose one promise, give two facts with their sources, say what should have been done and what must be done now. End with a respectful but firm request.

Useful language: Dear Member of Parliament, I am writing to you about… Although…, … I therefore urge you to… Yours sincerely,

Written record. Copy your four-part plan (promise, what was done, what was not done, recommendation) and the first four lines of your open letter.

How you are assessed

The full grids are on the assessment page. In short: content and sources (6), language (6), interaction (4), respect for facts and points of view (4). Total: 20.

Homework. Finish the open letter. Self-assessment: give yourself a mark for each criterion and explain one mark. Write one sentence: what I will remember from this unit.

Emergency exit · 8 min

If time runs short, replace the unfinished plan with this condensed ending (inside the 52 minutes, not 52 plus 8).

  • 0 to 2. Everyone says this sentence aloud: “I can report a promise, give a figure with its source, and say what should happen now.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record: Copy the four-part plan of your dossier and one request for the open letter.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework: Finish the 200-to-250-word letter and a short self-assessment.
  • 7 to 8. Check one answer in the answer key.
