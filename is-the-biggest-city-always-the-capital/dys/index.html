Is the Biggest City Always the Capital?

From the USA to the Commonwealth.

Seven lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes, plus a final-task assessment (a podcast or an article). You start in the United States, then travel to six countries of the Commonwealth, and you end by answering the guiding question: Is the biggest city always the capital?

Road map

Seven lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes, plus a final-task assessment.

  1. Lesson 1. Water first, cities next 52 min
  2. Lesson 2. Comparing Cities 52 min
  3. Lesson 3. A capital built for a deal 52 min
  4. Lesson 4. Capitals by compromise 52 min
  5. Lesson 5. One Commonwealth, many capitals 52 min
  6. Lesson 6. Debate 52 min
  7. Lesson 7. The Biggest City game 52 min
  • Written record. Each lesson ends with a short written record, copied in the notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once in every lesson.
  • Homework for next lesson. Each lesson page states the homework; lesson 7 prepares the final task.

Stopping point. Each lesson page marks a stopping point, so that the end of the lesson can be condensed if need be.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short, a condensed eight-minute ending (inside the 52 minutes, not 52 plus 8) still leaves every student with the written record, one spoken sentence, and the homework.

The assessment page has two options, with marking grids at B1+ (and A2+ / B1). The article can be written as a 52-minute lesson; the oral (3 minutes plus 2 minutes of questions per student) is organised separately.

Official programme, lycée général et technologique, Seconde. Langues vivantes : arrêté of 5 May 2025, BO n° 22 of 29 May 2025 (NOR MENE2504621A), annexe 4. In force for Seconde from the 2025 to 2026 school year. Target level: LVA B1+ (the official table also gives LVB A2+ at the end of Seconde). Axe 6 is compulsory; two axes may be joined in one sequence around a common question. This sequence joins:

  • Axe 6. Les pays du Commonwealth : héritages, unité, diversité. Official wording: « Quelle place occupe-t-il dans l’aire anglophone ? Comment les pays membres parviennent-ils à y évoluer tout en affirmant leur singularité culturelle ? » Study objects: « De l’empire aux indépendances : transitions postcoloniales » and « Permanence et rupture dans les héritages politiques britanniques » (lessons 4 and 5).
  • Axe 4. Défis et transitions. Official wording: « Dans quelle mesure les grands bouleversements écologiques, démographiques et sociaux changent-ils notre rapport à l’espace, à la nature ? » Study object: « Transports et (re)structuration des espaces » (lessons 1 to 3, on the United States, which is not a Commonwealth member).

The language work is written for B1+ (complex comparatives, the passive, used to, whose, a range of connectors, the -ate ending, and mediation in a jigsaw and a debate).

You already know, if you have studied The Capital Trap. That official sequence, on this same site, keeps the fuller United States road-trip path: the complete story of Inès, the three-step Juneau mystery, the fifteen-pair memory game, Route 66 and a postcard. The present Seconde sequence does not repeat those documents or exercises. It keeps a short recap where it is useful, then develops what is specific here: the 1790 deal that created Washington, D.C., a jigsaw on six Commonwealth capitals, a debate, and a B1+ final task (a podcast or an article).

Why this unit?

Houston or Austin? Sydney or Canberra? Lagos or Abuja? All over the English-speaking world, the biggest city is often not the capital. Behind each choice, there is a story of rivers, roads, rivalries and compromises, and sometimes of empire and independence.

At the end of this unit, you will be able to locate places precisely, compare with complex structures, tell the history of a capital with the passive, explain a document to others (mediation), and defend a point of view in a debate.

Language tables and exercises are also collected on the language page. Cut-out cards are on the cards page. The student sheet is in the downloads. To revise, make your own bristol flashcards (Carole’s method): the word or the country on the front, the meaning or the capital on the back, studied in both directions, in short daily sessions.
