Lesson 4. A good Bond, a good employee?

Students can distinguish screen appeal from professional suitability; evaluate a stereotype; use comparisons and qualified language; and support a claim with evidence.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Six lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure. Lesson 6 is the final-task interview.

  1. Retrieve the casting debate 0 to 5
  2. Read and distinguish the two panels 5 to 13
  3. Evidence and inference worksheet 13 to 21
  4. Grammar practice 21 to 31
  5. Prepare balanced statements 31 to 35
  6. Paired discussion and challenge 35 to 44
  7. Every student gives one qualified conclusion 44 to 47
  8. Homework 47 to 48
  9. Written record 48 to 52
  • Written record. Complete the written record on purposes, suitability, recruitment documents and absolute claims.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student gives one similarity, one difference, one example and one response to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. 80 to 100 words on what Bond can show and what Bond might hide, plus two cards: stereotype, integrity.

Stopping point. Stopping point: minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, this eight-minute ending replaces unfinished extension work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 47: every student gives one difference between casting and recruitment;
  • 47 to 48: homework;
  • 48 to 52: written record.

Student document A. Two different selection panels

Original classroom document:

A casting panel chooses a performer for a fictional role. It considers acting, character interpretation and the needs of the story.

A professional recruitment panel considers whether an applicant can do a job responsibly. A memorable screen presence is not the same as reliable professional judgement.

We should therefore avoid moving directly from “This actor is convincing as Bond” to “This person would be an excellent intelligence officer.”

Student document B. Work behind the image

MI5 describes intelligence work as gathering, assessing and connecting information. Its officer-development material describes collaboration with analysts, operational teams and partners.

This challenges the idea that one glamorous individual performs every task alone. However, an agency’s careers page is also a recruitment document. It tells us how the organisation presents its work; it is not an independent account of every employee’s experience.

Worksheet 4A. Which panel?

Write casting, professional recruitment or both.

  1. Can the person interpret a complex character?
  2. Can the person check information responsibly?
  3. Can the person communicate clearly?
  4. Does the performance serve the film’s story?
  5. Can the person cooperate with colleagues?

Possible answers

  1. Casting.
  2. Professional recruitment.
  3. Both.
  4. Casting.
  5. Both, although assessed in different contexts.

Worksheet 4B. Claim and evidence

ClaimEvidence available?Better wording
Every officer works alone.
Teamwork matters in MI5’s account of its work.
Careers pages prove everyone enjoys the job.

Answer key

  • First: no; official material describes collaboration.
  • Second: yes; “MI5 presents teamwork as important.”
  • Third: no; “Careers pages present the organisation’s account of the job.”

Building your vocabulary: grammar practice

Exercise 1. Comparatives

  1. Evidence is useful than a stereotype.
  2. This claim is careful than the first claim.
  3. “Some officers” is absolute than “all officers”.

Answer key

  1. more
  2. more
  3. less

Exercise 2. Too or enough?

  1. This statement is general to be reliable.
  2. We do not have evidence to confirm it.
  3. The explanation is clear to follow.

Answer key

  1. too
  2. enough
  3. enough

Exercise 3. Qualify the claim

Rewrite using may, some or not necessarily.

  1. Screen heroes are good employees.
  2. Officers enjoy dangerous situations.
  3. Confidence proves competence.

Possible answers

  1. Screen heroes are not necessarily good employees.
  2. Some fictional officers may enjoy dangerous situations.
  3. Confidence does not necessarily prove competence.

Exercise 4. Link an argument

Complete with because or although.

  1. Teamwork matters one person cannot do every task.
  2. a careers page is useful, it has a recruitment purpose.

Answer key

  1. because
  2. Although

Protected oral intervention

Question: should the qualities used to choose the next Bond also be used to recruit an intelligence officer?

Each student gives:

  • one similarity;
  • one difference;
  • one example;
  • one response to a partner.

Communication could matter in both contexts. However, a casting panel assesses a performance, whereas a recruitment panel assesses suitability for a job. A convincing screen hero is not necessarily a reliable employee.

  • □ I gave a complete spoken answer.
  • □ I listened to my partner.
  • □ I asked or answered a question.

What can Bond show us, and what might Bond hide?

Include one comparison, one qualified claim and one reference to a document.

Make two cards: stereotype, integrity.
