Lesson 5. Pipelines on Unceded Land

Goal: I can explain disagreement about a project without assuming that one speaker represents everyone.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 5 of 8. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure.

  1. Recall the difference between testimony and fact-checking. 0 to 4
  2. Read the legal and cultural introduction. 4 to 10
  3. Coastal GasLink extract: two listenings. 10 to 21
  4. Trans Mountain report: two listenings. 21 to 30
  5. Complete the comparison sheet. 30 to 35
  6. Grammar and vocabulary practice. 35 to 40
  7. Protected evidence meeting. 40 to 44
  8. Written record. 44 to 49
  9. Homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student has 30 uninterrupted seconds in a research role, then asks one question.
  • Homework for next lesson. Complete the four-row argument and evidence table, and make two flashcards.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: each student reports one attributed viewpoint.
  • 46 to 49: copy the written record.
  • 49 to 51: record homework.
  • 51 to 52: explain why an offer is not a completed agreement.

Student text

Read

Coastal GasLink and Trans Mountain generated disagreement among and within First Nations.

In the Coastal GasLink dispute, elected councils signed agreements, while hereditary leadership opposing the project stressed responsibilities connected with wider traditional territory. These positions should be attributed carefully.

The 1997 Delgamuukw decision is important for understanding Aboriginal title and oral history. It should not be described as a simple final award of the entire disputed territory to particular individuals.

Consultation means meaningful discussion and consideration of concerns. Consent means agreement. These are different concepts, and their legal implications depend on the circumstances.

For an optional current update, the November 2026 federal budget records an offer of a collective 15% Trans Mountain ownership share to 128 Indigenous groups. This is an offer, not evidence that every group agreed or that the transfer was completed.

Building your vocabulary

  • consent: agreement;
  • consultation: meaningful discussion before or during decision-making;
  • elected council: a council selected through an election;
  • hereditary leadership: leadership under a nation’s own inherited governance traditions;
  • injunction: a court order requiring or prohibiting an action;
  • stake: an ownership share.

Listening documents

Listening protocol. 1. Read the listening purpose. 2. Listen once for the main message. 3. Note keywords, not complete sentences. 4. Listen again for the named details. 5. Prepare a short restitution. 6. Only then consult the support text and the answer key. Label your notes: H I heard it; R I read it afterwards; I I inferred it; ? I am unsure.

Grammar exercise

Complete with although, whereas, must or should.

  1. Some leaders supported the project, others opposed it.
  2. agreements were signed, disagreement continued.
  3. A report identify whose position it presents.
  4. Decision-makers explain how concerns were considered.

Expanding your vocabulary

Choose:

  1. A stake is an ownership share / a court order.
  2. Consent means agreement / merely hearing about a project.
  3. An injunction is a court order / a public opinion poll.
  4. Consultation and consent are identical / different.

Protected evidence meeting

Use research roles, not impersonations of Indigenous identity.

  • Researcher A reports a supporting argument.
  • Researcher B reports an opposing argument.
  • Researcher C identifies a legal uncertainty.
  • Researcher D recommends the next question to investigate.

Each has 30 uninterrupted seconds. Then each asks one question. In pairs, each student covers two roles.

  • □ I gave a complete spoken answer.
  • □ I listened without interrupting.
  • □ I asked or answered a question.

Answer key

A: arrests/enforcement; consultation now takes place, according to Trudeau; rule of law; agreements with First Nations along the route; the hereditary chiefs’ position.

B: land/water; agreements with the company; jobs and tax revenue; no.

Grammar: whereas; although; must; should. Other defensible modal choices are acceptable if students explain the intended force.

Vocabulary: ownership share; agreement; court order; different.

Comparison: assess accurate attribution and qualification, not agreement with one political position.

Written record

The pipeline reports show different positions among First Nations leaders and communities. Agreements signed by some representatives do not prove agreement by everyone. Consultation and consent are different concepts. We must attribute claims and avoid oversimplifying Aboriginal title.
