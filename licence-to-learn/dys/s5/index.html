Lesson 5. Necessary, forbidden or optional?

Goal: I can use must, mustn’t and don’t have to.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 5 of 6. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Split the lesson into two blocks: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Recall a quality 0 to 5
  2. Read rules 5 to 13
  3. Exercise 1 13 to 21
  4. Grammar Exercise 2 21 to 29
  5. Scenario Exercise 3 29 to 36
  6. Write and practise rules 36 to 44
  7. Say one rule 44 to 47
  8. Homework 47 to 48
  9. Written record 48 to 52
  • Written record. Complete the written record: must means necessary; mustn’t means forbidden; don’t have to means not necessary.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: read one rule and say whether it is necessary, forbidden or optional.
  • Homework for next lesson. prepare a short interview card: service, quality, must rule, mustn’t rule; Route P adds a past example.

Stopping point. Stopping point: minute 44. Stop the main exercises. Keep the written record and the homework, even if an exercise is unfinished.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, this eight-minute ending replaces unfinished extension work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 47: three minutes of speaking;
  • 47 to 48: one minute for homework;
  • 48 to 52: four minutes for the written record.
Short text

These are fictional training-office rules.

You must check information.

This is necessary.

You mustn’t invent evidence.

This is forbidden.

You don’t have to wear a suit.

This is optional.

Exercise 1

Match the meaning

Worked example. must → necessary.
Now you.

Match:

  1. mustn’t
  2. don’t have to

With:

a. not necessary   b. forbidden

Self-check

  1. b
  2. a
Exercise 2

Grammar

Worked example. Sharing a password is forbidden. You mustn’t share a password.
Now you.
  1. Checking is necessary. You check.
  2. Wearing a suit is optional. You wear a suit.
  3. Inventing evidence is forbidden. You invent evidence.

Extension example: He musts check. Correct: He must check. Now correct: She doesn’t has to wear a suit.

Self-check

  1. must / have to
  2. don’t have to
  3. mustn’t

Extension: She doesn’t have to wear a suit.

Exercise 3

Solve a problem

Worked example. I do not understand. I have to ask for help.
Now you.

Now: a claim has no source.

Complete: I must . I mustn’t .

Self-check

I must check the source. I mustn’t present the claim as confirmed.

Exercise 4

Write your code

Worked example. You must protect confidential information.

Now write:

  • S: two rules;
  • C: three rules, including don’t have to;
  • P: five rules and two reasons.

Self-check

  • □ Necessary and forbidden are different.
  • □ I did not write musts.
  • □ I did not put to after must.

Speaking

Read one rule and say: it is necessary / forbidden / optional.

If you are in a CFA, also transfer one rule to your workplace, for example: a receptionist must protect customer information. These are language-transfer examples, not complete workplace regulations.

Homework

Prepare a short interview card:

Service:

Quality:

Must rule:

Mustn’t rule:

Route P: add a past example.

Written record

Must means .

Mustn’t means .

Don’t have to means .

Answer key

necessary; forbidden; not necessary.
