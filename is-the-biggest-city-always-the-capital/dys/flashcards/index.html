Flashcards: make your own

Carole’s method, in the language of the sequence. The useful work is the cards you make yourself, not a ready-made pack to look at once.

Take small bristol cards (or the blank sheet in the downloads). On the front, write the English word, the name of a country or a state, or a verb. On the back, write the meaning, the capital, or an example sentence. Study in both directions: front to back, then back to front. A short daily session is better than a long one the night before the test.

Suggested packs for this sequence:

  • the Commonwealth pairs (and the four US pairs of this unit), plus the trap city;
  • the B1+ locating phrases (not far from, on the banks of, at the far end of);
  • complex comparatives: twice as big as, nowhere near as… as, by far;
  • three passives from the 1790 deal or from your expert country;
  • the names to stress: Canberra, Wellington, Abuja, Ottawa, Houston.

When you can say the capital without looking, and then the country from the capital, the card can leave the pack for a few days. Bring it back later for a last check.
