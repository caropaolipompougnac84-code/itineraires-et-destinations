Lesson 5. Guess the city

Describe and guess: fewer clues, more points. Then compare two cities.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 7 lessons, plus a written and an oral assessment.

  1. Zoom game: a detail, then the full photograph 10 min
  2. A short video and three questions 12 min
  3. Guess the city, with cards C 22 min
  4. Grammar: compare two cities from the cards (exercise 3) 8 min
  • Written record. Choose two cities and write three comparative sentences, including one superlative if you can.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student gives at least one clue, or names a city, in a full sentence.
  • Homework for next lesson. Write three sentences comparing two cities from the cards. If possible, watch a short English video about one city (2 to 4 minutes).

Stopping point. After Guess the city, keep exercise 3 even if it is reduced to one oral example per pair.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson inside the 52 minutes (this is not 52 plus 8). Dictate a short written record (two sentences). A quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each). Write the homework on the board.

Zoom game · 10 min

Show the cropped detail first, and collect ideas. Then show the full photograph. Do this with the Golden Gate and with the Las Vegas sign (or with the Space Needle).

A short video · 12 min

Choose one English video of 2 to 4 minutes (each link on the cards page opens a YouTube search, not a single fixed film). Then three questions:

  1. Which city is it? How do you know?
  2. Write two things you saw.
  3. Is this city a capital? Explain.

Guess the city · 22 min

Each student receives a city ID card and gives the clues one by one. The fewer clues needed, the more points. The interactive box below can be used with the whole class; the same clues are visible as a list, so the activity works without JavaScript and on paper.

JavaScript is not required: use the printed cards. If it is available, this box lets you reveal one clue at a time.

Chicago. 1. It’s in the Midwest. 2. It’s next to a big lake. 3. It’s the biggest city in Illinois, but not the capital. 4. Its name starts with “Ch”.

San Francisco. 1. It’s on the West Coast. 2. It’s famous for a red bridge. 3. It’s in California. 4. The bridge is the Golden Gate.

New Orleans. 1. It’s in the South, near the Gulf of Mexico. 2. The Mississippi flows through it. 3. It’s famous for jazz. 4. It’s in Louisiana.

Las Vegas. 1. It’s in the desert. 2. It’s famous for casinos and shows. 3. It’s in Nevada, but it isn’t the capital. 4. Its name means “the meadows” in Spanish.

Boston. 1. It’s on the East Coast. 2. It’s a very old city with famous universities. 3. It’s the capital of Massachusetts. 4. A famous “Tea Party” took place there in 1773.

Honolulu. 1. It’s on an island in the Pacific Ocean. 2. It’s very far from the other big cities. 3. It’s the capital of Hawaii. 4. Pearl Harbor is very close.

Miami. 1. It’s in the south-east. 2. It’s famous for beaches and sunshine. 3. It’s in Florida, but it isn’t the capital. 4. Many people speak Spanish there.

Seattle. 1. It’s in the north-west. 2. It rains a lot there. 3. It’s in Washington State, but it isn’t the capital. 4. It’s famous for the Space Needle.

Exercise 3. Compare two cities · 8 min

Choose two cities from the city ID cards and write three sentences to compare them.

Example: Miami is hotter than Seattle. Seattle is rainier than Miami. I think Miami is the best city for holidays.

Answer key

Open answers. Accept any accurate comparative or superlative, including irregular forms (better, worse). A strong answer names a capital or a trap where it is relevant.
