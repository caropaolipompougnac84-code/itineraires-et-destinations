Lesson 2. The girl who was not meant to reign

A painting of 1933, an abdication in 1936, a family tree, and a princess in uniform.

Key question: How did a little girl who was never supposed to be Queen end up on the throne?

1The teacher’s briefing8 min

For the teacher

To tell the story of a childhood that suddenly changes course, students need the past simple (successive facts), the past continuous (background), used to and would (past habits), and the future in the past (was going to, would, was to) to say what was not expected. Start from the family tree: Nobody expected that she would become Queen.

Starting point

In 1933 Elizabeth was seven. She was sitting for a portrait painter. Nobody thought she would ever be Queen.

Past simple vs past continuous

  • Completed events in sequence: Edward VIII abdicated and her father became King.
  • Background or interrupted action: She was playing at home when the news arrived.

Past habits: used to / would

  • She used to live at 145 Piccadilly (state: only used to).
  • She would ride her ponies every weekend (repeated action: would or used to).

The future in the past

  • was going to: a plan seen from the past. Edward was going to marry Wallis Simpson.
  • would: a prediction seen from the past. Nobody knew she would reign for 70 years.
  • was to: destiny. She was to become the longest-reigning monarch in British history.
Help: past simple = finished action. Past continuous = was/were + verb-ing.

2The document8 min

The facts. Princess Elizabeth of York was born on 21 April 1926, the elder daughter of the Duke of York, the King's second son. She was third in line to the throne, and it was widely expected that her uncle, the Prince of Wales, would marry and have children. Philip de László painted her in 1933, when she was seven; her father had commissioned the portrait.

In January 1936 George V died and her uncle became Edward VIII. In December 1936 he abdicated in order to marry Wallis Simpson, an American who had been divorced twice. Elizabeth's father became King George VI, and the ten-year-old princess became heir presumptive.

On her 21st birthday, 21 April 1947, in a radio broadcast from Cape Town, she said:

I declare before you all that my whole life whether it be long or short shall be devoted to your service and the service of our great imperial family to which we all belong.Princess Elizabeth, broadcast from Cape Town, 21 April 1947. Royal Family website, royal.uk.
Little-known fact: Second Subaltern Windsor. In February 1945, aged 18, the princess joined the Auxiliary Territorial Service, the women's branch of the British Army, with the service number 230873. She trained as a driver and mechanic at Aldershot and passed out as a fully qualified driver. She was the first female member of the royal family to serve full-time in the armed forces, though she went home to Windsor Castle every night. Sources: National Army Museum; The Gazette.

The official Season 1 trailer (2016) presents Claire Foy as the young Queen "who took the throne as a matter of duty not desire", in Netflix's own words.

Understanding the document

  1. Who commissioned the portrait, and when?
  2. Describe the portrait: setting, clothes, colours, mood.
  3. Why was she not expected to reign?
  4. What happened in December 1936?
  5. What did she promise in 1947? Which verb form in the quotation is unusual?
  6. What is surprising about her war service?
Answers
  1. Her father, the Duke of York (later George VI), in 1933.
  2. A seven-year-old girl with fair curls in a white dress with a blue sash, seated outdoors with a basket of flowers; soft light, gentle colours, a private, childlike mood, not a state portrait.
  3. Her father was the second son; her uncle, the Prince of Wales, was expected to marry and have children of his own.
  4. Edward VIII abdicated to marry Wallis Simpson; her father became George VI and she became heir presumptive.
  5. To devote her whole life to the service of the Commonwealth. "Whether it be" is a subjunctive (formal).
  6. A princess trained as an army driver and mechanic in 1945.

4Grammar in practice

From recognition to open production.

RecognitionExercise 3. Identify the form

Name the verb form in bold: past simple, past continuous, used to, would (habit), future in the past.

  1. She was sitting in a garden chair.
  2. Edward VIII abdicated in December 1936.
  3. The princesses used to live at 145 Piccadilly.
  4. Every morning she would feed the corgis.
  5. Nobody knew she would reign for seventy years.
  6. She was to become Queen at 25.
Answers
  1. past continuous
  2. past simple
  3. used to (past state)
  4. would (habit)
  5. future in the past
  6. future in the past (destiny)

Controlled practiceExercise 4. Past simple or past continuous?

Put the verbs in the correct form.

  1. Elizabeth (play) ______ when she (hear) ______ the news.
  2. While the painter (work) ______, the girl (try) ______ to keep still.
  3. In 1945 she (join) ______ the ATS.
  4. She (learn) ______ to change wheels while other cadets (study) ______ map reading.
  5. George VI (die) ______ on 6 February 1952 while she (travel) ______ in Kenya.
Answers
  1. was playing, heard
  2. was working, was trying (tried)
  3. joined
  4. was learning, were studying
  5. died, was travelling

TransformationExercise 5. The future in the past

Rewrite with the words given.

  1. "I will marry Wallis," Edward said. (was going to)
  2. People expected: "Edward will have children." (would)
  3. Destiny: she became the longest-reigning British monarch. (was to)
  4. "I'll be Queen one day," she realised. (would)
Answers
  1. Edward was going to marry Wallis.
  2. People expected that Edward would have children.
  3. She was to become the longest-reigning British monarch.
  4. She realised she would be Queen one day.

TransformationExercise 6. Used to or would?

  4. The family ______ live in Piccadilly before 1936.
Answers
  1. used to (state of possession)
  2. would / used to

5Figures of speech

  • Antithesis: opposed ideas side by side. a little girl / a sovereign; private childhood / public destiny.
  • Hyperbole, solemn style: "my whole life ... shall be devoted to your service".
  • Archaism: "whether it be long or short" (subjunctive), giving gravity to a promise.

Exercise 8. Spot the figure

Antithesis, archaism or hyperbole?

  1. "whether it be long or short"
  2. "a girl in a garden, a Queen in an abbey"
  3. "I've told you a million times!"
  4. "my whole life ... shall be devoted"
Answers
  1. archaism (subjunctive)
  2. antithesis
  3. hyperbole
  4. solemn hyperbole (and the archaic modal shall)

6Idioms

born with a silver spoon in one's mouth
born into a rich, privileged family.
Silver spoons were a traditional christening gift in wealthy families.
Elizabeth was born with a silver spoon in her mouth, yet her future was uncertain.
a twist of fate
an unexpected change in one's life.
Figurative use of "twist" (a sudden turn).
By a twist of fate, the second son became King.
to step into someone's shoes
to take over somebody's role.
The image of wearing another person's shoes.
George VI had to step into his brother's shoes.
to be thrown in at the deep end
to face a difficult task without preparation.
From swimming pools: the deep end is where you can't touch the bottom.
She was thrown in at the deep end at 25.

+Going further

An extension for students who want a more academic register. It is not an official C2 target: the spécialité LLCER aims towards C1 by the end of Terminale.

Read and react (C1+). In 1947 the princess speaks of "our great imperial family". In 150 words, explain how this phrase sounds today, and how the Empire became the Commonwealth. Use was to, would and one mixed conditional.

Language point: the formal subjunctive survives in set phrases: be that as it may, come what may, God save the King, so be it. Use two of them in sentences about the abdication.
