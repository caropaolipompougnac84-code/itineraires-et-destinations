Paintings (public domain)

Then and now. In 1903 Monet painted the new Palace of Westminster as a dark silhouette against the sunset. More than a century later the same towers still stand for Parliament, and journalists still say "Westminster" when they mean the MPs inside.

Then and now. In 1900 Normand imagined King John pressing his seal under the eyes of the barons. Today Runnymede is a quiet water meadow by the Thames, and two clauses of the charter sealed there are still law in England.

Then and now. In 1834 Turner watched the old Palace burn. The building raised after the fire is the one where the Commons and the Lords sit today, and where the Hereditary Peers Act was passed in 2026.
