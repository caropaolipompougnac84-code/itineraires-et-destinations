A Day in London

Theme
Voyage et culture · a day of everyday life in London, avec Leo et Amy
Level
A1 towards B1 · vocational lycée, CAP, years 6 to 9, also offered in general lycée
Aims
Follow nine missions (train and Tube, shopping, a flight, the doctor, a change of plans, the chemist’s, asking the way, a café, a call to the work placement), speak at least once in every lesson, leave a written record, and make flashcards for the 127 words of the game
Duration
9 lessons of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes
Downloads
Student sheet (PDF) · DOCX · ODT · Gap-fill worksheets · Blank flashcards

Leo, 19, comes from France and is going to London for his work placement. He buys his ticket on his own, takes the train and then the Eurostar. His London friend Amy meets him on arrival and comes back in the dialogues. The student moves Leo on the map and completes nine missions, in the order of the story.

This is not a simple quiz. Each mission asks students to make choices (a menu, a destination, symptoms, a diary, a diet, a work-placement location), which the game takes up again in the dialogue and the mini-game. They have to choose under constraints, justify, produce their own dialogue, then recall the words from memory. 127 words and expressions are recorded with British voices (a few American variants). The notebook offers spaced revision.

Each mission opens with an episode of the video series A Day in London (about two minutes, also in a vertical format for the phone). Leo and Amy act out the situation; the narrator then has the key sentences repeated.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 9 lessons.

  1. Lesson 1 · Catch the right train 52 min

    Enter the game with Leo and Amy, then buy the right ticket and discover the Oyster card on arrival.

    • 8 min · Starter and rules (Leo, Amy, le plan)
    • 5 min · Video of episode 1 (station and Tube)
    • 22 min · Mission 1 in the game (departures board, ticket, Oyster)
    • 10 min · Plenary: each student says a ticket-office sentence
    • 7 min · Written record et homework instruction
    • Written record. Take the … train to Paris. I’d like a single / a return, please.
    • Participation. everyone speaks at least once: a sentence at the ticket office, with their train and their type of ticket.
    • Homework for next lesson. Revise eight station words on bristol cards; reread the ticket-office sentences.

    Stopping point. End of this lesson. Next time: Shopping on a budget.

    Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, we condense: we dictate a short written record (Take the … train to Paris. I’d like a single / a return, please.) ; a round of the class, a sentence at the ticket office, with their train and their type of ticket, so that everyone has spoken at least once; the homework (revise eight station words on bristol cards; reread the ticket-office sentences.) is written on the board.

My bristol flashcards · how to use them

Principle: each student makes their own cards by hand, on bristol cards. Writing the word is already a way of learning it. The pack stays in their bag: they can revise anywhere, with no screen. The game already offers spaced revision on the screen; the bristol cards make it possible to revise without a screen, including before an assessed role play.

Materials

A7 bristol cards (74 × 105 mm), or A6 bristols cut in half. Coloured felt-tip pens. An elastic band or an envelope for the pack. A small cardboard box with three compartments for practice (a shoe box with two dividers is enough). You can also print the blank template and cut it out.

My Flashcards · A Day in London

Make your own cards by hand, on bristol cards. The page does not give you a full ready-made deck. The game already has 127 recorded words: choose the ones you still find hard.

  1. Front: the English word or sentence, in big letters, with the colour of the category.
  2. Back: the French, then one example sentence from the mission, or one of your own.
  3. Corner: write the category and the date.
  4. Check: show your card to a partner before you start the next one.

For the class

Differentiation

Level A1: more support. Basket total shown, arrival times shown, route text visible, first letter given in the recall, production model open, 12 words minimum.

Level A2 towards B1: more challenge. Extra trap replies, mental arithmetic for the basket and arrival times, listening to the routes without the text, extra production criteria (because, comparatives, future), 25 words minimum.

The Traduction button shows the instructions in French; the dialogues stay in English, with the translation underneath. Everything is done with the mouse, a finger or the keyboard. Animations stop if the device asks for reduced motion.

In class, at home, on the school platform

In class, with the projector, the class can vote on each reply and justify it (« Why is it rude? »). In the computer room or on a phone, students work in pairs, then play their dialogue. At home, one mission corresponds to one lesson: the student copies their text or downloads their recording for the school’s digital workspace (ENT). The gap-fill worksheets can be filled in on the screen or printed (and saved as PDF). The production of each mission can be used as an assessed role play in oral interaction (being understood, politeness, accurate personal information, words from the mission).

Role plays to go further

  • Catch the right train: a station announcement; a « train cancelled » role play (What’s the next train?).
  • Shopping: cashier / customer with a leaflet of prices in pounds; taking an item back (I’d like to return this…).
  • Flights: a debate in pairs, then a booking email.
  • The doctor: doctor / patient (I’ve had… for…); a « health record » for a stay.
  • Change of plans: leave a voicemail to move an appointment.
  • The chemist’s: reformulate a dosage for a classmate.
  • Asking the way: each student records a route; a treasure hunt with directions.
  • Café: create the canteen menu (with allergens) and play waiter / customer.
  • Work placement: act out the call on the first day, a delay, an absence; a list of questions for the tutor.

Data and voices

No account, no advertising. The first name, the choices, the texts and the progress stay only on the device (local storage). The audio recordings do not leave the device: the student can download them for the school’s digital workspace (ENT). On a shared computer, the Recommencer button in the notebook erases the progress.
