Finding a Job

Many first jobs are in hospitality, farms, tourism and care. Some travellers later look at camp work or FIFO (see Getting there, getting around). Whatever the job, you need a clear resume, a short letter, and a ready answer in an interview.

  • Hospitality: cafés, restaurants, hotels, kitchens.
  • Farms: fruit picking, packing, horticulture (often "specified work" for a second visa year).
  • Tourism: hotels, tours, ski resorts. Speaking French is an advantage with European tourists.
  • Care and services, if you have the right certificates and English.

Reading: a job ad CE

Kitchen hand, Salt and Sun Café, Byron Bay

Busy beachside café looking for an energetic kitchen hand. Casual position, 25 to 35 hours a week, weekends essential. AUD 33 to 35 per hour. Experience in a professional kitchen is a plus. French speakers welcome! Must have working rights in Australia. Start ASAP. Send your resume to jobs@saltandsun.example

Tips for your resume

Australian employers are not very formal about resumes. What they want is a concrete profile: what can you actually do, when can you start, and who can say you are reliable? Many jobs start with a trial shift: you work a few hours and the boss decides on the spot. Keep your resume to one page, with facts, not big words.

  • Be concrete: not "I am motivated" but "I cooked 80 lunches a day during my placement".
  • Your school level: give the French name of your diploma and its Australian equivalent ("equivalent to Year 12"). Mention your work placements: Australian employers love experience.
  • Your English level: be honest. Use the CEFR (A2, B1, B2) or a test score if you have one (IELTS, TOEIC).
  • Your sport: put it in a good place! Rugby, football, surfing or swimming show team spirit, fitness and reliability. Rugby is a passion in Australia (the Wallabies, rugby league in New South Wales and Queensland): it is a great way to start a conversation and to join a local club.
  • No photo, no date of birth, no marital status. Two referees: Australian employers often call them.
  • Print a few copies and hand them in yourself, with a smile: in cafés and on farms, it often works better than email.

Interaction: the job interview EOI

In pairs: one student is the café manager, the other is the candidate. Then swap.

  • Tell me about yourself.
  • Have you ever worked in a kitchen?
  • Can you work weekends?
  • How long are you staying in Australia?
  • Do you have any questions for me?

Trace écrite

My resume is one page, with facts, no photo. I give my diploma and its equivalent (Year 12), my placements, my English level and two referees. A trial shift is common. I can also explain what FIFO is, if I am asked about later plans.
