Step 3

Grammar lesson: comparatives and multiplying

To compare planes with figures and explain why Britain took a supersonic gamble, we need comparatives and multiplying
On the board, to copy

Rule

faster than. more expensive than. twice as fast as.

Form

  • -er than: faster than the 707
  • more ... than: more expensive than planned
  • as ... as: The Tu-144 was not as successful as Concorde.
  • multiplying: twice the speed of sound; nearly three times the speed of a conventional jet
  • nuance: The 707 was nowhere near as fast as Concorde.

Examples from the lesson

  • “If fast is good, faster is clearly better.” (07:41)
  • “twice the speed of sound” (08:40)
  • “just over half the time of America's 707” (09:06)

Common mistakes

  • Not more fast than, but faster than
  • Not twice faster than, but twice as fast as

Expression task

Now say it

Oral: one minute to explain why Britain chose a supersonic project in the late 1950s and early 1960s (homework task 2).

EntryThree sentences with two comparatives and one figure from the film.

Sentence starters:

Show a model answer

In 1952 the British Comet was about fifty per cent faster than any propeller airliner, but it was withdrawn after fatal accidents, and when it came back the Americans had the lead with their 707. Britain needed something radical. With France, it chose Mach 2: twice the speed of sound, across the Atlantic in about three and a half hours, just over half the time of the 707. The faster the plane, the better, or so they thought: it was a gamble, but a necessary one.

Avionics

Reading a Mach number

In the Concorde cabin, a Mach meter showed passengers their speed (National Museums Scotland). Explain to a passenger what Mach 1 and Mach 2 mean. Then convert: 1,300 miles per hour is about how many kilometres per hour (1 mile = 1.609 km)?

Expected answer

Mach 1 is the speed of sound; Mach 2 is twice the speed of sound. The Mach number compares the speed of the aircraft with the local speed of sound, which changes with temperature and altitude. 1,300 × 1.609 = about 2,092 km/h, so over 2,000 km/h.

Systems

From piston engines to jet engines

« From piston engines, you get jet engines, and so from jet engines it seemed like the natural evolution would be supersonic » (07:46). Explain in about 100 words why the jet engine changed aviation (04:31 to 04:42). Use: which allows, thanks to, as a result.

Expected answer

The jet engine « changed aviation forever »: it allowed planes to fly « further, faster, higher ». A jet engine produces thrust by pushing a fast stream of hot gas backwards, which allows aircraft to fly much faster than with propellers. As a result, the Comet was 50 % faster than any propeller airliner and cut the Atlantic crossing from fifteen to seven hours.

Structure

A defect report

The Comet « had structural defects and there were several fatal accidents » (06:15). Training exercise: you inspect a fuselage panel and find a small crack near a window corner. Fill in a defect report in English: aircraft area, finding, possible cause, immediate action, signature. The situation is imaginary.

Expected answer

Area: fuselage, cabin window frame, left side. Finding: a crack about 5 mm long at the corner of the window frame. Possible cause: metal fatigue caused by repeated pressurisation cycles. Immediate action: the aircraft is grounded, the crack is measured and photographed, the finding is reported to the engineering office. Signature and date. Style: short, factual, passive voice.

At home

Task 2. One minute

Record yourself (one minute): « Why did Britain choose to build a supersonic airliner with France? » Use: gamble, rivals, to beat.

Model answer

Britain had lost its lead in the jet age because of the Comet accidents, and the Americans dominated with the 707. To come back to the top, Britain had to do something radical, so it partnered with France. Supersonic flight was a gamble, but a necessary one: if the future was supersonic, Britain had to be in it.
