Family trees: the Windsors and Europe

Used with lesson 3, and as a reference for the whole sequence, whenever names or dates need checking.

How to read the trees. Each box gives a name, the dates of birth and death, and in colour the reign or title. Boxes with a thick border are monarchs or consorts at the centre of the story. Read from top to bottom: each indent is one generation.

Read the tree aloud. George V was succeeded by his eldest son, Edward VIII, who abdicated after less than a year. His younger brother Albert, who had been Duke of York, became George VI. His elder daughter would become Elizabeth II and was to reign for seventy years. Today her eldest son is King and her grandson William is heir apparent.

BEuropean connections

  • Saxe-Coburg and Gotha becomes Windsor. Queen Victoria married her German cousin Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha in 1840, so their descendants belonged to his house. In 1917, during the First World War against Germany, George V adopted the name Windsor for the royal house. The same year the Battenbergs anglicised their name to Mountbatten.
  • The grandmother of Europe. Victoria's nine children married into royal families across the continent: her grandchildren included a German Emperor, a Russian Empress and the Queens of Norway, Spain, Greece and Romania. In 1914 three cousins ruled the great powers: George V and Wilhelm II were first cousins through Victoria; George V and Nicholas II were first cousins through their mothers, two daughters of Christian IX of Denmark, called "the father-in-law of Europe"; and Nicholas II had married Victoria's granddaughter Alix.
  • Philip. Born on Corfu on 10 June 1921, Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark belonged to the Danish house of Glücksburg on his father's side and to the Battenbergs on his mother's side. He and Elizabeth were both great-great-grandchildren of Queen Victoria. Before his marriage he became a British subject and took his mother's family name, Mountbatten.
  • The Queen Mother. Lady Elizabeth Bowes-Lyon was the daughter of the 14th Earl of Strathmore and Kinghorne, from the Scottish aristocracy; she grew up partly at Glamis Castle. Her marriage to the Duke of York in 1923 brought a Scottish noble family into the royal line, rather than a foreign princess.
  • Diana Spencer. The Spencers are an ancient English aristocratic family. Diana descended twice from Charles II, through two of his illegitimate sons: Henry FitzRoy, 1st Duke of Grafton, and Charles Lennox, 1st Duke of Richmond. "FitzRoy" comes from Anglo-Norman and means "son of the king". So William, her son, would be the first monarch descended from Charles II. Source: History of Royal Women.

Exercise 1. Complete with the tree

Use the vocabulary and the right tense.

  1. George V ______ his son Edward VIII in January 1936. (succeed)
  2. Diana ______ Charles II through two illegitimate sons. (descend)
  3. George V and Wilhelm II were ______ : their parents were siblings.
  4. Before 1936, Albert ______ Duke of York for many years. (past perfect)
  5. In 1930 nobody knew that the little Elizabeth ______ Queen. (future in the past)
  6. Camilla ______ the royal family in 2005.
  7. Philip was the ______ of Elizabeth II, not a king.
  8. In 1917 the Battenbergs ______ their name to Mountbatten.
  9. Princess Margaret was Charles's ______.
  10. Prince George is the Queen's ______.
Answers
  1. was succeeded by
  2. descended from
  3. first cousins
  4. had been
  5. would become
  6. married into
  7. consort
  8. anglicised
  9. aunt
  10. great-grandson

Exercise 2. True or false? Correct the false statements.

  1. Elizabeth II and Philip were both descendants of Queen Victoria.
  2. George V and Nicholas II were first cousins through Victoria.
  3. The royal house was called Windsor from 1840.
  4. Edward VIII had children.
  5. William is heir apparent.
  6. The Queen Mother was a foreign princess.
Answers
  1. True: both were her great-great-grandchildren.
  2. False: through their mothers, daughters of Christian IX of Denmark.
  3. False: from 1917; before, Saxe-Coburg and Gotha.
  4. False.
  5. True.
  6. False: she came from the Scottish aristocracy (Bowes-Lyon).

Exercise 3. Open production

In 10 lines, tell the story of the crown from Victoria to William using was succeeded by, had been, would become, was to, descended from, married into.
