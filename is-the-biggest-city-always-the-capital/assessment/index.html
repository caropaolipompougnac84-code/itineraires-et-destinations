Assessment · Évaluation sommative

Final task, graded out of 20, LVA target B1+. Two options: a podcast or an article.

The article (option B) can be written as a lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. The oral (option A: 3 minutes plus 2 minutes of questions per student) is organised separately; it does not fit a whole class inside a single 52-minute lesson.

Option A: oral presentation (3 minutes + 2 minutes of questions)

You are invited to a podcast for English learners called Capital Stories. Compare one US capital (Washington, D.C., or a state capital) and one Commonwealth capital: when and why it was chosen, who decided, what it says about the country. End with your answer to the question: Is the biggest city always the capital, and should it be?

  • Speak from a card with five key words only.
  • Use at least two complex comparatives and two verbs in the passive.
  • Pronounce the names correctly.

Sentence starters. Today I’m going to compare… and… · … was chosen in… by…, mainly because… · Unlike…, … was built from scratch. · To sum up, I believe that…

Option B: article for a school web magazine (180 to 220 words)

Write an article titled Is the biggest city always the capital? Compare one US capital and one Commonwealth capital, explain the reasons for each choice and give your opinion. Use paragraphs and a range of connectors.

Model article

Is the biggest city always the capital?

If you ask people to name the capital of Australia, many will say Sydney. They are wrong, and the mistake tells a story. In 1901, Sydney and Melbourne were rivals, and neither accepted that the other one should win. As a result, a brand-new capital, Canberra, was chosen in 1908 and Parliament moved there in 1927.

The United States made a similar choice more than a century earlier. In 1790, the North and the South agreed on a deal: the capital would be built on the Potomac River. Today Washington, D.C. is nowhere near as big as New York City: with 8.8 million inhabitants, New York City is more than twelve times as big.

Moreover, these capitals were not chosen by chance. Their location was a political message: a compromise between rivals and a place that belonged to nobody in particular.

In my opinion, a capital does not need to be the biggest city. Whereas big cities attract business, a smaller capital can be fairer for all the regions. However, a new capital costs a fortune, so the choice must be made carefully.

About 210 words. Figures: 2020 United States census.

Student checklist

  • I compare one US capital and one Commonwealth capital.
  • I give dates, the people who decided and the reasons.
  • I use complex comparatives, the passive and connectors.
  • I give and justify my opinion.
  • For the oral: I speak from five key words and I pronounce the names correctly.

Compréhension de l'oral (/10), CO

Document d'entraînement (environ 1 min 20), écrit pour la classe.

Transcript (version prof seulement)

> If you ask people to name the capital of Australia, many will say Sydney. They are wrong, and the mistake tells a story. In 1901, Sydney and Melbourne were rivals, and neither accepted that the other one should win. A new capital, Canberra, was chosen later, as a compromise. The United States had made a similar deal more than a century earlier: Washington, D.C. would stand on the Potomac, between North and South, and it would not be New York. Geography helps to explain the first big cities: they grew near water, on harbours and rivers. Politics helps to explain the capitals: they were often chosen to be central, or fair, or far from the power of business. So the question of this sequence is not a riddle for a quiz. It is a question about power: is the biggest city always the capital, and should it be?

Consigne : « Écoutez trois fois le document. Rendez compte en français : quelle erreur est citée, quels exemples de capitales sont donnés, comment le texte distingue villes et capitales, et quelle question il pose. »

Corrigé (version prof). Beaucoup de gens prennent Sydney pour la capitale de l'Australie ; l'erreur vient de la rivalité entre Sydney et Melbourne, d'où le compromis de Canberra. Les États-Unis ont fait un choix semblable avec Washington, D.C., sur le Potomac, et non New York. Les grandes villes ont souvent grandi près de l'eau ; les capitales sont souvent un choix politique (position centrale, compromis, distance vis-à-vis des affaires). La question n'est pas un quiz : elle porte sur le pouvoir.

Compréhension de l'écrit (/10), CE

Texte d'entraînement (environ 2 600 signes), écrit pour la classe à partir des lessons de la séquence.

> Two hundred years ago, the first big cities in the United States grew where boats could stop: on the coast, on a river, or on a lake. New York had a harbour; New Orleans stood near the mouth of the Mississippi; Chicago grew on Lake Michigan. Transport shaped the map. Politics shaped the capitals. When Americans chose a capital, they often avoided the biggest city. They wanted a place near the centre of the state, easier to reach for everybody, and sometimes far from the power of business. That is why Albany, not New York City, is the capital of New York State, and Sacramento, not Los Angeles, is the capital of California. > > Washington, D.C. itself was a deal. In 1790, the North and the South agreed: the capital would be built on the Potomac. It was not chosen because it was already the largest city. It was chosen so that no existing rival would quite win. In the Commonwealth, the same pattern appears. Canberra was a compromise between Sydney and Melbourne. Ottawa was a compromise in Canada. These cities were not built to be the most populated. They were built to be possible. > > So the biggest city is not always the capital, and it is not always the most important one either: importance depends on what we measure, population, money, or political power. The sequence ends with a debate and a final task, an article or a podcast, because the question is not only a fact. It is a judgement: should a capital be the biggest city, or should it belong to nobody in particular?

Source : texte d'entraînement écrit pour la classe.

Questions de guidage (en français) :

  • Présentez le document et la distinction qu'il pose entre villes et capitales.
  • Pourquoi les premières grandes villes américaines ont-elles grandi près de l'eau ? Donnez deux exemples.
  • Quel raisonnement explique le choix d'Albany ou de Sacramento ?
  • En quoi Washington, Canberra et Ottawa sont-ils des compromis ?
  • Quelle question le texte laisse-t-il ouverte à la fin ?

Corrigé (version prof). Le texte distingue la géographie des grandes villes (l'eau, les transports) et le choix politique des capitales. Exemples : New York et son port, La Nouvelle-Orléans sur le Mississippi, Chicago sur le lac Michigan. Albany et Sacramento sont choisis comme lieux plus centraux, parfois loin du pouvoir économique, plutôt que les plus grandes villes de l'État. Washington (1790, Potomac, Nord et Sud), Canberra (Sydney et Melbourne) et Ottawa sont des compromis : des villes « possibles », non les plus peuplées. La question finale porte sur ce que l'on mesure (population, argent, pouvoir) et sur ce que devrait être une capitale.

Expression écrite (/10), PE

Traitez une des questions :

  • Is the biggest city always the capital, and should it be? Compare at least one US capital and one Commonwealth capital, with facts from the sequence.
  • Write a short article for a school web magazine (180 to 220 words) titled « Is the biggest city always the capital? ». Use paragraphs, comparatives, and at least two precise figures or dates.

Éléments de corrigé (version prof). Une comparaison claire (Washington et New York, ou Canberra et Sydney, ou les deux) ; au moins une date ou un fait (1790, 1908, un chiffre de population) ; un avis justifié. Structures attendues : comparatives et superlatatives, passif (was chosen, was built). LVA B1+.

Production orale (/10), PO

Compare one US capital and one Commonwealth capital: when and why it was chosen, who decided, what it says about the country. End with your answer to the question: is the biggest city always the capital, and should it be? Speak from a card with five key words only.

Questions d'interaction : Why do people confuse Sydney and Canberra? What is the difference between a harbour city and a capital? Would you place a new capital far from business, or in the biggest city?

Éléments de corrigé (version prof). Deux capitales précises, un compromis expliqué, un avis final. Prononciation des noms (Washington, D.C., Canberra, Ottawa, Albany, Sacramento). Pas de lecture.

Compréhension orale par lesson (version prof)

Une activité de compréhension orale à insérer dans chaque lesson, en Version prof pour le transcript et le corrigé. L'élève entend le document trois fois, ou deux fois si le temps est court. Durée de chaque enregistrement : environ une minute. Consigne identique : « Écoutez. Puis répondez en anglais ou en français, selon la consigne de la lesson. »

Sortie de secours, si le fichier audio manque : l'enseignante lit le transcript une fois à voix normale, une fois un peu plus lentement ; les élèves écrivent trois informations, puis une phrase.

Lesson 1. Water first, cities next · CO

Transcript. Two hundred years ago, people and goods travelled by water. The first big American cities grew where boats could stop: New York on a harbour, New Orleans near the mouth of a great river, Chicago on a lake. Later, canals, railways and highways changed the map. But the first rule was simple: follow the water, and you will find the city.

Corrigé. Les premières grandes villes ont grandi près de l'eau, là où les bateaux pouvaient s'arrêter. Exemples : New York (port), La Nouvelle-Orléans (fleuve), Chicago (lac). Les canaux, le rail et les routes ont ensuite modifié la carte.

Lesson 2. Comparing Cities · CO

Transcript. New York City is much bigger than Washington, D.C. With more than eight million inhabitants, it is a centre of business and culture. Washington is smaller, and it was built as a capital. If you only compare size, New York wins. If you ask where Congress meets, the answer is Washington. The biggest city is not always the most important one: it depends on the question you ask.

Corrigé. New York est bien plus peuplée que Washington (plus de huit millions d'habitants) et c'est un centre d'affaires et de culture. Washington, plus petite, a été construite comme capitale, là où siège le Congrès. La « plus importante » dépend de la question posée.

Lesson 3. A capital built for a deal · CO

Transcript. In 1790, the North and the South of the United States made a deal. The capital would not be New York, and it would not belong to one state. It would be built on the Potomac River, on land given for that purpose. Washington, D.C. was not chosen because it was already large. It was chosen so that no rival city would quite win.

Corrigé. En 1790, le Nord et le Sud concluent un accord : la capitale ne sera pas New York, ni la propriété d'un État ; elle sera construite sur le Potomac. Washington n'est pas choisie pour sa taille, mais pour qu'aucune ville rivale ne l'emporte vraiment.

Lesson 4. Capitals by compromise · CO

Transcript. Look at a map of the United States and find the capital of each state. Very often, it is not the biggest city. Albany is the capital of New York State, not New York City. Sacramento, not Los Angeles, is the capital of California. A capital is sometimes placed near the centre, so that more people can reach it, and sometimes away from the power of business.

Corrigé. La capitale d'un État n'est souvent pas la plus grande ville : Albany et non New York ; Sacramento et non Los Angeles. On la place parfois au centre, pour qu'elle soit accessible, parfois à distance du pouvoir économique.

Lesson 5. One Commonwealth, many capitals · CO

Transcript. In the Commonwealth, the same question comes back. Many people say Sydney when they mean the capital of Australia, but the capital is Canberra, a city chosen as a compromise between Sydney and Melbourne. In Canada, Ottawa was chosen rather than Toronto or Montreal. These capitals were built to be possible for a whole country, not to be the largest place on the map.

Corrigé. Dans le Commonwealth, Sydney n'est pas la capitale de l'Australie : c'est Canberra, un compromis entre Sydney et Melbourne. Au Canada, Ottawa a été choisie plutôt que Toronto ou Montréal. Ces capitales doivent convenir à tout un pays, pas être les plus grandes villes.

Lesson 6. Debate · CO

Transcript. Speaker A: A capital should be the biggest city. That is where people work, where ideas circulate, and where visitors arrive. Putting the government somewhere small is a fiction. Speaker B: I disagree. If the capital is also the biggest business centre, one city holds all the power. A smaller capital can belong to the whole country, not to one harbour or one bank. The question is not only « what is », but « what should be ».

Corrigé. A soutient que la capitale doit être la plus grande ville (travail, idées, visiteurs). B répond qu'une capitale-centre d'affaires concentre trop de pouvoir, et qu'une capitale plus petite peut appartenir à tout le pays. Le débat porte sur le fait et sur le devoir-être.

Lesson 7. The Biggest City game · CO

Transcript. Here is a short radio quiz. Question one: What is the capital of Australia? If you said Sydney, try again. Question two: Which American city is larger, New York or Washington? New York, by far. Question three: Why was Washington, D.C. built where it is? Because of a political deal, not because of its size. Remember the rule we have practised: check the map, check the date, and do not trust the first name you think of.

Corrigé. 1. Canberra, non Sydney. 2. New York, nettement plus grande que Washington. 3. Washington a été construite là où elle est à cause d'un accord politique, non à cause de sa taille. Règle : vérifier la carte et la date, et se méfier du premier nom qui vient à l'esprit.

L'élève ne peut pas venir en classe ou est hospitalisé

L'épreuve blanche peut se faire à la maison ou à l'hôpital, sans aide, en respectant la durée. L'élève note l'heure de début et de fin. Le document audio est déposé sur l'ENT ; l'élève l'écoute trois fois, avec une minute de pause entre les écoutes, sans revenir en arrière. L'oral se fait par enregistrement (présentation), puis par un échange au téléphone ou en visioconférence avec l'enseignante, ou à défaut par trois questions écrites envoyées après l'enregistrement.
