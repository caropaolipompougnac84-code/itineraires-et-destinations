Lesson 8. A Citizens’ Hearing

One promise, one fact, one request: you can do this alone.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

Name one film or one painting from this sequence, and one fact about it.

Self-check

For example: The Grizzlies is set in Kugluktuk, Nunavut. A team becomes a family.

4 to 8 min

Today’s goal

I can present one promise, one fact with its source, and one request.

Route S · SEGPA

I can: say one promise, one fact, and one request.

Today you choose one dossier: the White Paper, a dam, a pipeline, or drinking water. You say: what was promised, one fact, and what you ask for now. If you work alone, write the sentences and say them aloud. Then start a short open letter to a Member of Parliament. Finish the letter for homework if you need more time.

Route C · CAP

I can: write the four parts in short sentences, with one source.

Prepare four short parts: the promise (who, what, when); what was done (a fact and its source); what was not done; your recommendation. Use a figure if you have one, and give its date. You can do this alone, from your notes. Then start an open letter of about 120 words: Dear Member of Parliament… Yours sincerely. Finish it for homework.

Route P · Bac pro

I can: draft the four parts and the opening of the letter, with two sourced facts.

A citizens’ hearing presents both sides and then makes a recommendation. Use reported speech for the promise, the passive for what was done, and should have or failed to for what was not done. End with ought to or have to. The open letter (200 to 250 words in the standard sequence; here, aim at 150 if that is more comfortable) must stay respectful and firm, with two facts and their sources.

Exercise 1

Choose your dossier and name the promise.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: Dossier D. In 2015 the government promised to end all long-term drinking-water advisories by March 2021.

Your turn. Write your dossier letter (A, B, C or D) and one sentence about the promise.

Self-check

Any clear sentence is fine if the promise is real and comes from this sequence.

Exercise 2

Add one fact with its source.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: 39 advisories were still in effect on 21 October 2026 (Indigenous Services Canada).

Your turn. Write one fact for your dossier, with a source (and a date if it is a figure).

Self-check

The fact must come from the lessons and include a source.

Exercise 3

Write one request.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: The government ought to finish the work on safe drinking water now.

Your turn. Write your request with ought to or have to.

Self-check

Possible sentence: The government has to keep its promises and publish clear dates.

CFA task · instead of Exercise 3

If you are in a training centre, do this task instead of Exercise 3. You may follow route C or route P for the reading.

Worked example (already corrected). A trainee prepares a one-minute oral for a meeting: promise, fact, request. Example: ‘In 2015 the government promised safe water by 2021. 39 advisories remained on 21 October 2026 (Indigenous Services Canada). We recommend a clear new deadline.’

Your turn. Write your one-minute oral in five or six sentences, then say it aloud. You do not need a partner.

Self-check

A complete answer has the promise, one sourced fact, what was not done, and a request.

Speaking frame · 40 to 44 min

Say this aloud, and change the words in brackets if you need to:

I am talking about [the water promise / a dam / a pipeline / the White Paper]. The government promised that […]. One fact is […], according to [source]. I ask that […].

Written record · 44 to 48 min

Route S. I can name one promise, one fact and one request.

Route C. My four parts: the promise, what was done, what was not done, and my recommendation, with one source.

Route P. I can report a promise, give a figure with its source, and write a respectful request in an open letter.

Progress record · 48 to 50 min

  • I read the short text for my route.
  • I did two exercises and checked the self-check key.
  • I said one sentence aloud.
  • I copied the written record.

Homework. Finish a short open letter (Route S: about 80 words; Route C: about 120; Route P: about 150). Give two facts if you can, and one request. Write one sentence: what I will remember from this unit.

Emergency exit · 8 min

  • 0 to 2. Say this sentence: “I can report a promise, give a fact with its source, and say what should happen now.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record for your route.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework.
  • 7 to 8. Check one self-check answer.
