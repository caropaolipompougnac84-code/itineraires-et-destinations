Lesson 3. Backstage: three role-plays

Debatable question: Who really makes a star: the star or the team?
By the end of this lesson, I can: ask for something, offer help, make a suggestion, say no politely and pay a compliment in a professional situation.

For the teacher: timing (52 minutes)
Step Time Activity
1 0-4 Recap and flashcard duel
2 4-7 The question of the day
3 7-13 Expanding your vocabulary: professions and polite phrases
4 13-19 The teacher’s point: polite requests, offers, suggestions
5 19-25 Exercises 1 to 4
6 25-30 Preparation of the role-play (cards)
7 30-46 Role-plays: every pair plays, peer assessment with the grid
8 46-50 Written record
9 50-52 Homework

Stop point (minute 46). Every pair has played at least once.

Emergency exit (8 minutes). Pairs play a short version (four lines) of Scene 1 using only the five lines of useful phrases. Two pairs perform in front of the class.

Step 1. Recap

  1. Give two words for hair and two words for bling. 2. Put in order: dress / sparkly / pink / a. 3. Past simple of make, wear, write.

    Answer key

    Answer key: 1 wig, curls; rhinestones, sequins · 2 a sparkly pink dress · 3 made, wore, wrote.

Step 2. The question of the day

Ask: Before a concert, who works with the star? Make a list on the board (dresser, hairdresser, wig maker, make-up artist, sound engineer, lighting technician, stage manager...). Then: Who really makes a star: the star or the team? Students vote with a show of hands and keep their vote for the end of the lesson.

Transition. At work, we must speak politely to the boss and to colleagues. Today, you play the team.

Step 3. Expanding your vocabulary

A. The backstage team

Word Meaning Example
a dresser the person who helps an artist dress and change costumes The dresser prepares the quick change.
a hairdresser / a wig stylist the person who styles hair or wigs The wig stylist adds volume.
a make-up artist the person who does professional make-up The make-up artist chooses colours for the lights.
a fitting trying on a garment to adjust it The fitting is at six.
a quick change a very fast change of costume during a show She has a quick change after song three.
stage lights the strong lights of the stage Under the stage lights, colours look paler.
a dressing room the room where artists get ready Her dressing room is full of wigs.

B. Useful phrases (to interact)

Function Useful phrases
Requests Could you...? · Can I have...? · Could you make it a bit more...?
Offers Would you like me to...? · Shall I...? · Do you want me to...?
Suggestions What about...? · Why don’t we...? · It might look better if...
Polite disagreement I’m not sure about... · I’d rather... · I see what you mean, but...
Compliments I love the way... · That looks amazing! · You look fabulous!

Exercises on vocabulary

Exercise C1. Who says it? dresser, hairdresser or make-up artist?
1. “Close your eyes, I’m doing your eyeliner.” 2. “I’ll take it in at the waist.” 3. “Shall I add more volume on top?”

Answer key

Answer key: 1 make-up artist · 2 dresser · 3 hairdresser.

Exercise C2. Fill in the gaps: fitting · quick change · dressing room · stage lights
Dolly is in her (1) __. The (2) _ is at six. After song three, she has a (3) of two minutes. The colours must be strong for the (4) ___.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 dressing room · 2 fitting · 3 quick change · 4 stage lights.

Flashcards

Front Back
a quick change (blue) changement rapide · She has a quick change after song three.
I’d rather + base verb (red) je préférerais · I’d rather wear the white one. · I’d = I would
I see what you mean, but... (green) je comprends, mais... · polite disagreement

Step 4. The teacher’s point

Teacher script. Mime a situation with a student: you are the hairdresser, the student is Dolly. Make an offer, a suggestion, then accept a polite refusal. Students note the phrases; sort them into five lines on the board.

Point Form Example
Requests Could you + base verb? (more polite than Can you) Could you make it a bit shorter?
Offers Would you like me to + base verb? · Shall I + base verb? Shall I add more volume?
Suggestions What about + noun / verb-ing? · Why don’t we + base verb? · It might look better if + present or past simple What about the pink suit?
Preferences I’d rather + base verb · I’d prefer + noun I’d rather wear the white one.
Nuance a bit more / a bit less + adjective a bit higher, a bit less glitter

Quick check. Make it more polite: Give me the pink one. → Could you give me the pink one, please?

Step 5. Exercises

Exercise 1. Request, offer, suggestion, disagreement or compliment?
1. Shall I add some glitter? 2. I’m not sure about the green. 3. Could you make it a bit tighter? 4. What about false lashes? 5. I love the way it shines.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 offer · 2 polite disagreement · 3 request · 4 suggestion · 5 compliment.

Exercise 2. Complete the dialogue with: Shall I · I’d rather · Could you · What about · I love
A: ... the white dress? B: Hmm, ... wear the pink one. A: OK. ... take it in at the waist? B: Yes, please. ... make it a bit shorter too? A: Of course. B: ... the way you work!

Answer key

Answer key: What about · I’d rather · Shall I · Could you · I love.

Exercise 3. Make these sentences polite.
1. Add hairspray. 2. I want the golden wig. 3. No, not the green eyeshadow. 4. Do it faster.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 Could you add some hairspray, please? 2 I’d rather have the golden wig. / Could I have the golden wig? 3 I’m not sure about the green eyeshadow. 4 Would it be possible to do it a bit faster?

Exercise 4. Offer or suggestion? Transform.
1. I’ll add more volume. → (Shall I...?) 2. We use a zip. → (Why don’t we...?) 3. The pink lipstick. → (What about...?)

Answer key

Answer key: 1 Shall I add more volume? 2 Why don’t we use a zip? 3 What about the pink lipstick?

Step 6-7. The three backstage role-plays

Work in pairs. One student plays Dolly, the other plays the professional. Prepare for five minutes with your card, play for two or three minutes, then change roles. Use the Help or Going further starters if you need them.

Scene 1. Dolly and her dresser

Situation. Twenty minutes before the show. Dolly tries on her stage costume. There is a quick change between two songs: she has less than two minutes to change outfits.

Role card A · Dolly
1. Choose between the pink rhinestone suit and the white fringe dress.
2. Ask for one change (shorter, tighter, more sparkle).
3. Check that the quick change is possible.
4. Pay a compliment at the end.

Role card B · The dresser
1. Show two outfits and describe them (adjective order).
2. Check the size and the shoes.
3. Suggest a zip or Velcro for the quick change.
4. Accept or politely disagree with Dolly’s ideas.

Help (both roles): I want... · I like... · It’s too... · Do you want...? · Here is...
Core: Could you make it a bit more...? · I’d rather... · Would you like me to...? · Shall I...?
Going further: It might look better if... · For the quick change, I’d suggest... · If we use a zip instead of buttons, you’ll...

Professional words: fitting, costume, rhinestone suit, sequins, fringe, hem, zip, seam, to take in, to let out, quick change, rack, steamer, safety pin.

Model dialogue (read it after your own performance)
Dresser: Here are your two outfits: the pink rhinestone suit and the white fringe dress.
Dolly: Oh, I love the pink one! Could you make the jacket a bit tighter?
Dresser: Of course. Shall I take it in at the waist?
Dolly: Yes, please. And the quick change, is it possible in two minutes?
Dresser: It might be faster if we use a zip instead of buttons.
Dolly: Good idea. I’d rather have a zip, then. You’re a star!

Scene 2. Dolly and her hairdresser

Situation. Dolly chooses a wig for the concert. The hairdresser styles it and adds volume. The stage lights are very bright.

Role card A · Dolly
1. Say what kind of hair you want tonight (big, curly, platinum).
2. Ask for more volume or softer curls.
3. Politely refuse one suggestion.
4. Thank the hairdresser.

Role card B · The hairdresser
1. Show two wigs and describe them.
2. Suggest a style for the outfit and the lights.
3. Explain what you are doing (curling, teasing, spraying).
4. Check that Dolly is happy.

Help: I want big hair. · Not this one. · Do you like...? · More or less?
Core: Could you make it a bit higher? · I’d rather have... · Would you like me to add...? · What about curls?
Going further: I’m not sure about the colour, could we...? · Under the stage lights, a lighter shade would... · I’d recommend...

Professional words: wig, hairpiece, wig cap, styling, volume, curls, waves, teasing, hairspray, hairpins, bleached, highlights, shade, platinum, golden.

Model dialogue
Hairdresser: Would you like the platinum wig or the golden one tonight?
Dolly: The platinum one, please. The bigger the better!
Hairdresser: Shall I add more volume on top?
Dolly: Yes, and could you make the curls a bit softer?
Hairdresser: Sure. Under the stage lights, a little hairspray will keep them in place.
Dolly: I love the way it shines. Thank you, honey!

Scene 3. Dolly and her make-up artist

Situation. The make-up artist prepares Dolly’s stage make-up. The colours must be strong enough for the lights and the cameras.

Role card A · Dolly
1. Choose the colours for your eyes and your lips.
2. Ask for false lashes.
3. Ask for more sparkle, or less.
4. Pay a compliment.

Role card B · The make-up artist
1. Explain the steps (foundation, eyes, lips) with First I’ll..., then I’ll...
2. Suggest colours that work under the lights.
3. Ask Dolly to close her eyes, look up, and so on.
4. Check the result with Dolly.

Help: I want... · More colour, please. · Close your eyes, please. · Do you like...?
Core: Could you make it a bit more...? · Would you like me to...? · What about a pink lipstick?
Going further: That’s lovely, but maybe... · Under the lights, this shade will... · I’d suggest a lighter...

Professional words: foundation, concealer, contouring, highlighter, blush, eyeshadow, eyeliner, mascara, false lashes, lip liner, lipstick, stage make-up, setting spray, glitter.

Model dialogue
Make-up artist: First I’ll put on the foundation, then we’ll do the eyes.
Dolly: Lovely. I’d like lilac eyeshadow, like on the Warhol portrait.
Make-up artist: Good choice. Would you like me to add false lashes?
Dolly: Yes, please! And could you make the lips a bit brighter?
Make-up artist: What about this red? Under the lights, it will look perfect.
Dolly: I love the way you work. I’m ready for the show!

Peer assessment grid for the role-plays

Criterion Not yet Nearly Reached Beyond
Interaction: I react and keep the conversation going I read my card only short answers I answer and ask questions I react to everything, suggest and negotiate
Polite requests, offers, suggestions none Can I / I want Could you / Would you like me to / What about It might look better if / I’d rather / Would it be possible
Professional vocabulary fewer than 3 words 3 to 5 words 6 to 9 words 10 words or more, used precisely
Pronunciation and fluency hard to understand understandable with pauses clear, few pauses clear and natural, good intonation
Language: vocabulary reused and syntax many errors some errors in questions questions and modals correct varied and correct

Back to the vote. After the role-plays, ask again: Who makes a star? Students explain if they changed their vote. Possible answer: I changed my mind: without the dresser, the quick change is impossible, so the team makes the star possible, but the star makes the choices.

Step 8. Written record

Lesson 3. Backstage
Behind a star, there is a team: a dresser, a hairdresser or wig stylist, a make-up artist.
To be polite at work: request = Could you...?; offer = Shall I...? / Would you like me to...?; suggestion = What about...? / Why don’t we...?; preference = I’d rather...; disagreement = I’m not sure about...; compliment = I love the way...
My answer: I think the star / the team... because...

Step 9. Homework

  1. Write the dialogue you played (ten lines), or record it.
  2. Learn the five lines of useful phrases with flashcards (one red card per line).
  3. Help box: learn three polite phrases by heart and say them to someone at home.

Passing the torch. Dolly gave young artists advice. Miley Cyrus remembered this one: “You do you, I’ll do me, and together we’ll be us.” (reported by Billboard, 2025). What advice would you give a young colleague before a first day at work?
