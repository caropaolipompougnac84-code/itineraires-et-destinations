How US Elections Work: from the Primaries to the Midterms

Première baccalauréat professionnel. Contextes du programme : vie quotidienne, personnelle, sociale et civique. Thème : histoire, institutions, élections. Niveau B1, with an entry level.

For the student who cannot come to class, or who is in hospital. Work through the lesson in order. The answer keys are available in this version: try first, then open them to check. Send your work to your teacher.

Assessment out of twenty

Written test, thirty minutes, at the end of lesson seven. The final task (lesson eight) is marked with the grid below.

Test

Part A: knowledge (12 points)

QuestionPoints
A1. How many electors are there in total?1
A2. How many electoral votes does a candidate need to win?1
A3. On which day of the week is Election Day?1
A4. Name the three branches of government.2
A5. What is a primary?1
A6. What is a swing state? Give one example.2
A7. How many representatives and how many senators are there?1
A8. How long is the term of a representative? Of a senator?1

A10. Who drew the first party donkey and elephant?1

Part B: grammar, the passive and the conditional (8 points)

QuestionPoints
B1. Rewrite in the passive: Congress makes the laws.1
B2. Rewrite in the passive: Voters elect the House every two years.1
B3. Rewrite in the passive: Congress counted the votes in January.1
B4. Complete: The bill ______ (sign) by the president last week.1
B5. Complete: If the Democrats ______ (win) 218 seats, they ______ (control) the House.2
B6. Complete: If a candidate ______ (win) California, she ______ (get) fifty-four electoral votes.1
B7. Answer with a full sentence: What would happen if nobody got 270 electoral votes?1
Answer key

Part A

  • A1. 538 (1 pt)
  • A2. 270 (1 pt)
  • A3. A Tuesday (the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November) (1 pt)
  • A4. Congress, the President, the Supreme Court (2 pts)
  • A5. A vote to choose a party's candidate (1 pt)
  • A6. A state where both parties can win; for example Arizona, Georgia, Michigan, Nevada, North Carolina, Pennsylvania or Wisconsin (2 pts)
  • A7. 435 representatives and 100 senators (1 pt)
  • A8. Two years; six years (1 pt)
  • A9. The elections in the middle of the president's term; 3 November 2026 (1 pt)
  • A10. Thomas Nast (1 pt)

Part B

  • B1. The laws are made by Congress. (1 pt)
  • B2. The House is elected (by voters) every two years. (1 pt)
  • B3. The votes were counted by Congress in January. (1 pt)
  • B4. was signed (1 pt)
  • B5. win, will control (2 pts)
  • B6. won, would get (or wins, will get: accept both if the student explains the choice) (1 pt)
  • B7. If nobody got 270 electoral votes, the House would choose the president. (1 pt)
