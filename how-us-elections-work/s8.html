What we need to say

In this lesson, you need to explain the whole system clearly to a French colleague.

To organise your explanation, we need these words: first of all, then, because, for example, to sum up.

To explain how the system works, we need the passive; to give possible results, we need the conditional: The president is elected by 538 electors. If nobody gets 270 votes, the House will choose the president.

The task

Situation

Situation. You are on your work placement. A French colleague has seen the news about the American elections and asks you: How do they elect their president? And what are these midterms? You explain in English, because a visitor from an American partner company is listening too.

Grammar to say it: the passive and the conditional

Why we need it

To explain how the system works, we need the passive; to give possible results, we need the conditional: The president is elected by 538 electors. If nobody gets 270 votes, the House will choose the president.

Grammar point: board explanation

Teacher script
  1. Ask students to give you one passive sentence and one conditional from the sequence. Write them on the board.
  2. Build a table of all the forms seen: present passive, past passive, by + agent, first and second conditional.
  3. Teach question word order for the colleague's questions: How is the president elected? Who chooses the president? What would happen if ...?
  4. Write the linking words of a presentation in order: first of all, then, after that, finally, to sum up.
  5. Model a thirty-second presentation using the board, then students copy.
  6. Quick check before the final task.
Board: students copy

Review: passive and conditionals; question word order; linking words for a presentation

Rule
Passive: be + past participle. Real: if + present, will. Imaginary: if + past, would.
Form
The president is elected by 538 electors. Lincoln was chosen in 1860. If nobody gets 270, the House will choose. If I were American, I would vote.
Use
Passive to explain how the system works; conditionals to give possible results and to imagine.
Examples from the lesson
  • How is the president elected? He or she is elected by the Electoral College.
  • Who chooses the president? The electors do.
  • What would happen if nobody got 270 votes? The House would choose.
Common mistakes
  • How the president is elected? → How is the president elected?
  • Who does choose the president? → Who chooses the president? (subject question)
  • Firstly of all → First of all.
French and English
French can keep statement order in questions: Le président est élu comment ? English never does: How is the president elected?
Also on the board: Linking words for a presentation
Order: first of all, then, after that, finally. Example: for example. Contrast: however. Conclusion: to sum up.

Modal and perfect passives, reporting passive, third and mixed conditionals, inversion: use two or three in your final task to show your level.

Text analysis: two systems, one goalGoing further

In France, the president is elected directly by the people, in two rounds if necessary. In the United States, the president is chosen by electors, state by state. Both systems were designed to give power to the voters, yet they produce very different campaigns: French candidates speak to the whole nation, while American candidates concentrate on a handful of swing states. Neither system is perfect; each reflects the history of the country that created it.

  1. Find three passives.
  2. Which words show contrast?
  3. What does a handful of mean?
  4. Explain the last sentence in your own words.
  5. Which system do you prefer? One reason.
Answer key
  1. is elected, is chosen, were designed.
  2. yet, while, neither.
  3. a small number of.
  4. Each country built its system according to its own history; no system is perfect.
  5. Open opinion with one argument.
