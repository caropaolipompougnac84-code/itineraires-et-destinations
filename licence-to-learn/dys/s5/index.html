Lesson 5. The rules of a spy

I can use must, mustn’t and don’t have to.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 5 of 6. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Split the lesson into two blocks: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Say one sentence from the previous lesson (Lesson 4: in the films the boss is M; in real life the boss is C) 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Read the goal and the word box 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Read or listen to your route’s text 8 min (minutes 8 to 16)
  4. Exercise 1, then check it 8 min (minutes 16 to 24)
  5. Pause: look away from the text or move 2 min (minutes 24 to 26)
  6. Exercise 2, then check it 8 min (minutes 26 to 34)
  7. Exercise 3, then check it 6 min (minutes 34 to 40)
  8. Say your final answer aloud with the speaking frame 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  9. Written record 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  10. Progress record 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  11. Read and write down the homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. One sentence for your route (see below), copied or typed.
  • Participation. Say at least one answer aloud. In class, use a quiet voice; at home, say it to yourself. You do not need to record yourself.
  • Homework for next lesson. Make two flashcards: mustn’t and don’t have to. Prepare three qualities for the interview. Say your written record twice.

Stopping point. Minute 44: stop the main exercises. Keep the written record and the homework, even if an exercise is unfinished.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: say the emergency sentence of the lesson aloud.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: complete the short written record.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: write down the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: check one answer with the key. Do not rush through all the unfinished exercises.

Choose your route: Route S · SEGPA · Route C · CAP · Route P · Bac pro · CFA. Do only your route.

Route S · SEGPA

Short text

A spy must keep secrets.

A spy mustn’t tell the secret to everyone.

A spy doesn’t have to drive a film car.

Mustn’t is not the same as don’t have to.

You mustn’t lie: it is forbidden.

You don’t have to wear a film suit: it is not necessary.

Exercise S1

Find the right form

Worked example. “A spy must keep secrets.” Must = it is necessary.

Now you.
1. A spy tell the secret to everyone. (forbidden)
2. A spy drive a film car. (not necessary)

Self-check

1. mustn’t.
2. doesn’t have to.

Exercise S2

Complete

Worked example. “You must be discreet.”

Now you.
Use: must, mustn’t.
1. You keep secrets.
2. You post photos of your job.

Self-check

1. must.
2. mustn’t.

Exercise S3

Write and speak

Worked example. “I must keep secrets. I mustn’t tell everyone.”

Now you.
Write two rules: one with must, one with mustn’t. Say them aloud.

Self-check

Possible answer: “I must be discreet. I mustn’t tell the secret.” Other accurate rules are accepted.

Route C · CAP

Short text

Must and have to express obligation: it is necessary.

Mustn’t expresses prohibition: it is forbidden.

Don’t have to (or doesn’t have to) means there is no obligation: it is not necessary.

These two last forms are not the same.

An MI6 officer has to travel a lot. An MI5 officer doesn’t have to live abroad.

Real agents don’t have to drive an Aston Martin. That is only in the films.

Exercise C1

Choose the form

Worked example. “You mustn’t lie” = it is forbidden. “You don’t have to wear a suit” = it is not necessary.

Now you.
1. You ______ be British to work for MI6. (necessary)
2. You ______ post photos of your job. (forbidden)
3. You ______ drive an Aston Martin. (not necessary)

Self-check

1. must (or have to).
2. mustn’t.
3. don’t have to.

Exercise C2

Complete two more

Worked example. “You must be discreet.”

Now you.
4. You ______ be discreet. (necessary)
5. You ______ tell anyone your password. (forbidden)

Self-check

4. must (or have to).
5. mustn’t.

Exercise C3

Write your Spy Code

Worked example. “A new agent must be discreet. He or she mustn’t post photos of the job. He or she doesn’t have to drive a film car.”

Now you.
Write three rules for a new agent: one must, one mustn’t, one doesn’t have to. Say one of them aloud.

Self-check

Your three sentences must use the three forms correctly. Meaning can vary if it stays accurate.

Route P · Bac pro

Short text

The contrast between prohibition and absence of obligation is a frequent professional need: safety rules, uniforms, hours, tools.

Mustn’t closes a choice. Don’t have to leaves a choice.

In this sequence, a spy must keep secrets and mustn’t publish them. A real officer doesn’t have to copy the films.

You can now write a short Spy Code of five rules, using all three forms.

Going further, if you have time: in Skyfall, M reads the end of Tennyson’s poem “Ulysses” (1842, public domain). The poem says that much is lost, however the will remains: “to strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield.”

Exercise P1

Choose and justify

Worked example. “You don’t have to drive an Aston Martin, because that is not necessary in real work.”

Now you.
For each sentence, choose the form and add because.
1. You ______ tell your password. (Why?)
2. You ______ wear a dinner jacket every day. (Why?)

Self-check

1. mustn’t, because it is forbidden (or it would not be safe).
2. don’t have to, because it is not necessary in real work.

Exercise P2

Write a Spy Code

Worked example. “A new officer must be discreet, mustn’t discuss the file at home, has to follow the safety rules, and doesn’t have to imitate a film hero.”

Now you.
Write five rules. Use must, mustn’t, has to and doesn’t have to at least once each.

Self-check

All three ideas (obligation, prohibition, no obligation) must appear. Accurate professional wording is accepted.

Exercise P3

Optional poem or short comment

Worked example. “The poem says that an old institution can stay useful, however it must keep its will.”

Now you.
Either comment on the poem in 40 to 60 words, or explain in 40 to 60 words why don’t have to is not the same as mustn’t. Use however.

Self-check

The answer should use however and keep the two ideas distinct. The poem is optional.

CFA pathway

CAP apprentice: use Route C. Bac pro apprentice: use Route P. Do Exercises 1 and 2 of your route, then do this task instead of Exercise 3. Keep the same written record and homework.

CFA task

Rules at work

Worked example. “In a workshop, you must wear safety shoes. You mustn’t use a phone next to a machine. You don’t have to wear a film costume.”

Now you.
Write three rules for your workplace, or for a workplace you know: one must, one mustn’t, one don’t have to. Starter: “At work, I must . I mustn’t . I don’t have to .”

Self-check

Each form must be used once, with a believable workplace meaning. You may use the spy examples if you do not wish to describe your own workplace.

Written record

S: “I must keep secrets. I mustn’t tell everyone. I don’t have to drive a film car.”

C: “Mustn’t means it is forbidden; don’t have to means it is not necessary.”

P: “A professional rule must distinguish what is forbidden from what is simply not necessary.”

Speaking frame. “I must . I mustn’t . I don’t have to .”

Emergency sentence. “I must keep secrets. I mustn’t tell everyone. I don’t have to drive a film car.”

Homework

Make two flashcards: mustn’t and don’t have to. Prepare three qualities for the interview. Say your written record twice.
