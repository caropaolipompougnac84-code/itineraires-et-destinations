Lesson 9: The results of the 3 November 2026 midterms

Recap check: lesson 8
  1. How many electors are there, and how many electoral votes does a candidate need to become president?
  2. How many voting members are there in the House? How many seats make a majority?
  3. How many senators are there? How many seats make a majority?
  4. In your final task, what did you explain to your French colleague?
Answer key
  1. 538 electors; 270 electoral votes to win.
  2. 435; 218 seats make a majority.
  3. 100 senators; 51 seats make a majority (or 50 + the Vice President, who breaks a tie).
  4. How a party chooses its candidate, the Electoral College, Congress and what the midterms decide.
Question: Who won the midterms of 3 November 2026, and what does it change for the president?
Level: A2 towards B1. After the vote.
Important

You find the results yourself. This page gives no result figures on purpose: you look them up on reliable websites, you write them in the table, and you say where they come from and when.

Objectives

  • Find the official results yourself on reliable websites
  • Fill in a results table with a source and a date for every figure
  • Say who controls the House and the Senate and what it changes for the president
  • Use the past simple (won, lost, gained) and comparatives (more seats than)

Guided steps

1. Choose two websites

One news agency (AP or Reuters) or an official website, and one news organisation (CNN, BBC, NBC, CBS, ABC, The New York Times). Use the cards above.

2. Write the date and the time

Results change while the votes are counted, sometimes for several days. Note the date and the time shown on the page.

3. Find the House and the Senate

Look for the words balance of power, seats won, called, projected. Copy the figures into the table, with the name of the website.

4. Compare your two websites

Are the figures the same? If they are different, keep the more recent one, or the official one, and say why.

5. Read the majority

Compare with 218 (House) and 51 (Senate). Write which party controls each chamber, or not decided yet.

Read the results: what does it mean?

1. Who controls the House after the vote?

Method

Method: look at the number of seats won by each party. A party with 218 seats or more controls the House. If no party has 218 yet, write not decided yet and the date of your page.

2. Who controls the Senate after the vote?

Method

Method: add the senators elected in 2026 and the senators who were not up for election. 51 seats give the majority. With 50 seats each, the Vice President, JD Vance, a Republican, breaks the tie.

3. Compare with before the vote (lesson 7). How many seats did each party gain or lose?

Method

Method: copy the figures from lesson 7 (before the vote), then calculate: after the vote minus before the vote. Use gained or lost.

4. The President, Donald Trump, is a Republican. Is the government divided now?

Method

Method: if the Republicans control the House and the Senate, the government is not divided. If the Democrats control one chamber or both, it is divided government.

5. What changes for the president?

Method

Method: if his party keeps both chambers, he can pass laws more easily. If the opposition controls the House, it can block bills and the budget and open investigations. If it controls the Senate, it can refuse to confirm judges and ministers. Answer with your own results.

6. When does the new Congress start?

Method

On 3 January 2027 (lesson 7). The figures of 3 November can still change before the results are certified.

Grammar: the past simple and comparatives

Rule

The past simple tells finished events: the vote took place on 3 November. Regular verbs take -ed: gain, gained; flip, flipped; control, controlled. Irregular verbs must be learnt: win, won; lose, lost; take, took; keep, kept; become, became. Question: Did the Democrats win the House? Negative: They didn't win the Senate.
Comparatives with numbers: more seats than, fewer seats than, as many seats as.

Complete with the past simple or a comparative. These are past midterms, not 2026.

  1. In 1994, the Republicans ______ (win) the House for the first time in forty years.
  2. They ______ (go) from 176 to 230 seats.
  3. In 2010, the Republicans ______ (gain) many seats: from 178 to 242.
  4. In 2018, the Democrats ______ (take) back the House: from 194 to 235 seats.
  5. In 2022, the Democrats ______ (lose) the House: 213 seats against 222.
  6. In 2022, the Republicans had ______ seats ______ the Democrats.

Production

Oral, one minute: a news flash. You are a radio presenter. Give the results you found, say which party controls each chamber and what it changes for the president. Say where your figures come from.

Written, about eighty words: a news report for the school website: a title, the results, your sources and the date, one sentence about the president.

Sentence starters
  • Here is the news. On 3 November 2026, Americans voted in the midterms.
  • According to ______ (website), on ______ (date), the ______ won ______ seats in the House.
  • The ______ gained / lost ______ seats.
  • In the Senate, the ______ have ______ seats, so they control / don't control the Senate.
  • This means that the president ______ .
  • These results are not final yet: they will be certified by the states.
Method

Method: check that every figure has a source and a date; that the majority is read correctly (218 and 51); that the verbs are in the past simple (voted, won, lost, gained) and that there is at least one comparative (more seats than). No single model answer: the figures are the students' own.

Link with the world of work

At work, you often pass on figures: sales, stock, opening hours. A good professional always checks the source and the date before giving a figure to a customer or a colleague. That is exactly what you do today.

Teacher note

Students find and enter the results themselves. This page deliberately gives no result figures: no seats, no winners. The only figures given are facts known before the vote: the majorities (218 in the House, 51 in the Senate) and the date of the new Congress (3 January 2027). Counting can take several days and some races are decided later, so accept any figure given with a reliable source and a date and time. The answer keys explain the method, never the figures. Check the reading of the majority, the past simple and the comparatives.
