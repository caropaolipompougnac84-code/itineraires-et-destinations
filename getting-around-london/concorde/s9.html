Grammar lesson: upgrading a sentence

To write a convincing article or email that uses everything learnt, we need upgrading a sentence
For the teacher: how to run it (8 to 10 minutes)
  1. Write “Concorde was fast but expensive.” on the board. Ask: how can we make it richer?
  2. Board: the toolbox of the learning journey: comparison, the passive, past modals, third conditional, concession. Students copy.
  3. Upgrade the sentence step by step in front of the class.
  4. Quick check, then exercises.
On the board, to copy

Rule

To upgrade a sentence, keep the idea and add one grammar tool at a time: a comparison (twice as fast as), the passive (was abandoned), a past modal (might have), a third conditional or its inversion (had its costs been controlled), a concession (although). Do not use all the tools in every sentence: choose.

Form

  • comparison: twice as fast as the 707
  • passive: it was abandoned in 2003
  • concession: Although Concorde flew twice as fast as the 707, ...
  • inverted conditional and past modal: had its costs been controlled, it might have lasted longer

Use

  • For Part B: the article or the email.
  • To show the examiner the range of your grammar.
  • To revise the whole learning journey.

Examples from the lesson

  • “Although Concorde flew twice as fast as the 707, it was abandoned in 2003; had its costs been controlled, it might have lasted longer.”
  • “Concorde was built in Bristol and Toulouse.”

Common mistakes

  • Not Although it was fast, but it was expensive., but Although it was fast, it was expensive.
  • Not Had its costs controlled, ..., but Had its costs been controlled, ...
  • Not twice faster than, but twice as fast as

The hook: parallelism and rhetorical question

« Twice the speed of sound, and a price to match: was Concorde worth it? »

The hook balances two ideas with the same rhythm (parallelism): speed and price. It ends with a rhetorical question that announces the debate of the article.

Tools to use:

  • a hook
Show a model answer

Twice the speed of sound, and a price to match: was Concorde worth it? Admittedly, this delta-winged airliner crossed the Atlantic in about three and a half hours, just over half the time of the 707. Yet it was born of a race for prestige: the costs more than tripled by 1967, and the Soviets flew first on 31 December 1968. Had the governments shared their goals earlier, they might have saved years. Nonetheless, Concorde was built, it flew, and it still makes people dream.

Part B. Writing (55 minutes)

Choose one subject. At least 250 words (C1 target).

Subject A. Opinion article. Your school's English web magazine is publishing a special issue on innovations that changed the world. Write an article entitled « Concorde: a triumph of innovation or a costly race for prestige? ». Give your opinion, with examples from the documentary and the documents studied.

Subject B. Email. It is 1 January 1969. You are a British engineer working on Concorde in Bristol. You have just heard that the Soviet Tu-144 flew yesterday. Write an email to your French colleague in Toulouse: express your feelings and your regrets about the race, and share your hopes for Concorde's future.
