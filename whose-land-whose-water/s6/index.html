Lesson 6. Promises Kept, Promises Broken

Goal: I can separate a missed deadline from later progress and compare figures with the correct scope.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 6 of 8. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure.

  1. Recall consultation and consent. 0 to 4
  2. Read the promise and word box. 4 to 8
  3. CBC water report: two listenings. 8 to 16
  4. APTN assessment report: two listenings. 16 to 23
  5. CBC Calls to Action report: one listening and selected replay. 23 to 30
  6. Complete the dated evidence sheet. 30 to 36
  7. Grammar and vocabulary practice. 36 to 40
  8. Protected verdict with qualification. 40 to 44
  9. Written record. 44 to 49
  10. Homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student gives a 40-second verdict with a qualification, then answers “What exactly is being counted?”
  • Homework for next lesson. A 90 to 120-word fact-check of the claim “The water promise was fully completed”, and two flashcards.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: each student says one progress statement and one limitation.
  • 46 to 49: copy the written record.
  • 49 to 51: record homework.
  • 51 to 52: distinguish “active advisories” from “communities”.

Student text

Read

In 2015, Justin Trudeau promised to end long-term drinking-water advisories on First Nations reserves by March 2021.

The official page consulted for this sequence gives a snapshot dated 10 September 2026. It records 158 long-term advisories lifted since November 2015 and 38 active advisories in 36 communities.

These figures concern long-term advisories on public systems on reserves. They do not describe every water problem affecting every Indigenous person in Canada.

New advisories can be added. A cumulative count of lifted advisories is therefore not simply a count taken from one unchanged starting list. Progress and failure to meet the deadline can both be reported.

Building your vocabulary

  • pledge: a formal promise;
  • deadline: a date by which something should be done;
  • advisory: an official warning or instruction;
  • lift an advisory: officially end it;
  • cumulative: added together over time;
  • scope: what a statement or dataset includes.

Grammar exercise

  1. Put in the passive:
    “Authorities lifted 158 advisories.”
    “158 advisories .”
  2. Complete:
    “The government promised end the advisories.”
  3. Rewrite using failed to:
    “The government did not meet the deadline.”
  4. Extension using should have:
    “It was wrong not to meet the deadline.”

Expanding your vocabulary

Choose the correct word.

  1. A deadline / scope tells us when an action should be completed.
  2. A cumulative / identical count grows through additions over time.
  3. Lifted means officially ended / newly introduced.
  4. “Underway” means started or progressing / necessarily finished.

Protected spoken turn

Each student has 40 seconds:

The deadline was…
The retrieved snapshot shows…
This demonstrates progress, but…
The figures do not tell us…

The listener asks:

What exactly is being counted?

  • □ I gave a complete spoken answer.

  • □ I asked or answered a question.

Answer key

Listening A: a water-system emergency/contamination concern; 25 years in 2020; March 2021; construction delays or COVID-related disruption.

B: Archibald stresses serious human costs and slow progress; Chartrand acknowledges substantial attention while saying longstanding problems take time; Groulx stresses keeping promises to build trust.

C: an annual report/accountability mechanism; areas including child welfare, health, justice and education; missing data arrangements or reporting infrastructure; no. These are expected answers from the teaching dossier, not a verified transcript.

Evidence sheet: promise to end the specified long-term advisories; March 2021; 10 September 2026; 158; 38; 36; long-term advisories on public systems on reserves; new advisories can arise and the figures do not cover all water insecurity.

Grammar:

  1. were lifted;
  2. to;
  3. “The government failed to meet the deadline.”
  4. “The government should have met the deadline.”

Vocabulary: deadline; cumulative; officially ended; started or progressing.

Written record

The drinking-water deadline was March 2021. The retrieved official snapshot, dated 10 September 2026, records 158 long-term advisories lifted since November 2015 and 38 active advisories in 36 communities. This shows progress, but not completion. The figures concern a defined category of public systems on reserves, not all water insecurity.

Homework

Write a 90 to 120-word fact-check:

Claim: “The water promise was fully completed.”

Include the promise, a dated source, two relevant figures and a limitation.

A valid conclusion is:

The deadline was missed, although substantial progress was recorded.

Students may use a different justified formulation. “Partly kept” is not the only acceptable verdict.
