Whose Land, Whose Water? · adapted version

Who decides what happens to the land and the water? How can we check a promise?

Road map

Eight lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes, usable alone without an AESH. The stopping point in every lesson is minute 44. Lesson 8 ends with your own evidence and request.

  1. Lesson 1. What Can I See? 52 min
  2. Lesson 2. Equal Citizens and Existing Rights 52 min
  3. Lesson 3. A Dam Changes a Place 52 min
  4. Lesson 4. Listening to Campaigners 52 min
  5. Lesson 5. Different Views of a Pipeline 52 min
  6. Lesson 6. Checking a Promise 52 min
  7. Lesson 7. Young People and Hope 52 min
  8. Lesson 8. My Evidence and My Request 52 min
  • Written record. Every lesson ends with a short written record that you keep.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: in every lesson, say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. A short task with a worked example and a self-check, at the end of each lesson.

Stopping point. Minute 44 in every lesson.

Emergency exit · 8 min. Two minutes speaking, three minutes written record, two minutes homework, one minute check. This replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.

The film at home: The Grizzlies

  1. Watch the whole film The Grizzlies at home before lesson 7. It is homework, set in lesson 6.
  2. The film lasts 1 h 42. You can watch it in two parts.
  3. Turn on English subtitles if you can.
  4. Your teacher tells you how to watch it.
  5. The film talks about sad and hard things, including suicide. You can stop. You can talk to an adult you trust.
  6. Note one person who speaks and one thing they say. You will use it in the debate.

The method The Sentence I Cannot Finish Alone helps you start the study of the film. The student document includes an adapted route.

These are teaching routes, not labels attached to a diagnosis. Choose by your current need and the qualification you are preparing.

A CFA is a training setting, not a separate level: use Route C if you prepare a CAP, Route P if you prepare a bac pro. Using access support does not automatically mean reducing your language target.

Your independent lesson routine

Each lesson has this suggested 52-minute route:

TimeAction
0 to 4Read the goal and find your place.
4 to 12Read the short text and word meanings.
12 to 20Complete reading and vocabulary work.
20 to 30Complete listening or the labelled no-video route.
30 to 37Complete language practice.
37 to 44Prepare and say your answer.
44 to 49Keep the written record.
49 to 52Check answers and note homework.

At home, pause when necessary. You do not have to force your reading speed to match the classroom clock.

How to do an exercise

  1. Read the short text.
  2. Read the worked example.
  3. Do the exercise.
  4. Check with the self-check underneath.
  5. Correct.
  6. Say your answer aloud.
  7. Keep the written record.

You can use Listen (device voice) and Stop on each short text. All exercises and self-checks stay visible on the page.

When a video does not work

Use the short support account in the lesson.

Label the work:

Reading-support route.

Do not label it as unaided listening.

You can still complete the lesson and demonstrate understanding. Return to listening when access is available.

Speaking alone

  1. Read the model.
  2. Prepare your own sentence.
  3. Say it aloud.
  4. If useful, make a private recording.
  5. Listen once.
  6. Change one unclear word or sentence.

A recording is optional. Do not post it publicly.
