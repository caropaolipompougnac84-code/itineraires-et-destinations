Lesson 4. 9 to 5: working women

Debatable question: Can a pop song change the way people see work?
By the end of this lesson, I can: describe a working day, say what is fair and unfair at work, explain rules and give advice.

For the teacher: timing (52 minutes)
Step Time Activity
1 0-4 Recap and flashcard duel
2 4-7 The question of the day
3 7-17 Document 1 (reading) + video (2:34, watched twice)
4 17-23 Expanding your vocabulary: work and rights
5 23-30 The teacher’s point: frequency adverbs; obligation and advice
6 30-38 Exercises
7 38-47 Speaking task: “The workers’ meeting”
8 47-50 Written record
9 50-52 Homework

Stop point (minute 47). Every student has said one rule for a fair workplace.

Emergency exit (8 minutes). Play the video once (0:00 to 2:34) with the verb task, then each student says one sentence with mustn’t or don’t have to about their work placement.

Step 1. Recap

  1. Make it polite: Give me the wig. 2. Complete: ... I add more volume? (offer) 3. Give one compliment.

    Answer key

    Answer key: 1 Could you give me the wig, please? · 2 Shall · 3 I love the way it shines. / You look fabulous!

Step 2. The question of the day

Ask: Name a song about work, school or money. Do songs change anything, or are they just entertainment?

Transition. In 1980, Dolly wrote a song for a film about three office workers. It became an anthem. Let’s see why.

Step 3. Documents

Document 1. The film and the song (reading)

9 to 5 (1980) was Dolly Parton’s first film. She played Doralee, a secretary, with Jane Fonda and Lily Tomlin. The three women work for a sexist boss who steals their ideas and refuses to give them a promotion. The title echoes 9to5, an organisation of women office workers created in Boston in 1973 to fight for fair pay and respect.

Dolly wrote the song during the shooting. She used her long acrylic nails to imitate the sound of a typewriter. The song reached number one in the pop and country charts, was nominated for an Oscar (Best Original Song) and won two Grammy Awards.

Reading task. True or false? Justify.
1. Dolly played the boss. 2. The boss is fair. 3. The song won an Oscar. 4. Dolly used her nails as an instrument.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 False: she played Doralee, a secretary. 2 False: he steals their ideas and refuses a promotion. 3 False: it was nominated for an Oscar; it won two Grammys. 4 True: she imitated the sound of a typewriter.

Video. 9 to 5 (Official Video)

Watch or listen: Dolly Parton, 9 To 5 (Official Video), Dolly Parton channel: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbxUSsFXYo4
Play from 0:00 to 2:34 (the whole clip). Watch twice.

Exact link, to keep unchanged: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbxUSsFXYo4

Task 1 (first viewing, 0:00 to 0:33). Tick the verbs you hear: tumble, stumble, pour, yawn, stretch, cook, jump, drive.

Answer key

Answer key: tumble, stumble, pour, yawn, stretch, jump. Not cook, not drive.

Task 2 (second viewing, 0:33 to 1:25). Note three complaints of the worker.

Answer key

Answer key: she is barely getting by (not enough money); they never give her credit; she deserves a promotion; the boss seems to be out to get her.

Task 3 (1:25 to 2:34). What changes in the last part?

Answer key

Answer key: she says “the tide’s gonna turn”: things will change; workers are “in the same boat” and can act together.

Step 4. Expanding your vocabulary

A. Work and rights

Word Meaning Example
an office worker a person who works in an office Doralee is an office worker.
a boss / a manager the person in charge The boss is sexist.
a promotion a better job in the same company Violet wants a promotion.
equal pay the same salary for the same work Women fight for equal pay.
to get credit for to be recognised for your work They never get credit for their ideas.
harassment repeated behaviour that upsets or threatens someone Sexual harassment is illegal.
a shift a period of work I work the morning shift.
overtime extra hours of work Overtime must be paid.
a colleague a person you work with The three colleagues become friends.
a union an organisation that defends workers You can contact a union representative.
fair / unfair just / not just It is unfair to steal ideas.

B. Words to argue

Function Words
Give an opinion I believe that... · As far as I’m concerned,...
Agree / disagree I agree with... · I don’t agree because... · That’s true, but...
Add an argument Moreover,... · What’s more,... · Besides,...
Conclude To sum up,... · All in all,...

Exercises on vocabulary

Exercise D1. Fair or unfair? 1 equal pay · 2 unpaid overtime · 3 a boss who steals ideas · 4 a promotion for good work · 5 harassment

Answer key

Answer key: fair: 1, 4 · unfair: 2, 3, 5.

Exercise D2. Fill in the gaps: credit · shift · overtime · union · colleague
I work the night (1) __. Last week I did ten hours of (2) _, but they were not paid. My (3) told me to contact the (4) . I also want to get (5) ___ for my ideas.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 shift · 2 overtime · 3 colleague · 4 union · 5 credit.

Exercise D3. Reformulate with Moreover or To sum up.
1. The boss is rude. He steals ideas. → 2. Women are underpaid, harassed and never promoted: the office is unfair. →

Answer key

Answer key: 1 The boss is rude. Moreover, he steals ideas. 2 Women are underpaid, harassed and never promoted. To sum up, the office is unfair.

Flashcards

Front Back
equal pay (blue) égalité salariale · Women fight for equal pay.
mustn’t vs don’t have to (red) interdit / pas obligatoire · You mustn’t smoke. You don’t have to come early.
Moreover, (green) de plus · Moreover, he steals their ideas.

Step 5. The teacher’s point

Teacher script. Read the first words of the song and list the routine verbs. Then show the difference between don’t have to (it is not necessary) and mustn’t (it is forbidden), with examples from the students’ work placements.

Point Form Example
Present simple for routines She gets up at seven. She pours a cup of coffee.
Frequency adverbs before the main verb, after be: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never She always works late. The boss is never polite.
have to / must obligation Workers have to respect safety rules. You must wear your badge.
don’t have to no obligation You don’t have to work on Sundays.
mustn’t prohibition A boss mustn’t harass his staff.
should advice You should talk to a union representative.

Quick check. You (mustn’t / don’t have to) work on public holidays, but you can if you want. → don’t have to.

Step 6. Exercises

Exercise 1. Where does the adverb go?
1. Doralee arrives on time. (always) 2. The boss says thank you. (never) 3. Violet is tired. (often) 4. They work late. (sometimes)

Answer key

Answer key: 1 Doralee always arrives on time. 2 The boss never says thank you. 3 Violet is often tired. 4 They sometimes work late.

Exercise 2. Rules at work: have to · don’t have to · mustn’t · should
1. You ... wear safety shoes in a workshop. 2. A boss ... steal his employees’ ideas. 3. You ... come in early, but it is appreciated. 4. If you have a problem, you ... talk to your tutor.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 have to · 2 mustn’t · 3 don’t have to · 4 should.

Exercise 3. Match the situation and the rule.
1. Using your phone near a machine · 2. Wearing a uniform in a hotel · 3. Working on your day off · 4. Feeling stressed
a. You should talk to your manager. b. You don’t have to. c. You mustn’t. d. You have to.

Answer key

Answer key: 1c, 2d, 3b, 4a.

Exercise 4. Transform with the word in brackets.
1. It is forbidden to shout at customers. (mustn’t) 2. It is not necessary to bring lunch. (don’t have to) 3. It’s a good idea to ask questions. (should)

Answer key

Answer key: 1 You mustn’t shout at customers. 2 You don’t have to bring lunch. 3 You should ask questions.

Exercise 5. My working day (70 words) in your pathway with five frequency adverbs, then three rules for a fair workplace (have to, mustn’t, should).
Model answer: I am a sales assistant. I usually start at nine. I always check the shelves first, then I often help customers. I sometimes work late on Saturdays and I rarely have lunch before two. My tutor is never rude. Rules: Employees have to respect customers. A manager mustn’t shout at the staff. Everybody should get credit for good ideas.

Help box. Frame: I am a... I always... I usually... I sometimes... I never... You have to... You mustn’t... You should...
Going further. Compare 1980 and today (80 words): In 1980, women often used to... Today, they still... / no longer...
Possible answer: In 1980, women office workers often used to earn less than men and rarely got promotions. Many were harassed and could not complain. Today, the law forbids discrimination and harassment, and companies have to publish equality figures in some countries. However, women still earn less on average and they are still under-represented in top jobs. So the song is still relevant.

Step 7. Speaking task: “The workers’ meeting”

Groups of four: you are colleagues in Doralee’s office. Each student has one card.

  1. Card 1: describe a typical unfair day (frequency adverbs).
  2. Card 2: propose two rules with have to and mustn’t.
  3. Card 3: give advice to a new colleague with should.
  4. Card 4: answer the question of the day: Can a pop song change the way people see work? Use Moreover and To sum up.

Then one spokesperson per group presents the group’s three best rules to the class (30 seconds).

Possible answer for card 4: I believe a song cannot change a law, but it can change how people see their job. Moreover, 9 to 5 gave office workers a voice: they sang it together. To sum up, a song is not a revolution, but it can start a conversation.

Step 8. Written record

Lesson 4. 9 to 5
In 9 to 5 (1980), Dolly played Doralee, a secretary with a sexist boss. Her song reached number one, was nominated for an Oscar and won two Grammys. It became an anthem for office workers.
Grammar. Frequency adverbs before the verb, after be. have to / must = obligation; don’t have to = not necessary; mustn’t = forbidden; should = advice.
My answer: A song can / cannot change... because...

Step 9. Homework

  1. Revise all flashcards from Lessons 1 to 4 (the mid-point assessment is next lesson).
  2. Write five rules for a fair workplace in your future job.
  3. Help box: record yourself saying your five rules (one minute).

Passing the torch. In 2009, 9 to 5: The Musical, with songs by Dolly, opened on Broadway. What has changed at work since 1980? What is still the same?
