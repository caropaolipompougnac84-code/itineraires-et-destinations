Teacher notes · adapted version

Access without dependence: no student should be penalised because no AESH was available that day.

What this version changes

It removes dependence on: a partner being present; a support assistant reading instructions; a teacher explaining every task orally; a working results tracker; an internet search; a recording device; a live group debate. A student can complete the core route from these pages alone.

It does not promise that every student will find every task accessible without individual adjustment. When a learner is still blocked, the answer is to improve access, not to penalise the absence of an AESH.

Layout when you hand out the resource

  • give only the student’s chosen route;
  • a clear sans-serif font, about 14 to 16 point for print;
  • left-aligned text and generous line spacing;
  • one instruction per line;
  • no decorative backgrounds; colour is never the only source of meaning;
  • the key straight after the exercise, not on a distant page.

These choices follow accessible-format guidance; students may still adjust font, size and spacing to their own needs.

Models and keys

Models are access tools. To avoid simple copying: let the student read the model, cover it, change one detail, ask for a new answer. Do not remove the model just because the student needs it more than once.

Speaking when alone

Independent speaking can be reading one’s own completed sentence, answering a printed question aloud, rehearsing a short report, or recording if freely chosen and permitted. This is oral production practice; it does not reproduce real interaction. A short exchange with the teacher later can assess interaction.

Assessment

Separate the skill assessed from the access difficulty.

Comprehension: allow a selected answer and speech-to-text; avoid long copying; do not let spelling hide evidence of understanding.

Writing: say which language features are assessed and assess those explicitly; keep the access tools the student is entitled to.

Speaking: assess intelligibility and meaning; do not require public performance when a private answer gives enough evidence; do not infer oral achievement from a completed checklist.
