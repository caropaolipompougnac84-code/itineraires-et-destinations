Lesson 4. Dams Today

Two recent dams, and people who said ‘not like this’.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

Why did the people of Fort George have to move?

Self-check

Because the dam made the river twice as fast, and the island risked erosion.

4 to 8 min

Today’s goal

I can give one fact about Muskrat Falls and one fact about Site C, each with a source in mind.

Route S · SEGPA

I can: give one fact for each dam.

Muskrat Falls is a dam in Labrador. Some Inuit were afraid of a toxic product, methylmercury, in the fish of Lake Melville, downstream. In 2016, Billy Gauthier went on a hunger strike. He wanted the land cleared ‘right’. Site C is a dam on the Peace River, in British Columbia. It started full work in August 2025. Treaty 8 nations, such as West Moberly, objected.

Route C · CAP

I can: compare one fear and one argument in favour, with a figure.

Muskrat Falls is an 824-megawatt dam on the lower Churchill River. A study said methylmercury could rise by up to 380 per cent in Lake Melville if the reservoir was only partly cleared. Billy Gauthier, an Inuk artist, drank only water for days: he said bravery is doing it even when you are scared. The Innu Nation had signed a benefits agreement in 2011. Site C cost 16 billion dollars, nearly double its first price. BC Hydro says it can power 500,000 homes. West Moberly reached a partial settlement in 2022.

Route P · Bac pro

I can: say why ‘clean energy’ may not be clean for everyone.

At Muskrat Falls, protesters did not all reject the project: they wanted the reservoir fully cleared so that Inuit could keep fishing. At Site C, Chief Roland Willson said the government broke a promise after a report found the dam was not a good deal; he was not against all development, and he proposed wind, solar and geothermal energy. Flooding made mercury levels rise; Site C cost nearly double its first price. Clean energy for a city can still be a loss of land for a nation.

Exercise 1

Match the dam and the place.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: Muskrat Falls is in Labrador.

Your turn. Site C is on the ________ River, in ________.

Self-check

Site C is on the Peace River, in British Columbia.

Exercise 2

Who objected, and how?

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: Billy Gauthier went on a hunger strike in 2016.

Your turn. Which First Nations objected to Site C, and in the name of what?

Self-check

Treaty 8 First Nations, such as West Moberly, in the name of their treaty rights.

Exercise 3

Write one sentence with made or nearly double.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: Flooding made mercury levels rise.

Your turn. Write your sentence about Muskrat Falls or Site C.

Self-check

Possible sentence: Site C cost nearly double its first price.

CFA task · instead of Exercise 3

If you are in a training centre, do this task instead of Exercise 3. You may follow route C or route P for the reading.

Worked example (already corrected). A trainee writes a one-minute news flash: ‘Tonight we go to Labrador. Inuit asked for the reservoir to be cleared. The company says the dam will make power.’

Your turn. Write a four-line news flash: the place, one figure, one voice for, one voice against.

Self-check

Possible answer: We go to the Peace River. Site C can power 500,000 homes (BC Hydro). West Moberly objected in the name of treaty rights. The dam came online in August 2025.

Speaking frame · 40 to 44 min

Say this aloud, and change the words in brackets if you need to:

At Muskrat Falls, people feared [methylmercury] downstream. At Site C, Treaty 8 nations [objected] to the dam.

Written record · 44 to 48 min

Route S. Muskrat Falls: fear for the fish. Site C: a dam in British Columbia, with objections from Treaty 8 nations.

Route C. At Muskrat Falls, Billy Gauthier went on a hunger strike so that Lake Melville would stay safe. Site C came online in 2025 after Treaty 8 objections.

Route P. A dam can power hundreds of thousands of homes and still be a broken promise for a valley. Muskrat Falls and Site C both show two sides.

Progress record · 48 to 50 min

  • I read the short text for my route.
  • I did two exercises and checked the self-check key.
  • I said one sentence aloud.
  • I copied the written record.

Homework. Write 80 to 100 words: is hydroelectricity clean energy for everyone? Give one figure.

Emergency exit · 8 min

  • 0 to 2. Say this sentence: “A dam can be clean energy for a city, and a loss of land for a nation.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record for your route.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework.
  • 7 to 8. Check one self-check answer.
