Language and intelligibility4Suitable range, understandable pronunciation and sufficient control.

For each criterion:

  • 4: secure, effective and independent.
  • 3: generally effective, with minor lapses.
  • 2: partly effective, with support or recurring difficulty.
  • 1: limited assessable evidence.
  • 0: no assessable evidence.

These are classroom descriptors, not an automatic CEFR conversion.

Do not award a higher level simply because a student uses nevertheless or a complex conditional. An eight-minute group discussion is not eight minutes of individual speech: each student’s contribution is assessed separately.

Organising the oral assessment

Simultaneous discussions guarantee speaking time, but one teacher cannot reliably grade every student at once. Choose one arrangement before teaching:

  • record groups using school-approved procedures, then assess afterwards;
  • assess selected groups in Lesson 7 and the other groups during later independent work;
  • keep the group discussion formative and assess the individual written note.

Peer ticks do not replace sufficient teacher evidence.

Do not require every grammar structure taught in the sequence. Assess whether students choose language that serves their meaning. No model answer supplies election results: check each factual statement against the student’s source log.

Student self-check before handing in

  • Every number has a source, a time, a category and a status.
  • I have not inferred a result from an outdated list of members.
  • My cross-border consequence is presented as a hypothesis.
  • I mention Bingham, two institutional or representation facts and one limit.
