Lesson 7. The Biggest City game

Play, revise the Commonwealth and US capitals, and prepare the final task.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 7 lessons, plus a final-task assessment.

  1. Recap of the debate 4 min
  2. The Biggest City game, in teams 18 min
  3. Guess the capital, with the clue cards 14 min
  4. Preparing the final task 16 min
  • Written record. My final-task plan, in five key words: one US capital, one Commonwealth capital, when, who, why, and my opinion.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student gives at least one capital, one trap, or one clue, in a full sentence.
  • Homework for next lesson. Prepare your final task. For the oral, rehearse aloud twice with a timer and reduce your notes to five key words. For the article, write a first draft.

Stopping point. After the games, keep at least a short start on the final-task plan so that homework is clear.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson inside the 52 minutes (this is not 52 plus 8). Dictate a short written record (two sentences). A quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each). Write the homework on the board.

You already know a longer United States version of the team game if you have played The Capital Trap, lesson 6. This lesson does not repeat that fifteen-pair US game. It revises both the United States and the Commonwealth, then uses the clue cards of this sequence (Ottawa, Canberra, Wellington, Abuja, Pretoria, New Delhi, Washington, D.C., Juneau).

Recap · 4 min

  1. Give one argument for choosing the biggest city as the capital.
  2. Give one argument against.
  3. Complete: I see your point, ___ …

Answer key

  1. It already has airports, roads and hotels; business, media and government are in the same place.
  2. A central capital is fairer for all regions; it keeps political power away from business power.
  3. but

The Biggest City game · 18 min

Play in teams. A correct capital: 2 points. A team that falls into the trap answers a challenge: say two facts about the trap city and explain why it is not the capital.

The pairs (the trap is in brackets) are also on the cards page. If JavaScript is available, you can play in the box below; the list works without it.

If JavaScript is available, play here: three answers appear, including the trap.

The pairs (the trap is in brackets)

  • Canada: Ottawa (Toronto)
  • Australia: Canberra (Sydney)
  • New Zealand: Wellington (Auckland)
  • Nigeria: Abuja (Lagos)
  • India: New Delhi (Mumbai)
  • Pakistan: Islamabad (Karachi)
  • South Africa, executive capital: Pretoria (Johannesburg). Cape Town and Bloemfontein are the other two capitals.
  • California: Sacramento (Los Angeles)
  • Texas: Austin (Houston)
  • New York State: Albany (New York City)
  • Alaska: Juneau (Anchorage)

Guess the capital · 14 min

One student reads the clues one by one. The fewer clues you need, the more points you win (4, 3, 2, 1). Try it with the whole class, then play in pairs with the clue cards on the cards page.

JavaScript is not required: use the printed cards. If it is available, this box lets you reveal one clue at a time.

Preparing the final task · 16 min

Choose your option on the assessment page and fill the plan: one US capital, one Commonwealth capital, the story of each choice, a comparison, your opinion. Five key words only for the oral.
