Lesson 3. Citizens, but Equally Represented?

What do Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. reveal about federal representation?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 3 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55 stay free for collecting work, moving and the change of room.

  1. Paired retrieval: one shared and one separate power of Congress 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Locate Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C.; introduce the question 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Read Source C; complete Exercise 3A 9 min (minutes 8 to 17)
  4. Correct and discuss the limits of simplified statements 8 min (minutes 17 to 25)
  5. Contrast and concession, Exercise 3B 8 min (minutes 25 to 33)
  6. Paired explainer: repair the statements, Exercise 3C 7 min (minutes 33 to 40)
  7. Draft the opening of the paragraph, Exercise 3D 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  8. Written record 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  9. Exit ticket: “Citizenship and residence are not the same because…” 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  10. Homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. have federal representation, but not the same as states; we separate citizenship, residence and the powers of an office.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: in pairs, each partner repairs two misleading statements aloud (3C).
  • Homework for next lesson. Finish the paragraph (3D), make three flashcards, write two sentences linking this lesson with Bingham’s painting.

Stopping point. Minute 44, after the opening of the paragraph.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: each partner states one similarity and one difference.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: write a four-line comparison table.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: record the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: repair “Puerto Rico has no representation.”

Objectives

Separate citizenship, residence, representation and presidential voting.

Language: although, whereas, despite, can and cannot.

This lesson matches an objet d’étude named in the official programme for Première, axe 6: « Porto Rico : le 51e état ? »

Source C

Different forms of representation

Student text, newly written from official institutional information.

1. Puerto Rico

Puerto Rico is a US territory, not a state. Its people elect a Resident Commissioner for a four-year term. The Resident Commissioner can introduce bills, take part in debates, and serve and vote on committees. However, the Resident Commissioner cannot vote when the full House takes its final vote on a bill. (Final passage means the final vote to approve a bill in the full House.) Puerto Rico has no senators.

2. Washington, D.C.

Washington, D.C. is a federal district, not a state. It has a delegate in the House but no senators. The Twenty-third Amendment, ratified in 1961, gives the district presidential electors: it has three. Puerto Rico has no presidential electors.

3. Why residence matters

For presidential voting, where a person lives matters. Puerto Rican identity alone does not tell us whether a person can vote in a presidential election: eligible US citizens from Puerto Rico who live in a state or in Washington, D.C. can vote there, under the rules of that place.

Congress has the constitutional power to admit new states. A discussion of Puerto Rico’s future must also recognise that the island’s residents do not all favour the same status.

Sources: Office of the Resident Commissioner, “What is a Resident Commissioner?” · National Archives, Amendments 11 to 27 · National Archives, the Constitution.

Exercise 3B

Contrast and concession: choose, then explain

Complete with although, whereas or despite.

  1. Washington, D.C. has presidential electors, Puerto Rico does not.
  2. the Resident Commissioner has important responsibilities, the office has limits.
  3. having House representation, neither place has senators.
  4. Rewrite with despite: “Although the two places have representatives in the House, they have no senators.”

Then answer: which sentence compares two arrangements? Which two introduce a contrast between an expectation and a limit?

Answer key

  1. whereas.
  2. Although.
  3. Despite.
  4. “Despite having representatives in the House, the two places have no senators.”

Sentence 1 compares two arrangements. Sentences 2 and 3 introduce a concession.

Exercise 3C

Repair the explanation (pairs, aloud)

  1. Puerto Rico has no representation of any kind in Congress.
  2. The Resident Commissioner cannot vote anywhere in Congress.
  3. Anyone of Puerto Rican origin is barred from presidential voting.
  4. Washington, D.C. and Puerto Rico have identical electoral arrangements.

Answer key

  1. It has a Resident Commissioner.
  2. The Resident Commissioner can vote in committees but not on final passage in the full House.
  3. The broad claim confuses identity with residence and eligibility.
  4. Washington, D.C. has presidential electors; Puerto Rico does not.
Exercise 3D

A balanced paragraph

Question: What do Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. show about different forms of federal representation?

Common B1+ route: 110 to 140 words, two accurate comparisons, two connectors (one comparison, one concession), a careful conclusion.

Towards B2: explain why citizenship, residence and representation should not be treated as interchangeable ideas.

Answer key

Possible answer: “Citizenship does not always lead to the same form of federal representation. Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. both lack senators, although both have representation in the House. Puerto Rico elects a Resident Commissioner who can take part in committee work but cannot vote on final passage in the full House. Washington, D.C. has three presidential electors, whereas Puerto Rico has none. However, it would be misleading to describe this only as a difference between groups of people. Residence matters: eligible US citizens from Puerto Rico living in a state or in Washington, D.C. can vote there. These examples show that citizenship and electoral arrangements must be kept apart.” (110 words)

Quick exercise. Explain the difference between “has no representative” and “has no vote on final passage”.

Answer key

The first denies representation altogether; the second names a specific limit on the powers of an existing representative.

Written record

Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. have federal representation, but not the same arrangements as states. Neither has senators. Their presidential-elector arrangements differ. We must separate citizenship, residence and the powers attached to a representative’s office.
