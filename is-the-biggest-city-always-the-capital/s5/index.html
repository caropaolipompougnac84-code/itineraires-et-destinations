Lesson 5. One Commonwealth, many capitals

Understand what the Commonwealth is, and whether its capitals show permanence or a break.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 7 lessons, plus a final-task assessment.

  1. Recap of lesson 4 4 min
  2. Read: what is the Commonwealth? True or false 18 min
  3. Heritage: permanence or break? 18 min
  4. Grammar: used to and whose 12 min
  • Written record. The Commonwealth of Nations is a voluntary association of independent and equal countries. Most members used to be part of the British Empire, but not all of them. The USA has never been a member. Some capitals show permanence, some a break, and some both.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student corrects one true-or-false sentence, or justifies one capital as permanence, break, or both.
  • Homework for next lesson. Choose one capital from the table. Write 100 to 120 words: is it a sign of permanence or of a break? Use used to, whose and two connectors.

Stopping point. After the true-or-false correction, keep at least two capitals in the permanence-or-break table if time is short.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson inside the 52 minutes (this is not 52 plus 8). Dictate a short written record (two sentences). A quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each). Write the homework on the board.

Recap · 4 min

  1. Why was Canberra built?
  2. Who chose Wellington, and why?
  3. When did the Nigerian government move to Abuja?

Answer key

  1. As a compromise between Sydney and Melbourne, the two rival cities.
  2. Three commissioners from Australia, for its central position and its excellent harbour.
  3. On 12 December 1991.

Read: what is the Commonwealth? · 18 min

The Commonwealth of Nations is a voluntary association of 56 independent and equal countries. About 2.7 billion people, roughly a third of the world’s population, live in Commonwealth countries. (These figures can change: always check the official list of member countries.)

The modern Commonwealth was born in 1949 with the London Declaration, when it had only eight members. Most members used to be part of the British Empire, but not all of them: Mozambique, Rwanda, Gabon and Togo were never British, and they chose to join.

Some members, like Canada, Australia and New Zealand, still have King Charles III as their head of state. Others, like India, Nigeria and South Africa, are republics with their own presidents. King Charles III is the Head of the Commonwealth, a symbolic role. The members share the English language, similar legal and parliamentary traditions, and sporting events such as the Commonwealth Games.

And the United States? The Thirteen Colonies declared their independence in 1776, more than 170 years before the modern Commonwealth was created. The USA has never been a member.

True or false? Correct the false sentences.

  1. All Commonwealth members used to be British colonies.
  2. King Charles III is the head of state of every member.
  3. The USA left the Commonwealth in 1776.
  4. The modern Commonwealth was born in 1949.
  5. Members share sporting events.

Answer key

  1. False: Mozambique, Rwanda, Gabon and Togo were never British.
  2. False: India, Nigeria and South Africa are republics with their own presidents.
  3. False: the USA became independent in 1776, long before the modern Commonwealth existed; it has never been a member.
  4. True: with the London Declaration.
  5. True: the Commonwealth Games.

Heritage: permanence or break? · 18 min

For each capital, decide: is it mainly a sign of permanence (a heritage from the British Empire that was kept) or of a break (a new start after independence)? Some cases are both. Justify with a fact from lesson 4.

Sentence starters. New Delhi is a sign of permanence, since… · Abuja rather stands for a break: … · Pretoria is both: the Union Buildings were built…, yet in 1994…

Possible answers

  • New Delhi: permanence. It was planned by British architects and inaugurated in 1931; independent India kept it and the Viceroy’s House became the home of the President.
  • Abuja: break. It was decided in 1976, sixteen years after independence, to unite the country with a central, neutral capital.
  • Pretoria: both. The Union Buildings were completed in 1913 under British rule, yet Nelson Mandela was sworn in there in 1994.
  • Canberra: both. It was created by the new Australian nation, but the opening of the first Parliament was led by a British prince and the King is still head of state.
  • Wellington: permanence. It was chosen in 1864, in colonial times, and it is still the capital.

Grammar: used to and whose · 12 min

  1. Lagos ______ (be) the capital of Nigeria.
  2. Parliament ______ (meet) in Melbourne.
  3. Wellington is a city ______ harbour is excellent.
  4. Walter Burley Griffin was an architect ______ wife also designed Canberra.

Answer key

  1. used to be
  2. used to meet
  3. whose
  4. whose
