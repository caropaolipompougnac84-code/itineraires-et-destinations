Read and look · step one

Read, one sentence per line

  1. The campaign takes almost two years.
  2. Candidates announce that they want to run.
  3. To be president, you must be at least thirty-five years old, a citizen born American and a resident for at least fourteen years.
  4. From January to June of the election year, each party organises primaries (secret votes) or caucuses (party meetings) in every state to choose its candidate.
  5. The winners get delegates.
  6. In summer, from July to early September, each party holds a national convention: the delegates choose the candidate, who announces a running mate.

Source: USAGov, Presidential election process

Grammar · step one

Grammar on the board

To tell what happened in the past without naming who did it, we need the past passive: Abraham Lincoln was chosen in Chicago in 1860. The banners were printed in 1876.

Listen to your teacher, then copy the board.

On the board

The passive voice (3): the past; past simple and time prepositions

Rule
Past: was / were + past participle.
Lincoln was chosen in 1860.
Form
Lincoln was chosen in 1860. The banners were printed in 1876. Question: Was Lincoln chosen in Chicago? Negative: Seward was not chosen.
Examples
  • Abraham Lincoln was chosen in Chicago in May 1860.
  • The engraving was published by Harper's Weekly.
Watch out
  • Lincoln is chosen in 1860. → Lincoln was chosen in 1860 (finished date).
  • The banners was printed. → The banners were printed.
French and English
French uses the passé composé: Lincoln a été choisi en 1860. English uses the past simple with a finished date: Lincoln was chosen in 1860, not has been chosen.

Quick check

  1. Lincoln ______ (choose) in 1860.
  2. The posters ______ (print) in 1876.
  3. ______ May 1860
Answer key
  1. was chosen
  2. were printed
  3. in

Grammar · step three

Figure of speech

Figures of speech: the sports metaphor

Learn

American politics often speaks about elections as a race: this is a sports metaphor. A candidate runs for president, the leader is the front-runner, a surprise candidate is a dark horse. In 1860, William Seward was the front-runner, but the convention chose Abraham Lincoln.

  1. What does the race for the White House mean?
  2. What does to run for president mean?
  3. Who is the front-runner?
Answer key
  1. The competition to become president.
  2. To be a candidate.
  3. The candidate who is winning before the vote.

Finish · step one

Going further (optional)

Optional
Rule

Past perfect passive: had been + past participle, for an action before another past action: When the convention opened, the delegates had already been chosen. Informal passive: get + past participle: He got elected in 1860.

Translate into English

  1. Lincoln a été choisi à Chicago en 1860.
  2. La convention a eu lieu dans un immense bâtiment en bois.
Answer key
  1. Lincoln was chosen in Chicago in 1860.
  2. The convention was held in a huge wooden building.
