Promises and the Passive

Two tools for the whole sequence: how to talk about a promise, and how to say what was done.

Every example on this page is a real fact, with its source at the bottom. You will need this page in lessons 2 and 6.

A. Promises, kept and broken

1. Making a promise

PatternExample
promise to + base verbThe government promised to end long-term advisories by March 2021.
pledge to + base verbJustin Trudeau pledged to implement the Calls to Action.
commit to + verb-ingOttawa committed to implementing the UN Declaration. (to is a preposition here.)
will + base verb“Together, we will work tirelessly,” Justin Trudeau wrote in 2015.

2. Reporting a promise

DirectReported
willwould: He promised that he would implement the Calls to Action.
be going towas/were going to: The government said it was going to repeal the Indian Act.
a plan that did not happenThe government was going to abolish Indian status, but it withdrew the White Paper.

3. Judging a promise

Be fair. A good citizen checks both sides: what was done and what was not done. 158 advisories lifted is real progress. 39 still in effect is a promise not kept on time. Both facts are true (Indigenous Services Canada, 21 October 2026).

PatternExample
failed to + base verbThe government failed to end all advisories by March 2021.
should have + past participleIt should have met the deadline.
Despite + verb-ingDespite promising to act, it fell short.
wish + past perfectPeople in Neskantaga wish the water had been fixed years ago.

B. The passive

Use the passive to talk about what was done and what has been done, even when we do not say who did it: be (in the right tense) + past participle. Keep by only if the agent adds information.

FormExample
past simpleThe White Paper was released in June 1969.
present perfect158 advisories have been lifted since November 2015.
modalIndigenous rights must be respected.
personal passiveFirst Nations were promised clean water.
plan that did not happenThe Indian Act was going to be repealed.

Practice

Exercise 1. Choose: will, was going to, promised to, committed to.

  1. In 2015, the government ________ end long-term drinking water advisories by March 2021.
  2. The White Paper ________ abolish Indian status, but it was withdrawn.
  3. Ottawa ________ implementing the UN Declaration.
  4. “Together, we ________ work tirelessly to honour the trust Canadians have given us,” Justin Trudeau wrote in 2015.

Answer key

  1. promised to
  2. was going to
  3. committed to
  4. will

Exercise 2. Put the verb in brackets in the passive, in the tense given.

  1. The White Paper (release, past simple) in June 1969.
  2. 158 long-term advisories (lift, present perfect) since November 2015.
  3. The 94 Calls to Action (issue, past simple) by the Truth and Reconciliation Commission.
  4. Indigenous rights (respect, must).
  5. The UN Declaration Act (pass, past simple) in 2021.

Answer key

  1. was released
  2. have been lifted
  3. were issued
  4. must be respected
  5. was passed

Exercise 3. Rewrite each sentence with the word given. Keep the meaning.

  1. The government did not end all long-term advisories by March 2021. (failed)
  2. It was a mistake not to meet the deadline. (should have)
  3. The government promised to end every advisory by 2021, but 39 were still in effect in October 2026. (Despite)
  4. I am sorry that the Neskantaga advisory was not lifted earlier. (wish)

Answer key

  1. The government failed to end all long-term advisories by March 2021.
  2. The government should have met the deadline.
  3. Despite promising to end every advisory by 2021, the government still had 39 in effect in October 2026.
  4. I wish the Neskantaga advisory had been lifted earlier.

Exercise 4. Report the promise, then turn it into a passive.

  1. Fact: in December 2015, the Prime Minister committed to implementing the 94 Calls to Action. Reported with would: He promised that he ________. Passive: He promised that the Calls to Action ________.
  2. Fact: the 1969 plan was to repeal the Indian Act, but it did not happen. With was going to: The government ________. Passive: The Indian Act ________.
  3. People said the White Paper would make First Nations equal citizens. Start with It was said.
  4. The government promised First Nations clean water. Start with First Nations.

Answer key

  1. would implement the 94 Calls to Action · would be implemented
  2. was going to repeal the Indian Act · was going to be repealed
  3. It was said that the White Paper would make First Nations equal citizens.
  4. First Nations were promised clean water.

Production: one promise, two sides

Choose one promise: drinking water, the 94 Calls to Action, or the UN Declaration. Write 100 to 120 words.

  1. Say what was promised, when and by whom (reported speech).
  2. Say what has been done (present perfect passive, with a figure and its source).
  3. Say what has not been done and what should have been done.
  4. End with a fair conclusion: Although…, …

Homework. Learn the tables of parts A and B. Finish your production and add the source of your figure. Find one more real promise made to Indigenous peoples on an official site (canada.ca, sac-isc.gc.ca, rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca) and write two sentences: one passive, one with should have.
