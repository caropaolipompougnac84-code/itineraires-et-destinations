Lesson 7. The Grizzlies: Youth, Land and Hope

Goal: I can compare how the film, its official trailer and an interview present youth agency, and bring one voice from the film into the debate.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 7 of 8. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure.

  1. Recall a qualified verdict and share one moment from the film. 0 to 4
  2. Read the synopsis and source-status note. 4 to 8
  3. Watch the official trailer twice (0:00 to 2:20) and complete the viewing worksheet. 8 to 16
  4. Listen twice to the selected interview extracts. 16 to 24
  5. Complete the restitution sheet. 24 to 29
  6. Compare the film with Logan’s painting. 29 to 34
  7. Grammar and vocabulary practice. 34 to 39
  8. Protected spoken recommendation. 39 to 44
  9. Written record. 44 to 49
  10. Homework and final-task preparation. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student gives a 40-second spoken recommendation.
  • Homework for next lesson. A 120 to 150-word introduction to The Grizzlies for the debate, using one voice from the film; choose your lesson 8 dossier; two flashcards.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: each student says one sentence about agency.
  • 46 to 49: copy the written record.
  • 49 to 51: record homework and dossier choice.
  • 51 to 52: correct “Play together can help”.

Teacher note. Screen time in class: 7 min 44 in total (the official trailer, 2 min 20, shown twice; the three interview extracts, 1 min 32 in all, played twice), within the 10-minute limit. The whole film is watched at home before this lesson, never in class.

Student text

Read

The Grizzlies (2018) is a Canadian film directed by Miranda de Pencier. It is based on a true story involving young people and a lacrosse team in Kugluktuk, in what is now Nunavut.

You watched the whole film at home before this lesson: it is compulsory homework, set in lesson 6. The film opens up voices that you will use in the debate and in the lesson 8 hearing: students, parents, elders and a teacher from the south.

The interview presents young Inuit people as participants with their own relationships, abilities and ambitions. The story should not be reduced to one in which an outsider simply “saves” a community.

Today you compare three documents: the film you watched, its official trailer and an interview. A trailer is made to sell a film, so it selects moments. Always say which document your evidence comes from.

The whole film: compulsory home viewing

  • Reference: The Grizzlies, Canada, 2018. Directed by Miranda de Pencier. Written by Moira Walley-Beckett and Graham Yost. 1 h 42. In English and Inuktitut.
  • When: at home, before lesson 7. You may watch it in two parts.
  • How: in English, with English subtitles if possible. Your teacher tells you how to access the film legally. No legal streaming offer in France could be confirmed on 11 November 2026.
  • Care: the film deals with suicide, alcohol and violence in a community. You may pause at any time and talk to an adult you trust. Nobody will ask you to share personal experiences.
  • Task while watching: note two voices (who speaks and what they say) that you could quote in the debate.

Building your vocabulary

  • agency: the ability to act and influence events;
  • resilience: the ability to respond to difficulty;
  • reclaim: take back or strengthen something valued;
  • give up: stop trying;
  • stand up for: defend;
  • synopsis: a short account of a story;
  • trailer: a short advertisement for a film, made of selected moments.

Answer key

Trailer: 1. “6,000 years.” Inuit people have lived there for thousands of years: the teacher is the newcomer. 2. Lacrosse. 3. “We don’t need to defend our way of life to a southerner.” “Family comes first. Not school, not some white man’s game.” Also accept the man who asks the teacher who he is to say what they need. 4. It moves the focus from the outsider to the young people: they have made sacrifices, they have been dealing with difficulties for years and they are “still here”. 5. proud, strong, hope. 6. Accept any accurate moment from the film, labelled as coming from the film, not from the trailer.

Listening: an inspiring true story; the teacher says the young people changed him more than he changed them; hope and the emergence of a talented younger generation; justified reference to their actions, talents or mutual support. Only award direct-listening credit for material heard in the selected extracts. Wider information in the teaching dossier must be labelled as reading support.

Grammar: Playing; Watching; give up; for.

Vocabulary: 1b, 2a, 3d, 4c, 5e.

Comparison: accept justified analysis. Do not accept “all Inuit teenagers are isolated” or “sport solves every problem”.

Written record

The Grizzlies, its trailer and the interview highlight relationships, youth agency and hope. In the trailer, a student tells the teacher: “This is not about you.” Playing together can create support, but one story does not represent every Inuit experience. A trailer selects moments to sell a film, so evidence must say whether it comes from the film, the trailer or the interview.

Homework

Write 120 to 150 words:

An introduction to The Grizzlies for the class debate.

Include one voice from the film (who speaks and what they say), one idea from the interview and one limit: what a single film cannot show.

Prepare the final hearing using one dossier from lesson 8.
