Exercise 1. Complete with the map

  1. The Mississippi ______ into the Gulf of Mexico.
  2. Texas ______ Mexico.
  3. Alaska is ______ from Russia by the Bering Strait.
  4. Niagara Falls are located ______ New York State and Canada.
  5. Hawaii is a group of ______ in the Pacific Ocean.
  6. Denver is ______, far from the coast.

Answer key

flows, borders, separated, between, islands, inland

Exercise 2. In the north of or north of?

  1. Canada is ______ the USA.
  2. Chicago is ______ Illinois.
  3. Mexico is ______ Texas.
  4. Miami is ______ Florida.

Answer key

north of, in the north of, south of, in the south of

2. The origin of names

FrenchSpanishNative American
New Orleans, Baton Rouge, Detroit, St. Louis, Des Moines, Boise, JuneauLos Angeles, Las Vegas, San Francisco, Santa Fe, Nevada, Florida, ColoradoChicago, Seattle, Mississippi, Massachusetts, Ohio, Utah

Detroit comes from détroit; Boise from boisé. Baton Rouge: a red stick marked a border. Juneau takes its name from a French-Canadian gold prospector. Los Angeles means “the angels”; Nevada, “snowy”; Florida, “flowery”; Colorado, “red”. Chicago comes from a word for a wild onion. Mississippi means “great river”.

Many American place names come from French, Spanish or Native American languages. They tell the history of the country.

4. The preterite

Exercise 4, Lucas’s trip, and the pronunciation of -ed are in lesson 7. Answer key: flew, visited, took, drove, ate, listened, swam. You hear /ɪd/ only after a t or a d sound.

Exercise 5: rewrite your own itinerary in the past. First, we started in Denver. Then we drove to Salt Lake City…

5. Phonology

Silent letters: Illinoi(s), Arkansa(s), Conne(c)ticut, Des Moine(s).

Unexpected sounds: Houston (the beginning sounds like “hew”), Albany (like “all”), Juneau (like “jew-no”), Phoenix (like “fee”).

The -ed of the preterite: /t/ walked, stopped, looked · /d/ listened, explained, stayed · /ɪd/ visited, landed, started.
