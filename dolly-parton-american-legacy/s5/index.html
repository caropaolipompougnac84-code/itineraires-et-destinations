Lesson 5. Dollywood: a business and an employer

Debatable question: Is a theme park a gift to a region or simply a business?
By the end of this lesson, I can: present a company, its history and its numbers, and apply for a seasonal job.

For the teacher: timing (52 minutes)
Step Time Activity
1 0-3 Recap and flashcard duel
2 3-5 The question of the day
3 5-11 Document: Dollywood in numbers
4 11-16 Expanding your vocabulary
5 16-21 The teacher’s point: past simple, present perfect, since / for, big numbers
6 21-27 Exercises
7 27-35 Speaking task: “The job interview” (pairs, every student speaks)
8 35-50 Mid-point assessment (15 min)
9 50-52 Written record (to copy at home from the board photo) and homework

Stop point (minute 35). Start the assessment at minute 35 at the latest.

Emergency exit (8 minutes). If the assessment must be postponed: one interview question per student (“Why do you want to work at Dollywood?”), then the assessment opens the next lesson.

Step 1. Recap

  1. Explain: You mustn’t / You don’t have to. 2. Two words about work. 3. Who did Dolly play in 9 to 5?

    Answer key

    Answer key: 1 forbidden / not necessary · 2 promotion, colleague, shift, equal pay · 3 Doralee, a secretary.

Step 2. The question of the day

Ask: Would you like a big theme park near your town? What would it bring? What problems could it create (traffic, prices, seasonal jobs)?

Transition. Dolly opened a theme park near the mountains where she grew up. Let’s look at the numbers before we judge.

Step 3. Document. Dollywood in numbers

Dollywood opened on 3 May 1986 in Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, close to the mountains where Dolly grew up. She became a partner of an existing park and created Dollywood with Herschend Family Entertainment, which still runs it. Today, the Dollywood company includes a theme park, a water park, resorts, restaurants and shops.

Fact Figure
Opening 3 May 1986
Employees about 4,000 to 4,500, the largest employer in Sevier County (as reported by WATE and CNN Business)
Visitors about 3 million a year (as reported by WATE)
Jobs ride operators, lifeguards, chefs, housekeepers, sales assistants, security staff, musicians

Dolly’s coat of many colours is displayed in a museum inside the park (BBC Culture). Official job page: Dollywood Jobs.

In 1988, Dolly also created the Dollywood Foundation to fight the high school dropout rate in Sevier County (Knox News, 2026).

Reading task. Find: the date of opening · the town · the partner company · two jobs · one fact that shows the park is important for the region.

Answer key

Answer key: 3 May 1986 · Pigeon Forge · Herschend Family Entertainment · e.g. lifeguard, chef · it is the largest employer in Sevier County.

Step 4. Expanding your vocabulary

A. The company

Word Meaning Example
a theme park a park with rides and shows on a theme Dollywood is a theme park.
a resort a hotel with many activities The park has resorts.
an employer a company that gives jobs Dollywood is the largest employer in the county.
staff all the people who work for a company The staff wear name badges.
a seasonal job a job for one season Many students take seasonal jobs.
a job fair / a hiring event a day when a company meets candidates Dollywood organises hiring events.
to apply for a job to ask officially for a job I want to apply for a job as a lifeguard.
customer service helping customers Customer service is important in a park.
tourism travelling for pleasure, and the industry around it Tourism is a big business in Tennessee.
a local economy the jobs and businesses of a region The park helps the local economy.

Exercises on vocabulary

Exercise E1. Jobs and places. 1 ride operator · 2 lifeguard · 3 chef · 4 housekeeper · 5 sales assistant / a the water park · b the gift shop · c the roller coaster · d the restaurant · e the resort rooms

Answer key

Answer key: 1c, 2a, 3d, 4e, 5b.

Exercise E2. Advantage or drawback? 1 thousands of jobs · 2 traffic jams · 3 seasonal contracts only · 4 money for local shops

Answer key

Answer key: advantages: 1, 4 · drawbacks: 2, 3 (many jobs are seasonal; some are year-round).

Step 5. The teacher’s point

Teacher script. Draw a timeline from 1986 to today. Put opened on the 1986 point (past simple) and an arrow from 1986 to today for has created (present perfect). Practise reading numbers aloud.

Point Form Example
Past simple: finished time Dollywood opened in 1986.
Present perfect: result today, period up to now have/has + past participle The park has created thousands of jobs. Millions of people have visited it.
since / for since + starting point; for + duration It has existed since 1986. It has existed for forty years.
Big numbers no -s after hundred, thousand, million with a number 4,500 = four thousand five hundred; 3,000,000 = three million
Years 1986 = nineteen eighty-six; 2026 = twenty twenty-six

Quick check. Correct: Dollywood has opened in 1986. → Dollywood opened in 1986. (finished date: past simple)

Step 6. Exercises

Exercise 1. Read the numbers aloud, then write them: 1986 · 4,000 · 4,500 · 3,000,000.

Answer key

Answer key: nineteen eighty-six · four thousand · four thousand five hundred · three million.

Exercise 2. Past simple or present perfect?
1. Dollywood (open) ... in 1986. 2. Since then, the park (grow) ... a lot. 3. Millions of visitors (come) ... to Pigeon Forge. 4. In 1988, Dolly (create) ... the Dollywood Foundation. 5. The park (exist) ... for forty years.

Answer key

Answer key: 1 opened · 2 has grown · 3 have come · 4 created · 5 has existed.

Exercise 3. since or for? 1 ... 1986 · 2 ... two years · 3 ... last summer · 4 ... a long time

Answer key

Answer key: 1 since · 2 for · 3 since · 4 for.

Exercise 4. A short application (70 words). Email to apply for a seasonal job at Dollywood: the job, what you have done (present perfect), your availability, why you want the job.
Model answer: Dear Sir or Madam, I would like to apply for a job as a sales assistant this summer. I have studied retail for two years and I have done two work placements in shops. I have always enjoyed working with customers. I am available from June to August. I love music and I would be proud to work in a park created by Dolly Parton. Yours faithfully, ...

Help box. Frame: Dear Sir or Madam, I would like to apply for a job as... I have studied... for... years. I have worked in... I am available from... to... Yours faithfully,

Step 7. Speaking task: “The job interview”

Pairs: student A is the recruiter, student B is the candidate. Then swap.

Recruiter’s questions:
1. Why do you want to work at Dollywood?
2. What have you learnt in your training?
3. How long have you studied your trade?
4. How would you deal with an angry customer?

Going further. The recruiter adds: Do you think Dollywood is good for the region? The candidate answers with On the one hand... on the other hand...

Possible answer: On the one hand, Dollywood has created thousands of jobs in a poor region. On the other hand, many jobs are seasonal and tourism brings traffic. Even so, I think it is more a gift than a simple business, because Dolly chose her home county.

Step 8. Mid-point assessment (Lessons 1-4 and 5)

Student sheet

Name: __ Class: _ Date: ___ (15 minutes)

Part A. Reading (6 points)

Dolly Parton grew up in a one-room cabin in Tennessee. She was the fourth of twelve children. Her mother made her a coat with scraps of fabric, and the children at school laughed at her. In 1971, Dolly wrote a song about this coat. Later, she became famous for her big platinum wigs and her rhinestone suits. In 1980, she played a secretary in the film 9 to 5 and wrote the title song, which won two Grammy Awards. In 1986, she opened Dollywood, which has become the largest employer in her county.

  1. How many children were there in the family? (1)
  2. Why did the children laugh at Dolly? (1)
  3. Give two elements of her famous look. (1)
  4. What job did she play in 9 to 5? (1)
  5. Find one verb in the past simple and one in the present perfect. (1)
  6. True or false: Dollywood has created few jobs. Justify. (1)

Part B. Language (6 points)
1. Correct: She is having blonde hair. (1)
2. Put in order: gown / long / a / pink / sparkly (1)
3. Complete with used to: Her mother (sing) ... ballads. (1)
4. Make it polite: Give me the wig. (1)
5. mustn’t or don’t have to: You ... smoke in the workshop. (1)
6. since or for: Dollywood has existed ... 1986. (1)

Part C. Writing (5 points). In 60 to 80 words: describe Dolly’s look and say if you think she reinvented herself or stayed the same girl from the mountains. Use at least one comparative, one past simple, one although.

Part D. Speaking (3 points, 1 minute with the teacher or recorded): describe the Warhol portrait.

Answer key

A. 1 twelve · 2 because her coat was made of scraps of fabric / they thought it was rags · 3 big platinum wigs, rhinestone suits · 4 a secretary · 5 grew / was / made / laughed / wrote / became / played / opened; has become · 6 False: it has become the largest employer in her county.
B. 1 She has blonde hair. · 2 a long sparkly pink gown · 3 used to sing · 4 Could you give me the wig, please? · 5 mustn’t · 6 since.
C. Model: Dolly grew up poor and wore a coat made of scraps of fabric. Later, her look became much brighter than in her childhood: big platinum wigs, rhinestones and long nails. Although she reinvented her image, I think she stayed the same girl from the mountains, because she wrote songs about her childhood and opened her park near her home.
