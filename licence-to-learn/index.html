Licence to Learn

Are real intelligence officers like James Bond? MI5, MI6 and the world of James Bond.

Road map

Six lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure. The stopping point in every lesson is minute 44. If time runs short, an eight-minute emergency exit replaces unfinished extension work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.

  1. Lesson 1. Who will be the next James Bond?
  2. Lesson 2. Skyfall: listening to a hero under pressure
  3. Lesson 3. MI5 or MI6: understand before you compare
  4. Lesson 4. A good Bond, a good employee?
  5. Lesson 5. The responsible officer’s code
  6. Lesson 6. The interview: evidence, not just adjectives
  • Written record. Every lesson ends with a short written record that students keep.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once, through protected paired turns, not only volunteers.
  • Homework for next lesson. Student-made flashcards and a short written task, set at the end of each lesson.

Stopping point. Minute 44 in every lesson.

Emergency exit · 8 min. Each lesson page gives a replacement ending that still leaves students with the written record, at least one spoken turn, and the homework.

Your mission

Central question: are real intelligence officers like James Bond?

Cultural question: how does a screen hero shape the image of British intelligence?

Final task: take part in a fictional recruitment interview for MI5 or MI6. Explain your choice, describe your skills, support one quality with an example, explain security rules and respond to an unexpected question.

This is a classroom simulation, not a real recruitment exercise. Students use invented identities and experiences if they prefer. They must not disclose private information.

Written production across the unit: a five-rule Spy Code with explanations, completed in lesson 5.

Level and official programmes

The official end-of-year targets in the 2025 English programme (arrêté of 5 May 2025, BO n° 22 of 29 May 2025, NOR MENE2504621A) are:

ClassLVALVB
SecondeB1+A2+
PremièreB1+B1
TerminaleB2B1

These are annual targets, not a requirement that every activity in every unit must already operate at the target level. A B1 consolidation sequence is legitimate, but “B1, Seconde or Première LVA” should not be presented as the complete official annual expectation. The plus sign indicates that the next level is reached in at least one language activity. It should not be treated as a simple synonym for “more difficult vocabulary”.

The programme applies to Seconde from 2025-2026 and to Première and Terminale from 2026-2027. It therefore applies to both classes at the date of this page.

Recommended label: Seconde or Première, LVA: B1 consolidation towards B1+. The teaching level of this unit is a B1 core, with B1+ development in reception, interaction and mediation.

For bac pro and CAP, the 2019 programme remains the reference (BO spécial n° 5 of 11 April 2019, arrêté of 3 April 2019). The final published programme states:

QualificationThreshold stated in the 2019 programmeWider objective distinguished by that programme
CAPA2 for the relevant language unitProgress beyond A2 where appropriate
Bac pro LVAB1+ at the end of the course and examination framework describedB2 as the wider end-of-secondary objective
Bac pro LVBA2+B1 as the wider objective

Consequently, B1 can be a suitable working level in Seconde or Première bac pro. The programme does not assign a universal B1 end-of-year requirement to each of those two classes. B1 should not be labelled the final bac pro LVA target. CAP requires a distinct A2 pathway, not simply the same B1 tasks with fewer questions. CFA identifies a training setting, not one language level. The qualification being prepared determines the relevant pathway. The 2019 programme explicitly includes apprentices among its learners.

Cultural axes

Official axis titles are given here in English; their numbers refer to the French official programme.

Seconde. The strongest primary fit is Axis 5, “Creating and recreating”: study how a familiar fictional hero is reinterpreted and how a trailer reconstructs the myth. A strong secondary fit is Axis 1, “Self-representation and relationships with others”: appearance, credibility, stereotypes and professional self-presentation. Axis 3, “The past in the present”, is possible but not fully developed here. Axis 6, on Commonwealth countries, is not established by this sequence: a British topic alone does not constitute study of the Commonwealth. Suggested Seconde framing: how is a British screen hero recreated, and what does that image encourage us to believe?

Première. The strongest primary fit is Axis 3, “Art and power”: how cinema represents an institution, authority and national protection. A credible secondary fit is Axis 2, “Diversity and inclusion”, in this revised version: casting criteria, stereotypes, technical expertise and leadership. Axis 4, “Scientific innovation and responsibility”, is a possible secondary fit if ethical responsibilities attached to information and technology are developed. Axis 6, “American English-speaking areas”, is not established. In the 2025 programme, “Fictions and realities” belongs to Terminale, not to Première. Suggested Première framing: how can a fictional hero support, simplify or question the image of state power?

The sequence can combine two coherent axes. It should not claim to cover every axis touched by one isolated detail. In the general and technological pathway, the annual programme requires five axes, including Axis 6; this sequence does not replace that annual requirement.

Bac pro. Do not transfer the general and technological lycée axis system onto bac pro. The 2019 programme organises communication around everyday, personal, social and civic situations, and professional situations. This unit connects the two through British institutions and cultural representation; media literacy and stereotypes; teamwork and confidentiality; understanding a vacancy; presenting skills and experience; and participating in an interview. The spy setting remains a cultural simulation. For vocational transfer, students should also connect its language to their actual sector, for example: a receptionist must protect customer information; a mechanic has to check the instructions; a catering employee mustn’t ignore a safety rule. These are classroom transfer examples, not comprehensive sector-specific regulations.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the unit, students should be able to:

  • distinguish fiction, verified information, opinion and speculation;
  • explain the main difference between MI5 and MI6 without presenting it as an absolute geographical division;
  • identify how films construct an exciting image of intelligence work;
  • describe a past experience using the past simple;
  • compare fictional and institutional representations;
  • express obligation, prohibition and absence of obligation;
  • participate in an interview and ask for clarification;
  • revise independently using student-made flashcards.

Not every grammar point is a new lesson objective. Some are retrieval or extension. The compulsory core tasks fit the timings; optional challenges replace an easier task or become homework.

In real intelligence terminology, an officer works for a service, while an agent can be a person recruited to provide information. The everyday word “spy” is less precise. This distinction is introduced in lesson 3.

A speaking guarantee that works

Do not rely on volunteers or a whole-class round. In every lesson:

  1. Students prepare briefly.
  2. Partner A speaks.
  3. Partner B speaks.
  4. Each asks or answers one question.
  5. Both tick the participation box.

For a group of three, give each student a separate timed turn. A whole-class discussion is additional. It never replaces the protected paired turns. Reading a prepared sentence is an acceptable first contribution. It is not, by itself, evidence of B1 interaction.

Student participation box:

  • □ I gave a complete spoken answer.
  • □ I listened to my partner.
  • □ I asked or answered a question.
