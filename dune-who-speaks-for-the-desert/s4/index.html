Lesson 4. Making a Messiah

Step of the problématique: Je comprends. How is a legend written, and who writes it?

Timing (55 minutes, timed on 52)

15 minTwo homework recordings heard; the class reacts.EOI
28 minReading aloud Q3 (the litany against fear), with stress and pauses; chorus, then individual.EOC (phonology)

Stopping point

After stage 4. The rewriting can be finished at home.

Emergency exit (8 minutes)

The litany read aloud, three questions and answers, and one legend sentence in the passive.

Stage 1. Two views of ownership

Two recordings from the homework are heard. The class reacts: who sounded sure, and who left a doubt? A doubt is already Je comprends.

Stage 2. The litany (Q3)

"I must not fear. Fear is the mind-killer."

Q3. Book I, first chapter, the test of the gom jabbar. Read the litany, and the half page before it, in the class edition.

Read the litany aloud, with stress and pauses: first the class together, then volunteers. It is a ritual sentence, short, repetitive, with a rhythm. Religious language works by repetition. Why is a sentence you repeat stronger than a sentence you understand?

Teacher's note on Q3

Check the exact wording, and the lines that follow, in your edition. The two sentences given here are the opening of the litany and are widely attested; if your edition continues with more lines, you may read them aloud, still as a short extract.

Stage 3. Listening: how a prophecy works

Listen twice. Then work on your pathway.

B1 pathway

Complete a short legend with the verbs in the box, in the passive: plant / write / send / tell.

The prophecy ________ long ago. A legend ________ among the people. A mother and a son ________ from the outer world. Their story ________ as a sign.

B2 pathway

Write a legend of 60 words, then underline every place where the agent (who did it) is hidden.

Listening text, for the teacher (do not display during the first listening)

Read at natural speed, twice. Written for the sequence.

A prophecy is a story about the future. When people hear the same story for generations, they wait for someone who fits it. On Arrakis, the Fremen wait for a man from outside, the Lisan al-Gaib, the voice from the outer world. When Paul and his mother arrive in the desert, many signs seem to match. Paul knows the legend was planted, and he also sees that he can use it. The question is not whether the signs are true; it is who decided what the signs would be.

"Lisan al-Gaib" and "the voice from the outer world" are terms from the novel; check the spelling in your edition.

Key, B1 legend

The prophecy was written long ago. A legend was planted among the people. A mother and a son were sent from the outer world. Their story was told as a sign. Other correct passives are welcome, if the agent is discussed afterwards.

Stage 4. Press conference

One student is a Fremen witness; the others are journalists. Then swap. Every student asks or answers at least once. Useful questions: what did you see? what had you been told to expect? who taught you those words? what do you still not know?

Grammar. The passive, used to build a myth

Here the passive is the sacred, often agentless voice of a legend: "It was written...", "He was sent...". This is different from the Orwell use of the passive to hide responsibility ("Mistakes were made"). Here the passive creates an invisible author. Ask, each time: by whom? And who gains from that disappearance?

Exercise 4. Example (already corrected): The Bene Gesserit planted the legend. → The legend was planted (by the Bene Gesserit).

  1. Someone wrote the prophecy long ago.
  2. The Fremen will follow him.
  3. People are telling his story.
  4. They have seen the signs.
Key, exercise 4

a) The prophecy was written long ago. b) He will be followed (by the Fremen). c) His story is being told. d) The signs have been seen. Discuss: which agents disappear, and who gains from their disappearance?

Expanding your vocabulary

A prophecy, a prophet, a messiah, a saviour, a sign, to fulfil, a believer, faith, a ritual, a litany, a myth, to worship.

Front: to fulfil a prophecy

Back: to do what a prophecy said would happen.

Optional, if the class already knows the Orwell sequence (Terminale, axe Fictions et réalités): two ways of controlling language. In Orwell, Newspeak reduces words so that people cannot think against the Party. In Herbert, prophecy multiplies sacred words so that people believe in the leader (Q3, Q4). Is it more dangerous to take words away, or to give people the "right" words? This is a short bridge, not a second subject.

Written trace and homework

Complete: "A messiah is made, not born. In Dune, the legend was planted by..." Spiral, turn 3: Je comprends.

Homework. Revise all the flashcards for the mid-sequence assessment. Prepare a two-minute talk: "Who writes the legend of Paul? Use one quotation." (The oral part of the mid-sequence assessment begins in Lesson 5 and continues in Lessons 6 and 7.)
