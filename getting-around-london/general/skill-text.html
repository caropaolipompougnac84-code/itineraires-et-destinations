Text study

An insult to common sense.The Times on the plans for an underground railway, a few years before 1863 (quoted by The History Press).
The line may be regarded as the great engineering triumph of the day.The Times, January 1863, on the opening of the Metropolitan Railway (quoted by Stephen Halliday, Underground to Everywhere, and History Today).
A direct and expeditious means of conveyance for the enormous traffic between the east and west ends of London.The Times, January 1863, as quoted in the British Medical Journal.

The Metropolitan Railway opened to the public on 10 January 1863, between Paddington (Bishop's Road) and Farringdon Street. It was the first underground railway in the world. On the first day it carried 38,000 passengers, and 9.5 million in its first year. Steam locomotives pulled gas lit carriages. Most of the line was built by cut and cover: workers dug a deep trench under the New Road, built the tunnel inside it and covered it again. The day before, the Prime Minister, Lord Palmerston, aged 79, had refused his invitation to the opening, saying that he hoped to stay above ground a little longer.

Sources: Wikipedia, Metropolitan Railway; History Today, First Day of the London Tube.

Comprehension

  1. When did the line open to the public?
  2. How many passengers travelled in the first year?
  3. What is cut and cover?
  4. Why did Palmerston refuse to come?
  5. How does the tone of The Times change between the plans and the opening?
  6. What does expeditious mean? Find a synonym.

Language

  1. Put into the passive: workers dug a trench.
  2. Find three verbs in the past simple.
Key
  1. On 10 January 1863.
  2. 9.5 million.
  3. Digging a trench, building the tunnel inside, covering it again.
  4. At 79, he hoped to stay above ground a little longer: a joke about death.
  5. From an insult to common sense to a great engineering triumph: from contempt to admiration.
  6. Fast and efficient; quick.
  1. A trench was dug by workers.
  2. opened, carried, pulled.
