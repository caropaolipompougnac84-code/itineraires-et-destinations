Mid-sequence assessment (after lesson 4)

Assessment of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Written test, /20.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 8 lessons, plus this mid-sequence assessment.

  1. Instructions and a one-sentence recap of the method (everyone speaks once) 5 min
  2. Part A · unseen portrait 28 min
  3. Part B · short argument 19 min
  • Written record. The completed mid-sequence script (Part A and Part B, or as far as you have got if the emergency exit is used).
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: one step of the method, named in the opening recap, so that everyone has spoken at least once before writing.
  • Homework for next lesson. None, unless the teacher asks you to finish Part B. The next lesson is lesson 5.

Stopping point. End of the mid-sequence assessment. Next time: lesson 5.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short, finish Part A and write the opening sentence and one argument of Part B. Hand in what you have. Everyone has already spoken once in the opening recap. There is no extra homework unless the teacher asks you to finish Part B.

Part A, 28 minutes; Part B, 24 minutes. You may use your method notes. You may not use a phone or the internet.

Part A · Unseen portrait (/10)

Document: the Armada Portrait of Elizabeth I (about 1588), projected in class. It is shown below so that you can also work from the page.

  1. Describe the composition and the attributes of power. (3)
  2. Explain what the two windows in the background represent. (2)
  3. What image of the Queen is constructed? Use two adjectives from the method. (3)
  4. Compare with the Ditchley Portrait in two sentences. (2)

Part B · Short argument (/10)

150 to 180 words: “Elizabeth II’s vow of 1947 was a political act, not a personal one.” Quote the speech accurately, with quotation marks. Official text: royal.uk.

Answer key

  1. Three-quarter view, rich dress, pearls; her hand on a globe; a crown beside her.
  2. On one side the English fleet in sunlight, on the other the Spanish Armada wrecked in a storm: victory as divine favour.
  3. Powerful, idealised, triumphant, almost divine. (Accept other precise adjectives from the method, if they are justified.)
  4. Both place the Queen above the world or the map; both use weather as a sign of providence.

Part B is marked with the grid: position, concession and example (/4); accurate use of the quotation (/3); language (/3).
