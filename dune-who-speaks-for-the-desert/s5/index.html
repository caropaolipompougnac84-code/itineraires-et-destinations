Lesson 5. Mid-Sequence Assessment

Step of the problématique: J'anticipe. If the Fremen follow the hero, what will they gain and what could they lose?

This lesson is the mid-sequence assessment, out of 20 (CO 5, CE 5, EE 5, EO 5). The oral talks begin today and continue in Lessons 6 and 7.

Stopping point

After stage 4. The oral talks continue at the start of Lessons 6 and 7.

Emergency exit (8 minutes is not enough for a full test)

If time is very short: stages 2 and 4 only (listening and writing). The reading and the talks are taken in Lesson 6.

Part A. Compréhension de l'oral (10 minutes)

You will hear a short text three times. It was written for this assessment. It paraphrases an idea often reported from Herbert; it does not quote an interview. Answer in English (LVB may answer question 5 in French if needed).

  1. What do many readers think?
  2. What did Herbert want to show?
  3. Name two things the believers gain.
  4. What do they give?
  5. Explain the last sentence in your own words.
Listening text, for the teacher (do not display during the test)

Read at natural speed, three times. Written for the sequence. It paraphrases a widely reported idea; it does not quote Herbert.

Many readers finish Dune and think Paul is simply a hero. Herbert did not agree. In interviews, he said he wanted to show the danger of charismatic leaders. In the novel, the people who believe in Paul gain courage and unity. But they also give him the right to speak for them. When one man speaks for a whole people, the people may stop speaking for themselves.

If you wish to quote Herbert himself, check an interview source before you do so (see the bibliography). Do not present this listening text as his wording.

Part B. Compréhension de l'écrit (15 minutes)

Documents: a short reminder of Q4, and Q7.

Document 1 (scene summary, not a quotation). In Book I, in Arrakeen, Jessica understands that the Bene Gesserit, through the Missionaria Protectiva, planted legends on Arrakis long before her arrival, so that a sister of the order could one day be protected. The housekeeper's words echo a prophecy, and Jessica decides to use it. Quote the exact wording only from the class edition.

"warrior and mystic, ogre and saint, the fox and the innocent, chivalrous, ruthless, less than a god, more than a man"

Document 2, Q7. Book III, epigraph of a late chapter, Princess Irulan. The full epigraph is about five lines: check your edition.
  1. Explain the Missionaria Protectiva in two sentences.
  2. Find four pairs of opposites in Q7.
  3. What do the two documents show about who writes Paul's legend?

Part C. Expression écrite (14 minutes)

B1: 80 to 100 words. B2: 120 to 150 words.

"Is Paul the voice of the Fremen, or are the Fremen the voice of Paul's legend?"

Part D. Expression orale en continu (two minutes) and interaction

Talks begin today and continue in Lessons 6 and 7. Topic: "Who writes the legend of Paul? Use one quotation." After the talk, one follow-up question from the teacher or from a student.

Key, mid-sequence assessment

A. 1. That Paul is simply a hero. 2. The danger of charismatic leaders. 3. Courage and unity. 4. The right to speak for them. 5. If a leader talks for everyone, the people lose their own voice.

B. 1. A Bene Gesserit order that plants protective legends and prophecies on worlds like Arrakis, so that a sister can later use them. 2. Warrior and mystic; ogre and saint; the fox and the innocent; chivalrous and ruthless (also: less than a god, more than a man). 3. Paul's legend is written by others: the Bene Gesserit before him, the Princess Irulan after him.

Expected direction for C. B1: Paul helps the Fremen to win, but the legend was written by others, so the Fremen do not entirely decide; a hero can be dangerous. B2: Paul is both the voice of the Fremen and the instrument of a legend planted by the Bene Gesserit and written by Irulan; the price may be paid by the people who follow him.

Homework. Complete the self-correction grid. Write an improved version of your written answer, following the teacher grid.
