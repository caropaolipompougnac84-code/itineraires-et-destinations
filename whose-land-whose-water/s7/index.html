Lesson 7. The Grizzlies: Youth, Land and Hope

A film made in Nunavut, and a team that becomes a family.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Eight lessons in this sequence.

  1. Recap of lesson 6 0 to 4
  2. The film, from a short synopsis 4 to 12
  3. The director and the actors (optional interview) 12 to 28
  4. Link with Jim Logan’s painting 28 to 36
  5. Start a short review, then written record 36 to 48
  6. Homework: finish the review and prepare a dossier 48 to 52
  • Written record. One sentence about the story, one about hope or self-determination, and one comparison with the painting.
  • Participation. Each student says one answer from the interview questions, or one comparison sentence.
  • Homework for next lesson. Finish a 150-word review. With your group, choose a lesson-8 dossier and collect your sources.

Stopping point. Minute 36, after the comparison. The review can be finished for homework.

Emergency exit · 8 min. See the box at the end of the lesson.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

One promise, two answers.

  1. Give one promise made to Indigenous peoples that was partly kept, with a figure.
  2. Give one promise that was not kept on time.

Answer key

  • For example: 158 long-term drinking water advisories have been lifted since November 2015 (Indigenous Services Canada, 21 October 2026).
  • The promise to end all long-term advisories by March 2021: 39 were still in effect on 21 October 2026.

4 to 12 min

The film

The Grizzlies is a Canadian film directed by Miranda de Pencier. It tells a true story. In 1998, a young teacher from the south arrives in Kugluktuk, a small Inuit community in Nunavut, in the Arctic. Life is hard for many teenagers there. He starts a lacrosse team with them. The players later say they made their own family. One character says: “Broken promises are dangerous here.”

A separate film-study resource for The Grizzlies is not yet on this official site. Today’s lesson is written so that you can work from this synopsis and from the interview below, without that site and without watching the whole film in class.

12 to 28 min

The director and the actors

Preview this interview before class. It talks about hard subjects in the community, including the loss of young people, between 0:35 and 1:28 and between 3:50 and 4:46. The extracts advised for class are: 0:15 to 0:35 (what the film is about), 1:28 to 2:15 (the teacher and the students), and 4:47 to 5:12 (hope for the future). The questions can also be answered from the short key beside them if you do not play the video.

Optional video. The Grizzlies | Interview with Miranda De Pencier, Anna Lambe and Paul Nutarariaq. Telefilm Canada · YouTube, 8 April 2022 · 5 min 39. Use only the advised extracts listed above unless you have previewed the other parts. The questions on this page can also be answered from the short text beside them, if you cannot play the video. Open on YouTube.

  1. How does Miranda de Pencier describe the film?
  2. What did the teacher, Russ Sheppard, always say about his students?
  3. One character says: “Broken promises are dangerous here.” Why, in your opinion?
  4. What are many people in the community reclaiming today?
  5. What does one of the actors mean by “the wave from the north”?

Answer key

  • As an inspiring true story about a group of kids in a small Arctic community who are struggling because of the legacy of colonialism.
  • That those kids changed him more than he changed them.
  • Possible answer: these young people have already been disappointed many times; if an adult gets their hopes up and then walks away, they can lose hope completely.
  • Their culture, their way of life, their language and their tattoos.
  • A new generation of young Inuit, full of energy and talent, who are going to surprise everybody.

Grammar spotlight

The gerund as a subject: Playing lacrosse gave them a reason to stay in school. Making a film in Nunavut took years. Phrasal verbs: they did not give up. They stood up for each other. They had to figure out how to win. Their families cheered them up.

28 to 52 min

Link with the painting, a short review, homework

Go back to the description of Jim Logan’s painting in lesson 1: a boy has no one to play with but his dog. In The Grizzlies, a team becomes a family: “We made our own family.” Compare the two works in three sentences.

Answer key

Possible answer: In the painting, the boy is alone in a village where nothing seems to be organised for children. In the film, young people find each other through a sport and become a team. Both works show a hard reality, but the film adds hope and self-determination.

Start a review of The Grizzlies for your school newspaper (150 words): the story, the place, one strong moment, what the film says about promises, and your opinion. Use one gerund as a subject and two phrasal verbs. Finish it for homework if the stopping point has come.

Written record. The Grizzlies, set in Kugluktuk, Nunavut, tells how a lacrosse team became a family in a hard place. Playing together gave young Inuit a reason not to give up. Unlike Jim Logan’s lonely boy, they stand up for each other.

Homework. Finish the 150-word review. Prepare the final task: choose your dossier with your group (lesson 8) and collect your sources.

Emergency exit · 8 min

If time runs short, replace the unfinished plan with this condensed ending (inside the 52 minutes, not 52 plus 8).

  • 0 to 2. Everyone says this sentence aloud: “In The Grizzlies, a team becomes a family; broken promises are dangerous here.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record: Set in Kugluktuk, the film adds hope to a hard reality, unlike the lonely boy in Jim Logan’s painting.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework: Finish the review and choose a lesson-8 dossier.
  • 7 to 8. Check one answer in the answer key.
