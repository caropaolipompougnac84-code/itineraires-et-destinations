Session 4. 1689: the Bill of Rights and the end of absolute monarchy

Key question: How did Parliament become stronger than the King?

Marked recap · 3 points

What do you remember from session 3? Answer alone in five minutes, then check.

1. How many of the 63 clauses of Magna Carta are still valid?

2. Which American text speaks of due process of law?

3. What did Eleanor Roosevelt compare to Magna Carta in 1948?

Recap answers

  1. Four: 1 (part), thirteen, 39 and 40.
  2. The Fifth Amendment of the US Constitution (1791).
  3. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

Key words first

  • to suspend: to stop for a time
  • to levy money: to collect taxes
  • standing army: a permanent army
  • consent: permission, agreement
  • to impeach: here: to attack or question in a court
  • Glorious Revolution: the events of 1688 to 1689 that put William and Mary on the throne

Aims

  • Understand the Glorious Revolution in a few dates
  • Decode a text in seventeenth-century spelling
  • List what the King could no longer do after 1689

Today's language goal

To say what the King could no longer do after 1689, and on what condition, we need could no longer, without + noun, and unless (= except if).

Documents

Document A. The context (written for this sequence)

1642: King Charles I tries to arrest five MPs. Civil war.

1688: King James II leaves the throne.

1689: William and Mary accept the Bill of Rights.

The original text

In 1642, King Charles I entered the House of Commons with soldiers to arrest five MPs. He failed, and the country fell into civil war. Charles I was executed in 1649. The monarchy came back in 1660, but in 1688 King James II was forced out. His daughter Mary and her husband, William of Orange, were offered the crown by Parliament. In 1689 they accepted a list of rights: the Bill of Rights.

Source: UK Parliament, Key dates 1689 to 1714 · UK Parliament, Black Rod · legislation.gov.uk, Bill of Rights 1689

Document B. The Bill of Rights, 1689 (original spelling)

The King cannot stop a law without Parliament.

The King cannot take money (taxes) without Parliament.

The King cannot keep an army in peace time without Parliament.

MPs can speak openly in Parliament.

The original text

“That the pretended Power of Suspending of Laws or the Execution of Laws by Regall Authority without Consent of Parlyament is illegall.”

“That levying Money for or to the Use of the Crowne by pretence of Prerogative without Grant of Parlyament [...] is Illegall.”

“That the raising or keeping a standing Army within the Kingdome in time of Peace unlesse it be with Consent of Parlyament is against Law.”

“That the Freedome of Speech and Debates or Proceedings in Parlyament ought not to be impeached or questioned in any Court or Place out of Parlyament.”

Source: legislation.gov.uk, Bill of Rights 1689

Document C. A ritual that remembers 1642

Every year, the door of the House of Commons is closed in front of the King's messenger.

It shows that the Commons are independent.

The original text

At every State Opening of Parliament, Black Rod, an officer of the House of Lords, walks to the House of Commons to call MPs to hear the King's Speech. “Traditionally the door of the Commons is slammed in Black Rod's face to symbolise the Commons independence.” Black Rod then knocks three times before being let in.

Source: UK Parliament, Black Rod

Words in action

To say it precisely, we need the right words

EntryComplete with: suspend, levy, standing army, consent.

  1. To collect taxes is to ______ money.
  2. A permanent army is a ______.
  3. To stop a law for a time is to ______ it.
  4. Permission or agreement: ______.

Answer key

1. levy. 2. standing army. 3. suspend. 4. consent.

To say it: No longer, without, unless

To say what the King could no longer do after 1689, and on what condition

no longer = not any more.

without = with no ...

unless = except if.

EntryComplete with without or unless.

  1. The King cannot raise taxes ______ the consent of Parliament.
  2. He cannot keep an army ______ Parliament agrees.
  3. MPs can speak ______ fear of a court.

Answer key

1. without. 2. unless. 3. without.

Figures of speech in the documents

Anaphora, and a symbol

“That the pretended Power ... That levying Money ... That the raising or keeping ... That the Freedome of Speech ...” (Document B)

Each line starts with That: this repetition is called anaphora.

Expression task

Now say it

Oral. You are a guide at Westminster. A tourist asks why a door is slammed in Black Rod's face. Answer in 45 seconds.

EntryThree sentences: what happens, what it means, one date.

Tools to use:

  • could no longer, without, unless
  • consent, standing army, symbol
  • 1642, 1689

Sentence starters:

  • Every year, the door is slammed because ...
  • It is a symbol of ...
  • After 1689, the King could no longer ...
  • unless Parliament ...

Model answer

Every year, at the State Opening, the door of the Commons is slammed in Black Rod's face. It is a symbol: in 1642, Charles I entered the House to arrest five MPs. After 1689, the King could no longer govern without Parliament: no taxes, no laws suspended, no army in peace time unless Parliament agreed. So the door reminds everyone that the Commons are independent.
