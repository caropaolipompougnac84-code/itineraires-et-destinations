Lesson 2. Comparing Cities

Compare big cities and capitals with precision, and look at the trap in France.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 7 lessons, plus a final-task assessment.

  1. Recap of lesson 1 4 min
  2. Inès and the map: a short recall, then your turn 8 min
  3. Grammar: comparing with precision 20 min
  4. Pronunciation: the -ate ending, and four tricky names 10 min
  5. Speaking: the capital trap in France? 10 min
  • Written record. Houston is more than twice as big as Austin, yet Austin is the capital of Texas. New York City is almost ninety times as big as Albany: Albany is nowhere near as big as New York City.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student says one precise comparative, or presents one French example, in a full sentence.
  • Homework for next lesson. Exercise 1 on the language page (complex comparatives). Then write three sentences comparing two French cities with three different structures.

Stopping point. After the grammar models, keep the French speaking round even if it is shortened to one example per group.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson inside the 52 minutes (this is not 52 plus 8). Dictate a short written record (two sentences). A quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each). Write the homework on the board.

You already know the full story of Inès if you have read The Capital Trap, lesson 1. This lesson does not reprint that story. A short reminder is enough, then the work that is specific here: complex comparatives (twice as big as, by far, nowhere near as… as), the pronunciation of -ate, and the trap in France.

Recap · 4 min

  1. Why did the first big American cities grow near water?
  2. What did the Erie Canal link in 1825?
  3. Give one reason why a state capital is often a small city.

Answer key

  1. Because people and goods used to travel by boat.
  2. New York and the Great Lakes.
  3. A central position, easy to reach for everybody, or the wish to keep political power away from business power.

Inès and the map · 8 min

Inès arrived in Austin for an exchange year and said she wanted to visit “the capital, Houston.” Her host family laughed: she was already in the capital. Houston, near the Gulf of Mexico, grew with its port, ships, oil and business. Austin, in the heart of Texas, on the Colorado River, was chosen in 1839 by the leaders of the young Republic of Texas because it was in the middle of the land they wanted to develop. That night Inès wrote: “A map is not a list of names. It is a story of rivers, roads and choices.”

The complete story is on The Capital Trap, lesson 1.

Your turn

  1. What was Inès’s mistake, and why was it an easy mistake to make?
  2. Compare the reasons why Houston and Austin became important.
  3. Do you agree with Inès’s sentence? Give an example from France.

Sentence starters. Inès thought that…, whereas in fact… · Houston grew thanks to…, while Austin was chosen because… · I agree, because in France…

Answer key

  1. She thought Houston was the capital of Texas. It was an easy mistake because Houston is much bigger and more famous than Austin.
  2. Houston grew thanks to its port, oil and business; Austin was chosen in 1839 for its central position in the land the Republic of Texas wanted to develop.
  3. Open answer. Example: Lyon and Marseille grew on rivers and on the sea; Paris is both the biggest city and the capital.

Grammar: comparing with precision · 20 min

Read the figures, then compare the cities with the structures on the language page: twice as big as, X times as big as, by far, nowhere near as… as, much smaller than.

Sentence starters. … is more than … times as big as… · … is by far the biggest city in…, yet… · … is nowhere near as big as…, but…

Model answers

  • Los Angeles is more than seven times as big as Sacramento.
  • Houston is more than twice as big as Austin.
  • New York City is almost ninety times as big as Albany: Albany is nowhere near as big as New York City.
  • Chicago is more than twenty times as big as Springfield.

Pronunciation: the -ate ending · 10 min

The same spelling, two sounds. When a word ending in -ate is a verb, the ending is usually pronounced /eɪt/; in many nouns and adjectives it is reduced to /ɪt/.

/eɪt/ (verbs)/ɪt/ (nouns, adjectives)
to relocate, to separate, to debatethe Senate, separate (adjective), a delegate
to designate, to delegateclimate, private, a candidate

Then practise the tricky names: Houston, Albany, Illinois, Arkansas. (Silent letters: Illinois, Arkansas.)

Speaking: the capital trap in France? · 10 min

In small groups: is the biggest city of each French region always its capital? Think of one example where it is and one where it is not. Present your two examples to the class in 30 seconds.

Sentence starters. In…, the biggest city is also the regional capital: … · On the other hand, in…, the capital is…, whereas the biggest city is…

Written record. Houston is more than twice as big as Austin, yet Austin is the capital of Texas. New York City is almost ninety times as big as Albany: Albany is nowhere near as big as New York City.
