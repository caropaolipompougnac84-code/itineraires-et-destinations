Lesson 1. A Painting of an Election

Goal: I can describe a painting and say what I know.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 1 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Split the lesson into two blocks: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Say one sentence from the previous lesson (Lesson 1: what you already know about elections) 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Read the goal and the word box 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Read or listen to your route’s text 8 min (minutes 8 to 16)
  4. Exercise 1, then check it 8 min (minutes 16 to 24)
  5. Pause: look away from the text or move 2 min (minutes 24 to 26)
  6. Exercise 2, then check it 8 min (minutes 26 to 34)
  7. Exercise 3, then check it 6 min (minutes 34 to 40)
  8. Say your final answer aloud with the speaking frame 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  9. Written record 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  10. Progress record 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  11. Read and write down the homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. One sentence for your route (see below), copied or typed.
  • Participation. Say at least one answer aloud. In class, use a quiet voice; at home, say it to yourself. You do not need to record yourself.
  • Homework for next lesson. Make two flashcards: crowd and suggest. Say your written record twice.

Stopping point. Minute 44: stop the main exercises. Keep the written record and the homework, even if an exercise is unfinished.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: say the emergency sentence of the lesson aloud.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: complete the short written record.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: write down the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: check one answer with the key. Do not rush through all the unfinished exercises.

The painting is George Caleb Bingham’s The Verdict of the People, painted in 1854 to 1855. The museum describes different reactions in the crowd and connects the painting with questions about who could take part. You do not need to open the website to complete the lesson.

Choose your route: Route S · SEGPA · Route C · CAP · Route P · Bac pro · CFA. Do only your route.

Route S · SEGPA

Short text

George Caleb Bingham painted this picture.

The picture shows a crowd.

The people are waiting for election results.

Some people look happy.

Other people look unhappy.

The picture shows different reactions.

Exercise S1

Find information

Worked example. Question: What does the picture show? Answer: A crowd.

Now you.
1. Who painted the picture?
2. What results are the people waiting for?

Self-check

1. George Caleb Bingham.
2. Election results.

Exercise S2

Complete

Worked example. “The picture shows a crowd.”

Now you.
Use: happy, unhappy.
1. Some people look .
2. Other people look .

Self-check

1. happy.
2. unhappy.

Exercise S3

Write and speak

Worked example. “This is a painting. It shows a crowd.”

Now you.
Complete: “This is a painting by . It shows .” Say your answer aloud.

Self-check

“This is a painting by George Caleb Bingham. It shows a crowd.”

Route C · CAP

Short text

Bingham painted a crowd waiting for election results.

People react in different ways.

Some look pleased.

Others look disappointed.

The painting can help us ask questions about democracy.

However, a painting does not tell us what every person thinks.

Exercise C1

True or false

Worked example. “The people are waiting for a football score.” False. They are waiting for election results.

Now you.
1. Everyone reacts in the same way.
2. The painting shows an election event.
3. We know exactly what every person thinks.

Self-check

1. False. The reactions differ.
2. True.
3. False.

Exercise C2

Fact or possible meaning?

Worked example. “The picture contains a crowd.” This describes the picture.

Now you.
1. “Some figures look disappointed.”
2. “The picture may suggest that elections divide opinion.”

Self-check

1. A description.
2. A possible interpretation.

Exercise C3

Write and speak

Worked example. “The painting shows a crowd. Different reactions make the event look lively.”

Now you.
Write three sentences with: The painting shows… / Some people… / It may suggest…

Self-check

Possible answer: “The painting shows an election event. Some people look pleased. It may suggest that an election creates different reactions.” Other accurate answers are accepted.

Route P · Bac pro

Short text

Bingham represents an election result as a public event.

The crowd does not react as one group.

Different expressions suggest excitement, disappointment or uncertainty.

The painting may celebrate participation.

It also raises a question about exclusion.

An image can help us interpret the past.

However, we need context before making claims about people’s rights or intentions.

Exercise P1

Identify the main point

Worked example. “The painting is useful only because it is old.” This is not the main point of the text.

Now you.
Choose the best answer.
A. The painting gives complete information about every person.
B. The painting allows interpretation, but context is necessary.
C. Everyone agrees about the election.

Self-check

B.

Exercise P2

Qualify

Worked example. “Every person is happy.” Improved: “Some people appear happy.”

Now you.
Improve:
1. “The painter definitely supports everything in the scene.”
2. “The image tells us exactly what everyone thinks.”

Self-check

Possible answers:
1. “The painter may be celebrating participation.”
2. “The image suggests different reactions, but it does not show every person’s thoughts.”

Exercise P3

Explain

Worked example. “A painting is useful evidence, but it needs interpretation.”

Now you.
Write 50 to 70 words explaining that idea. Include however.

Self-check

Your answer should mention one feature of the painting, explain one possible meaning and name one limit.

CFA pathway

CAP apprentice: use Route C. Bac pro apprentice: use Route P. Do Exercises 1 and 2 of your route, then do this task instead of Exercise 3. Keep the same written record and homework.

CFA task

Describe without guessing

Worked example. “The visitor looks at the painting.” Not: “The visitor hates the painting.”

Now you.
Write one description and one cautious interpretation of the election painting. Starter: “I can see . This may suggest .”

Self-check

The description must be supported by the text. The interpretation must be labelled as possible (may, might).

Written record

S: “Bingham painted a crowd waiting for election results.”

C: “The painting shows different reactions to an election result.”

P: “The painting supports interpretation, but historical context is necessary.”

Speaking frame. “The painting shows . It may suggest .”

Emergency sentence. “The painting shows different reactions to an election.”

Homework

Make two flashcards: crowd and suggest. Say your written record twice.

My progress record

  • I read or listened to the text.
  • I checked my answers.
  • I said at least one answer aloud.
  • I kept my written record.
  • I know the homework.
