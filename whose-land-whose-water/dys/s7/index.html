Lesson 7. Young People and Hope

Goal: I can say who speaks in the film and what the trailer shows.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 7 of 8. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Pause when necessary: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Read the goal and find your place. 0 to 4
  2. Read the short text and word meanings. 4 to 12
  3. Complete reading and vocabulary work. 12 to 20
  4. Complete listening or the labelled no-video route. 20 to 30
  5. Complete language practice. 30 to 37
  6. Prepare and say your answer. 37 to 44
  7. Keep the written record. 44 to 49
  8. Check answers and note homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the short written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework at the end of this page, with its worked example and self-check.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: say the subject and one limit.
  • 46 to 49: keep the written record.
  • 49 to 51: note homework.
  • 51 to 52: check Playing together.
Short text

The Grizzlies is a Canadian film.

It is based on a true story.

It concerns young people and a lacrosse team in Kugluktuk.

You watch the whole film at home before this lesson.

Today you watch the trailer and listen to an interview.

A trailer is short. It shows only some moments.

Say where your idea comes from: the film, the trailer or the interview.

The whole film at home

  1. Watch the whole film at home before lesson 7. It is homework.
  2. The film lasts 1 h 42. You can watch it in two parts.
  3. Turn on English subtitles if you can.
  4. Your teacher tells you how to watch it.
  5. The film talks about sad and hard things, including suicide. You can stop. You can talk to an adult you trust.
  6. While you watch, note one person who speaks and one thing they say.

1. Start here

Your question:

Who helps shape the team’s future?

Your final task:

Give your opinion. Use one moment from the film. Add one limit.

You will:

  1. Read a short introduction.
  2. Watch the whole film at home.
  3. Make a few notes after each part.
  4. Check factual answers.
  5. Prepare a short spoken contribution.

You may watch over several evenings.

You do not need to write while the film is playing.

Important care note

The film includes suicide, grief, violence, substance misuse and strong language.

You do not need to describe a death.

You do not need to discuss your personal life.

If you feel upset:

  1. Pause or stop.
  2. Speak to a trusted adult.
  3. Ask your teacher for the supported alternative.

How to use the self-checks

  1. Read the worked example.
  2. Cover the self-check.
  3. Try the task.
  4. Uncover the answer.
  5. Correct in a different colour.

For an opinion, the self-check shows what a successful answer needs. It does not tell you what you must think.

2. Meet the film

The Grizzlies is a Canadian film.

Miranda de Pencier directed it.

It was released in 2018.

The story takes place in Kugluktuk, Nunavut.

It concerns young people and a lacrosse team.

It is based on real events. It does not represent every Inuit experience.

11. Use careful language

You are a student discussing a film.

You are not speaking in place of Inuit people.

Useful sentences:

  • I think …
  • In this scene, …
  • Miranda’s actions suggest …
  • The film presents …
  • This may show …
  • This does not tell us what everyone thinks.
Exercise 11

Choose the careful sentence

Worked example.
A. All Inuit young people think alike.
B. The film presents several young characters.
Better answer: B. It does not generalise.

Now choose.


  1. A. Adam’s grandparents are shown as distrusting school.
    B. All Inuit families reject education.

  2. A. Sport fixes everything.
    B. Sport can create support.

  3. A. I speak for this community.
    B. I am giving my interpretation of the film.

Self-check

  1. A.
  2. B.
  3. B.

14. Prepare for the debate

Our question:

Can help be useful without taking control?

You may also discuss:

  • Who helps the team succeed?
  • Who should define success?
  • Why should promises be realistic?
  • What knowledge is connected with land and water?
  • Who is heard when decisions are made?
Exercise 15

Build your debate card

Worked example.
My view: Young people help shape the project.
Evidence: Miranda helps raise funds.
Limit: Russ also contributes.

Now complete.

My view:

I think .

My film evidence:

In the film, .

Another contribution or viewpoint:

However, .

My limit:

This does not mean .

My question:

Who / Why / How ?

Self-check

  • My evidence is an action I watched.
  • I named the character.
  • I included a different contribution or viewpoint.
  • I did not speak for Inuit people.

Possible answer:

I think the project depends on several people. In the film, Miranda helps raise funds. However, Russ introduces lacrosse. This does not mean that one person saves everyone. How does the grandmother also influence the project?

Exercise 16

Connect with the sequence

Worked example.
Film idea: People should be heard in decisions affecting them.
Sequence link: We can ask who is heard in a land or water decision.

Choose the careful link.

A. This film proves the legal facts of every land dispute.

B. This film helps us ask whose knowledge and decisions matter.

Then complete:

To make a factual claim about a land or water dispute, I need .

Self-check

  • Choose B.
  • Possible completion: “a relevant source about that dispute”.

The connection is a question about decision-making. It is not proof of a separate legal claim.
