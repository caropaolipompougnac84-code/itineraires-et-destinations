Monarchy and property: who reigns, who rules, who owns?

Resource page, used with lesson 3 and for work on the constitutional monarchy. It is not a separate timed lesson.

Used in lessons 3 and 4. Figures checked on official sites in November 2026.

1How the constitutional monarchy works, through the Queen

The United Kingdom is a constitutional monarchy: the Sovereign is Head of State, but "the ability to make and pass legislation resides with an elected Parliament" (royal.uk). The monarch reigns but does not rule. For Parliament in depth, see the sequence The King Reigns, Parliament Rules.

  • Royal Assent. A bill becomes law only with the Sovereign's assent, but this is "regarded today as a formality": the last refusal was in 1708, when Queen Anne refused the Scottish Militia Bill (UK Parliament). Little-known fact: the assent is still pronounced in Norman French, Le Roy le veult ("the King wishes it"); Anne's refusal was La Reine s'avisera ("the Queen will consider it").
  • The weekly audience. Elizabeth II met her prime minister every week in private; no record was published. She had 15 prime ministers, from Winston Churchill to Liz Truss.
  • The State Opening of Parliament. She read the Queen's Speech from the throne in the House of Lords, but the speech was written by the government: it sets out the government's programme.
  • Head of the Commonwealth, a voluntary association of 56 independent countries today (The Commonwealth).
  • Supreme Governor of the Church of England, a title held by every monarch since Elizabeth I.
  • Political neutrality. She never voted and never expressed a public political opinion.

2Who owns what? The Crown's property

CategoryWhat it isWhere the money goes
The Crown EstateLand, property and seabed owned by the monarch "in right of the Crown": corporately, not personally. The monarch does not manage it. Since 1760 the revenue has been surrendered to Parliament. (Scotland has had its own Crown Estate Scotland since 2017.)All its net profit goes to HM Treasury. The Sovereign Grant that funds the monarch's official duties is calculated as a percentage of the Crown Estate's profits two years earlier: currently 12%. For 2025-26 the grant was 12% of £1,100.7 million, the Crown Estate's net revenue profit for 2023-24 (one hundred and thirty-two million pounds); it rises again slightly in 2026-27.
The Duchy of LancasterA private estate held in trust for the Sovereign as Duke of Lancaster, since Henry IV came to the throne at the end of the fourteenth century.Its net income goes to the Privy Purse, the monarch's private income, used mainly for official costs not covered by the Sovereign Grant.
The Duchy of CornwallA private estate held for the heir to the throne, today William, Prince of Wales.Its net income goes to the heir.
Private propertyBalmoral (Scotland) and Sandringham (Norfolk), inherited by Charles III from his mother.Personal, like any private estate.
Held in trust for the nationThe official residences (Buckingham Palace, Windsor Castle), the majority of the Royal Collection, the Crown Jewels.The monarch cannot sell them: they pass to the successor.

3Myth or fact? "The Crown owns half of England"

You may read online that "the King owns half of England" or "a sixth of the world's land". Check the claim with the documents below, then answer the questions.

Document 1: a legal theory. Since the Norman Conquest, English land law has rested on the doctrine of tenure: in theory, all land is held of the Crown, and the Crown has the ultimate ("radical") title. A private owner holds an estate in the land (in legal terms the fee simple), not an absolute or allodial title. One visible effect: when such an estate has no owner any more (for example after a company is dissolved and its property disclaimed), it can return to the Crown by escheat, a process The Crown Estate describes on its website.

Document 2: what The Crown Estate actually holds (England, Wales and Northern Ireland), according to its own website:

  • "the territorial seabed out to 12 nautical miles and around half of the foreshore" (FAQs); this is why it leases the seabed to offshore wind farms;
  • "a rural portfolio of 185,000 acres across England and Wales" (about 75,000 hectares) (Land), a very small fraction of the land area;
  • in London, an estate that "spans Regent Street" and St James's (Retail and leisure);
  • "In 2025/26 we generated a net revenue profit of £0.5 billion for the UK", all paid to the Treasury (About us).
  1. Is it true that the King personally owns half of England?
  2. What is the grain of truth behind the myth?
  3. Where is the Crown Estate really dominant?
  4. Why does this matter today?
  5. Who receives the Crown Estate's profit?
  6. How would you rewrite the claim so that it is accurate?
Answers
  1. No (myth). The King does not personally own the Crown Estate, and the Crown Estate's rural land is about 185,000 acres, a very small share of England and Wales.
  2. The legal theory of tenure: in theory all land is held of the Crown, which keeps the ultimate title (seen in escheat).
  3. At sea: the territorial seabed out to 12 nautical miles and around half of the foreshore.
  4. Offshore wind farms lease the seabed, which has greatly increased Crown Estate revenue, paid to the Treasury.
  5. HM Treasury; the Sovereign Grant is then calculated as 12% of the profit of two years earlier.
  6. "The Crown Estate, which belongs to the monarch in right of the Crown and pays its profits to the Treasury, owns almost all the seabed out to 12 nautical miles and around half the foreshore of England, Wales and Northern Ireland, plus a rural estate of 185,000 acres and prime London property such as Regent Street."

Exercise. Complete

  1. The monarch ______ but does not rule.
  2. A bill becomes an Act after Royal ______.
  3. Buckingham Palace is ______ for the nation: it cannot be sold.
  4. The Crown Estate pays its profit to the ______.
  5. Offshore wind farms ______ the seabed from the Crown Estate.
  6. Every week the Queen gave the Prime Minister an ______.
  7. The Duchy of Lancaster provides the monarch's private ______.
  8. Balmoral is the King's ______ property.
Answers
  1. reigns
  2. Assent
  3. held in trust
  4. Treasury
  5. lease
  6. audience
  7. income (the Privy Purse)
  8. private
