Lesson 7. Annigoni 1955, Freud 2001: two painters, one Queen

An icon and a confrontation: the portrait that flatters and the portrait that divides.

Key question: Is a royal portrait a likeness, a myth or a work of art?
Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes: Recap 5 min · Teacher’s briefing 8 min · Document 8 min · Vocabulary 7 min · Grammar 11 min · Figures and idioms 6 min · Expression task 7 min.

1The teacher’s briefing8 min

For the teacher

To compare two portraits and qualify a judgement, students need adverbs (manner, degree, sentence) in the right place, and nuanced comparatives (far more, slightly less, by no means as… as, nowhere near as). Start from two opposing judgements in the 2001 press.

Starting point

Annigoni painted her romantically; Freud painted her uncompromisingly. Arguably, Freud's tiny canvas is far more revealing.

Types of adverbs and position

  • Manner (after the verb or the object): He painted her boldly.
  • Degree (before the adjective): an utterly unflattering image, fairly small, rather severe.
  • Frequency (before the main verb): Royal portraits seldom show age.
  • Sentence adverbs (at the start, with a comma): Admittedly, ... Undeniably, ... Frankly, ... Ironically, ...

Nuanced comparison

  • far / much / considerably more flattering; slightly / marginally less formal
  • not nearly as / by no means as / nowhere near as + adjective + as
  • the more ..., the more ...: The longer you look, the more you see.
Help: sentence adverbs give your opinion: Admittedly (I agree that), Arguably (one can say), Frankly (honestly).

2The document8 min

Pietro Annigoni, 1955. Commissioned by the Fishmongers' Company, the Italian painter's portrait shows the young Queen in the dark robes of the Order of the Garter, standing against a distant landscape. She gave him about fifteen sittings. Exhibited in 1955, it became one of the most reproduced images of her, a romantic icon. It hangs in Fishmongers' Hall, London. Annigoni painted her again, more austerely, in 1969 (National Portrait Gallery). Both works are protected by copyright: see the Fishmongers' Company page and The Art Newspaper.

Lucian Freud, 2001. The Queen sat for Freud between May 2000 and December 2001 in a studio at St James's Palace. The painting is tiny (23.5 × 15.2 cm). At Freud's request she wore the Diamond Diadem, the crown she wears on stamps and banknotes. The BBC called it "uncompromising"; the director of the National Portrait Gallery called it "thought-provoking and psychologically penetrating" (BBC News, 20 December 2001). Others were shocked. The critic Adrian Searle wrote in The Guardian: "what an audacious portrait!" (The Guardian, 21 December 2001). See the painting on the Royal Collection Trust website (search "Lucian Freud").

Little-known facts. Freud compared painting such a famous face to "a polar expedition". When the work was finished, the Queen told him she had "very much enjoyed watching you mix your colours". They talked about horse racing, a passion they shared.

Compare with what you know. Think back to Sutherland and Churchill (lesson 4). Freud's Queen did not destroy her portrait: it entered the Royal Collection.

Understanding the document

  1. Who commissioned Annigoni's portrait?
  2. How does Annigoni present the Queen?
  3. What is surprising about the size of Freud's portrait?
  4. Why the Diamond Diadem?
  5. Sum up the reactions in 2001.
  6. How did the Queen react, unlike Churchill?
Answers
  1. The Fishmongers' Company, a London livery company.
  2. Young, calm, idealised, in Garter robes against a landscape: a romantic icon.
  3. It is tiny, 23.5 × 15.2 cm, for such a monumental subject.
  4. Freud asked for it: it is the crown of stamps and banknotes, the official image.
  5. Divided: praised as penetrating and audacious, criticised as harsh and unflattering.
  6. She accepted it graciously and joked about watching him mix his colours.

Controlled practiceExercise 4. Place the adverb

Insert it correctly.

  1. Freud painted her. (uncompromisingly)
  2. The painting is small. (remarkably)
  3. It is more revealing than Annigoni's. (far)
  4. Critics agree. (seldom)
  5. It is a masterpiece. (undeniably)
Answers
  1. Freud painted her uncompromisingly.
  2. The painting is remarkably small.
  3. It is far more revealing than Annigoni's.
  4. Critics seldom agree.
  5. Undeniably, it is a masterpiece. / It is undeniably a masterpiece.

TransformationExercise 5. Nuanced comparisons

Rewrite with the structure given.

  1. Annigoni's portrait is much more flattering than Freud's. (nowhere near as)
  2. Freud's canvas is very much smaller. (considerably)
  3. If you look longer, you see more. (the more ... the more)
  4. Annigoni is not as famous as Freud among critics. (by no means)
Answers
  1. Freud's portrait is nowhere near as flattering as Annigoni's.
  2. Freud's canvas is considerably smaller.
  3. The longer you look, the more you see.
  4. Annigoni is by no means as famous as Freud among critics.

5Figures of speech

  • Simile and metaphor: painting the Queen was like "a polar expedition".
  • Oxymoron: a monumental miniature, a cruel kindness.
  • Litotes: "not exactly flattering".

6Idioms

beauty is in the eye of the beholder
beauty is a matter of personal opinion.
A proverb in its modern form since the 19th century, popularised by Margaret Wolfe Hungerford's novel Molly Bawn (1878).
Some find Freud's Queen ugly; beauty is in the eye of the beholder.
a far cry from
very different from.
The image of a distant shout.
Freud's Queen is a far cry from Annigoni's.
to take something with a pinch of salt
to be sceptical about something.
From the Latin cum grano salis.
Take tabloid reviews with a pinch of salt.
a picture is worth a thousand words
an image conveys more than a description.
Popular in 20th-century American advertising.
This portrait proves a picture is worth a thousand words.

7Expression task7 min

Debate: "Should a royal portrait flatter or tell the truth?" Two teams, three minutes each, then a structured rebuttal: each speaker must start with a sentence adverb (Admittedly, Undeniably, Arguably).

Exit ticket · formative check
  1. Place: It is beautiful. (strikingly)
  2. Nuance: Freud's portrait is (much) smaller than Annigoni's. Use considerably.
  3. What did the Queen say to Freud?
Answers
  1. It is strikingly beautiful.
  2. Freud's portrait is considerably smaller than Annigoni's.
  3. That she had very much enjoyed watching him mix his colours.
