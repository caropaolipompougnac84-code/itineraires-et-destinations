Lesson 6. Checking a Promise

Goal: I can read a dated count without changing what it measures.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 6 of 8. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Pause when necessary: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Read the goal and find your place. 0 to 4
  2. Read the short text and word meanings. 4 to 12
  3. Complete reading and vocabulary work. 12 to 20
  4. Complete listening or the labelled no-video route. 20 to 30
  5. Complete language practice. 30 to 37
  6. Prepare and say your answer. 37 to 44
  7. Keep the written record. 44 to 49
  8. Check answers and note homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the short written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework at the end of this page, with its worked example and self-check.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: explain what 38 counts.
  • 46 to 49: keep the written record.
  • 49 to 51: note homework.
  • 51 to 52: check that 36 counts communities.
Short text

In 2015, the government promised to end certain long-term drinking-water advisories.

The deadline was March 2021.

The official page retrieved for this lesson shows a snapshot dated 10 September 2026.

It lists:

  • 158 advisories lifted since November 2015;
  • 38 advisories still active;
  • 36 communities with active advisories.

These numbers concern public systems on reserves.

An advisory is not a person.

An advisory is not a community.

Building your vocabulary

  • deadline: the date for completing an action;
  • active: still in effect;
  • lifted: officially ended;
  • source: where information comes from;
  • snapshot: information for a stated date.

Exercise 2

Match words

Worked example.

  2. source;

Exercise 6

Complete the language

Worked example.
The government promised to act.

Complete:

  1. It promised to the advisories.
    Choose: end / ending.
  2. Some advisories were .
    Choose: lifted / lift.
  3. Progress was made, the work was not complete.
    Choose: but / so.

General extension:

  1. “The government did not meet the deadline.”
    Rewrite with failed to.

Self-check

End; lifted; but; “The government failed to meet the deadline.”

Speaking: your route

Worked example.
The figures show progress, but active advisories remained.

Route S · SEGPA

Use the Route C sentences below. Say the first sentence aloud. Then say the second one. You may read them from the page, one at a time.

Route G · Lycée général, LVA

Route G · Lycée général, LVB

Self-check

Do not turn advisories into numbers of people.

Written record

I give a source and date with a figure. I check what the number counts. Progress does not always mean completion.
