Lesson 2. The Spice: One Resource, One System

Step of the problématique: Je repère. Who needs the spice, who extracts it, who controls it?

Stopping point

After stage 4. The definition paragraph can be finished at home.

Emergency exit (8 minutes)

The listening, then one role per student, in one sentence.

Stage 1. What we noticed

Three voice notes from the homework are heard. The class lists, without comment at first, what we noticed about Arrakis. This is still Je remarque, collected as a class memory.

Stage 2. A historical map, not a map of Arrakis

Look at this map of the medieval spice-trade routes. Who travelled? Who became rich? The map is a historical document about real trade between Asia, Africa and Europe. It is not a picture of the novel's planet.

Stage 3. Listening: the spice, explained

Listen twice. Then complete the work for your pathway.

B2 pathway

In four sentences, explain why "control of the spice" is "control of the empire". Use which and so.

Listening text, for the teacher (do not display during the first listening)

Read at natural speed, twice. Written for the sequence; not a quotation from the novel.

In the novel, the spice, also called melange, exists only on Arrakis. It extends life. It allows the navigators of the Spacing Guild to see a safe path through space, so without the spice there is no travel between planets. The Emperor gives the planet to noble families as a fief. They extract the spice with huge machines, which attract the giant sandworms of the desert. The Fremen, who live in the deep desert, are treated by the governors as a problem. Whoever controls the spice controls the empire.

A line often attributed to the novel, "The spice must flow", belongs only to the 1984 film. Do not attribute it to the book, and do not write it on the board as a quotation from Herbert.

Key, listening

B1, a possible completion: producers, the Fremen (who live on the land); extractors, the noble families (and their machines; the sandworms are a danger to those machines); consumers, the Guild navigators (and anyone who depends on long life or travel); controller, the Emperor (and, in practice, whoever holds the spice); what it makes possible: life, travel, the empire. B2: because the spice exists only on Arrakis, and because the Guild, which cannot travel without it, holds the empire together, so whoever controls the spice controls travel, and therefore the empire.

Stage 4. Role play in fours

In groups of four, take these roles: an Emperor's adviser, a Guild navigator, a Harkonnen governor, a Fremen. Each person says, in two or three sentences, what the spice means to them. Sentence starters: "Without the spice, I..." / "For my people, the spice is..." / "Whoever controls it..."

Grammar. Defining relative clauses

Use who, which, that, whose, where. Example (already corrected): The spice is a substance. It exists only on Arrakis. → The spice is a substance which exists only on Arrakis.

Exercise 2.

  1. The Fremen are a people. They live in the deep desert.
  2. Arrakis is a planet. Water is rare there.
  3. The Guild is an organisation. Its navigators need the spice.
  4. The Harkonnens are a family. The Emperor gave them the planet.
Key, exercise 2

a) The Fremen are a people who live in the deep desert. b) Arrakis is a planet where water is rare. c) The Guild is an organisation whose navigators need the spice. d) The Harkonnens are a family (that / which) the Emperor gave the planet to / to whom the Emperor gave the planet. Common error: which for people.

Expanding your vocabulary

A resource, to extract, extraction, a supply, to depend on, dependence, a monopoly, a fief, a governor, profit, a trade route.

Language for argument: because of, as a result, therefore, whoever controls...

Front: to depend on

Back: to need something in order to survive. "The empire depends on the spice."

Stage 5 and 6. Written trace, spiral, homework

Write a definition paragraph (40 to 70 words) beginning: "The spice is a resource which..." On the spiral, turn 2 (Je repère): who needs, who extracts, who controls. Outer side: the idea. Inner side: the listening and the map, not a guess.

Homework. Read Book I, the chapter of the crysknife (the scene in Arrakeen in which the housekeeper Shadout Mapes shows Jessica the knife). If you do not have the book, the teacher will give you a two-line summary. Learn six flashcards.

Two-line summary, for students without the book

In Arrakeen, the Fremen housekeeper Shadout Mapes shows Jessica a crysknife, a sacred blade. Jessica understands that Mapes's words follow a prophecy, and that her own order, the Bene Gesserit, planted such legends long ago. The exact wording is to be read in your edition; this summary is not a quotation.
