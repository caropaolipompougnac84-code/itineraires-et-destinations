Portrait of a Queen: Elizabeth II, from The Crown to the canvas

The Aura of a Woman Like No Other

Level
Première and Terminale, general pathway · tronc commun: B1+ towards B2 in Première, B2 in Terminale · spécialité LLCER anglais: towards C1 by the end of Terminale. Official wording from BO spécial no. 1 of 22 January 2019.
Enquiry
How can we paint a portrait of Elizabeth II in words, and how do we separate the face on the canvas, the face on the screen, and the historical record?
Language
Every tense of English, from the present simple to the future perfect; the passive in every tense; reported speech; conditionals 0 to 3 and mixed conditionals; past modals of deduction; wish / if only; adverbs; idioms (including colour idioms) and figures of speech.
Lessons
Nine lessons of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes, plus two resource pages (Family trees; Monarchy and property), a marked final task and a summative assessment. Each lesson has a stopping point, an emergency exit, and three visible outcomes: a written record, participation (everyone speaks at least once), and homework for the next lesson.
Assessment
A formative check in every lesson; a final task marked out of 20; a summative assessment in three parts (written comprehension, written expression, oral), each out of 20.
70 years on the throneQueen from 6 February 1952 to 8 September 2022: the longest reign in British history. Platinum Jubilee in 2022.
ContentA sequence on the portrait of Elizabeth II, built from extracts of the series The Crown (Netflix) and from real portraits: de László, Annigoni, Freud, the portraits of Elizabeth I and of Victoria, colour as a royal language, the family tree of the Windsors and of Europe, the constitutional monarchy and the property of the Crown, fiction and history. Copyrighted portraits are described and linked, not reproduced. Public-domain paintings are shown with their credits.
AudiencePremière and Terminale, general pathway. Tronc commun: B1+ towards B2 in Première, B2 in Terminale. Spécialité LLCER anglais: towards C1 by the end of Terminale. A Going further extension is offered for students who want a more academic register.
ThemesArt and power; Fictions and realities; Private space and public space; Identities and exchanges. A possible LLCER extension: Arts and debates of ideas; Expression and construction of the self.
Official sourcesIn the tronc commun, LVA aims at B2 in Terminale (B1+ towards B2 in Première). The spécialité LLCER anglais aims towards C1 by the end of Terminale. Sources: BO special no. 1 (22 January 2019) · éduscol, voie GT.

Watch

The Crown (Netflix, created by Peter Morgan), official trailer. Watch on YouTube (Netflix channel).

While you watch

  1. What name does the new Queen choose to reign under, and how does she brush aside the question?
  2. Philip asks: “Are you my wife or my Queen?” What does she answer, and what does he refuse to do?
  3. Complete her line: “For better or worse, the crown has ______ ______ ______ ______.”
Answers
  1. “My name is Elizabeth.” She keeps her own name and says: “Let’s not overcomplicate matters unnecessarily.”
  2. “I am both.” He refuses to kneel before her: “I will not kneel before my wife.”
  3. “…has landed on my head.” She knows she is surrounded by people who feel they could do the job better.

My bristol flashcards · how to use them

Principle: each student makes their own cards by hand, on bristol cards. Writing the word is already a way of learning it. The pack stays in their bag: they can revise anywhere, with no screen.

Materials

A7 bristol cards (74 × 105 mm), or A6 bristols cut in half. Coloured felt-tip pens. An elastic band or an envelope for the pack. A small cardboard box with three compartments for practice (a shoe box with two dividers is enough). You can also print the blank template and cut it out.

What to put on the cards

There is no ready-made deck. Make your own pack from this sequence. Suggested items (choose at least 15):

  • Vocabulary. reign, succession, abdication, coronation, anointing, sitter, likeness, decay, duty, grief, investiture, jubilee, wardrobe, sash, tiara, heir, monarchy, Crown Estate.
  • Grammar. present simple vs present continuous; stative verbs; must / might / can't; used to / would; future in the past; the passive in every tense; impersonal passive; reported speech and backshift; past modals; third and mixed conditionals; present perfect vs past simple; nuanced comparison; future continuous and future perfect.
  • Idioms. to get on with it; to look one’s age; to keep calm and carry on; a twist of fate; to step into someone’s shoes; to be thrown in at the deep end; to roll out the red carpet; pomp and circumstance; warts and all; to call a spade a spade; a stiff upper lip; to put on a brave face; to show one’s true colours; a red-letter day; beauty is in the eye of the beholder; a picture is worth a thousand words; to pass the torch; the end of an era; to steal the show; in the limelight.
  • Figures of speech. understatement, antithesis, chiasmus, metonymy, anaphora, personification, synecdoche, oxymoron.

How to write a card

Front: the word, idiom or structure, in English. Back: a short definition or a translation, and a precise example from this sequence (not only a translation).

Practice in pairs, with three boxes

A shows the front, B gives the meaning and a sentence. If the card is right, it moves on one box. If it is wrong, it goes back to box 1. Target: 12 cards in box 3.
