Step 3

Reading: the end of the supersonic dream

This text is about a real accident in which people died. We read it calmly and with respect. There are no pictures of the accident in this sequence.

The end of the supersonic dream (2000 to 2003)

On 25 July 2000, Air France flight 4590, a Concorde, took off from Paris Charles de Gaulle airport for New York. It was a charter flight: the passengers were German tourists on their way to join a cruise ship in New York (BBC News, 25 July 2000). Shortly after take-off, the aircraft crashed onto a hotel in Gonesse, a town near the airport. The French air accident investigation bureau, the BEA, gives the toll in one sentence: « The one hundred passengers, nine crew members and four persons on the ground were killed. »

The BEA reconstructed the chain of events. During the take-off run, a tyre of the left landing gear « ran over a strip of metal, which had fallen from another aircraft ». Debris was thrown against the wing, « leading to a rupture of tank 5 ». « A major fire, fuelled by the leak, broke out almost immediately under the left wing. » Engines 1 and 2 lost thrust, and the aircraft was unable to gain height or speed. According to the BBC, it was the first crash in Concorde's history.

The whole fleet was grounded. Engineers fitted Kevlar linings to the fuel tanks and new Michelin tyres (The Guardian, 7 November 2001). On 7 November 2001, Concorde returned to service, but, as The Guardian wrote, « during one of the worst slumps in aviation history », two months after the attacks of 11 September 2001.

On 10 April 2003, British Airways and Air France announced that they would retire Concorde. British Airways spoke of « passenger revenue falling steadily against a backdrop of rising maintenance costs » (BBC News, 10 April 2003). On 24 October 2003, three Concordes landed at London Heathrow within five minutes of each other, « watched by thousands of onlookers »: one came from New York, one from Edinburgh, and one from a round trip over the Bay of Biscay (BBC News, 24 October 2003). On 26 November 2003, Concorde G-BOAF made the very last Concorde flight, from Heathrow to Filton, near Bristol, where it had been built. It is now on display at Aerospace Bristol.

5. Explain the chain of events in your own words (first, then, as a result, finally).

Help: first, then, as a result, finally

Answer 5

First, a strip of metal fell from another plane onto the runway. Then a tyre of Concorde ran over it. As a result, debris hit the wing and tank 5 ruptured; the leaking fuel caught fire under the wing. Finally, engines 1 and 2 lost thrust and the plane could not climb or accelerate.

6. Give two reasons why Concorde was retired in 2003, even though it had been modified.

Answer 6

Falling passenger revenue and rising maintenance costs (British Airways); the slump in air travel after the attacks of 11 September 2001.

7. Why does The Guardian describe the return to service as taking place « during one of the worst slumps in aviation history »?

Answer 7

Concorde came back two months after the attacks of 11 September 2001, when many people had stopped flying: it returned just when its customers were travelling less.

8. At the end of the documentary, a witness says the supersonic dream « fell apart enormously quickly » (45:16). Does the text confirm or qualify this idea? Explain.

Answer 8

It confirms it in part: the end came fast, between 2000 and 2003. But it qualifies it: the witness speaks about the 1970s, when most orders disappeared, whereas Concorde itself flew passengers for twenty-seven years, from 1976 to 2003.

9. In your opinion, should Concorde have been retired in 2003, or could it have continued to fly? Use at least one past modal (60 to 80 words).

Answer 9

Personal answer. Example: Concorde had to be retired because it was no longer profitable, but it could have become a flying museum if the airlines had found the money. The teacher checks the past modal and the argument.

Grammar lesson: cause and consequence

To explain why the supersonic dream ended, linking causes and consequences, we need cause and consequence
On the board, to copy

Rule

Cause: because (+ sentence), because of (+ noun). Result: so, as a result.

Form

  • because + clause: because fuel leaked from tank 5
  • because of + noun: because of falling revenue and rising maintenance costs
  • as a result, + clause: As a result, the whole fleet was grounded.
  • leading to + noun: leading to a rupture of tank 5

Examples from the lesson

  • “Debris was thrown against the wing, ‘leading to a rupture of tank 5’.” (reading text, quoting the BEA)
  • “A major fire, fuelled by the leak, broke out almost immediately under the left wing.” (BEA)
  • “The whole fleet was grounded.”

Common mistakes

  • Not because rising costs, but because of rising costs
  • Not because of the costs rose, but because the costs rose

Show a model answer

The end of Concorde was not caused by one event but by a chain. In July 2000, a tyre ran over a strip of metal, leading to a rupture of tank 5 and a fire: all one hundred passengers, nine crew and four people on the ground were killed. As a result, the planes were grounded and modified. When they came back, aviation was in one of its worst slumps, which led to falling revenue, while maintenance costs kept rising. Admittedly, the plane was still admired, but the dream fell apart enormously quickly, and British Airways retired it in 2003.

Bac pro Aéronautique · technical task cards

Read the BEA summary as a technician: a chain of causes, not a story. Stay factual and respectful.

Avionics

Indications on the flight deck

According to the BEA summary, the crew shut down engine 2 « following an engine fire alarm » and « noticed that the landing gear would not retract ». List the indications the crew received, in order, in a short factual table (time order, system, indication).

Expected answer

1. Engine 2: problems, then an engine fire alarm, engine 2 shut down. 2. Landing gear: would not retract. 3. Engine 1: loss of thrust. Wording: short, factual, no judgement.

Systems

From the tyre to the engines

Draw the causal chain in five boxes, from the strip of metal to the loss of thrust. Then write one sentence for each arrow, with a linker of consequence (which led to, as a result, consequently).

Expected answer

Strip of metal on the runway, which led to a damaged tyre; debris thrown against the wing, as a result tank 5 ruptured; the leak caught fire under the left wing; consequently engines 1 and 2 lost thrust.

Structure

Modifications before the return to service

The Guardian reports Kevlar linings in the fuel tanks and new Michelin tyres. Explain the purpose of each modification in one sentence, with « so that » or « in order to ».

Expected answer

Kevlar linings were fitted inside the fuel tanks so that a tank would not leak if it was hit by debris. New tyres were fitted in order to resist damage and reduce the risk of heavy pieces being thrown against the wing.
