Grammar lesson: the passive in a historical narrative

To tell a spy story as a historical narrative and as a news report, we need the passive in a historical narrative
For the teacher: how to run it (8 to 10 minutes)
  1. Play the extract: “He was arrested in 1964.” Ask: who arrested him? We do not know, and it does not matter.
  2. Board: be at the right tense + past participle; by + agent only if useful. Students copy.
  3. Show the tenses: is arrested, was arrested, was being built, has been found.
  4. Quick check, then exercises.
On the board, to copy

Rule

The passive puts the action or the victim first: be (at the right tense) + past participle. Add by + agent only if it is useful. Historical and news writing use it when the doer is unknown, obvious or secret.

Form

  • present: the head of Aeroflot in France is arrested
  • past: He was arrested in 1964.
  • plural: These were left in the toilets of first class cabin.
  • past continuous: A supersonic airliner was being built by the Soviets.

Use

  • To tell a spy story as history.
  • To write a news report.
  • When the doer is unknown or secret.

Examples from the lesson

  • “He was arrested in 1964.” (22:15)
  • “These were left in the toilets of first class cabin...” (22:52)
  • “All the drawings for Concorde were done on drawing boards.” (11:38)

Common mistakes

  • Not He was arrest in 1964., but He was arrested in 1964.
  • Not Concorde was build by the British and the French., but Concorde was built by the British and the French.
  • Not The documents were sent by someone., but The documents were sent. (leave out a useless agent)

To say it: headline grammar

Newspaper headlines drop a, the and the verb be: Spy arrested in Brussels = A spy was arrested in Brussels. A past participle alone means a passive; to + verb means the future: Concorde to fly in 1969.

StretchWrite the full sentence, then a headline.

  1. Spy arrested in Brussels
  2. Documents were sent from Switzerland.
  3. Concorde will fly next year.
Show the answers

1. A spy was arrested in Brussels. 2. Documents sent from Switzerland. 3. Concorde to fly next year.

Role play: interrogation room, Brussels, 1964

Student A, the Belgian investigator

Use the facts of the documentary: the lifestyle, the travels, the checkpoint, the toothpaste tubes, the microfilms, the documents on electronics. Ask precise questions, react, push for a confession. Use the passive: You were seen..., You were followed...

Student B, Jean-Paul Soupert

Defend yourself first: explain your money and your travels. Then, under pressure, confess. Use modals of deduction to escape: They must have mistaken me for someone else... You can't have seen me...

Model exchange

A: Mr Soupert, you were followed to East Berlin. You were waved through the checkpoint like an old friend. How do you explain that? B: You must have mistaken me for someone else. A: You are retired, yet you stay in the most luxurious hotels. Where does the money come from? B: ... All right. I was asked to collect information on industrial projects. The microfilms were hidden in toothpaste tubes and left in the trains to Warsaw.

Figures of speech in the documents

Metaphor and simile

« closed regimes behind the Iron Curtain »; « like an old friend »

The Iron Curtain, made famous by Winston Churchill in 1946, is a metaphor for the closed border between the Soviet bloc and the West. Like an old friend is a simile: the spy is welcomed as a friend, which makes the betrayal stronger.

EntryWhat did the Iron Curtain separate?

Show the answers

The Soviet bloc and the West.

CoreWhy is iron a good image for this border?

Show the answers

Iron is hard and impossible to cross: people and information could not pass easily.

StretchWhy does the simile « like an old friend » make the spy story more disturbing?

Show the answers

Because espionage works through trust: the spy looks like a friend, so nobody suspects him.

Bac pro Aéronautique · technical task cards

Industrial espionage targeted technical documents. Your angle: protecting documentation in an aeronautical workshop.

Avionics

Why electronics?

« Many of them were about electronics » (23:05 to 23:30). Explain in about 100 words why electronic systems are a sensitive field in aeronautics, then write three rules to protect wiring diagrams and software in a workshop.

Expected answer

Electronics controls navigation, communication and flight controls; it changes fast and gives a technological lead. Rules: diagrams are stored on a secure system and access is restricted; each copy is numbered and signed out; software updates are only loaded from approved sources and recorded.

Systems

Traceability

Write five rules, using modals (must, must not, should, may), for the use of maintenance manuals in a hangar: who can consult them, how a page is updated, how a task is signed.

Expected answer

Technicians must always use the latest revision of the manual. Printed pages must not leave the hangar. Each task must be signed and dated by the technician and checked by an inspector. Obsolete pages should be destroyed. Visitors may not consult the manuals without permission.

Structure

A suspicious request

A visitor you do not know asks you for the drawings of a wing panel « for a school project ». Write the email you send to your manager to report it (100 to 120 words, formal register).

Expected answer

Subject: Unusual request for technical drawings. Dear Ms Martin, I would like to report a request I received this morning... The visitor, who did not show any badge, asked for the drawings of a wing panel. I did not give any document and I asked him to contact your office. I would be grateful if you could tell me how to deal with such requests in the future. Kind regards...
