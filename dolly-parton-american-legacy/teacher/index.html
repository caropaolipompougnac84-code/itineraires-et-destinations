Teacher's guide

2.1 Level and class

  • Class: Première bac pro, all pathways (production and services).
  • Target level: B1 of the CEFR, in line with the programme’s expectation for the end of the bac pro cycle (B1+ for LVA). Students who are still at A2+ follow the same lessons with the Help boxes; confident students use the Going further boxes. There is one single progression for the whole class: differentiation happens inside each lesson, not through separate levels.
  • Length: nine lessons of 55 minutes, each planned over 52 minutes, with a stop point and an 8-minute emergency exit.

2.3 How every lesson works

Each lesson follows the same order so that students always know where they are.

  1. Recap and flashcards (the last lesson in three questions).
  2. The question of the day (a short debate starter).
  3. Documents (text, image, song or video, always with a task).
  4. Expanding your vocabulary (theme words + words to analyse and argue).
  5. The teacher’s point (one grammar point useful for the task of the day).
  6. Exercises (from recognition to production, with answer keys).
  7. Speaking task (every student speaks at least once).
  8. Written record (what goes into the notebook).
  9. Homework.

Each lesson ends with three results: a written record, a turn to speak for every student, homework for the next lesson.

2.4 Flashcards: the method

Students make their own flashcards in every lesson. They can use index cards (A7) or a flashcard app on their phone.

How to make a card

  1. Front: the word or the structure, in English.
  2. Back, line 1: the meaning (English definition or French translation).
  3. Back, line 2: an example in context, taken from the lesson.
  4. Back, line 3: a sound tip (stressed syllable underlined, or a word that rhymes).
  5. Colour code: navy = theme vocabulary; gold = words to argue and analyse; red = grammar structures.

Blank template

FRONT BACK
Word or structure: __ Meaning: __
Colour: navy / gold / red Example from the lesson: __
Lesson n° __ Sound tip: __

How to revise

  • In class: two minutes at the start of every lesson (“flashcard duel”: student A shows the front, student B gives the back, then they swap).
  • At home: the 3-box system. Box 1 = cards I don’t know (every day); box 2 = cards I almost know (every two days); box 3 = cards I know (once a week). A card moves up a box when you get it right, and goes back to box 1 when you get it wrong.
  • Before each assessment: one full revision of all cards of the lessons concerned.

2.5 Assessment calendar

Assessment When What Weight
Diagnostic Lesson 1, steps 1-2 (10 min) describe a picture, say what you know about the USA and country music not marked: used to group students
Mid-point Lesson 5, last 15 min (planned in the timing) reading + short writing + one minute of speaking on Lessons 1-4 /20, coefficient 1
Summative Lesson 9 final task (speaking, interaction) + reading + writing /20, coefficient 2

All three assessments have a student sheet, a teacher’s marking grid and a self-correction grid. Every grid includes a Language criterion (vocabulary reused, correct syntax).

Self-correction method (for all three assessments).

  1. Read the teacher’s grid and your mark.
  2. In the self-correction grid, write what you missed, criterion by criterion, using the words of the teacher’s grid.
  3. Write a new, improved version of your work that follows the grid.
  4. Underline in your new version the vocabulary and the structures you have added.

2.6 Teacher’s notes on sensitive content

  • Dolly Parton’s death is recent (25 August 2026). Lessons 7 and 8 deal with it respectfully: no fake video is shown in class; we describe it.
  • In September 2026, a legal dispute between the company managing her business interests (She’s Alive LLC) and her nephew Bryan Seaver was reported by the press (Tennessean, 24 September 2026). It contains allegations that have not been ruled on by a court. It is not used with students; it is mentioned here only so that the teacher is not taken by surprise by a student’s question. If asked, answer: “There is a legal case; the court has not decided; we don’t comment on allegations.”
