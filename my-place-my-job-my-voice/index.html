Introduction

On a first placement, a student is given a place, a badge and a list of tasks. This sequence helps them find their own voice in English: say who they are, ask, refuse politely, report a problem, explain a document, and present themselves to a tutor.

Our question

Does my job define me, or can I make it my own?

The skill tags. Every activity shows its skill: CO listening (compréhension de l'oral), CE reading (compréhension de l'écrit), PO speaking (expression orale), PE writing (expression écrite). Every lesson works the four skills.

The Gorse spiral of the sequence

Six questions, one per stage of the sequence, each one narrower than the last, until the point: the question the students answer in their final presentation.

  1. 1What is a job title?A word on a badge: it says what I do, not who I am.
  2. 2How do young people get into a job?Mustafe, Andrew, Sian and Stuart tell a path: a course, a placement, a choice.
  3. 3What is the work behind the job title?Tasks, but also reasons: we check, clean and measure for someone.
  4. 4Is a trainee allowed to ask and to refuse?Yes: polite does not mean powerless. Asking protects the work and the people.
  5. 5Why explain a document to someone else?A message is useful when the other person can act on it.
  6. 6What do I want my tutor to know about me?A skill, an example and a goal: my own voice.

At the point. Does my job define me, or can I make it my own? An existential question that every student meets on a first placement: the place we are given, the job we learn, and the person we choose to be in it.

Programme and level

  • Programme: langues vivantes, voie professionnelle (CAP and baccalauréat professionnel), arrêté du 3 avril 2019, Bulletin officiel spécial n° 5 du 11 avril 2019.
  • Level: A2 at the start of Seconde professionnelle, towards B1 (CEFR). The dys version and the help boxes give access from A1+.
  • Activities: listening, reading, speaking (in interaction and continuous), writing, and mediation, which the programme names: « présenter, exposer, expliquer, expliciter, rendre compte, résumer, reformuler, traduire » (lesson 7).
  • Follow-up: the programme suggests an « outil de suivi individuel (journal, carnet de bord, portfolio) »: here, « My voice file ».
  • Length: eight lessons of 52 minutes, about four weeks. Lessons 5 and 8 are assessments.

The five directives of the programme, and where they are worked

1. All four skills, for the tools of the language

« La pratique de toutes les activités langagières est indispensable à l'acquisition des outils de la langue (vocabulaire, grammaire, syntaxe, prononciation) »

Every lesson works CO, CE, PO and PE, and every lesson has a Language focus: grammar, lexis and pronunciation, practised and graded.

2. Sounds and spellings

« l'étude du rapport entre mots écrits et mots prononcés (à savoir le lien et les éventuelles distances entre phonie et graphie) constitue une étape indispensable vers une meilleure maîtrise de la langue »

A pronunciation point in each lesson: weak /ə/, -ed and -s endings, word stress, polite intonation, thirteen and thirty.

3. Writing to remember

« le recours à l'écrit permet la mémorisation, le réemploi et la fixation des acquis »

Flashcards, a written record in every lesson, and « My voice file », the student's portfolio that becomes the final presentation.

4. Understandable, not perfect

« Ce n'est pas l'authenticité parfaite de la langue parlée par un locuteur natif qui est visée. Il s'agit avant tout de lever les éventuels blocages et inhibitions chez l'élève et d'assurer une expression compréhensible. »

Speaking in pairs first, never forced in front of the class, assessed orals recorded in pairs, a « Taking a correction » routine and an emotional storm tab.

5. Authentic documents

« ces documents, qu'ils soient textuels, iconographiques, sonores ou audiovisuels, sont nécessairement authentiques, variés et richement représentatifs de la réalité quotidienne, sociale, économique, professionnelle, culturelle, etc. du ou des pays dont on étudie la langue. »

Seven real videos and job profiles of the National Careers Service (England), and advice of the Health and Safety Executive for young workers.

Quotations: programme de langues vivantes de la voie professionnelle, 2019 (in French, as published).

Objectives

  • Introduce yourself in a professional way: name, course, interests, a skill with an example, a goal.
  • Understand real young workers talking about their job, their path and their skills.
  • Ask for help, make polite requests, refuse politely and offer an alternative.
  • Report a problem clearly, with the past simple and the past continuous.
  • Pass on useful information from a French document to an English speaker (mediation).
  • Pronounce clearly enough to be understood: weak forms, endings, word stress, intonation.

Four lanes, one class

A Seconde pro class often mixes several pathways. Students choose a lane for some tasks; the language and the skills are the same for everyone.

  • R · the shop (customer relations, retail, reception): video and texts with Mustafe.
  • C · the site (construction, building, wood): video and texts with Andrew.
  • H · the kitchen (hospitality, catering, food): video and texts with Alex.
  • M · the workshop (maintenance, vehicles, electricity, industry): video and texts with Sian and Stuart.

Another pathway? The student chooses the closest lane, or uses their own workplace.

Language focus · a / an + job, job titles, the weak sound of a PEPO

For students. Language focus: the short lesson.

Grammar

Rule. In English, a job title after be needs a or an. French does not: « je suis vendeur » = I'm a sales assistant.

  • a before a consonant sound: I'm a bricklayer (Andrew), a sales colleague (Mustafe), a university course (« university » starts with the sound /j/).
  • an before a vowel sound: an apprentice, an electrician, an hour (the h is silent).
  • the when there is only one: I'm the head chef here (Alex).

Short forms: I'm, he's, she's, they're. Sian and Stuart are apprentices (plural: no a).

Lexis

  • Job titles: a sales assistant, a bricklayer, a chef, a motor mechanic, a technician, an apprentice, a trainee, a plumber, a hairdresser.
  • Introducing yourself: My name is... · I work at / for... · I'm studying... · I'm interested in... · I'm good at...
  • Remember: a job (un emploi), work (le travail, uncountable: not « a work »).

Pronunciation

The weak sound /ə/. In a sentence, a is short and soft: /ə/, like the end of « le ». I'm a chef = /aɪm ə ʃef/. an links to the next word: an_apprentice /ən‿əˈprentɪs/.

Word stress. Say the big syllable louder: apPRENtice · MEchanic · techNIcian · BRICKlayer · HAIRdresser.

Practice.

  1. Complete with a, an or the: I'm ___ apprentice. · Andrew is ___ bricklayer. · Alex is ___ head chef at JÖRO. · She's ___ electrician. · It's ___ university course. · I need ___ hour.
  2. Repeat after your teacher, three times, faster and faster: I'm a trainee. I'm an apprentice. He's a mechanic.
  3. Tell your partner: « I'm a student and I want to be a / an ... »

Homework

Work at home.

Say your five-line profile aloud twice at home (you can record it if you want, it is not compulsory). Learn your two flashcards: look at the front, say the back, turn, check.

Emotional storm

Some students do not want to talk about themselves or their course (a course they did not choose, a difficult situation at home). They may introduce an imaginary person, « Alex », or one of the four workers of the reading. Say: « You can use a character today. Your English is what counts. » No one is asked to share private information.

Emergency exits (plan B)

  • No video or no sound: read the first paragraph of Mustafe's transcript (Documents tab) aloud, twice.
  • The class is very quiet: the speaking task is done in writing first, then whispered to a partner.
  • Eight-minute exit (if the lesson starts late): 2 min CO, the teacher reads « I'm a trainee. I can listen. I want to learn. »; 2 min CE, students underline the skill; 2 min PE, « I'm ... I can ... »; 2 min PO, say it to a partner.
  • Extra time: watch the end of Mustafe's video: what are his three skills? (problem-solving, confidence, communication, patience).

Language focus · polite requests and polite refusals, polite intonation PEPO

For students. Language focus: the short lesson.

Grammar

Asking politely. Could you + verb...? (the most useful): Could you say that again more slowly, please? · Can I + verb...?: Can I ask a question? · B1: Would you mind + verb-ing...?: Would you mind waiting for a minute? (careful: -ing after mind).

Refusing politely. I'm afraid I can't + verb · I'm not allowed to + verb · I haven't been trained to + verb yet. Then offer: But I can + verb.

Lexis

  • Clarification: Sorry? · Could you repeat that, please? · Could you speak more slowly? · What does ... mean? · Do you mean ...? · Let me check. · I'll ask my tutor.
  • Refusing and offering: I'm afraid... · I'm not allowed to... · Would you like me to...? · If that helps.
  • Remember: « I'm agree » is wrong. Say I agree.

Pronunciation

Polite intonation. A polite question starts high and moves: Could you rePEAT that, please? A flat voice sounds rude or bored, even with « please ». Sorry? with a rising voice means « I did not hear ».

Linking. Could you sounds like /kʊdʒu/; Would you like /wʊdʒu/.

How a Language focus works (12 to 13 minutes)

Taking a correction

A corrected paper is not a verdict: it is a map that shows where to go next. This step is done after every assessment, in class or at home, and it counts in the sequence mark.

Six steps

For students.

  1. I read all the comments before I look at my mark again.
  2. I compare my work with the criteria, line by line: what I have, what is missing.
  3. I find my errors with the code in the margin (table below).
  4. I rewrite three sentences, correctly, in another colour.
  5. I write what I keep: one success I want to repeat.
  6. I set my goal for the next task: one rule to revise, one flashcard to make.

The correction code

LetterWhat it shows
Ggrammar: verb form, -s, tense

The teacher writes the letter in the margin and does not correct for the student. The student corrects. For speaking, the teacher notes two strengths and one sound or one structure to work on.

Sheet marked /5: one point per step done carefully, from step 2 to step 5, and one point if the three rewritten sentences are correct and the rule is named.

Emotional storm and emergency exits

Speaking a foreign language in front of others, talking about yourself or your future, hearing your own voice on a recording: all this can bring strong emotions (anger, shame, nervous laughter, tears, blocking). This tab gives simple moves for the student and for the teacher.

Exercise 1. My inner weather (2 minutes)

For students.

I choose my weather: sunny, cloudy, rainy, stormy. I write one sentence: « Today I feel ... because ... » I keep it, or I give it to my teacher.

Exercise 2. Square breathing (1 minute)

For students.

I breathe in for 4. I hold for 4. I breathe out for 4. I hold for 4. Three times, feet flat on the floor. This exercise is optional.

Exercise 3. Give the feeling to a character (5 minutes)

For students.

I choose a worker from the videos (Mustafe, Andrew, Alex, Sian, Stuart, Lauren, Will, Matt) or « Alex », an imaginary trainee. I write three lines: how could this person feel on the first day of a placement? What could they say to feel better? Three intensities: a little nervous, quite nervous, very nervous.

Exercise 4. The bridge sentence (in an assessment)

For students.

If I am stuck: I breathe, I read the question again, I underline the key word, I go on to the next question and I come back later. I write at least one short answer for each question, even if it is not complete.

In this sequence, watch out for

  • Talking about yourself (lessons 1, 8): never ask for private details; a student can invent a profile, or speak about a job they would like. « My voice file » is never shown to the class without permission.
  • Orientation not chosen (lesson 2): some students did not choose their pathway. Accept « I didn't choose this course, but... »; the question is what they can make of it. Refer to the professeur principal or the PsyEN if needed.
  • Accidents and danger (lesson 6): the reports are imaginary; a student who has lived an accident at work, or in the family, may write about another situation. The infirmière and the CPE are the people to see.
  • Being recorded (lessons 5, 8): recordings are made in pairs, kept on school devices, deleted after marking; a very anxious student can record alone with the teacher, at an appointment.

General emergency exits (plan B)

  • The digital equipment does not work: all the sheets exist in PDF, Word and LibreOffice; print the whole set at the start of the sequence and keep it in the room. Every video has its official transcript in the Documents tab: read it aloud.
  • The video does not play at school: the links point to the official channel of the National Careers Service on YouTube; check them on the school network before the lesson. Plan B: the transcript.
  • The class is restless: go to the individual writing task of the lesson, in silence, timer on the board; do the speaking task next time.
  • A student is in crisis: do not keep them in front of the class; they leave with the printed sheet to another adult (vie scolaire, CDI, infirmerie), as agreed at the start of the year. Never send a student out without an adult to receive them.
  • The lesson is too short: each lesson has an eight-minute exit in its plan B, with the four skills.
  • Unexpected replacement: the teacher's sheet is enough to teach without preparation: follow the timed plan and read the « What the teacher says » column.

The lessons in one line

  1. Lesson 1. A name is not a job title. Introduce yourself in a simple professional way (name, course, interest, one skill); meet four real young workers from England; give the teacher a first picture of your listening, reading, speaking and writing (diagnostic, not part of the term average).
  2. Lesson 2. My course opens doors. Talk about your path (school, course) with the past simple, and about your plans with « I want to / I'd like to »; understand a real training route in England and compare it with your own; name the training words that are false friends.
  3. Lesson 3. The work behind the job title. Describe what you do in your trade and why (present simple, he / she + -s, to + verb for a purpose); understand a head chef talking about his daily tasks; read real task lists in four trades and explain one to classmates.
  4. Lesson 4. Polite does not mean powerless. Take a correction of the mid-sequence assessment step by step; understand how two apprentices learnt their trade progressively; read real British advice for young workers; report a doubt or a small incident to your tutor (B1: past simple and past continuous).   8. Lesson 8. My job, my voice: final assessment. Show what you can do at the end of the sequence: understand a young worker talking about his job (CO), read a real profile (CE), give a two-minute presentation « My job, my voice » (PO), write an email to your placement tutor (PE); a separate language part (grammar, lexis, pronunciation).

Corrigé (version prof). Erin appelle de l'accueil, pour un premier jour. Elle rappelle qu'un nom n'est pas un intitulé de poste. Au programme : rencontrer le tuteur, porter le badge, deux phrases utiles (« Hello, I work here », « Can you show me? »). L'anglais n'a pas à être parfait : une voix claire, une question polie, oser dire qu'on n'a pas compris. Demain, un courriel court : nom, groupe, une chose à apprendre en PFMP. Compréhension de l'écrit, CE

Texte d'entraînement (le jour du CCF, prendre un document authentique inconnu de 15 lignes au plus) :

> A workplace is a place and a voice. On the first days of PFMP, many students are afraid of English. They think they must speak like a book. In fact, colleagues need something simpler: a greeting, a name, a question when a machine or a rule is new. A welcome booklet, if it is clear, helps. A voicemail or an email, if it is short and polite, helps too. Your job title will come. Your voice starts now: « I work here », « Can you show me? », « I don't understand, could you repeat? ». That is already professional.

Corrigé (version prof). Le texte s'adresse à un élève en début de PFMP. La peur vient de l'idée qu'il faudrait parler « comme un livre ». Les collègues ont besoin d'une salutation, d'un nom, d'une question. Un livret d'accueil clair, un message vocal ou un courriel court aident. Les phrases professionnelles déjà possibles : I work here, Can you show me, I don't understand, could you repeat.

Expression écrite, PE

Choisissez un sujet. Écrivez 100 à 120 mots au moins.

  • Sujet 1, vie professionnelle : Write an email to your PFMP tutor: introduce yourself, say what you can already do, and ask one question about your first week.
  • Sujet 2, vie quotidienne : A British friend asks what your workplace is like. Describe the place, the people, and how you feel when you speak English there.

1. Je remarque (I notice)Qu'est-ce qui saute aux yeux ?Un lieu de travail est un lieu et une voix.Deux mots sont posés ensemble : l'endroit, et la façon de parler.
2. Je repère (I spot)Quel détail devient plus net ?Aux premiers jours de PFMP, beaucoup d'élèves ont peur de l'anglais. Ils croient qu'il faut parler comme un livre.La peur et un faux modèle (parler comme un livre) deviennent le problème, pas le vocabulaire en général.
3. Je comprends (I understand)Que permettent de comprendre ces éléments ?Les collègues ont besoin de phrases simples et utiles, pas d'un anglais de manuel.Le critère se resserre : l'usage, au poste, pendant la PFMP.
4. J'anticipe (I anticipate)Que vise le texte ?Il prépare le livret d'accueil, le message, la présentation du métier, pas une rédaction sur « le monde du travail ».On anticipe le hors-sujet : un texte long et général, sans voix utile au tuteur ou à l'équipe.
5. Je trouve le sens (I find the meaning)Quel est l'essentiel, et sa limite ?Trouver sa voix au travail, c'est oser des phrases simples que l'équipe peut utiliser. Limite : ces phrases ne dispensent pas d'écouter le lieu et les règles du poste.Une phrase défendable, avec sa limite.

L'élève ne peut pas venir en classe ou est hospitalisé

L'épreuve blanche peut se faire à la maison ou à l'hôpital, sans aide, en respectant la durée. L'élève note l'heure de début et de fin. Le document audio est déposé sur l'ENT ; l'élève l'écoute trois fois, avec une minute de pause entre les écoutes, sans revenir en arrière. L'oral se fait par enregistrement (présentation), puis par un échange au téléphone ou en visioconférence avec l'enseignante, ou à défaut par trois questions écrites envoyées après l'enregistrement.
