Written assessment · 55 minutes, timed for 52 · /20

This is a lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes, like the others. The oral is not added on top of these 52 minutes; it is organised separately (10 minutes of preparation, then 5 minutes per student).

Part 1. Reading · /6

Last summer, Lucas visited the USA with his family. They landed in Chicago, the biggest city in Illinois. Lucas thought it was the capital, but his guide explained that the capital is Springfield, a much smaller city. Then they drove south along the Mississippi River to New Orleans, where they listened to jazz in the streets. In Louisiana, Lucas learned that Baton Rouge, the capital, has a French name. His favourite moment was the end of the trip in Florida: he swam in the ocean in Key West, the southernmost city in the continental USA.

  1. Where did Lucas’s trip start?
  2. What mistake did Lucas make?
  3. Which river did the family follow? In which direction?
  4. What did they do in New Orleans?
  5. What is special about the name of Baton Rouge?
  6. Why is Key West special?

Part 2. Language · /6

A. Vocabulary (/2). Complete with: borders, flows into, island, inland.

  1. The Mississippi ______ the Gulf of Mexico.
  2. Texas ______ Mexico.
  3. Oahu is an ______ in the Pacific Ocean.
  4. Denver is ______, far from the coast.

B. Comparatives and superlatives (/2).

  1. Houston is ______ (big) Austin.
  2. Montpelier is ______ (small) state capital in the USA.
  3. Las Vegas is ______ (famous) Carson City.
  4. Alaska is ______ (large) state.

C. Preterite (/2).

Last year, I ______ (fly) to Seattle. I ______ (visit) the Space Needle. Then I ______ (take) a boat to Alaska and I ______ (see) Juneau, the capital.

Part 3. Writing · /8

You are on a road trip in the USA. Write a postcard (80 to 100 words) to an English-speaking friend.

  • Say where you went (at least 3 cities, including 1 capital).
  • Use the past tense.
  • Compare two places.
  • Give one surprising fact about a city or its name.

Answer key, parts 1 and 2

Part 1. 1. In Chicago. 2. He thought Chicago was the capital of Illinois. 3. The Mississippi; they drove south. 4. They listened to jazz. 5. It is a French name. 6. It is the southernmost city in the continental USA. Accept any accurate answer even if the language is imperfect.

2A. flows into, borders, island, inland (0.5 point each).

2B. bigger than, the smallest, more famous than, the largest (0.5 point each).

2C. flew, visited, took, saw (0.5 point each).

Oral assessment · 5 minutes per student · /20

The student draws a card E. Ten minutes of preparation: keywords are allowed, not written-out sentences. Two minutes of presentation in front of the projected map, with three chosen photographs. Three minutes of exchange with the teacher.

Possible questions. Why did you choose this city? What is the capital of this state? Is it the biggest city? Which city did you prefer? Why? Can you compare two places of your trip? Do you know where this name comes from? Would you like to live in Juneau? Why or why not?

Oral grid · /20

CriterionLevels
Content /51: fewer than 3 cities, no capital. 3: 3 or 4 cities, 1 capital, a clear itinerary. 5: 4 cities, 2 capitals, 1 natural feature, one surprising fact.
Geography vocabulary /41: a few isolated words. 2: two or three words from the list. 4: at least four words or phrases, used well.
Accuracy /41: frequent errors that block understanding. 2: simple sentences, a comparative or superlative attempted. 4: comparative and superlative correct, varied connectors.
Interaction /41: answers with one word, or does not understand. 2: answers simply, with help. 4: answers spontaneously, develops, justifies.
Pronunciation and fluency /31: many hesitations, names badly pronounced. 2: understandable, a few errors on the names. 3: fluent, tricky names and -ed well pronounced.

Student checklist before the oral

To get a good mark, I must:

  • talk for 2 minutes about my road trip;
  • name at least 4 cities and 2 capitals;
  • use 4 geography words;
  • compare two places;
  • pronounce the city names correctly;
  • answer the teacher’s questions with full sentences.
