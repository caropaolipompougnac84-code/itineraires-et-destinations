Séance 13 · CE, EOI

Living in Australia: Is It Easy?

The good side

Nature, beaches, sun, a relaxed lifestyle, good wages, a multicultural society where about one person in three was born overseas, and the famous "mateship" (loyalty between friends).

The difficult side

  • Homesickness and distance: France is 24 hours by plane; there is a 9 to 10 hour time difference. (See also the flight and the distances.)
  • Loneliness: it takes time to make Australian friends, and many backpackers only meet other French people.
  • Cost of living: rent and food are expensive in big cities.
  • Exploitation: some employers underpay backpackers, especially on farms.
  • Scams: fake job ads, fake flats asking for a deposit.
  • Nature: strong sun, heat, bushfires, the ocean (swim between the flags), long empty roads. The animals that frighten some people are discussed in séance 4: the official rules matter more than the myth.
  • Culture: respecting Aboriginal cultures and sacred sites, understanding humour and indirect politeness.

Reading: Tom's blog CE

"My first month in Perth was hard. I didn't understand the accent, I missed my family and I spent too much money. I only hung out with French guys from my hostel. Then I got a job on a farm near Margaret River. The work was tough, 5 am starts in the heat, but the farmer, Sue, was great. She taught me to drive a ute and invited me to her family barbie. That's when I really discovered Australia. My advice? Leave your comfort zone, speak English, even badly, and say yes to invitations."

  1. ★ List three problems Tom had at the beginning.
  2. ★ What changed his experience?
  3. ★★ Explain his advice and say if you agree.
