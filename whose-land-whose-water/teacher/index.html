Teacher notes

How this version of the sequence was revised, what to check before class, and how the lessons are organised.

Level

This sequence is suitable for Première générale et technologique, with an end-of-year target of B1+ in LVA and B1 in LVB, provided that the amount of compulsory information stays limited, the legal vocabulary is scaffolded, and core language is distinguished from extension work. Bac pro and CFA apprentices follow the 2019 programme (B1+ in LVA and B1 in LVB as examination expectations at the end of the course, not compulsory Première-year attainment). CAP follows an A2 route.

What changed in this version

Language load

Keep three recurring production tools at the centre:

  1. “The government promised to…”
  2. “The project was approved / the agreement was signed…”
  3. “However… We recommend…”

Revisit them throughout the sequence.

Treat the following as supported extensions rather than simultaneous minimum requirements:

  • past perfect;
  • habitual would;
  • complex causatives;
  • should have;
  • wish followed by the past perfect;
  • multiple passive constructions in the same sentence.

The programme supports increasingly precise and independent communication. It does not require every student to demonstrate every listed grammar feature in one final product.

Listening

In the previous version of this sequence, many video questions could be answered from the neighbouring text. This provides access, but it does not establish what a student understood through listening.

In this version:

  • students first listen without the written support;
  • they record what they actually heard;
  • a second listening targets selected details;
  • the support text becomes available afterwards;
  • they distinguish “heard”, “read” and “inferred” information.

Accessible support remains available without being misrepresented as unaided listening.

Participation

A group presentation does not guarantee that every student speaks. This version replaces “each group presents” with a protected individual turn inside pairs or small groups.

Each student receives preparation time, an uninterrupted turn and a response opportunity. Reading a prepared sentence is an acceptable access route, but students should gradually move towards reformulation.

Cultural care

Points of care:

  • Do not infer that a village is a reserve, northern, or poor from a painting alone.
  • Do not present “Indigenous views of nature” as one uniform worldview.
  • Do not make students invent personal testimony about displacement or speak as though they possess an Indigenous person’s lived experience.
  • Do not ask students to defend assimilation as their personal position.
  • Do not assess whether students agree with the teacher’s political conclusion.

Instead, distinguish observation, interpretation, attributed argument and evidence.

Legal accuracy

The previous version gave the impression that the 1997 Delgamuukw decision established ownership of the whole disputed Wet’suwet’en territory by named hereditary chiefs. That is too categorical. Teach it as a major decision explaining Aboriginal title and the importance of oral histories, not as a simple final award of the disputed territory. The judgment also describes consultation and consent in more qualified terms than the previous emergency-exit sentence suggested.

Use:

Consultation involves meaningful discussion and consideration of concerns. Consent means agreement. The legal requirements depend on the rights, territory and circumstances involved.

Do not teach:

Every project everywhere is legally impossible without consent.

Figures and dates

Two corrections were made.

  1. The previous version gave 158 advisories lifted, 39 active and 37 communities, dated 21 October 2026. The official page consulted for this version displays a snapshot dated 10 September 2026: 158 lifted, 38 active and 36 communities. The later snapshot could not be independently confirmed, so this version uses the September snapshot and labels it explicitly.
  2. The November 2026 federal budget describes an offer of a collective 15% Trans Mountain ownership share to 128 Indigenous groups, not 129. An offer is not a completed transfer of ownership.

Further corrections:

  • Cubic metres per second measures river discharge, not current speed. Replace “the river flowed twice as fast” with “the reported discharge increased substantially”.
  • Do not calculate the proportion of the original 2015 advisories resolved by dividing cumulative lifted advisories by cumulative lifted plus current active advisories. The official page records new advisories being added over time.
  • Describe the White Paper as a policy proposal, not a promise equivalent to the drinking-water pledge.
  • The Canadian Encyclopedia dates its withdrawal to 1970; another educational source uses 1971. For assessed work, accept “withdrawn after strong opposition” or “withdrawn in 1970, according to the Canadian Encyclopedia”.
  • The whole film The Grizzlies (2018, Miranda de Pencier) is compulsory home viewing before lesson 7, set as homework in lesson 6: it opens up the voices students use in the debate and in the lesson 8 hearing. In class, only short extracts are shown: the official trailer (0:00 to 2:20, twice) and three interview extracts, 7 min 44 of screen time in all. Students say whether each piece of evidence comes from the film, the trailer or the interview. The film viewing worksheet and its answer key are on lesson 7. Organisation, listening and permissions

Common organisation

Each lesson occupies 55 minutes, with 52 minutes of planned learning and three minutes for arrival, equipment and transition.

The normal stopping point is minute 44. If necessary, replace everything remaining from minute 44 with the eight-minute emergency exit. Do not add eight minutes to the lesson.

Protected speaking takes place simultaneously in pairs or small groups. It is not a succession of whole-class speeches.

Students may select different support levels while working together:

  • LVA: explain and qualify.
  • LVB: state the main point and justify it.
  • Access support: use a frame, then add one chosen detail.

Listening protocol

For every video task:

  1. Read the listening purpose.
  2. Listen once for the main message.
  3. Note keywords, not complete sentences.
  4. Listen again for the named details.
  5. Prepare a short restitution.
  6. Only then consult the support text and answer key.

Use these evidence labels:

  • H: I heard it.
  • R: I read it afterwards.
  • I: I inferred it.
  • ?: I am unsure.

The expected video answers in the lessons are based on the descriptions and keys in the teaching dossier. They are not newly verified transcripts. Preview the videos before teaching and accept accurate paraphrases. All eleven original URLs are retained unchanged.

Adapted version

The adapted version is a separate student route for SEGPA, CAP, CFA and bac pro, usable alone without an AESH. Presentation choices:

Use a single-column layout, short paragraphs, generous spacing, clearly numbered tasks and separate answer sections. Make text selectable and resizable. Keep keyboard access and visible focus. Do not make colour the only way to identify an answer. Keep video controls and direct links available. Place descriptions beside the paintings.

These are design requirements for this version, not a claim that one layout will meet every student’s needs.

The Grizzlies viewing worksheet

Lesson 7 extension: The Grizzlies

Full-film home-viewing worksheet and independent dys-friendly version

This resource extends the supplied lesson 7. It moves from introducing a film through a synopsis and an interview to analysing a film students have actually watched. It retains the lesson’s distinction between observation and interpretation, its emphasis on youth agency, and its rejection of the idea that an outsider simply “saves” a community.

Teacher guidance

Purpose and curriculum links

Enquiry question:

How can a film about a lacrosse team help us debate belonging, responsibility and the right to speak?

Sequence link:

When people make decisions about a community’s future, whose knowledge counts, whose promises matter, and whose voices are heard?

For Première, the principal links to the English programme published in BO no. 22 of 29 May 2025 are:

  • Axis 6, “Les aires anglophones américaines”: studying a Canadian film set in an Inuit community.
  • Axis 2, “Diversité et inclusion”: participation, representation and access to decision-making.
  • Axis 5, “L’être humain et la nature”: examining the film’s representations of land, water, hunting and belonging.

These are the Première axes in the new programme, rather than the former Première axis “Territoire et mémoire”. The working routes here are LVA B1+ and LVB B1.

For bac pro, CFA and CAP, the worksheet supports the 2019 language programme through audiovisual comprehension, intercultural understanding, expressing a justified viewpoint, interaction and mediation. The CAP route reduces the linguistic load to A2 without removing the central questions about responsibility and representation. These are teaching connections, not a claim that vocational programmes use the same axes as Première.

Viewing arrangements and cultural care

The film is The Grizzlies, directed by Miranda de Pencier, 2018. It dramatises a story involving a youth lacrosse team in Kugluktuk, Nunavut. Treat it as a film based on real events, not as an exhaustive documentary account of a community.

The film includes suicide, bereavement, domestic violence, substance misuse and strong language. Its educational resources explicitly connect discussion of the film with colonial structures, relationships, youth resilience and hope.

Before assigning it:

  1. Check age suitability, institutional requirements and lawful access.
  2. Inform students and families about the sensitive content.
  3. Offer a supported alternative without penalty where needed.
  4. Never require students to describe personal experiences.
  5. Do not assess willingness to watch distressing material.
  6. Do not ask students to explain a death through a single cause.

The intended route is viewing the film in its entirety at home, including the closing material. Students may divide viewing across several evenings. They must be allowed to pause or stop for wellbeing. The full film is not shown in class, in accordance with the supplied standing orders.

The parts below use narrative landmarks rather than invented timestamps. Students record a timestamp from their own edition when useful. They should read the questions for one part, watch that part, then make brief notes. They should not write continuously while watching.

No existing lesson 7 interview activity is replaced. Its selected interview extracts remain a separate source, and students must not present an interview statement as film dialogue.

The student worksheet and the complete answer key are on lesson 7. The adapted worksheet is on the adapted lesson 7. Both can be downloaded from the downloads page.

The Sentence I Cannot Finish Alone

This method opens the study of The Grizzlies in lesson 7. It is a way of organising thought, not the whole lesson. Students begin with wide, personal questions, then look at the film’s choices, then reach the tip of the spiral, where a political, civilisational or existential problem emerges.
