Pronunciation: Australians often shorten words and end them in "-o" or "-ie" (arvo, brekkie). "Day" can sound like "die".

Listening CO

Your teacher reads a message from Jake, an Australian, to Léa, a French backpacker. Listen and answer.

  1. ★ When and where do they meet?
  2. ★ What must Léa bring? (three things)
  3. ★ What can she find at the servo?
  4. ★★ Rewrite the message in standard English.

Script à lire (deux fois, avec l'accent si possible)

"G'day Léa! Fancy a barbie this arvo at Bondi? We're meeting at four near the surf club. Bring your thongs, your sunnies and heaps of sunscreen, mate, it's gonna be a scorcher. I'll bring the esky with drinks. If you're hungry before, there's a servo on the corner with snacks. No worries if you're late, she'll be right! Cheers, Jake."

Trace écrite

G'day, arvo, barbie, thongs, sunnies, esky, servo, no worries, she'll be right. I can listen for these words and say them in standard English.

Travail à la maison

Learn eight Aussie words from the table. Write a four-line message to a friend, using at least five of them, then write the same message in standard English. We listen again and correct in séance 13.

Court, à rendre par écrit. La correction se fait au début de la séance 13.

Corrigé (pour le professeur)

1. Cet après-midi, à quatre heures, près du club de surf de Bondi. 2. Tongs, lunettes de soleil, beaucoup de crème solaire. 3. Des en-cas. 4. "Hello Léa! Would you like a barbecue this afternoon at Bondi? We are meeting at four near the surf club. Bring your flip-flops, your sunglasses and a lot of sunscreen, it's going to be extremely hot. I'll bring the cool box with drinks. If you're hungry before, there's a petrol station on the corner with snacks. It's all right if you're late, everything will be fine! Best wishes, Jake."
