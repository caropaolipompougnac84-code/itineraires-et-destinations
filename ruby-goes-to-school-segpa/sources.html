Sources

Every fact in this sequence comes from one of these sources. The model postcard in lesson 4 was written for this sequence. The pictures on the Happy Families cards are drawings made for the game. The two pictures of Victorian cards on the Relax time page, Mrs. Bones and Mrs. Chip, are real cards from the old English game Happy Families. All the other pictures come from the painting.

  1. Norman Rockwell Museum, The Problem We All Live With (collection page: 1963, oil on canvas, Look, 14 January 1964, NRM.1975.01)
  2. Norman Rockwell Museum, « The Problem We All Live With » was published sixty years ago today (source of the reproduction used on this site)
  3. Norman Rockwell Museum, 2010 press release (36 × 58 in; Licensed by Norman Rockwell Licensing, Niles, IL)
  4. Ruby Bridges, In 1960 Little Ruby Bridges Bravely Entered an All-White School, Guideposts (her own account: the marshals drove her and her mother, two in front and two behind, she held her mother's hand, it reminded her of Mardi Gras, the day in the principal's office)
  5. US Marshals Service, Deputy U.S. Marshals Escort Ruby Bridges to School in 1960
  6. Robert F. Kennedy Human Rights, Ruby Bridges (born 8 September 1954; New Orleans; Barbara Henry)
  7. Video: Ruby Bridges Remembers, Scholastic channel, 21 October 2010 (« Mrs Henry became my best friend »; the cafeteria and the playground)
  8. PBS, Ruby Bridges Goes to School (« I immediately thought that it was Mardi Gras »)

Relax time: classic version, Mrs. Bones

Mrs. Bones the Butcher's Wife, card from the game Happy Families, John Jaques & Son, London. Drawings attributed to John Tenniel (1820-1914), not signed. The game was shown at the Great Exhibition of 1851; its first publication is dated 1851 or 1861 depending on the source. Colour lithograph, edition and date of this card unknown. Public domain.

  1. The World of Playing Cards, Jaques’ Happy Families (drawings commissioned from John Tenniel in 1851, game shown at the Great Exhibition of 1851, first published by John Jaques in 1861 according to Michael Thomson, 2022)
  2. Wikipedia, Happy families (devised by John Jaques Jr., first published before the Great Exhibition of 1851, illustrations possibly by John Tenniel, no official credit; the Bones family, the butcher, is one of the eleven original families)

Relax time: dys version, Mrs. Chip

Mrs. Chip the Carpenter's Wife, card from the game Happy Families, A New and Diverting Game for Juveniles, John Jaques & Son, London, about 1860 (1855-1864). Hand-coloured lithograph, card 9.2 × 5.7 cm. Designed by John Tenniel (1820-1914), according to the museum. Victoria and Albert Museum, London, MISC.47-1975. Photograph © Victoria and Albert Museum, London.

  1. Victoria and Albert Museum, Happy Families, A New and Diverting Game for Juveniles, MISC.47-1975 (boxed card game published by John Jaques & Son about 1860; hand-coloured lithographed cards; designer: Sir John Tenniel)
