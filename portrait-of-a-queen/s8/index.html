Lesson 8. The Crown: fiction, history and the right to invent

Should drama about living people carry a warning?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 8 lessons.

  1. Recap (marked /3) 5 min
  2. Official trailer and the historical record (Aberfan, or the 1997 broadcast) 16 min
  3. Debate: should drama about living people carry a warning? 14 min
  4. Prepare the final task: format, three sources, one objection 12 min
  5. Exit ticket and homework 5 min
  • Written record. One example of invention or compression in the series, and the three sources chosen for the final task.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: one argument for or against a warning, with a concession if possible.
  • Homework for next lesson. Prepare the final task: choose a format, gather three sources, and write the objection you will answer.

Stopping point. End of this lesson.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson: dictate a short written record (two sentences); a quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each); write the homework on the board.

Recap · marked /3
  1. How does the 1997 broadcast answer public criticism?
  2. Why does the 2020 broadcast recall the Second World War?
  3. What is mediation, in the task you have just done?

Answer key

  1. By speaking “as your Queen and as a grandmother”, from the heart, after being criticised for silence.
  2. To use collective memory and a familiar wartime promise (“we will meet again”) in order to unite the country.
  3. Explaining a document for another audience (here, in French, for a French newspaper), without translating it word for word.

The Crown is studied as fiction, compared with the historical record. It is not used as a source for what happened. No film stills are reproduced.

Watch

Official trailer, Netflix channel. Watch on YouTube. Compare the trailer with a real speech or event studied in this sequence (for example the 1997 broadcast), or with the historical record of Aberfan, 21 October 1966.

The historical record: Aberfan, 21 October 1966

A colliery spoil tip collapsed on the village of Aberfan in Wales. 144 people were killed, 116 of them children, most of them in the village school. The Queen visited eight days later. She was later criticised for the delay; some accounts say she came to regret not going sooner. The Crown dramatises the disaster and the delay. Compare: what is invented, what is compressed, what is accurate? Why does fiction invent? (Use official historical accounts and, if you wish, the 1997 broadcast already studied. Do not treat the series as evidence.)

  • The debate on disclaimers. In 2020 and 2022, some politicians and public figures asked Netflix to say clearly that the series is fiction. Should drama about living people carry a warning?
  • Prepare the final task (gallery talk, op-ed or commission letter): choose your format, list three sources (one portrait, one speech, one other), and note one objection you will answer.

Answer key · arguments

For a warning: viewers take fiction for history; real people can be hurt. Against: drama has always reinterpreted history (Shakespeare’s history plays); audiences can tell the difference; fiction can reveal emotional truths that archives cannot. A good spoken answer uses a concession and a rebuttal.

Exit ticket
  1. Give one example of something fiction can invent or compress, and say why that is a problem for historians, or why it might be legitimate for drama.
  2. Name the three sources you will use in the final task.

Homework

Prepare the final task: choose one format, gather three sources from the sequence (one portrait, one speech, one other), and write the objection you will answer. Rehearse if you have chosen the gallery talk.
