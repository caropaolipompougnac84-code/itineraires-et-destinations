Lesson 4. Can anti-commercial art escape the market?

The question today. If critical art is sold for a lot of money, does the criticism die, or does it travel?

Two short films

Your teacher will show two short extracts, each twice. First extract: a work is shredded at an auction (00:00 to 02:00). Second extract: the trailer for Dismaland (00:00 to 02:07). For each film:

  1. Write three things you can see. Do not explain them yet.
  2. Write one contrast, with whereas or although.

Worked example. I can see a castle that looks damaged. A usual theme park looks cheerful, whereas Dismaland looks grim. Although the shredding looked like an attack on the sale, the work later sold for even more money (this last fact comes from later reporting, not from the two-minute film).

Expanding your vocabulary

Word or phraseMeaningExample
auctiona public sale, the highest bidder winsThe work was sold at an auction.
to shredto cut into stripsPart of the work was shredded.
contrasta clear differenceThere is a contrast between fun and decay.
whereasused to set two facts against each otherA park promises fun, whereas this park looks grim.
althoughused to admit one fact, then add a contrastAlthough the work was shredded, it was sold again.

Exercise. Combine with the word in brackets. 1. A theme park is cheerful. Dismaland looks grim. (whereas) 2. The shredding looked like an attack. The work became more valuable. (although)

Corrigé

1 A theme park is cheerful, whereas Dismaland looks grim. 2 Although the shredding looked like an attack, the work became more valuable.

The Walled Off Hotel

This hotel opened in Bethlehem in 2017, beside a barrier. Guests pay to stay next to a wall that limits movement. That is a contrast worth describing carefully.

Write three sentences: where it is; what you can see; one question you would ask a visitor.

Homework

Make flashcards for whereas and auction. Do not say that a sale price proves the artist's exact intention.
