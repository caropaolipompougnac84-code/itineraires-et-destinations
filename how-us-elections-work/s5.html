Question: Why do candidates visit some states again and again?

Objectives

  • Understand what a swing state is
  • Read a bar chart of election results

What we need to say

In this lesson, you need to predict what a candidate will get if she wins a swing state.

To compare states, we need these words: swing state, safe state, popular vote, to campaign, dispute.

To predict a real possibility, we need the first conditional: If a candidate wins Pennsylvania, she will get nineteen electoral votes.

Documents

Key document

In most states, everybody knows in advance which party will win: these are safe states. In a few states, both parties can win: they are swing states, and the candidates spend most of their time and money there. In 2024, seven states were called swing states by most news media: Arizona, Georgia, Michigan, Nevada, North Carolina, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin. Donald Trump won all seven and the election, with 312 electoral votes against 226 for Kamala Harris. A candidate can win the popular vote and lose the Electoral College: this happened in 2000, in 2016 and three times in the nineteenth century. In 1876, the result was disputed and a special commission chose Rutherford B. Hayes, although Samuel Tilden had more votes from the people.

Grammar to say it: the conditional (1), real possibilities

Why we need it

To predict a real possibility, we need the first conditional: If a candidate wins Pennsylvania, she will get nineteen electoral votes.

Grammar point: board explanation

Teacher script
  1. Show the swing state table. Ask: What happens if a candidate wins Pennsylvania? Write the answer.
  2. Colour the two parts: if + present simple / will + verb. Insist: no will after if.
  3. Show that the two parts can change places; a comma only when if comes first.
  4. Explain the link with when and unless: When the polls close, the counting will start. Unless she wins Pennsylvania, she will lose.
  5. Write the linking words of cause and consequence: because, so, if, unless, as long as.
  6. Students copy, then do the quick check aloud.
Board: students copy

The first conditional; linking words of cause and consequence

Rule
Real possibility in the future: If + present simple, will + verb.
Form
If a candidate wins Pennsylvania, she will get nineteen electoral votes.
She will get nineteen votes if she wins Pennsylvania. (no comma)
Use
Predictions, warnings and plans that can really happen.
Examples from the lesson
  • If she wins Georgia, she will get sixteen votes.
  • If nobody gets 270 votes, the House will choose the president.
  • Unless she wins a swing state, she will lose.
Common mistakes
  • If she will win Georgia ... → If she wins Georgia ...
  • If he win → If he wins (third person -s).
  • When the polls will close → When the polls close.
French and English
French: Quand les bureaux fermeront, on comptera. English: When the polls close, they will count. No future after if or when.
Also on the board: Linking words: cause and consequence
Cause: because, as. Consequence: so, that is why. Condition: if, unless (= if not), as long as.

Figures of speech: metaphors of war, nature and play

Learn

Election words are often metaphors. A battleground state is a state where the parties fight like armies. A landslide is a huge victory, like a mountain falling. A swing state moves from one side to the other, like a swing in a playground. A wave election is a big movement towards one party.

  1. Which metaphor comes from war?
  2. Which comes from nature?
  3. Which comes from a playground?
  4. Explain a blue wave.
  5. In Nast's cartoon, the papers say Tilden or Blood. Is it a metaphor or a real threat? Discuss.
  6. Write a sentence about a sports match with one of these metaphors.
Answer key
  1. battleground.
  2. landslide (and wave).
  3. swing.
  4. Many Democratic victories at the same time.
  5. Discuss: in 1877 some people really threatened violence; Nast shows the danger.
  6. Example: Our team won by a landslide: ten to nothing.

Finer grammar: the third conditionalGoing further

Rule

Imaginary past: If + had + past participle, would have + past participle. If Gore had won Florida in 2000, he would have become president.

  1. If Tilden / win / one more electoral vote in 1877, he / become / president.
  2. If the Supreme Court / not stop / the recount in 2000, the result / be / different?
  3. If Clinton / win / more swing states in 2016, she / win / the election.
  4. If Harris / win / Pennsylvania, Michigan and Wisconsin in 2024, she / still / not reach / 270.
Answer key
  1. If Tilden had won one more electoral vote in 1877, he would have become president. (Hayes won 185 to 184)
  2. If the Supreme Court had not stopped the recount in 2000, would the result have been different? (nobody can know)
  3. If Clinton had won more swing states in 2016, she would have won the election.
  4. If Harris had won Pennsylvania, Michigan and Wisconsin in 2024, she would have reached exactly 270 (226 + 19 + 15 + 10 = 270): so she would have won.

Answer key
  1. If she wins Pennsylvania, she will get nineteen electoral votes.

Text analysis: Florida, 2000Going further

On election night in 2000, the presidential race came down to a single state. In Florida, George W. Bush led Al Gore by only a few hundred votes, which triggered recounts and weeks of legal battles. In December, the Supreme Court stopped the recount, and Bush won Florida's electoral votes and the presidency, although Gore had won the national popular vote. Had a few hundred Floridians voted differently, history would have taken another course.

  1. Find the third conditional with inversion. Rewrite it with if.
  2. What does triggered mean?
  3. Find a contrast word.
  4. Why was Florida decisive?
  5. What is the tone of the last sentence?
Answer key
  1. Had a few hundred Floridians voted differently, history would have taken another course. = If a few hundred Floridians had voted differently, ...
  2. caused, started.
  3. although.
  4. Its electoral votes decided who reached the majority.
  5. Reflective: it shows how small events change history.
