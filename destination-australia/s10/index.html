Studying in Australia

The school system

The school year starts in late January or early February and ends in December: summer holidays are at Christmas!

After school: tertiary education

  • VET (Vocational Education and Training): practical courses taught by TAFE institutes and private colleges, in IT, business, hospitality, construction, care... A TAFE diploma can lead to university.
  • University: Bachelor's degrees (usually 3 years), then Master's and PhD. Australian students enter with their ATAR, a ranking from 0 to 99.95.
  • Careful: in Australia, "college" often means a TAFE or a private college, not a university.
  • International students pay full fees: tuition is expensive.

The Student visa (subclass 500)

  • First, a place in a registered course and a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE).
  • Proof of English (IELTS, PTE or another approved test), depending on the course.
  • Proof of money for tuition, travel and living costs.
  • OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover): compulsory health insurance for the whole stay.
  • The Genuine Student requirement: you explain why you chose this course and how it fits your plans.
  • Work: up to 48 hours per fortnight during the course, more during the holidays.
  • Fee: AUD 2,500 since July 2026. (Study Australia, official announcement of the increase from AUD 2,000 to AUD 2,500 on 1 July 2026.)
