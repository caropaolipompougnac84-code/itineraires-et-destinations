Problem. Racing to the skies: was the supersonic dream a triumph of human ingenuity or a costly race for national prestige, and how do Britain and France remember it today?

Why Concorde? You travelled by bus, by Tube and by train, in London and in India. This is the air chapter.

In 1962, Britain and France signed a treaty to build a supersonic airliner together.

The first prototype flew from Toulouse on 2 March 1969. The British prototype flew from Filton, near Bristol, on 9 April 1969.

From 1976 to 2003, British Airways and Air France flew passengers at twice the speed of sound.

Today, Concordes are in museums: in Bristol (England), at East Fortune (Scotland), at Brooklands, and in France.

You will learn to:

  • understand a long authentic documentary, extract by extract, and report on it
  • compare figures and achievements with precision (twice as fast as, nowhere near as)
  • tell a story of spies and politics with the passive
  • express regret and imagine an alternative past (should have, third conditional, inversion)
  • explain a technical solution clearly, and mediate a document from French into English
  • argue, concede and refute in a public hearing

Themes. Territory and memory: how a plane becomes a heritage object and a place of memory. The United Kingdom and its nations: a British and French project, remembered in England and in Scotland. Also: science fiction and innovation, political speeches (Kennedy, Nixon).

Final tasks. Session 7: The Supersonic Hearing (speaking and interaction). Sessions 8 and 9: a two-part assessment, listening and reading, then writing.

Bac pro Aéronautique. Same documents, same C1 level, plus a technical task card for each option (avionics, systems, structure) in sessions 1 to 6.

Links with what you know. A Long Heritage: British Public Transport: London Buses, Mind the Gap: the London Underground, Leaving by train and Indian Railways.

Then and now: in 1914, in Hommage à Blériot, Delaunay celebrated early flight across the Channel. In 2003, Concorde made its last flight, over Bristol.

Notes for the teacher

Sequence fact sheet

ClassesTerminale, general and technological pathways (LVA, LVB); bac pro Aeronautics, avionics, systems and structure options.
FrameworkNew modern-languages syllabus, arrêté of 5 May 2025 (BO no. 22 of 29 May 2025), in force in Terminale from the start of the 2026 school year. Bac pro: arrêté of 17 June 2020, annex V (contrôle en cours de formation, situations A and B).
Main themeAxe 2, Territoire et mémoire. Object of study: the making of sites of memory (Concorde no. 1 listed as a historic monument on 5 May 2025; museums in Bristol, East Fortune, Brooklands; the memory of 2000 and of 2003).
Secondary themeAxe 6, Le Royaume-Uni et ses nations. Object of study: a United Kingdom still united? (a British project led by Filton and Bristol, a heritage kept in England and in Scotland, the fly-past of the Scottish Parliament in 1999). Compulsory theme on the general pathway.
OpeningsAxe 3, Fictions et réalités: « Quand la science-fiction nourrit l’innovation scientifique » (00:28). Axe 4: « Forme et portée du discours politique » (Kennedy 10:35, Nixon 43:11).
Target levelTronc commun: B2 in Terminale · spécialité LLCER English: towards C1 by the end of Terminale. Bac pro Aeronautics aims at B1+ in LVA; the sequence also offers C1 challenges. There is no lighter version: the adapted version keeps the same content.
LengthNine lessons of 52 min (lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52): six study lessons, the oral final task (lesson 7), the summative assessment in two parts (lesson 8: listening and reading; lesson 9: written expression).

Changes from the source document

  • Themes: the document cited the 2019 syllabus (axe 6 « Innovations scientifiques et responsabilité », axe 8). In Terminale, those themes no longer apply from the start of the 2026 school year; the sequence is re-anchored on themes 2 and 6 of the new syllabus. « Innovations scientifiques et responsabilité » now belongs to the class of Première.
  • Assessment: 1 hour 30 does not fit into one lesson; it is split into part A (lesson 8, 52 min) and part B (lesson 9, 52 min), each with its own mark scheme and rubric.
  • Text on 2000 to 2003: the earlier written text is replaced by a factual text that quotes real sources (BEA, BBC, The Guardian), soberly.
  • Timings and quotations: all checked against the original subtitles (transcript.txt). Corrections: « fly the farthest » 12:11 (not 12:08); Q33 ends at 26:16; Q31 starts at 23:05. Timings between the thirteenth and the fourteenth minute are rounded to follow the rule on writing that number.
  • Q28: the quotation at 20:40 contains a word from the prohibited list; it is paraphrased (« an open society »).
  • Q47: the documentary does not date the 74 options; the question says « at this point » (Brooklands places these options in 1967).
  • Final task: the « resident of Gonesse » role becomes « aviation-safety expert »; a curator role is added (themes 2 and 6), and a bac pro role.
