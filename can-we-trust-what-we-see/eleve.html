At a glance

  • Class: Première Bac Pro, all vocational fields. Level: from B1 towards B1+ (B1+ is the level expected for language A at the exam).
  • Seven lessons of 55 minutes, each planned over 52 minutes, with a stop point and a fallback plan.
  • Question of the sequence: Can we still believe our eyes in a world of filters, edited photos and AI-generated images?
  • Final task (Lesson 7): as a trainee social media assistant, reply to a customer complaint, present an Honest Image Charter and defend it in a role-play with your manager.
Programme requirements, objectives and skills
  • Reference: Programme de langues vivantes des classes préparant au CAP et au baccalauréat professionnel, arrêté du 3 avril 2019, BO spécial n° 5 du 11 avril 2019.
  • Action-oriented approach (mise en situation active): every lesson ends in a task students carry out in English.
  • The five language activities are practised and evaluated: reading (CE), listening (CO), writing (EE), speaking in continuous form (EOC), spoken interaction (EOI). Mediation is practised in Lesson 5.
  • Professional tasks from the programme covered here: understanding a professional advert (L2, L3); understanding and handling a complaint (L4, L7); advising a professional contact (L4); highlighting the qualities and defects of a product (L2, L7); proposing solutions (L6, L7); formulating proposals for a project (L6).
  • Expected levels at the exam: B1+ for language A; the written exam asks students to reply to a written message, which the evaluations in Lessons 4 and 7 reproduce.
  • Individual language profile: the diagnostic evaluation (L1) gives each student's starting point in each activity.

Official texts: BO spécial n° 5 du 11 avril 2019 · programme (PDF) · évaluation au baccalauréat professionnel (PDF) · Éduscol.

Objectives of the sequence
  • Cultural and civic: understand how product photos, filters, reviews and AI images are made, and what the law says in France, the UK, Norway, the USA and the EU (dated facts).
  • Critical thinking: apply three questions (Full Fact) and the SIFT method before sharing.
  • Professional: write honest product descriptions and posts, reply to complaints, define rules for a company's social media.
  • Language: describing pictures; present passive; must / mustn't / don't have to; past simple and present perfect; must / might / can't for deduction; first conditional and unless.
  • Methods: flashcards made by students, a French spelling corner each lesson, a framed written summary, short homework checked at the start of every lesson.

Classroom routines
  • Talking round: every student says at least one sentence, using the frame on the board. Tick names on the class list; nobody leaves without speaking. A student who freezes may read the frame aloud and add one word of their own.
  • Flashcards: students make four cards each lesson (front: word and drawing; back: translation and their own sentence). Two-minute flashcard battle at the start of the next lesson.
  • Homework: short, always corrected on the board in the first three minutes of the next lesson.
  • French spelling corner: two minutes, one common French spelling difficulty linked to the topic, with a corrected exercise. Students who need more practice can use the Étude de la langue materials allowed by the school.
  • Written summary (trace écrite): framed, copied in the exercise book, read aloud by one student.

Étude de la langue: Describing a picture

  • Present continuous for what is happening in the picture: In the picture, a man is holding a phone.
  • Present simple for facts and opinions: It shows a canvas. I think it is fake.
  • look / seem / appear + adjective: It looks real. It seems old.
  • look like + noun: It looks like a real canvas.

  2. shows

  4. seem

Étude de la langue: The present passive

  • be (am / is / are) + past participle.
  • Active: The stylist places the food. Passive: The food is placed by the stylist.
  • We use the passive when the action matters more than the person: Silicone ice is used on set.
  • Negative and question: It isn't edited. Is the photo edited?

Exercise. Complete.

  1. Fake ice ______ (use) because real ice melts.
  2. The camera ______ (move) by a robotic arm.
  3. Product photos ______ often ______ (edit).
  4. The pizza ______ (heat) with a handheld broiler.
  5. ______ the food ______ (eat) after the shoot? (question)
  6. The portion ______ (not / change) in the photo.
  1. is used
  2. is moved
  3. are / edited
  4. is heated

Trace écrite

Lesson 1 · Seeing is believing?
  • Problem of the sequence: Can we still believe our eyes in a world of filters, edited photos and AI-generated images?
  • A trompe-l'oeil (Gijsbrechts, c. 1670) shows that images have always been able to fool us.
  • Before sharing, ask three questions: Where is it from? What is missing? How does it make me feel?
  • Étude de la langue: In the picture, a man is holding... / It looks real. / It looks like a canvas.
Lesson 2 · The perfect product photo
  • Product photos are prepared: food is placed, lit and sometimes replaced (silicone ice).
  • An advert must not mislead the customer: the portion in the photo must be the portion sold.
  • Étude de la langue: the present passive, be + past participle: The photo is edited. Are the colours changed?
Lesson 3 · Filters, retouching and the law
  • France (1 October 2017): label "Photographie retouchée" on certain commercial photos of models.
  • UK (rulings early 2021, explained by the ASA on 11 February 2021): filters must not exaggerate the effect of a product.
  • Norway (1 July 2022): an official label on adverts with retouched bodies or skin.
  • Étude de la langue: must / have to (obligation), mustn't (prohibition), don't have to (no obligation).
Lesson 4 · Five stars, really? (mid-point evaluation)
  • Warning signs: many positive reviews on the same day, the same wording, no verified purchase, a reviewer who only posts extreme reviews.
  • UK: fake reviews banned since 6 April 2025 (DMCC Act 2024). USA: FTC rule in force since 21 October 2024.
  • Étude de la langue: past simple for a finished moment (I bought it last week); present perfect for a link with now (It has broken).
Lesson 5 · Deepfakes and AI-generated images
  • Six tips: zoom in; find the origin (reverse image search); look for typical mistakes; check body proportions; ask if it is too good to be true; check the background.
  • March 2023: the image of the Pope in a puffer coat was AI-generated (AFP, 28 March; Reuters, 29 March).
  • EU: Article 50 of the AI Act applies from 2 August 2026; deepfakes must be disclosed.
  • Étude de la langue: must (almost sure, yes), might (possible), can't (almost sure, no).
Lesson 6 · Posting for a company: check before you share
  • SIFT: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace to the original context.
  • A company protects its reputation with clear rules: real photos, permission, no fake reviews, checking before sharing.
  • Étude de la langue: If + present, will + verb. Unless = if not.
Lesson 7 · Trust check at work (summative evaluation)
  • Answer to the question of the sequence: we cannot always believe our eyes, but we can learn to check.
  • At work: real photos, honest descriptions, no fake reviews, polite replies to complaints, check before sharing (SIFT).
  • Graphs can mislead too: look at the labels, the numbers, the scale and the context.
