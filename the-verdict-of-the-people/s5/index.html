Lesson 5. Results: Establish, Date and Qualify

What can we responsibly say about the election results?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 5 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55 stay free for collecting work, moving and the change of room.

  1. Partners share one sourced homework claim 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Result status and the source packet 5 min (minutes 4 to 9)
  3. Source A research, Exercise 5A 9 min (minutes 9 to 18)
  4. Source B comparison, Exercise 5B 9 min (minutes 18 to 27)
  5. Teacher correction of categories and discrepancies 6 min (minutes 27 to 33)
  6. Language practice, Exercise 5C 5 min (minutes 33 to 38)
  7. Paired 60-second bulletins, Exercise 5D 6 min (minutes 38 to 44)
  8. Written record and dated finding 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  9. Exit ticket: “We can establish… / We cannot yet establish…” 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  10. Homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. “As of [date and time], according to [source], we can establish [finding]. The information is described as [status]. We cannot yet establish [limit].”
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each partner gives a 60-second bulletin with four required moves (5D).
  • Homework for next lesson. Write a 120 to 150-word bulletin from the class’s verified evidence, with source and date, one past simple sentence, one present perfect sentence and one qualification.

Stopping point. Minute 44, after the paired bulletins.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: each student states one sourced finding or one unresolved question.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: complete the four-line written record.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: record the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: check that every number has a source and a category.

Objectives

Gather comparable information, identify the status of a result and produce a qualified bulletin.

Language: past simple, present perfect, attribution and uncertainty.

No result is given here

This page does not give any 2026 winner, seat total, gain or loss. The midterm elections took place on 3 November 2026. You establish the results yourselves from dated, identifiable sources. If the evidence is missing or incomplete, the right answer is a qualified statement, not an invented figure.

Teacher preparation

Do not begin with unrestricted searching across an untested list of links. Prepare a packet:

  • Source A: one accessible news report or results tracker.
  • Source B: a second report with an identifiable publisher and update time.
  • Source C: a state election-office page to check one selected race. Directory: USAGov, state election offices.

Record the exact working URL and access date of each (note the time zone: Europe/Paris and US Eastern time differ). Keep offline copies or factual summaries with full references. Do not use a current membership list of Congress as if it were a list of newly elected members: keep the outgoing Congress, the election outcomes and the incoming Congress separate. The reading level of real reports cannot be judged before they are chosen: a short explanatory report and a dense interactive tracker do not make the same demand.

Common B1+ route: one chamber, two teacher-selected sources, one question, a partly completed source log. Suggested question: “As of the source update, what can we establish about projected control of this chamber?” Towards B2: compare the second chamber, explain a category difference, investigate why two sources seem to disagree.

Source E

Reading a results page

Original classroom guidance.

A media call is an organisation’s assessment of who has won a race. It is not the same act as legal certification.

Reported vote totals may be incomplete. A source may count projected winners, races not yet called, seats not contested that year or caucus alignments. Read the labels before comparing figures.

For the Senate, the number of races won this year is not the same as the total make-up of the chamber: many senators are continuing their terms.

A newer number is not automatically comparable with an older one. First check whether both sources count the same thing.

CategoryMeaning
CalledA news organisation has assessed the winner.
Not yet calledThat organisation has not assessed a winner.
Projected chamber controlThe source’s assessment of which party will control the chamber.
CertifiedFormally confirmed through the legal process.

Answer key

There is no fixed answer: it depends on the pages in your packet. A good log names the actual page, records its date and status, separates House results from Senate make-up and says exactly what is counted. A reliable-looking source and a timestamp are necessary, but not sufficient: a figure can still be misread.

Exercise 5B

Build a defensible results table

Leave a cell blank if you cannot establish it.

ItemFindingSource and timeQualification
House seats called or reported for each party
House races not yet called
Projected incoming House control, if established
Projected incoming Senate make-up
How independents or caucus alignments are classified
One selected race checked against an official page

Then answer:

  1. Do the sources agree?
  2. If not, are they counting the same category?
  3. Is one page older?
  4. Does either page explain its method?
  5. What can you state confidently?
  6. What remains uncertain?

Answer key

There is no fixed numerical answer. A satisfactory response separates House results from Senate make-up, explains category differences and does not infer a result from missing data. If chamber control is not established in your sources, the correct answer is “not established as of [time]”.

Exercise 5C

Tenses and status

These are language-practice sentences, not election claims. Choose and justify the tense.

  1. “We checked / have checked the page at 10 a.m. yesterday.”
  2. “The page changed / has changed several times since our first visit.”
  3. “The report was published / has been published on Monday.”
  4. We not yet a certification notice. (find)
  5. Rewrite responsibly: “The result is final because a television channel announced a winner.”

Optional B2 extension, only with a verified calculation: “If the party had won [verified number] more seats, it would have reached [verified threshold].” Do not complete it with invented figures; it is not required in the bulletins.

Answer key

  1. checked: a finished action at a finished past time.
  2. has changed: a period continuing to the present.
  3. was published: a specific finished past occasion.
  4. have … found.
  5. “The channel has called the race, but that does not by itself show that the result has been legally certified.”

Then write two sentences about your own research process: this tests tense choice without any invented election claim.

Exercise 5D

News bulletin (pairs, 60 seconds each)

Use four required moves:

  1. “Our source is…”
  2. “Updated on…, it reports…”
  3. “This means we can say…”
  4. “However, we cannot yet say…”

If chamber control is not established, say so.

Answer key

Model frame: “According to [publisher], updated at [time and date], [verified finding]. The page describes this as [status]. This may affect [institutional consequence]. However, we cannot yet conclude [limit], because [reason].” The brackets must be completed from evidence or left unresolved.

Quick exercise. Why is “The party gained seats” incomplete?

Answer key

We need the chamber, the number, the source, the status and the starting point of the comparison.

Written record

As of , according to , we can establish . The information is described as . We cannot yet establish . A media call, a reported count and legal certification are not the same thing.

Exit ticket

Complete both: “We can establish…” and “We cannot yet establish…”.

Homework

Write a 120 to 150-word bulletin from the class’s verified evidence. Include the source and date, one past simple sentence, one present perfect sentence where appropriate and one qualification. If the results are still unresolved, explain the uncertainty. Do not fill gaps by guessing.
