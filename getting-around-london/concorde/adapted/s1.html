Step 2

Today's language goal

To describe Concorde as an icon and explain why people still miss it, we need superlatives (the fastest, the most famous) and compound adjectives (a delta-winged, four-engined airliner).

Before watching

1. Look. What can you see? When do you think this plane was built? What makes it special?

  • It looks like... / It reminds me of...
  • I would say it was built in the... because...
  • What strikes me is the shape of the wing / the nose...

Possible answers

A Concorde seen from below in flight: a very long pointed nose, a thin body, a large triangular (delta) wing and four engines in two pairs under the wing. It was designed in the 1960s, flew for the first time in 1969 and carried passengers from 1976 to 2003. It is special because it flew at twice the speed of sound.

Step 4

C1 challenge: how a legend is built

« It looked like a piece of science fiction arriving in the real world » (00:28). In the first three minutes, how does the documentary turn a plane into a legend? Think about the music, the witnesses, the superlatives, the famous names and the vocabulary of war. Prepare a structured answer of about one minute.

  • The film relies on... to...
  • The superlatives (the fastest, the most futuristic) suggest that...
  • Not only... but also...
  • This is reinforced by...

Possible answer

The opening uses dramatic music and the roar of the engines, emotional witnesses (« I miss the speed and the excitement »), superlatives (the fastest airliner on earth, the most futuristic aircraft that's ever been built for passengers), famous passengers and images of space (the edge of space, the curvature of the earth). The vocabulary of war (a brutal three-way dogfight, a cold war warrior) turns a technical project into an epic. The comparison with science fiction links the sequence to the idea that fiction can feed scientific innovation.

Speak

In your own words, why is Concorde called « an icon of speed » (00:20) « and beauty » (00:27)? Two or three students speak, the others add or qualify.

Written summary

Together, write a short paragraph (60 to 80 words) that sums up the extract. Use at least one superlative.

Model summary

Concorde is presented as the fastest airliner on earth, an icon of speed and beauty. It flew at twice the speed of sound and carried the rich and famous. Former crew members still miss it: from Concorde they could see the curvature of the earth. But the documentary announces a darker story: Concorde was born after the Second World War, out of a brutal race between Europe, Russia and America.

Grammar lesson: superlatives and compound adjectives

To describe Concorde as an icon and explain why people still miss it, we need superlatives and compound adjectives
On the board, to copy

Rule

the fastest (short word). the most famous (long word). a four-engined plane.

Form

  • the + adjective-est: the fastest airliner on earth
  • the most + adjective: the most futuristic aircraft
  • irregular: good, the best; bad, the worst; far, the farthest
  • compound adjective: a delta-winged, four-engined airliner; a three-and-a-half-hour flight

Examples from the lesson

  • “Concorde, the fastest airliner on earth” (00:03)
  • “the most futuristic aircraft” (03:23)
  • “to see who could fly the farthest” (12:11)

Common mistakes

  • Not the most fast, but the fastest
  • Not the famousest, but the most famous

Show a model answer

Concorde was the fastest airliner on earth: this delta-winged, four-engined plane flew at twice the speed of sound. For a witness, it looked like a piece of science fiction arriving in the real world. Bac pro Aéronautique · technical task cards

Same documents, same level, a technical angle. Each option works on its own card, then presents it to the other two options.

Avionics

A flight deck with three seats

Concorde was flown by three people: a captain, a first officer and a flight engineer. At Aeroscopia, in Blagnac, Concorde n°1 still has its cockpit and its flight engineer station, open to visitors (French Ministry of Culture, 5 May 2025). Prepare a 90-second briefing for visitors: what does a flight engineer monitor during a flight? Use the passive (is checked, are monitored) and at least five technical words.

Expected answer

The flight engineer monitors the engines, the fuel system (quantities and transfers between tanks), the electrical and hydraulic systems, air conditioning and pressurisation. Example: « On this panel, the fuel quantity in each tank is monitored. On Concorde, fuel was transferred between tanks during the flight to move the centre of gravity. »

Systems

Four engines in two pairs

Look at the photo from below. Concorde had four Rolls-Royce and SNECMA Olympus 593 engines, a British and French engine. Describe their position and explain in three sentences why an engine built by two countries was both a strength and a risk.

Expected answer

The four engines are under the wing, in two pairs, one pair on each side of the fuselage. Strength: shared costs and skills (Rolls-Royce in Britain, SNECMA in France). Risk: two industries, two languages, two measurement systems, so coordination is harder (see session 5).

Structure

Reading a photograph

Describe the airframe in the photo from nose to tail: nose, fuselage, wing, fin. Use: slender, pointed, swept, leading edge, trailing edge, wingspan. Then explain why the wing is so large compared with the fuselage.

Expected answer

The nose is long and pointed, the fuselage is slender, the delta wing is very large, with a curved leading edge and a straight trailing edge, and the fin is tall. The wing is large because a delta wing must carry the whole plane at low speed, during take-off and landing, as well as at Mach 2 (session 3).
