Animals that make some people hesitate

Snakes, spiders, crocodiles, box jellyfish, sharks: these animals have a terrifying reputation. For some students, and for some families, this fear is enough to stop the project. Before you decide, look at the facts, and at the advice given in Australia itself.

Reading ★ B1

Australia is famous for dangerous animals. The fear is easy to understand. The numbers, however, are often smaller than people think.

Each year in Australia, between 1 and 4 people die from snake bites, according to healthdirect, the official government health site. There is antivenom, and hospitals know what to do. If someone is bitten, you call 000, keep the person still, and put on a pressure bandage. You do not wash, cut or suck the wound, and you do not try to catch the snake.

Most spider bites are not dangerous. A bite from a funnel-web or another big black spider is a medical emergency: call 000. A redback bite is usually very painful, but healthdirect says it is not life-threatening.

Crocodiles live in the north (Queensland, the Northern Territory, the north of Western Australia). In "Croc Country", no natural waterway is ever completely safe. The official advice is simple: obey the signs, stay away from the water's edge, never swim in a place that is not a designated safe area, and never feed a crocodile. (Queensland Government)

Box jellyfish and other marine stingers are a higher risk in tropical waters in the warmer months (often November to May). Swim at patrolled beaches, between the flags, and inside the stinger nets when they are there. If someone is stung, call 000 and pour vinegar on the sting.

For sharks, the same beach rule applies: swim between the red-and-yellow flags, not at dawn or dusk, and not in murky water.

Millions of people live, work and travel in Australia every year. With a few clear rules, the animals that frighten us do not have to stop the journey.

  1. ★ How many people die from snake bites in Australia each year, according to healthdirect?
  2. ★ What must you do, and what must you not do, after a snake bite?
  3. ★ Where do saltwater crocodiles live? Give one safety rule.
  4. ★ When is the risk of box jellyfish higher, and where should you swim?

Reading ★★ B2

The reputation is stronger than the risk, but the risk is not zero. That is the honest position.

The Queensland Government notes that, Australia-wide, snakes and sharks are each responsible for the deaths of just less than three people a year, "making them just slightly more dangerous than bees and wasps." To put this in perspective, it adds that over 1,300 people were killed in road accidents in Australia in 2025. (Staying safe around wildlife)

A 2023 comparison, used in the Queensland Parliament, gives a sense of scale: accidental falls 4,105 deaths, drowning 204, sharks 5, snakes 2, saltwater crocodiles 1.

Crocodile attacks do happen, mostly when people go into the water, fish from the bank or swim at night in known habitat. Box jellyfish venom can act very fast: official pages tell swimmers never to enter a closed beach, and to use stinger nets or a full-body suit in the north. Funnel-web antivenom exists; so does redback antivenom. The first reflex is still 000.

Two other risks kill more visitors than any snake: the sun, and the ocean itself (rips, not only sharks). Long empty roads, fatigue and animals on the bitumen are part of the same picture: Australia is beautiful, and it is not a theme park.

Aboriginal rock paintings, like the hunter and the kangaroo at Anbangbang in Kakadu, remind us that people have lived with these animals for tens of thousands of years. Respect for Country, and for local knowledge, is also a safety rule.

So the question is not "Are there dangerous animals?" The question is: Would this stop you from going? What would you need to know, and to do, to go anyway?

  1. ★★ Compare the risk from snakes or sharks with the risk from the roads, in your own words.
  2. ★★ Why do official sites insist on flags, nets and signs, if deaths are rare?
  3. ★★ Would this stop you from going? Give a reasoned answer, with one fact from the texts.

Discussion EOI

In pairs, then as a class: "Would this stop you from going?" One student is worried (or plays a worried parent). The other answers with facts and with safety advice, not with jokes. Useful phrases: I understand why you're afraid, but..., According to..., The official advice is..., I would still go if..., I would not go because...

Trace écrite

Some Australian animals can kill, but deaths are rare. I call 000, I follow official advice, I swim between the flags, and I stay away from the water in Croc Country. The sun and the roads are serious risks too.

Copiez cette trace dans votre cahier. Gardez un exemple personnel.

Travail à la maison

Write 8 to 10 lines to a worried parent: three official facts, two safety rules, and your decision (I would go / I would not go) with one reason. Bring it for séance 5.
