21. from 12:57According to the engineer, what is « the biggest problem with supersonic transport »?

Answer 21

22. about 14:00 to 14:21What is a delta wing good for? What is its main drawback for an airliner?

Answer 22

It is great for supersonic flight (streamlined, like a dart). But it does not create enough lift at low speed, so taking off and landing on a conventional runway is a problem: otherwise « it's useless ».

23. 14:46 to 15:19What are vortices? Why are they usually « bad news »? How did the designers use them?

Answer 23

Tiny whirlwinds, great spirals of air. They usually create drag. In the right conditions they create extra lift, so the designers enlarged them and directed them over the surface of the wing. « This was utterly revolutionary » (15:47).

Grammar lesson: purpose and means

To explain how engineers solved a technical problem and why Kennedy wanted an American SST, we need purpose and means
For the teacher: how to run it (8 to 10 minutes)
  1. Ask: why did engineers pump fuel between tanks? How did they solve the problem? Write “why?” and “how?” on the board.
  2. Board: purpose, to / in order to + verb, so that + subject + can / could; means, by + -ing. Students copy.
  3. Show the passive for processes: fuel was transferred, the wing was designed.
  4. Quick check, then exercises.
On the board, to copy

Rule

To give a purpose (why?): to or in order to + base verb; so that + subject + can / could when the subject changes. To give the means (how?): by + verb-ing. In technical explanations, the passive (be + past participle) describes the process.

Form

  • to / in order to + verb: in order to move the centre of gravity
  • so that + subject + could: so that the plane could stay balanced
  • by + -ing: by pumping fuel between tanks
  • passive for a process: Fuel was transferred between tanks.

Use

  • To explain a technical solution.
  • To present a project and its aims.
  • In the technical task cards.

Examples from the lesson

  • “Fuel was transferred between tanks during the flight in order to move the centre of gravity.”
  • “They enlarged the vortices so that the wing could ...”
  • “The wing was designed in order to ...”

Common mistakes

  • Not for move the fuel, but to move the fuel
  • Not by change the wing, but by changing the wing
  • Not so that to balance the plane, but so that the plane could stay balanced / to balance the plane

To say it: purpose and means

To explain how engineers solved a technical problem and why Kennedy wanted an American SST

Purpose (why): to / in order to + verb; so that + subject + can / could. Fuel was transferred between tanks during the flight in order to move the centre of gravity.

Means (how): by + -ing. Engineers balanced the plane by moving fuel between tanks.

Careful: never for to move; for + noun only (for prestige).

Figures of speech in the documents

Metaphor and hyperbole in a political speech

Kennedy wanted the USA to « dominate the skies »; « The day Concorde flew into the history books »

Dominate the skies is a metaphor of conquest: the sky becomes a territory, and the plane a weapon of prestige. Flew into the history books is another metaphor: the plane flies into the pages of history. Political speech often uses such images to make technical projects heroic.

Show a model answer

The problem was balance: the plane had to stay stable at low speed and at Mach 2. Engineers solved it by moving fuel between tanks during the flight, in order to shift the centre of gravity. The American design was even more radical and enormous, but Concorde flew into the history books first.

Bac pro Aéronautique · technical task cards

Explain a process at C1: clear steps, passive voice, linkers of purpose.

Avionics

Electrical flight controls

Concorde had electrical flight controls: the pilot's commands were sent to the control surfaces as electrical signals (Airbus, « The day Concorde flew into the history books »). Explain the difference with mechanical controls (cables and rods) in about 100 words, and give one advantage and one risk.

Expected answer

With mechanical controls, the pilot's movement is transmitted by cables and rods to the control surfaces. With electrical controls, the movement is turned into an electrical signal sent by wire to the actuators, which move the surfaces. Advantage: lighter, more precise. Risk: it depends on electrical power and wiring, so systems must be duplicated (redundancy).

Systems

Moving the centre of gravity with fuel

On Concorde, fuel was transferred between tanks during the flight to move the centre of gravity (Airbus). Explain why the centre of gravity must move when a plane goes from subsonic to supersonic flight, and describe the process in four steps.

Expected answer

When the plane goes supersonic, the centre of lift moves backwards. To keep the aircraft balanced without extra drag from the control surfaces, the centre of gravity is moved backwards too. Steps: the flight engineer selects the transfer; fuel is pumped from the front tanks to the rear tank; the centre of gravity moves back; before descent, fuel is pumped forward again.

Structure

A wing drawn by hand

All the drawings were done on drawing boards (11:38). Write a short procedure (five steps, imperative or passive) for checking a technical drawing before it is sent to the workshop: title block, units, tolerances, revision, signature.

Expected answer

1. Check the title block: part number, title, scale. 2. Check that all dimensions are in the same unit system. 3. Check the tolerances on each dimension. 4. Check that the latest revision is used. 5. The drawing is signed and dated by the checker before release.
