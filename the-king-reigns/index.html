Road map

Lessons of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 18 lessons: each of the eight sessions is taught in two lessons (documents first, then language and expression), and the final task takes two lessons.

  1. Lesson 1 · Who's in charge? Documents and powers 52 min

    Name the three actors of British politics and what each one can do, from two official documents.

    • 5 min · Marked recap (3 points)
    • 12 min · Monet's Westminster as a landmark, then Document A in pairs
    • 10 min · Document B and the class grid: King, Prime Minister, Parliament
    • 5 min · Key words
    • 12 min · Exercise: who does what?
    • 8 min · Written record and homework
    • Written record. In the UK, the King is the Head of State and stays impartial; the Prime Minister leads the Government; MPs are elected and make the laws.
    • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student gives one power for the class grid (the King, the Prime Minister or MPs).
    • Homework for next lesson. Make one bristol flashcard for each of the six key words (method on the start page) and learn them.

    Stopping point. End of lesson 1, after the exercise. Next time: modals of power and the 45-second oral.

    Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short, stop at the class grid (the exercise becomes homework). In the last 8 minutes: dictate the written record (In the UK, the King is the Head of State and stays impartial; the Prime Minister leads the Government; MPs are elected and make the laws); a quick round of the class, one power for the grid each, so that everyone has spoken at least once; write the homework on the board (Make one bristol flashcard for each of the six key words (method on the start page) and learn them).

The King Reigns, Parliament Rules: From Magna Carta to Downing Street

Key question
Who really holds power in the United Kingdom today, and how did the King lose it?
Classes
Terminale (main) and Première, general and technological lycée. Tronc commun: B1+ towards B2 in Première, B2 in Terminale. Spécialité LLCER: towards C1, through the C1 extensions.
Curriculum
French national curriculum of 29 May 2025. Terminale: theme 6, The United Kingdom and its nations (study topic: a Kingdom still united?), with theme 5, Citizenship and virtual worlds, and the study topic on political speech in theme 4. Première: theme 3, Art and power.
Organisation
Eight sessions, each taught in two lessons of 55 minutes timed for 52 minutes (16 lessons), plus two lessons for the final task. Each session opens with a marked recap and ends with Homework. In every session, the language comes from what students need to say: words in action, one grammar point with its rule, figures of speech found in the documents, and an expression task, each with entry, core and stretch tiers. Answers are printed under each exercise.
Final task
A three-minute speech as a new MP, or an op-ed of 250 to 300 words, with an assessment grid.
Adapted version
Every page exists in an adapted version for dyslexic students: key words first, short sentences, audio, sentence starters.

Then and now. In 1903 Monet painted the new Palace of Westminster as a dark silhouette against the sunset. More than a century later the same towers still stand for Parliament, and journalists still say "Westminster" when they mean the MPs inside.

The eight sessions

  1. Session 1. Who's in charge? The King, the Prime Minister and Parliament. Who has the real power in the United Kingdom: the King, the Prime Minister or Parliament?
  2. Session 2. Runnymede, 15 June 1215: the barons against King John. Why did a king accept to put limits on his own power?
  3. Session 3. Magna Carta's long life: from England to the world. Why does a medieval charter still matter today?
  4. Session 4. 1689: the Bill of Rights and the end of absolute monarchy. How did Parliament become stronger than the King?
  5. Session 5. The King reigns but does not rule. If the King has no real power, what is he for?
  6. Session 6. Parliament: the Commons and the Lords. How does a bill become a law in Westminster?
  7. Session 7. Number 10: the Prime Minister at the centre. How does someone become Prime Minister, and how can they lose the job?
  8. Session 8. Citizens decide: four nations, a referendum and votes at sixteen. Who has a voice in British democracy, and is the United Kingdom still united?
  9. Final task and assessment. Final task: From Runnymede to Westminster. Speech or op-ed, with the assessment grid
  10. Sources and images. Every fact and every painting, with its link. Checked on 2 November 2026

How to use this sequence

  • Start each session with the marked recap: five minutes, alone, then check together.
  • The documents come from official sources (UK Parliament, GOV.UK, the Royal Family, legislation.gov.uk, the National Archives, the Electoral Commission). Each one has its link.
  • Answers, model answers and quiz keys are printed under each exercise, so the page can be projected or printed as it is.
  • Homework closes every session and prepares the next recap.
  • The adapted version keeps the same lessons and the same final task.

Teacher notes

My bristol flashcards · how to use them

Principle. Each student makes their own cards by hand, on bristol cards. Writing the word is already a way of learning it. The pack stays in the bag, so students can revise anywhere, with no screen. There is no ready-made deck: this page gives the method, a few model cards and a blank template.

Materials

A7 bristol cards (74 × 105 mm), about 25 per student, or A6 bristols cut in half. Felt-tip pens in four colours. An elastic band or an envelope for the pack. A small cardboard box with three compartments for practice (a shoe box with two dividers is enough). You can also print the blank template and cut it out.

What goes on a card

What to write
FrontOne English word, phrase, date or structure, in the colour of its category.
BackA short meaning in English (a French translation only if you need it) and one example sentence of your own, linked to The King Reigns.
CornerThe category and the session where you met the item.
CheckCan I give the meaning and an example without turning the card over?

Practice in pairs, with three boxes

Student A shows the front; student B gives the meaning and a sentence. A card that is right moves on one box. A card that is wrong goes back to box 1. Target before the final task: 12 cards in box 3.

Model cards

What to put on cards

Choose the items you still hesitate on. One item per card. Do not copy the whole list.

  • Vocabulary: Head of State, impartial, to appoint, charter, to seal, the law of the land, due process, consent, standing army, to reign, to rule, dignified, efficient, Royal Assent, constituency, first past the post, hereditary peer, to command the confidence of, by-election, turnout, devolution, to register
  • Dates: 1215, 1297, 1689, 1708, 1834, 2014, 2016, 4 July 2024, 18 March 2026, 20 July 2026
  • Grammar: modals of power; past simple and time connectors; the passive; no longer, without, unless; contrast and concession; conditionals 0, 1 and 2; should and comparatives
  • Figures of speech and linkers: metonymy, accumulation, antonomasia, anaphora, antithesis, metaphor, rhetorical question; whereas, although, however

Going further in Terminale

Who Rules Britain? Sovereignty, Crown and People is the advanced Terminale version of this sequence (tronc commun B2, with C1 tasks; a good bridge towards the spécialité LLCER, towards C1). It does not repeat these lessons: it starts from what students learnt here (Magna Carta, 1689, Bagehot, Royal Assent, the 2016 referendum, votes at sixteen) and takes them further, with unadapted primary sources, Dicey, the 2019 prorogation case, the 2022 Scottish referendum ruling, a mid-sequence test and a written exam.

A further sequence in Terminale, The Queen Who Had to Be Seen: Elizabeth II, Image and Power, starts from Bagehot’s dignified power (already studied here, especially in lesson 9) and asks how Elizabeth II used her image as its instrument. It does not repeat the two-column sort, the reign-or-rule exercise, or the extract read here.
