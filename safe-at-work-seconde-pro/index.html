1. Reading: the four colours

CE

British workplace signs follow the Safety Signs and Signals Regulations 1996, the same European colour system:

Colour and shapeMeaning
Blue, circleYou must do it (mandatory): wear ear protection, wear gloves.
Red, circle with a lineIt is forbidden (prohibition): no smoking, no entry.
Yellow, triangleBe careful (warning): hot surface, slippery floor, risk of falling.
Green, rectangle or squareSafe condition: fire exit, first aid, assembly point.

Corrigé (prof), the four colours. Blue = must; red = forbidden; yellow = be careful; green = fire exit and first aid. A warning sign is a triangle.

2. Listening: First Day

CO

A supervisor shows the signs to a new worker on the first day. First listening: who, where? Second listening: which sign is blue, red, yellow, green in this workplace?

Corrigé (prof), First Day. The supervisor explains the signs in the workshop. Students identify at least one mandatory, one prohibition, one warning and one safe-condition sign from the dialogue.

3. Explain a sign, in pairs

PI

Student A describes a sign (colour, shape, meaning). Student B repeats the rule with must or mustn't, then they swap.

Written record

Safety signs use four colours. ______ means you must do it. Red means it is ______. Yellow means be ______. Green shows the fire ______ and first aid. We say: You ______ wear gloves. You ______ smoke.

Word bank: Blue · forbidden · careful · exit · must · mustn't

Corrigé (prof), written record. Blue, forbidden, careful, exit, must, mustn't.

Plan B

Today's question: Why do safety signs use only a few colours and shapes?

Copy the four colours and the written record. Then write two rules: You must... You mustn't...

Homework

Make four flashcards. Write 4 rules for a workplace in your field (real or invented): 2 with must, 2 with mustn't.

Corrigé (prof), four rules. Model (kitchen): You must wash your hands before cooking. You must tie your hair back. You mustn't smoke. You mustn't leave a knife in the sink.

Récapituler la séance

Compulsory and marked, out of 4. A short recap of today's lesson, to be handed in.

  1. What does a blue circular sign mean?
  2. What shape is a warning sign, and what colour is it?
  3. Translate into English: une Plan B.
  4. Write one rule with must and one with mustn't for a workplace you know or invent.

Corrigé (prof), Recapitulate lesson 1. 1. You must do it (mandatory). 2. A yellow triangle. 3. A fire exit. 4. Accept accurate rules with must / mustn't + base verb.

Étude de la langue · must, mustn't and imperatives

Palier 1. must and mustn't

Must + base verb: You must wear gloves. No -s. Mustn't = it is forbidden: You mustn't smoke. The imperative is also used on signs: Wear ear protection. Don't enter.

Complete: You ___ wear safety boots. You ___ use your phone near the machine.

Corrigé (prof), palier 1. must; mustn't.

Palier 2. From a sign to a sentence

Turn these signs into sentences: No entry. Wear goggles. Slippery floor.

Corrigé (prof), palier 2. You mustn't enter / No entry: you must not go in. You must wear goggles. Be careful, the floor is slippery.

Palier 3. Four rules of your own

Write two rules with must and two with mustn't, for a workplace of your field.

Corrigé (prof), palier 3. See the homework model.

French corner

a sign = un panneau · a warning = un avertissement · a fire exit = une Plan B · the assembly point = le point de rassemblement · goggles = des lunettes de protection · safety boots = des chaussures de sécurité · jewellery = les bijoux.

1. Reading: Before Every Shift

CE

BEFORE EVERY SHIFT

  1. Take off your watch, rings and bracelets.
  2. Put on a clean uniform and an apron. Tie long hair back or wear a hat.
  3. Wash your hands with soap and warm water for at least 20 seconds. Dry them with a paper towel.
  4. Cover any cut with a blue plaster.
  5. Check the fridge temperature and write it on the sheet.
  6. Feeling ill? Tell the chef before you start.

Source: routine written for the class. The kitchen is invented; the steps follow common food hygiene practice.

Corrigé (prof), the routine. Hands: at least 20 seconds, soap and warm water. Cuts: a blue plaster (so it can be seen if it falls into food). Ill: tell the chef before you start. Jewellery and watches come off.

2. Listening: The Inspection

CO

A hotel kitchen in Glasgow, 7 a.m. Chef Rosa talks to her team. Kevin is a young commis chef. The inspector is coming, but they do not change anything, because they do the same thing every day. Kevin asks why the plasters are blue.

Corrigé (prof), The Inspection. They keep the same routine every day. Plasters are blue so that they can be seen if they fall into the food. The team always washes hands, checks fridges, and tells the chef if someone is ill.

3. Ask how often, in pairs

PI

How often do you wash your hands / check a machine / wear gloves? Always, usually, often, sometimes, never. Then write five sentences about your workplace routine.

Written record

In a professional kitchen, we ______ wash our hands before every shift. We check the fridges ______ a day. Plasters are ______ so that we can see them. We ______ come to work if we are ill: we have to tell the chef. Have to means it is necessary; don't have to means it is not necessary.

Corrigé (prof), written record. always; twice (or the figure given in class); blue; mustn't / can't / don't. Have to = necessary; don't have to = not necessary (not the same as mustn't).

Plan B

Today's question: Why do professionals repeat the same hygiene routine every day?

Copy: always, twice, blue, don't have to, must. Write three sentences about a routine in your field.

Homework

Write your workshop routine in five sentences, with at least two adverbs of frequency and one have to.

Corrigé (prof), the routine. Five present-simple sentences, with frequency adverbs in the correct place (before the main verb, after be), and one have to + base verb.

Récapituler la séance

Compulsory and marked, out of 4. A short recap of today's lesson, to be handed in.

  1. How long must you wash your hands before a shift?
  2. Why are plasters blue in a kitchen?
  3. Complete: We ___ (check) the fridges twice a day. You ___ wear a hat in the staff room (not necessary).
  4. Write two sentences: one with always, one with never, about a workplace.

Corrigé (prof), Recapitulate lesson 2. 1. At least 20 seconds. 2. So that they can be seen if they fall into the food. 3. check (or checks if the subject is he/she); don't have to. 4. Accept accurate present-simple sentences.

Étude de la langue · present simple, adverbs of frequency, have to

Palier 1. The present simple for routines

We check the fridges twice a day. He checks (he / she / it + s). Questions: How often do you...?

Corrigé (prof), palier 1. Do / does + base verb in questions; -s with he, she, it.

Palier 2. always, usually, often, sometimes, never

The adverb goes before the main verb: We always wash our hands. After be: The plasters are always blue.

Complete: We ___ run in the kitchen. (0%) She ___ (wash) her hands before a shift. (100%)

Corrigé (prof), palier 2. never; always washes.

Palier 3. have to and don't have to

Have to = it is necessary. Don't have to = it is not necessary (not the same as mustn't, which is forbidden). Write one sentence of each about a workplace.

Corrigé (prof), palier 3. Example: We have to wear safety boots. We don't have to wear a hat in the staff room.

French corner

a shift = une prise de poste, un service · to wash = se laver, laver · a plaster = un pansement · a fridge = un réfrigérateur · to check = vérifier · ill = malade (here: unfit to handle food).

1. Reading: accidents at work in Great Britain

CE

In 2024/25, 680,000 workers had an injury at work, according to a national survey. Employers reported 59,219 injuries to the HSE. Here are the most common kinds:

  • Slips, trips or falls on the same level: 30%
  • Handling, lifting or carrying: 17%
  • Struck by a moving object: 10%
  • Acts of violence: 10%
  • Falls from a height: 8%

In the same year, 511,000 workers had a musculoskeletal disorder: a problem with their back, neck, arms or legs caused or made worse by work.

Source: figures from the Health and Safety Executive (HSE), summary statistics for Great Britain and kinds of accident statistics (RIDDOR, 2024/25, provisional figures). Text written for the class from these figures.

Say in words: 17%; 680,000. Which kind of accident is the most common? What is a musculoskeletal disorder?

Corrigé (prof), HSE figures. 17% = seventeen per cent; 680,000 = six hundred and eighty thousand. The most common kind: slips, trips or falls on the same level (30%). Handling, lifting or carrying: 17%. A musculoskeletal disorder is a problem with the back, neck, arms or legs caused or made worse by work.

1. Reading: the 1974 Act, two short extracts

The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act is the main safety law in Great Britain. It was passed in 1974 and is still in force. Short extracts, punctuation slightly simplified for the class. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0, legislation.gov.uk.

Section 2 (1): "It shall be the duty of every employer to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health, safety and welfare at work of all his employees."

Section 7: "It shall be the duty of every employee while at work: (a) to take reasonable care for the health and safety of himself and of other persons who may be affected by his acts or omissions at work; and (b) to co-operate with him [the employer] so far as is necessary."

Transcript (Teacher version seulement)

Corrigé (Teacher version). Le tuteur rappelle de lire les panneaux : rouge, interdiction ; jaune, avertissement ; bleu, obligation (gants, chaussures, se laver les mains). Reading, CE

Texte d'entraînement (le jour du CCF, prendre un document authentique inconnu de 15 lignes au plus) :

> Safety at work: whose job is it? Signs are a language. If you can read them, you already protect yourself and your team. Hygiene, in a kitchen or in a workshop, is part of the same language: clean hands, clean tools. Lifting right is not a detail; backs are not replaced at the end of PFMP. When something is wrong, silence is dangerous. The tutor is responsible, the company is responsible, and you are responsible too, in the limit of your role. A welcome to the workshop should say that clearly, in simple English, the first day.

Le tuteur, l'entreprise et l'élève sont responsables, chacun dans son rôle. L'accueil du premier jour doit le dire, en anglais simple.
