Lesson 2. Congress and the President

Goal: I can explain one limit on the president’s power.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 2 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Split the lesson into two blocks: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Say one sentence from the previous lesson (Lesson 1: what you already know about elections) 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Read the goal and the word box 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Read or listen to your route’s text 8 min (minutes 8 to 16)
  4. Exercise 1, then check it 8 min (minutes 16 to 24)
  5. Pause: look away from the text or move 2 min (minutes 24 to 26)
  6. Exercise 2, then check it 8 min (minutes 26 to 34)
  7. Exercise 3, then check it 6 min (minutes 34 to 40)
  8. Say your final answer aloud with the speaking frame 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  9. Written record 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  10. Progress record 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  11. Read and write down the homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. One sentence for your route (see below), copied or typed.
  • Participation. Say at least one answer aloud. In class, use a quiet voice; at home, say it to yourself. You do not need to record yourself.
  • Homework for next lesson. Make cards for bill and approve. Review your Lesson 1 cards.

Stopping point. Minute 44: stop the main exercises. Keep the written record and the homework, even if an exercise is unfinished.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: say the emergency sentence of the lesson aloud.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: complete the short written record.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: write down the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: check one answer with the key. Do not rush through all the unfinished exercises.

Choose your route: Route S · SEGPA · Route C · CAP · Route P · Bac pro · CFA. Do only your route.

Route S · SEGPA

Short text

The United States has a president.

It also has Congress.

Congress has two chambers.

They are the House and the Senate.

The president does not make every decision alone.

Exercise S1

Choose

Worked example. “How many chambers does Congress have?” Answer: two.

Now you.
1. Congress has one / two chambers.
2. The chambers are the House and the Senate / the courts.

Self-check

1. two.
2. the House and the Senate.

Exercise S2

Complete

Worked example. “The United States has a .” Answer: president.

Now you.
Complete: “Congress has the and the .”

Self-check

House; Senate.

Exercise S3

Say a fact

Worked example. “The president does not decide everything alone.”

Now you.
Say two sentences: “Congress has .” “The president does not .”

Self-check

“Congress has two chambers.” “The president does not make every decision alone.”

Route C · CAP

Short text

Congress has the House and the Senate.

Both chambers generally need to approve the same bill.

The bill is then presented to the president.

The president may veto it.

The president therefore needs to work with Congress.

An election can change who has power in Congress.

Exercise C1

Order

Worked example. First, a bill is proposed.

Now you.
Put these steps in order.
A. The bill is presented to the president.
B. Both chambers approve the same bill.

Self-check

B, then A.

Exercise C2

Complete

Worked example. “A bill is a proposed .” Answer: law.

Now you.
Use: Congress, veto.
1. The president may a bill.
2. The president needs to work with .

Self-check

1. veto.
2. Congress.

Exercise C3

Explain

Worked example. “The president cannot decide everything alone because Congress has powers too.”

Now you.
Write two or three sentences explaining this.

Self-check

Mention the two chambers and the approval of a bill. Do not say that the president alone makes every law.

Route P · Bac pro

Short text

Midterm elections can change the balance of power in Congress.

They do not directly elect a new president.

Congress has two chambers.

Ordinary federal laws generally need both chambers to approve the same bill.

The bill is then presented to the president.

The president may veto it.

A president may therefore need to negotiate, even after winning a presidential election.

Exercise P1

Correct

Worked example. “Midterms directly elect a new president.” Correction: “Midterms do not directly elect a new president.”

Now you.
Correct: “The president’s proposals automatically become law.”

Self-check

“The president’s proposals do not automatically become law. Congress has a role in approving legislation.”

Exercise P2

Passive voice

Worked example. “Both chambers approve the bill.” becomes “The bill is approved by both chambers.”

Now you.
Transform: “Both chambers must approve the bill.”

Self-check

“The bill must be approved by both chambers.”

Exercise P3

Explain to someone

Worked example. Opening: “Winning the presidency does not remove the powers of Congress.”

Now you.
Write 60 to 80 words for a student who thinks the president decides everything.

Self-check

Include the House and the Senate, the approval of a bill and one reason why negotiation may be necessary.

CFA pathway

CAP apprentice: use Route C. Bac pro apprentice: use Route P. Do Exercises 1 and 2 of your route, then do this task instead of Exercise 3. Keep the same written record and homework.

CFA task

Explain responsibilities

Worked example. “At work, different people can have different responsibilities.”

Now you.
Explain why “the president decides everything” is misleading. Starter: “Congress also has .”

Self-check

Mention the powers of Congress. Do not claim that a workplace and the US government work in the same way.

Written record

S: “Congress has the House and the Senate.”

C: “The president needs to work with Congress.”

P: “Midterms can change the power of Congress without directly electing a new president.”

Speaking frame. “The president cannot decide everything alone because .”

Emergency sentence. “Congress has powers too.”

Homework

Make cards for bill and approve. Review your Lesson 1 cards.
