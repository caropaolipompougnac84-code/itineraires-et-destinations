Lesson 2. Gloriana: the first Elizabeth’s mask

How did Elizabeth I use a painted face, and a map, to make power visible?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 8 lessons.

  1. Recap (marked /3) 5 min
  2. Five-step analysis of the Ditchley Portrait 20 min
  3. Hayter’s Victoria as a later mask of youth 6 min
  4. Writing, 150 words 14 min
  5. Key points, exit ticket and homework 7 min
  • Written record. The five-step notes on the Ditchley Portrait, and a definition of the mask of youth.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: one symbol in the Ditchley Portrait, or one sentence of the 150-word argument.
  • Homework for next lesson. Find one widely shared image of Elizabeth II in old age (give the link, do not copy it). Was it controlled or not? Write 8 to 10 lines.

Stopping point. End of this lesson.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson: dictate a short written record (two sentences); a quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each); write the homework on the board.

You already know the idea of dignified power from lesson 1, and, if you have studied the constitution sequences, that ceremony can hold a state together. What is new here: a five-step analysis of a public-domain royal portrait, and the concept of the mask of youth. The Armada Portrait will appear in the mid-sequence assessment as an unseen document: we do not analyse its two windows in this lesson.

Recap · marked /3
  1. Name the five steps of the portrait method.
  2. What does “dignified power” mean, in one sentence?
  3. Who commissioned the 1933 portrait of Princess Elizabeth, and why does that matter?

Answer key

  1. Identify, describe, read the symbols, interpret, reception.
  2. Power that works by winning reverence and loyalty, not by taking day-to-day decisions.
  3. The Duke of York, later George VI; it shows a daughter who was not then expected to reign.

In groups, analyse the Ditchley Portrait with the five-step method. Notice the map under her feet, and the storm and sunlight behind her. Concept: the mask of youth. Elizabeth I’s ageing face was not shown: official portraits followed approved face patterns. Hayter’s Victoria, more than two centuries later, still offers a young, official body to the public.

Writing (B2), 150 words. What political message does the Ditchley Portrait convey? Use at least one concession and two words from the method (for example idealised, iconic, to convey).

Answer key · key points

  • Standing on the map: the Queen literally embodies the kingdom.
  • Light and storm: she brings peace and order out of danger.
  • The idealised face: the body of the monarch must not age, because the State must not weaken.
Exit ticket
  1. What is the mask of youth?
  2. Give one symbol in the Ditchley Portrait and say what it conveys.

Answer key

  1. An official, ageless, approved face that hides the monarch’s real ageing.
  2. Accept: the map (she rules and protects the land); storm and sunlight (danger and peace, or providence).

Homework

Look for one image of Elizabeth II in old age that was widely shared (give the link, do not copy the picture). Was it controlled or not? Write 8 to 10 lines, using the method.
