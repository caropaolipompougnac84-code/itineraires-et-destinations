Lesson 4. Dams Today: Muskrat Falls and Site C

Two recent dams, and the voices that objected to them.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Eight lessons in this sequence.

  1. Recap of lesson 3 0 to 4
  2. Goal and word box 4 to 8
  3. Muskrat Falls, Labrador, and Billy Gauthier 8 to 24
  4. Site C, British Columbia 24 to 36
  5. A one-minute news flash 36 to 44
  6. Written record and homework 44 to 52
  • Written record. One fact with its source about each dam, and one voice that objected.
  • Participation. Each student speaks in the news flash, or says one sentence as a reporter.
  • Homework for next lesson. A paragraph of 120 words: is hydroelectricity “clean” energy for everyone? Use one causative structure and one complex comparison.

Stopping point. Minute 44, after the news flash.

Emergency exit · 8 min. See the box at the end of the lesson.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

Remember lesson 3.

  1. What happens to the fish when a reservoir is created?
  2. Give one positive and one negative effect of the James Bay agreement.

Answer key

  • Mercury levels can rise in the fish.
  • Positive: economic development, Cree institutions, funding for education. Negative: flooded land, relocation, mercury.

8 to 24 min

Case 1: Muskrat Falls, Labrador

What? An 824-megawatt hydroelectric project on the lower Churchill River, about 30 km west of Happy Valley-Goose Bay, built by Nalcor Energy, a Crown corporation of Newfoundland and Labrador.

The fear. Flooding a 41 km² reservoir can produce methylmercury, which could flow downstream to Lake Melville, where many Inuit live and fish. The Nunatsiavut Government started “Make Muskrat Right”. A study with Harvard scientists said that methylmercury could rise by up to 380 per cent in Lake Melville if the reservoir was only partly cleared. Nalcor agreed to remove the trees, but not the soil.

October 2016. The Inuk artist Billy Gauthier and two other people started a hunger strike. Protesters said they did not oppose the project itself: they wanted it done “right”.

The other side. In 2011, the Innu Nation signed the Tshash Petapen, or New Dawn, agreements with the governments, including an impact and benefits agreement for the lower Churchill project.

Optional video. Emotional hunger striker protesting Muskrat Falls. CBC News NL · YouTube, 18 October 2016 · 2 min 40. The questions on this page can also be answered from the short text beside them, if you cannot play the video. Open on YouTube.

  1. How does Billy Gauthier feel after five days, and what is he eating or drinking?
  2. What would make him stop?
  3. Why did he tell his daughter that he was doing it for her?
  4. How does he define bravery?

Answer key

  • He feels “okay”, “strong” and “confident”. Nothing but water.
  • That they clear the vegetation, so that the people of Labrador can safely practise their traditional way of life.
  • Her favourite activity when she visits him is fishing: he wants future generations to keep fishing and living on the land.
  • “That’s what bravery is: when you’re scared and you do it anyway.”

Sources: CBC News, Battle over Muskrat Falls, 27 October 2016 · Government of Newfoundland and Labrador, A New Dawn for the Labrador Innu, 2011

24 to 36 min

Case 2: Site C, British Columbia

What? A dam on the Peace River, built by BC Hydro since 2015. Its sixth and final generating unit came online in August 2025. It cost 16 billion dollars, nearly double its first price, and its reservoir is 83 km long.

Who objected? Treaty 8 First Nations, such as West Moberly. In 2018, West Moberly lost a court bid to stop the project. In 2022, it reached a partial settlement with BC Hydro and the governments, including land and an impact and benefits agreement.

The other side. BC Hydro says that Site C adds eight per cent to the province’s electricity production and can power 500,000 homes with clean energy.

Optional video. B.C. First Nations continue fight against controversial Site C dam. APTN News · YouTube, 26 January 2018 · 2 min 22. The questions on this page can also be answered from the short text beside them, if you cannot play the video. Open on YouTube.

  1. Who is Chief Roland Willson?
  2. What promise did the new provincial government make, according to him, and what happened after the Utilities Commission report?
  3. Is he against all development? What does he propose?

Answer key

  • The chief of West Moberly First Nations.
  • To send the project to the British Columbia Utilities Commission, and to cancel it if the report said it was not a good deal for British Columbia. The report said that it was not a good deal, but the government approved the project anyway: for him, it is a broken promise.
  • No: “we’re not opposed to development”; he is opposed to the unnecessary destruction of the valley. He proposes alternatives: wind, solar and geothermal energy.

Sources: CBC News, Site C fully operational, 9 August 2025 · Government of British Columbia, West Moberly settlement, 27 June 2022

Grammar spotlight

Cause and result: flooding made mercury levels rise. The dam caused the river to flow twice as fast. Comparison: methylmercury could rise by up to 380 per cent. Site C cost nearly double its first price.

36 to 52 min

A one-minute news flash, written record, homework

In groups, prepare a one-minute news flash on Muskrat Falls or on Site C. Give the facts, one figure with its source, and two points of view.

Useful language: Good evening. Tonight, we go to… According to CBC News, … Supporters say that… Opponents say that… The dam made… / caused… to…

Written record. At Muskrat Falls, Inuit campaigners feared methylmercury downstream in Lake Melville (CBC News, 2016). At Site C, a dam that can power 500,000 homes came online in August 2025, after Treaty 8 nations such as West Moberly objected in the name of their treaty rights (CBC News, 2025; B.C. government, 2022).

Homework. Write a paragraph of 120 words: is hydroelectricity “clean” energy for everyone? Use one causative structure and one complex comparison.

Emergency exit · 8 min

If time runs short, replace the unfinished plan with this condensed ending (inside the 52 minutes, not 52 plus 8).

  • 0 to 2. Everyone says this sentence aloud: “A dam can be clean energy for a city, and a loss of land and fish for a nation.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record: Muskrat Falls: fear of methylmercury in Lake Melville. Site C: online in 2025, after Treaty 8 objections.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework: Write 120 words with a causative and a comparison.
  • 7 to 8. Check one answer in the answer key.
