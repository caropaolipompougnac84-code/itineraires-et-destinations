Lesson 7. Young People and Hope

Goal: I can say who speaks in the film and what the trailer shows.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 7 of 8. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Pause when necessary: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Read the goal and find your place. 0 to 4
  2. Read the short text and word meanings. 4 to 12
  3. Complete reading and vocabulary work. 12 to 20
  4. Complete listening or the labelled no-video route. 20 to 30
  5. Complete language practice. 30 to 37
  6. Prepare and say your answer. 37 to 44
  7. Keep the written record. 44 to 49
  8. Check answers and note homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the short written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework at the end of this page, with its worked example and self-check.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: say the subject and one limit.
  • 46 to 49: keep the written record.
  • 49 to 51: note homework.
  • 51 to 52: check Playing together.
Short text

The Grizzlies is a Canadian film.

It is based on a true story.

It concerns young people and a lacrosse team in Kugluktuk.

You watch the whole film at home before this lesson.

Today you watch the trailer and listen to an interview.

A trailer is short. It shows only some moments.

Say where your idea comes from: the film, the trailer or the interview.

The whole film at home

  1. Watch the whole film at home before lesson 7. It is homework.
  2. The film lasts 1 h 42. You can watch it in two parts.
  3. Turn on English subtitles if you can.
  4. Your teacher tells you how to watch it.
  5. The film talks about sad and hard things, including suicide. You can stop. You can talk to an adult you trust.
  6. While you watch, note one person who speaks and one thing they say.

Film viewing worksheet: The Grizzlies

The film viewing worksheet is on its way and will be added here.

Building your vocabulary

  • team: people working or playing together;
  • hope: a feeling that something good is possible;
  • give up: stop trying;
  • support: help;
  • synopsis: a short account of a story;
  • trailer: a short video with some moments from a film.
Exercise 1

Read and choose

Worked example.
What sport is involved?
Lacrosse.

Now choose:

  1. The story concerns a team / a pipeline.
  2. A synopsis is a short account / the whole film.
  3. A trailer shows the whole film. Yes / No.

Self-check

A team; a short account; no.

Exercise 2

Match words

Worked example.
give up → stop trying.

Match:

  1. support;
  2. hope;
  3. team.
  1. people playing or working together;
  2. help;
  3. feeling that something good is possible.

Self-check

1b, 2c, 3a.

Exercise 3

Watch the trailer

Viewing document. THE GRIZZLIES Trailer [HD] Mongrel Media. Mongrel Media · YouTube, 19 February 2019 · 2 min 20. Official trailer of the film. Watch from 0:00 to 2:20. Open on YouTube.

Watch from 0:00 to 2:20. Watch it twice.

Worked example.
Where is the story?
In the Arctic. The trailer says: “Welcome to the edge of the world.”

Watch for:

  1. What sport does the teacher propose? Lacrosse / hockey.
  2. The teacher asks: “You lived up here a long time?” The answer is: “6,000 years.” / “Ten years.”
  3. A student says to the teacher: “This is not about you.” / “Thank you for saving us.”
  4. Complete: “We are proud, strong, full of .”
Support account

The trailer shows a teacher from the south in Kugluktuk. He proposes lacrosse. A parent says: “Family comes first.” A student tells the teacher: “This is not about you.” The team says: “We are proud, strong, full of hope.”

Use it only if you cannot listen. Label your answer “Reading-support route”.

Self-check

Lacrosse; “6,000 years.”; “This is not about you.”; hope.

Exercise 6

Say where your idea comes from

Worked example.

Speaking: your route

Worked example.
The film is about young people and a team. In the trailer, a student says: “This is not about you.”

Route S · SEGPA

Use the Route C sentences below. Say the first sentence aloud. Then say the second one. You may read them from the page, one at a time.

Route C · CAP or CFA

Say:

The film is about young people and a team.

Route P · Bac pro or CFA

Prepare a film recommendation for colleagues:

It shows…

Route G · Lycée général, LVA

Give a short introduction and explain why youth agency matters. Use one voice from the film.

Route G · Lycée général, LVB

Give three linked sentences: the subject, one voice from the film and the message.

Self-check

Say where your idea comes from. Do not claim one story represents all Inuit experiences.

Written record

I watched the film at home. A trailer shows only some moments. I say where my idea comes from: the film, the trailer or the interview.

Homework

Worked example.
One voice: a student tells the teacher, “This is not about you.” This shows that the young people act for themselves.

Write one voice from the film: who speaks, what they say and what it shows.

Self-check

Say who speaks, what they say and what it shows.
