Déroulé de la séance

Point d'arrêt. Fin de l'évaluation et de la séquence Tube.

Issue de secours · 8 min. Si le temps manque à ce point d'arrêt, on condense l'écrit : on dicte une trace courte à recopier dans la copie (deux phrases clés) ; à la remise des copies, une phrase orale chacun si besoin ; le travail à la maison (relire sa copie) est écrit au tableau.

On 20 points.

Part 1. Reading (6 points)

In 1890 the City and South London Railway opened between the City and Stockwell, under the River Thames. It was the first successful deep level tube railway in the world and the first major railway to use electric traction. Its tunnels were dug with a tunnelling shield under the public roads. Its small carriages with high backed seats were nicknamed padded cells. Today it is part of the Northern line.

In 1931, Harry Beck, an employee of the Underground, drew a new map in his spare time. Because the trains run mostly underground, he decided that the real position of the stations did not matter to passengers: only the connections did. He used straight lines, only vertical, horizontal or at 45 degrees, ticks for stations and diamonds for interchanges. His colleagues saw that it looked like an electrical circuit, and Beck once drew a joke version where the Bakerloo line became the Bakerlite line. The map was published in 1933. Beck was paid ten guineas for it.

Sources: Wikipedia, City and South London Railway; Tube map; Harry Beck.

  1. What was new in 1890? (2)
  2. Why did Beck ignore real distances? (2)
  3. What was the Bakerlite line? (2)

Part 2. Lines (4 points)

  1. Give a route from Paddington to Westminster. (2)
  2. Give a route from Heathrow to Piccadilly Circus. (2)

Part 3. Writing (10 points)

Write a whiteboard message and a short paragraph on British humour on the Tube. 100 words.

Key
  1. The first deep level tube and electric traction.
  2. Underground, only the connections matter to passengers.
  3. A joke version of the map with electrical symbols.
  1. The Circle line, direct.
  2. The Piccadilly line, direct.
