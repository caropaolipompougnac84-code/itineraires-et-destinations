Lesson 2. 1969: the White Paper and the Red Paper

Equal citizens, or Citizens Plus?

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Eight lessons in this sequence.

  1. Recap of lesson 1 0 to 4
  2. Goal and word box 4 to 8
  3. Read: the White Paper, June 1969 8 to 22
  4. Harold Cardinal and The Unjust Society 22 to 30
  5. The Red Paper, 1970, and was going to 30 to 40
  6. Debate in pairs, then written record 40 to 48
  7. Homework 48 to 52
  • Written record. What the White Paper was going to do, and why the Red Paper said “Citizens Plus”.
  • Participation. In the debate, each student speaks for about one minute, then says which argument convinces them more.
  • Homework for next lesson. In 80 words, explain why First Nations rejected the White Paper. Use was going to once and said that it would once. Read part A of the language page.

Stopping point. Minute 40, before the debate. If time runs short, each student says one sentence for or against, then the written record.

Emergency exit · 8 min. See the box at the end of the lesson.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

Remember lesson 1.

  1. Name the three Indigenous peoples recognised by the Constitution.
  2. What is unceded land?

Answer key

  • First Nations, Inuit and Métis.
  • Land that was never given up by treaty.

4 to 8 min

Today’s goal

I can explain what the White Paper was going to do, and why the Red Paper answered “Citizens Plus”.

8 to 22 min

Read: the White Paper, June 1969

In June 1969, the government of Prime Minister Pierre Elliott Trudeau published a Statement of the Government of Canada on Indian Policy, known as the White Paper. It was presented by the Minister of Indian Affairs, Jean Chrétien.

The government said that it wanted equality: First Nations people should be citizens like all other Canadians. So it was going to repeal the Indian Act, abolish Indian status, give its responsibility for First Nations to the provinces, and transfer the control of reserve lands to individuals.

“The separate legal status of Indians and the policies which have flowed from it have kept the Indian people apart from and behind other Canadians.”

Statement of the Government of Canada on Indian Policy, 1969, quoted by History of Rights.

  1. Who published the White Paper, and when?
  2. What was the government going to do? Find four measures.
  3. Why did the government think that this was a good idea?
  4. What would happen to the collective ownership of reserve lands?

Answer key

  • The federal government of Pierre Elliott Trudeau, in June 1969. The minister Jean Chrétien presented it.
  • Repeal the Indian Act, abolish Indian status, transfer responsibility to the provinces, transfer control of lands to individuals.
  • It said that the separate status had kept First Nations people “apart from and behind” other Canadians: it wanted equality.
  • It would disappear: land would belong to individuals, not to the nation.

Sources: History of Rights, 1969 White Paper on Indian Policy · The Canadian Encyclopedia, The White Paper, 1969

22 to 30 min

Harold Cardinal and The Unjust Society

Optional video. Harold Cardinal | National Aboriginal History Month. APTN News · YouTube, 19 June 2017 · 1 min 04. The questions on this page can also be answered from the short text beside them, if you cannot play the video. Open on YouTube.

  1. How old was Harold Cardinal when he became the leader of the Indian Association of Alberta?
  2. Pierre Elliott Trudeau spoke of “A Just Society”. What was the title of Harold Cardinal’s book?
  3. Why is this title a play on words?

Answer key

  • He was 23, in 1968.
  • The Unjust Society.
  • He turns Trudeau’s words against him: for First Nations, the White Paper was not just, it was unjust.

30 to 40 min

Read: the Red Paper, 1970

In 1970, the Indian Chiefs of Alberta answered with Citizens Plus, known as the Red Paper. They said that First Nations were citizens, plus they had rights from the treaties and from their history. For them, the White Paper “offers despair instead of hope”.

The Red Paper played a major role in the decision of the government to withdraw the White Paper in 1970. The debate also made the Indigenous rights movement stronger: four national Indigenous associations appeared after it.

  1. What does Citizens Plus mean?
  2. What happened to the White Paper?
  3. Why can we say that the White Paper had an effect opposite to what the government wanted?

Answer key

  • First Nations people are citizens, and they also have special rights from the treaties.
  • It was withdrawn in 1970.
  • The government wanted to end Indian status, but the White Paper made First Nations organise and defend their rights.

Grammar spotlight

A promise that was not kept: the government was going to repeal the Indian Act, but it withdrew the White Paper. In reported speech, will becomes would: the government said that it would give First Nations equality. See the language page, part A.

40 to 48 min · stopping point before the debate if need be

Debate in pairs, then the written record

Student A defends the argument of the White Paper (1969). Student B defends the argument of the Red Paper (1970). Speak for one minute each, then answer your partner. Then say which argument convinces you more, and why.

Useful language: In 1969, the government said that… The chiefs answered that… Treating everyone the same is not always fair, because… Although…, …

Written record. In 1969 the government was going to repeal the Indian Act and abolish Indian status. The Red Paper answered that First Nations were Citizens Plus: citizens with treaty rights. The White Paper was withdrawn in 1970.

Emergency exit · 8 min

If time runs short, replace the unfinished plan with this condensed ending (inside the 52 minutes, not 52 plus 8).

  • 0 to 2. Everyone says this sentence aloud: “The government was going to abolish Indian status, but First Nations answered: Citizens Plus.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record: The White Paper of 1969 was withdrawn in 1970 after the Red Paper.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework: Write 80 words with was going to and said that it would.
  • 7 to 8. Check one answer in the answer key.
