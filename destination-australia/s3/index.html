Séance 3 bis · EOI, CE

Australian Society: Relaxed, but Demanding

The image: "No worries", "She'll be right", barbecues, beaches, first names with everybody, even the boss.

The reality at work: Australians are relaxed in style, but strict in practice. Employers expect a lot, and quickly.

  • Be on time. "On time" means five minutes early. Late twice? You may not get a third shift.
  • Never miss a shift without calling. A "no-show" is the fastest way to lose a casual job.
  • Safety rules are not optional. Hat, boots, sunscreen, water, high-visibility vest: if the rule says so, you do it.
  • Show initiative. When you have finished a task, ask "What can I do next?" Standing with your phone is badly seen.
  • Feedback is direct but friendly. "Mate, that's not good enough" is not an insult: it is a warning.
  • Casual work is flexible both ways. You can leave easily, but you can also lose your hours easily.

Society today

  • Multicultural: many Australians were born overseas or have parents born overseas.
  • Egalitarian: people do not like showing off. The "tall poppy syndrome" means that people who boast are criticised.
  • Mateship: loyalty, helping a friend in trouble, especially in the bush.
  • Sport and outdoor life: footy, cricket, surfing, swimming.
  • Rules everywhere: alcohol is forbidden in many public places, ID is checked in bars, fines are high, and the sun is dangerous.
  • Respect for First Nations: Acknowledgement of Country, sacred sites (do not climb Uluru), Aboriginal art and culture.

What would you do? EOI

Discuss in pairs, then compare with the class. There is no single answer, but some answers can cost you your job.

  1. Your bus is late. Your shift at the café starts in ten minutes.
  2. The farmer says: "Hats on, mate. No hat, no work." It is 7 am and it is not hot yet.
  3. You have finished cleaning the tables. Your manager is busy.
  4. A colleague says: "Nice job today, but you were a bit slow on the coffee." You feel hurt.
  5. At a party, an Australian makes fun of your French accent.
  6. Your French friends from the hostel want to stay together all year. An Australian colleague invites you to a barbecue.

Nuancez avec les élèves : ce sont des tendances, pas des vérités sur chaque Australien. L'objectif est d'éviter les malentendus, pas de créer des clichés.

Trace écrite

Australians can be relaxed in style and strict at work. I must be on time, call if I cannot come, follow safety rules and ask: What can I do next?

Corrigé (pour le professeur)

Pistes : 1. Appeler ou envoyer un message tout de suite, s'excuser, donner une heure d'arrivée. 2. Mettre le chapeau : les règles de sécurité ne se discutent pas. 3. Proposer son aide (« What can I do next? »). 4. Remercier et s'améliorer : c'est un retour normal, pas une attaque. 5. Rire avec lui : l'autodérision (« taking the mickey ») est une marque d'amitié. 6. Accepter : c'est ainsi qu'on s'intègre.
