Lesson 6. Promises Kept, Promises Broken

A promise, two figures, and a fair verdict.

  • Written record. A short sentence for your route, copied in the notebook.
  • Participation. Say the speaking frame aloud, even if you work alone.
  • Homework for next lesson. Copy the two figures with their date and source. Write two sentences: what was done, and what was not done.

Stopping point. Minute 44, after the speaking frame.

Emergency exit · 8 min. See the box at the end of the lesson.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

What is the difference between consultation and consent?

Self-check

Consultation means asking. Consent means saying yes, so that a project can go ahead.

4 to 8 min

Today’s goal

I can use two official figures, with their date and source, to judge a promise.

WordMeaning
a promisesomething you say you will do
a deadlinethe date by which something must be done
an advisoryan official warning about drinking water
to liftto end an advisory officially

Route S · SEGPA

I can: copy the two figures, with the date and the source.

In 2015, Justin Trudeau promised to end all long-term drinking-water advisories on First Nations reserves by March 2021. Official figures, 21 October 2026, Indigenous Services Canada: 158 advisories have been lifted since November 2015. 39 advisories are still in effect, in 37 communities. The promise was not kept on time. There was real progress. These figures change: check them before you use them.

Route C · CAP

I can: give a verdict, kept, partly kept or broken, with two figures and their source.

In 2015 the government pledged to lift every long-term advisory by March 2021. By October 2020, 96 had been lifted and 60 remained. As of 21 October 2026, 158 have been lifted since November 2015, and 39 are still in effect, in 37 communities (Indigenous Services Canada). Neskantaga, in Ontario, has had a boil-water advisory since February 1995. A fair verdict is ‘partly kept’: there was progress, but the deadline was missed.

Route P · Bac pro

I can: judge the water promise with failed to or should have, and keep both sides.

Be fair: 158 lifted is real progress; 39 still in effect means the promise was not kept on time. Both facts are true. The government failed to meet the March 2021 deadline; it should have done so. Despite promising to act, it fell short. People in Neskantaga wish the water had been fixed years ago. Other readings of the record also disagree: the government says most Calls to Action are under way; the Yellowhead Institute counted about one in seven as complete in 2024.

Exercise 1

Write the two figures, with the date and the source.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: 158 advisories have been lifted since November 2015 (Indigenous Services Canada, 21 October 2026).

Your turn. How many advisories were still in effect on that date, and in how many communities?

Self-check

39 advisories were still in effect, in 37 communities (Indigenous Services Canada, 21 October 2026).

Exercise 2

Was the promise kept by March 2021?

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: No. The deadline was missed.

Your turn. Why can ‘partly kept’ still be a fair answer?

Self-check

Because 158 advisories have been lifted: there was real progress, even if 39 remained.

Exercise 3

Write one sentence with failed to or should have.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: The government failed to end all advisories by March 2021.

Your turn. Write your sentence about the water promise.

Self-check

Possible sentence: The government should have met the deadline of March 2021.

CFA task · instead of Exercise 3

If you are in a training centre, do this task instead of Exercise 3. You may follow route C or route P for the reading.

Worked example (already corrected). A trainee writes a short report: ‘The company promised safe water by 2021. 158 problems have been fixed. 39 remain (official figures, 21 October 2026).’

Your turn. Write four lines for a supervisor: the promise, one figure for progress, one figure for what remains, and a fair verdict. Give the date and the source.

Self-check

Possible answer: In 2015 the government promised to end all long-term advisories by March 2021. 158 have been lifted since November 2015. 39 were still in effect on 21 October 2026 (Indigenous Services Canada). The promise was partly kept: there was progress, but not on time.

Speaking frame · 40 to 44 min

Say this aloud, and change the words in brackets if you need to:

In 2015 the government promised to end all long-term advisories by [March 2021]. On 21 October 2026, [158] had been lifted and [39] were still in effect (Indigenous Services Canada).

Written record · 44 to 48 min

Route S. 158 lifted, 39 still in effect, 21 October 2026, Indigenous Services Canada. The promise was not kept on time.

Route C. The 2015 water promise was not kept by March 2021. 158 advisories have been lifted and 39 remained on 21 October 2026 (Indigenous Services Canada). ‘Partly kept’ is fair.

Route P. The government failed to meet the 2021 deadline. Despite real progress (158 lifted), 39 advisories were still in effect on 21 October 2026. Both facts matter.

Progress record · 48 to 50 min

  • I read the short text for my route.
  • I did two exercises and checked the self-check key.
  • I said one sentence aloud.
  • I copied the written record.

Homework. Copy the two figures with their date and source. Write two sentences: what was done, and what was not done.

Emergency exit · 8 min

  • 0 to 2. Say this sentence: “One hundred and fifty-eight advisories have been lifted; thirty-nine were still in effect on 21 October 2026.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record for your route.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework.
  • 7 to 8. Check one self-check answer.
