Lesson 6. Promises Kept, Promises Broken

Justin Trudeau, 2015 to 2025: the promises and the figures.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Eight lessons in this sequence.

  1. Recap of lesson 5 0 to 4
  2. The promises of 2015 4 to 10
  3. Neskantaga and the water deadline 10 to 22
  4. Official figures as of 21 October 2026 22 to 32
  5. Two readings of the record, then a fact-check 32 to 44
  6. Written record and homework 44 to 52
  • Written record. One promise, what was done, what was not done, and a fair verdict, each with a source.
  • Participation. Each group presents a one-minute verdict: kept, partly kept, or broken.
  • Homework for next lesson. Language page, exercises 3 and 4. Finish the fact-check table.

Stopping point. Minute 44, after the fact-check.

Emergency exit · 8 min. See the box at the end of the lesson.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

Remember lesson 5.

  1. What is the difference between consultation and consent?
  2. Why did the Federal Court of Appeal quash the Trans Mountain approval in 2018?

Answer key

  • Consultation means asking for people’s opinion. Consent means that they say yes: without it, the project cannot go ahead.
  • Because Canada had failed to consult Indigenous peoples properly.

4 to 10 min

The promises

“No relationship is more important to me and to Canada than the one with Indigenous Peoples.”

Justin Trudeau, mandate letter to the Minister of Indigenous and Northern Affairs, 12 November 2015.

“We will, in partnership with Indigenous communities, the provinces, territories, and other vital partners, fully implement the Calls to Action of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission.”

Justin Trudeau, statement, 15 December 2015.

During the 2015 campaign, Justin Trudeau also pledged to end all long-term drinking water advisories on First Nations reserves by March 2021.

Sources: Prime Minister of Canada, mandate letter, 12 November 2015 · Prime Minister of Canada, statement, 15 December 2015

10 to 22 min

The water deadline, October 2020

Optional video. Government poised to miss deadline for ending First Nation boil water advisories. CBC News: The National · YouTube, 30 October 2020 · 2 min 03. The questions on this page can also be answered from the short text beside them, if you cannot play the video. Open on YouTube.

  1. Why are the people of Neskantaga in a hotel, 450 km from home?
  2. How long had Neskantaga lived with a boil water advisory in 2020?
  3. What did Justin Trudeau promise in 2015?
  4. How many advisories had been lifted, and how many remained, in October 2020? What reasons do communities give for the delays?

Answer key

  • An oily sheen was found in the reservoir, so they were evacuated to have safe drinking water.
  • 25 years: it was the longest-standing boil water advisory in the country.
  • “A Canadian government led by me will address this as a top priority”: he pledged to lift all long-term advisories on reserves by March 2021.
  • 96 had been lifted; 60 remained, in 41 communities. Construction delays, and slowdowns caused by COVID-19.

22 to 32 min

The official figures, 21 October 2026

These figures change. They are those of Indigenous Services Canada as of 21 October 2026, checked against the teaching dossier on 4 November 2026. Verify them before you use them in class.

Long-term drinking water advisories on public systems on reserves:

  • 158 advisories have been lifted since November 2015;
  • 39 advisories are still in effect;
  • in 37 communities.

Neskantaga First Nation, Ontario. 374 people live on the reserve. A boil water advisory was set in February 1995 and became long-term in February 1996. A new treatment system was commissioned in December 2020, but some deficiencies remain. The design of a new water treatment plant should be completed in fall 2027.

  1. Was the promise kept by March 2021? Was there progress? Use both figures.
  2. How long have the people of Neskantaga been told to boil their water?
  3. Look at the figures: why is “partly kept” a fair answer?

Answer key

  • No, the promise was not kept on time: 39 advisories were still in effect in October 2026. But there was real progress: 158 have been lifted since November 2015.
  • Since February 1995: more than 31 years.
  • Because most advisories have been lifted, but the deadline was missed and some communities, like Neskantaga, still cannot trust their taps.

Sources: Indigenous Services Canada, Ending long-term drinking water advisories · Indigenous Services Canada, Neskantaga First Nation

32 to 44 min

Two readings of the record, then a fact-check

Optional video. Trudeau promised Indigenous people radical change in 2015. Has he lived up to that?. APTN News · YouTube, 22 September 2021 · 1 min 33. The questions on this page can also be answered from the short text beside them, if you cannot play the video. Open on YouTube.

  1. What does National Chief RoseAnne Archibald say about the slowness of progress?
  2. Why is David Chartrand, of the Métis National Council, more positive?
  3. What does Lynne Groulx, of the Native Women’s Association of Canada, ask for?

Answer key

  • That “it’s costing us lives” and the suffering of children.
  • He says that no prime minister has ever devoted so much time to Indigenous files. It is “not perfect”, but you cannot solve 150 or 200 years of problems in four or five years.
  • The government made a lot of promises and it needs to keep them, if there is going to be trust.

The United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act became law on 21 June 2021. An Action Plan with 181 measures followed in June 2023. Of the 94 Calls to Action, 76 involve the federal government. The government says: “More than 85% of the 76 calls requiring the federal government’s sole or shared leadership have been completed or are well underway.” The Yellowhead Institute only counts a Call as complete if every part of it is done. In 2024, its researcher Eva Jewell counted about one Call in seven as complete, and wrote: “A Decade of Disappointment”.

Fact-check in groups. Each group takes one promise: drinking water, the 94 Calls to Action, or the UN Declaration. Decide: kept, partly kept or broken. Justify with two facts and their sources, then present your verdict in one minute.

Useful language: It was promised that… Since 2015, … have been lifted / passed / completed. However, the government failed to… It should have… Despite…, …

Optional further video:

Optional video. Why several TRC Calls to Action are still unanswered. CBC News: The National · YouTube, 29 September 2025 · 3 min 24. The questions on this page can also be answered from the short text beside them, if you cannot play the video. Open on YouTube.

Answer key · optional video

The Prime Minister’s annual report on the state of Aboriginal peoples, which should compare results in child welfare, health, justice and education, has not been started. According to Eva Jewell, the infrastructure to produce it does not exist yet, for example data agreements between ministries.

Grammar spotlight

The government failed to end all advisories by March 2021. It should have met the deadline. Despite promising to act, it fell short. People in Neskantaga wish the water had been fixed years ago. See the language page, parts A.3 and B.

Written record. In 2015 the government promised to end all long-term drinking-water advisories by March 2021. As of 21 October 2026, 158 advisories had been lifted since November 2015, and 39 were still in effect, in 37 communities (Indigenous Services Canada). The promise was not kept on time; “partly kept” is a fair verdict because there was real progress.

Emergency exit · 8 min

If time runs short, replace the unfinished plan with this condensed ending (inside the 52 minutes, not 52 plus 8).

  • 0 to 2. Everyone says this sentence aloud: “One hundred and fifty-eight advisories have been lifted, but thirty-nine were still in effect on 21 October 2026.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record: The 2015 water promise was not kept on time; ‘partly kept’ is fair because there was real progress (Indigenous Services Canada).
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework: Finish the fact-check table and do language exercises 3 and 4.
  • 7 to 8. Check one answer in the answer key.
