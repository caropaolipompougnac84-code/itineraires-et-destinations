Lesson 1. Look Before You Judge

A boy, a loon and a fish, and a bond with the land.

Recap · 0 to 4 min

What is the capital of Canada? Who lived on this land first?

Self-check

Ottawa. Indigenous peoples: today the Constitution names First Nations, Inuit and Métis.

4 to 8 min

Today’s goal

I can name the three Indigenous peoples and say that land can be kin, not only a resource.

WordMeaning

Route S · SEGPA

I can: name the three peoples and copy one sentence about land.

Canada’s Constitution names three Indigenous peoples: First Nations, Inuit and Métis. Some land was given by a treaty. Some land in British Columbia was never given: it is unceded land. For many First Nations, land and water are like family. They are not only a resource.

Route C · CAP

I can: explain treaty land and unceded land in one short sentence each.

The Constitution of 1982 recognises First Nations, Inuit and Métis. Treaty land was agreed with the Crown. Unceded land was never given up. Two paintings (not shown on this page) talk about life on the land: a lonely boy in a village, and a loon joined to a fish. The lines in the second painting are often read as a bond: land and water can be kin, not only a resource.

Route P · Bac pro

I can: say why ‘kin or resource’ changes the guiding question.

Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982 recognises First Nations, Inuit and Métis. In much of Canada the Crown signed treaties; in British Columbia most land is unceded. Jim Logan’s painting, not reproduced here, shows a boy with only his dog to play with. Norval Morrisseau’s Woodland painting joins a sun, a loon and a fish with lines that are often read as relationships. For many Anishinaabe and other First Nations teachings, land and water are living relatives, almost sacred. That changes the question of who decides when a dam or a pipeline is planned.

Exercise 1

Complete with First Nations, Inuit or Métis.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: The Indigenous people of the Arctic are the Inuit.

Your turn. The Cree and the Wet’suwet’en are ________. The third people recognised by the Constitution are the ________.

Self-check

The Cree and the Wet’suwet’en are First Nations. The third people are the Métis.

Exercise 2

Choose: kin or resource.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: If a river is treated only as a resource, it can be dammed for power.

Your turn. If land is ________, people may speak of a sacred bond and of stewardship.

Self-check

If land is kin, people may speak of a sacred bond and of stewardship.

Exercise 3

Write one sentence with unceded land.

Worked example (already corrected). Worked answer: Most of British Columbia is unceded land, because it was never given up by treaty.

Your turn. Write your sentence. Use unceded and treaty once if you can.

Self-check

Possible sentence: Unceded land was never given up by treaty, unlike treaty land.

CFA task · instead of Exercise 3

If you are in a training centre, do this task instead of Exercise 3. You may follow route C or route P for the reading.

Worked example (already corrected). A trainee writes: ‘In my workplace we must check who owns the site before we build.’ This is a clear, short sentence.

Your turn. Write two sentences for a supervisor: one about asking the community, and one about land that was never ceded.

Self-check

Possible answer: We ought to ask the community before we start. Some land is unceded, so a contract with one council may not be enough.

Speaking frame · 40 to 44 min

Say this aloud, and change the words in brackets if you need to:

The Constitution names [First Nations, Inuit and Métis]. Land can be [kin], not only a [resource].

Written record · 44 to 48 min

Route S. The Constitution names First Nations, Inuit and Métis. Land can be kin.

Route C. Treaty land was agreed with the Crown; unceded land was never given up. Land can be kin, not only a resource.

Route P. The three peoples are recognised by section 35. If land is kin, who decides about a dam is also a cultural question.

Progress record · 48 to 50 min

  • I read the short text for my route.
  • I did two exercises and checked the self-check key.
  • I said one sentence aloud.
  • I copied the written record.

Homework. Learn the four words. Write two sentences: one about the three peoples, one about unceded land.

Emergency exit · 8 min

  • 0 to 2. Say this sentence: “The Constitution names First Nations, Inuit and Métis.”
  • 2 to 5. Copy the written record for your route.
  • 5 to 7. Write down the homework.
  • 7 to 8. Check one self-check answer.
