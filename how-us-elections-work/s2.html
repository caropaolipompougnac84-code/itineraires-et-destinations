What we need to say

In this lesson, you need to describe the cartoons, explain how the symbols are used, and correct wrong ideas.

To describe the cartoons and the parties, we need these words: party, symbol, cartoon, independent, term.

To explain how a symbol is used and to correct a wrong idea, we need passive questions and negatives: Is the donkey used by the Democrats? Yes, it is. The elephant is not used by the Democrats.

Documents

Key document

Two big parties dominate American politics: the Democratic Party and the Republican Party. Other parties and independent candidates exist, but they rarely win. The symbols of the two parties come from cartoons. The cartoonist Thomas Nast drew a donkey for the Democrats in 1870 and an elephant labelled The Republican Vote in 1874, in the magazine Harper's Weekly. The parties later adopted these animals. In the 1874 cartoon, the panic is about a third term for President Grant. Since the 22nd Amendment (1951), a person can be elected president only twice. In 2026, the President is Donald Trump, a Republican, and the Vice President is JD Vance.

Read the source: National Archives, Amendments (22nd) · National Archives, 2024 results

Grammar to say it: the passive voice (2), questions and negatives

Why we need it

To explain how a symbol is used and to correct a wrong idea, we need passive questions and negatives: Is the donkey used by the Democrats? Yes, it is. The elephant is not used by the Democrats.

Grammar point: board explanation

Teacher script
  1. Show Nast's elephant. Say something wrong on purpose: The elephant is used by the Democrats. Ask the class to correct you.
  2. Write the correction: The elephant is not used by the Democrats. Underline is not + used.
  3. Write the question: Is the elephant used by the Republicans? Show that is moves before the subject, as with all questions with be.
  4. Practise short answers in a chain: Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
  5. Explain the position of adjectives: always before the noun, never plural. Write: a big grey elephant, the Republican voters.
  6. Students copy the board, then do the quick check in pairs.
Board: students copy

The passive voice (2): questions and negatives; adjectives

Rule
Negative: is / are not + past participle. Question: Is / Are + subject + past participle?
Form
The donkey is not used by the Republicans.
Is the donkey used by the Democrats? Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
Use
To correct a wrong idea, to check information, to ask about how something is done.
Examples from the lesson
  • The colours are not fixed by law.
  • Are independents often elected? No, they aren't.
  • Is the president elected for six years? No, he isn't.
Common mistakes
  • Does the donkey used by the Democrats? → Is the donkey used ...?
  • The elephant don't used. → The elephant is not used.
  • the Republicans voters → the Republican voters (an adjective never takes -s).
French and English
French: un gros éléphant gris (adjective after the noun). English: a big grey elephant (adjectives before the noun; opinion, size, age, colour, origin).
Also on the board: Word order: adjectives
Adjectives go before the noun and never take -s: a famous cartoon, two political parties. Order: opinion, size, age, colour, origin, noun: a funny old American cartoon.

Figures of speech: personification and the fable

Learn

In Nast's cartoons, animals act like people: this is personification. A fable is a short story with animals and a lesson. The 1874 cartoon plays with Aesop's fable The Ass in the Lion's Skin: a donkey wears a lion skin to frighten the other animals.

  1. What does the elephant stand for in 1874?
  2. Why does the donkey wear a lion skin?
  3. What is the lesson of Aesop's fable?
  4. Which animals are frightened and run away?
  5. The idiom the elephant in the room means a big problem nobody talks about. Use it in a sentence about your school.
  6. Which animal would you choose for your class, and why?
Answer key
  1. The Republican vote.
  2. To look strong and frightening.
  3. You can look dangerous, but people will find out who you really are.
  4. A giraffe, an ostrich, a unicorn, rabbits, ducks.
  5. Example: The noise in the canteen is the elephant in the room.
  6. Open answer with because.

Finer grammar: the reporting passiveGoing further

Rule

It is said / believed / thought that + clause, or subject + is said to + infinitive. It is said that Nast created the elephant symbol. = Nast is said to have created the elephant symbol.

  1. People say that Nast invented the party animals. (It is said ...)
  2. People believe that cartoons influence voters. (Cartoons are believed ...)
  3. People think that the colours were fixed around 2000. (The colours are thought ...)
  4. People know that independents rarely win. (It is known ...)
Answer key
  1. It is said that Nast invented the party animals.
  2. Cartoons are believed to influence voters.
  3. The colours are thought to have been fixed around 2000.
  4. It is known that independents rarely win.

Text analysis: a cartoonist who shaped politicsGoing further

Thomas Nast, a German-born illustrator, worked for Harper's Weekly for more than twenty years. His drawings were seen by hundreds of thousands of readers at a time when many Americans could not read long articles. He did not invent the donkey from nothing, but his cartoons made both animals famous, and the parties eventually adopted them. Nast also attacked corruption in New York City: his cartoons against the political boss William Tweed were so effective that Tweed is reported to have complained about those damned pictures.

  1. Find a reporting passive in the text.
  2. Why were cartoons powerful at that time?
  3. Find a word meaning in the end.
  4. What does effective mean? Give a synonym.
  5. Is the author for or against Nast? Justify with two words from the text.
Answer key
  1. Tweed is reported to have complained.
  2. Many readers could not read long articles; pictures reach everybody.
  3. eventually.
  4. successful, efficient.
  5. Rather for: made famous, so effective.

Key points: Nast's cartoons reached readers who did not read long texts; symbols are easy to remember; memes today work the same way; limits: images simplify, can mislead, need context; balanced conclusion. Use one reporting passive and one concession (although, however).
