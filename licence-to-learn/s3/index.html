Lesson 3. MI5 or MI6: understand before you compare

Students can explain different institutional missions; form present-simple questions; distinguish officers from agents; and correct an oversimplification.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Six lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure. Lesson 6 is the final-task interview.

  1. Flashcard retrieval 0 to 5
  2. Read the documents and highlight key information 5 to 13
  3. Complete the institutional grid 13 to 21
  4. Grammar practice and correction 21 to 31
  5. Prepare an information-gap briefing 31 to 36
  6. Paired briefing, questions and role swap 36 to 44
  7. Every student corrects one misleading statement 44 to 47
  8. Homework 47 to 48
  9. Written record 48 to 52
  • Written record. Complete the written record on missions, cooperation and the officer/agent distinction.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student explains one service, asks one question and corrects one false statement.
  • Homework for next lesson. three cards (officer, agent, whereas) and four questions and answers for a class quiz, including one question beginning with What does…?

Stopping point. Stopping point: minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, this eight-minute ending replaces unfinished extension work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 47: every student explains one service;
  • 47 to 48: homework;
  • 48 to 52: written record.

Student document A. Two connected services

MI5 is the Security Service. Its work protects UK national security. Its staff work in London, across the UK and overseas.

MI6 is the Secret Intelligence Service. Its mission concerns foreign intelligence. Its intelligence officers work in the UK and overseas.

The difference is therefore about their main responsibilities, not a rule that one service never crosses a border. Cooperation is important.

Student document B. Officer or agent?

MI5 uses the word officer for its employed staff. An agent provides information but is not an MI5 employee. Officers and agents have different roles.

Intelligence work also involves analysis, language skills, technology and coordination. It is not one single job performed by one type of person.

Worksheet 3B. Correct the statement

  1. MI5 staff never work overseas.
  2. Every MI6 officer lives abroad.
  3. An MI5 agent is an employee.
  4. Intelligence work only means chasing suspects.

Answer key

  1. MI5 has staff overseas.
  2. MI6 officers work in the UK and overseas.
  3. MI5 agents are not employees; its employed staff are officers.
  4. Intelligence work also includes analysis, languages, technology and coordination.

Building your vocabulary: grammar practice

Exercise 1. Present-simple agreement

  1. MI5 UK national security. (protect)
  2. Officers with partners. (work)
  3. An agent information. (provide)
  4. The services different responsibilities. (have)

Answer key

  1. protects
  2. work
  3. provides
  4. have

Exercise 2. Form the questions

  1. MI5 / what / protect / does?
  2. officers / where / work / do?
  3. an agent / does / information / provide?

Answer key

  1. What does MI5 protect?
  2. Where do officers work?
  3. Does an agent provide information?

Exercise 3. Compare with whereas

Join:

  1. MI5 focuses on UK national security. MI6 focuses on foreign intelligence.
  2. An officer is employed by MI5. An agent is not.

Model answers

  1. MI5 focuses on UK national security, whereas MI6 focuses on foreign intelligence.
  2. An officer is employed by MI5, whereas an agent is not.

Exercise 4. Define with who

  1. An agent is a person who .
  2. An analyst is a person who .

Answer key

  1. provides information or assistance.
  2. examines information to understand it.
