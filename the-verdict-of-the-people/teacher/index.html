Teacher notes

Level check against the Bulletin officiel, changes from the first version, differentiation and a final checklist.

1. What the Bulletin officiel requires

For Première in the 2026 to 2027 school year, the applicable text is the English programme annexed to the arrêté of 5 May 2025, Bulletin officiel n° 22 of 29 May 2025 (NOR MENE2504621A), annexe 4 (BO text). Article 4 makes it applicable to Première and Terminale from the start of the 2026 to 2027 school year; it replaces the previous language programme for those classes.

ClassLVB targetLVA target
SecondeA2+B1+
PremièreB1B1+
TerminaleB1B2

These are end-of-year targets, not a requirement that every sequence certifies a complete CEFR level. The programme explains that a “+” means that the next level is reached in at least one language activity. B1+ is therefore not “slightly more vocabulary than B1”: it is a profile that includes some B2-level performance. Full B2 across all activities is not the Première target.

The programme describes Première as the consolidation of B1 and its overtaking (« la consolidation du niveau B1 […] et son dépassement »), with a focus on the Americas in axe 6. Puerto Rico appears in the official list of objets d’étude for axe 6: « Porto Rico : le 51e état ? ».

Wording used on the home page: Première générale, LVA. Official end-of-year target: B1+. This sequence consolidates independent B1 performance and develops B2-level performance in selected activities, especially source comparison, supported interpretation and qualified argument.

2. How the level was checked

A political topic is not automatically B2, and a short text is not automatically easy. Difficulty depends on the language, background knowledge, task, support and independence required. Word counts and vocabulary limits on this site are planning choices, not BO prescriptions.

6. Differentiation

Partners do not have to use the same support level.

Support route: a short glossary, numbered source paragraphs, highlighted institutional terms, sentence starters (“The document shows…”, “According to the source…”, “This suggests…, but it does not prove…”, “The two cases are similar because…”, “They differ because…”) and extra rehearsal before speaking.

Towards B2: identify a misleading omission, reconcile two source categories, explain a counterargument fairly, answer an unprepared clarification question, separate a rule, its implementation and its possible consequences. Extension is not just longer writing.

Accessible presentation: short, separated instructions; the source just before its questions; no dense justified paragraphs; clear headings and spacing; oral rehearsal before writing; a partly completed comparison table when needed. Students who need it can use the adapted version, which works without an AESH.

7. Listening, history and copyright

Listening. The Lesson 6 script is an original teacher-read text; do not label it as an authentic broadcast. If you add a real report, first check publisher, date, availability, transcript accuracy, speed and whether it contains results that need updating.

Historical and political care. Do not infer a person’s legal status from appearance alone; attribute historical identifications to the museum; do not treat nineteenth-century exclusion and present territorial arrangements as identical; do not ask students to disclose personal or family political loyalties; do not assume that all Puerto Rican residents favour one status; assess reasoning and language, not agreement with a political position.

Copyright. The three Bingham paintings are in the public domain (Saint Louis Art Museum rights statement). The photographs are in the public domain or under CC BY 2.0 or CC0, credited under each picture. All student texts, scripts and fictional exchanges are newly written; no historical speech is quoted. For any later addition: reproduce a complete text only when its public-domain status is established, give the exact source URL, check speeches against an official transcript, and never turn a remembered paraphrase into a quotation.

8. Final checklist

  • every lesson’s plan totals 52 minutes;
  • every student has a protected speaking turn;
  • minute 44 is a visible stopping point;
  • the written record and homework stay protected;
  • the emergency exit replaces unfinished work;
  • every election figure has a source, time, category and status;
  • no result has been inferred from an outdated membership list;
  • no prediction is presented as an established consequence;
  • the final task rewards accurate, qualified communication rather than a pile of figures.
