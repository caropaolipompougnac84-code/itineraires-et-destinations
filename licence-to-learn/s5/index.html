Lesson 5. The responsible officer’s code

Students can distinguish obligation, prohibition and no obligation; apply rules to a professional scenario; produce a justified code; and transfer useful language to a vocational setting.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Six lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure. Lesson 6 is the final-task interview.

  1. Retrieve qualities 0 to 5
  2. Read rules and establish meanings 5 to 12
  3. Grammar exercises and correction 12 to 23
  4. Scenario discussion 23 to 30
  5. Write the Spy Code 30 to 39
  6. Peer checking and vocational transfer 39 to 44
  7. Every student explains one rule 44 to 47
  8. Homework 47 to 48
  9. Written record 48 to 52
  • Written record. Complete the written record on must, mustn’t, don’t have to, and one justified rule.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student says one rule, its meaning and its reason, then transfers one structure to a familiar workplace.
  • Homework for next lesson. prepare an interview card (service, motivation, three qualities, one past example, one must rule, one mustn’t rule, one question) and cards for mustn’t and don’t have to.

Stopping point. Stopping point: minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, this eight-minute ending replaces unfinished extension work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 47: each student says one rule and its meaning;
  • 47 to 48: homework;
  • 48 to 52: written record.

Student document A. Fictional training-office rules

These are invented teaching rules, not a reproduction of agency employment policy.

  • Staff must protect confidential information.
  • Staff have to check a report before sending it.
  • Staff mustn’t invent evidence.
  • Staff mustn’t share passwords.
  • Staff don’t have to wear a suit in this office.
  • A trainee has to ask for help when unsure.

Student document B. The unsupported report

A colleague sends you a report. One important claim has no source. The deadline is close.

The colleague says, “Send it now. We can check later.”

What should you do?

Grammar reference

MeaningFormExample
Necessarymust / have toYou must check.
Forbiddenmustn’tYou mustn’t invent evidence.
Not necessarydon’t have toYou don’t have to wear a suit.
Necessary, third personhas toA trainee has to ask.
Not necessary, third persondoesn’t have toA trainee doesn’t have to wear a suit.

Worksheet 5A. Meaning first

Label necessary, forbidden or not necessary.

  1. You must check.
  2. You mustn’t share.
  3. You don’t have to wear a suit.
  4. She has to ask.

Answer key

  1. necessary
  2. forbidden
  3. not necessary
  4. necessary

Worksheet 5B. Grammar practice

Exercise 1. Complete

  1. You invent evidence. It is forbidden.
  2. You check the source. It is necessary.
  3. You wear a suit. It is optional.
  4. A trainee ask for help. It is necessary.

Answer key

  1. mustn’t
  2. must / have to
  3. don’t have to
  4. must / has to

Exercise 2. Correct the errors

  1. She musts check.
  2. He doesn’t has to travel.
  3. You mustn’t to share passwords.
  4. An officer have to be responsible.

Answer key

  1. She must check.
  2. He doesn’t have to travel.
  3. You mustn’t share passwords.
  4. An officer has to be responsible.

Exercise 3. Keep the meaning

  1. It is forbidden to invent evidence. You .
  2. Wearing a suit is not necessary. You .
  3. Checking the source is necessary. You .

Answer key

  1. mustn’t invent evidence.
  2. don’t have to wear a suit.
  3. must / have to check the source.

Worksheet 5C. Resolve the scenario

The problem is .

First, I have to .

I mustn’t .

This matters because .

Model

The problem is an unsupported claim. First, I have to ask for its source. I mustn’t present it as a confirmed fact. This matters because other people may act on the report.

Minimum: explain two rules.

Model answer

An officer must check sources because decisions depend on reliable information.

An officer has to ask for help when unsure.

An officer mustn’t invent evidence because it can mislead others.

An officer mustn’t share passwords.

In our fictional office, an officer doesn’t have to wear a suit.

Protected oral intervention

Each student says:

  1. one rule;
  2. its meaning;
  3. its reason.

Then transfer one structure to a familiar workplace:

  • A receptionist must protect customer information.
  • A trainee has to check instructions.

These are language-transfer examples, not complete workplace regulations.

  • □ I gave a complete spoken answer.
  • □ I listened to my partner.
  • □ I asked or answered a question.

Homework

Prepare an interview card:

  • service;
  • motivation;
  • three qualities;
  • one past example;
  • one must rule;
  • one mustn’t rule;
  • one question.

Make cards for mustn’t and don’t have to.
