The Verdict of the People

US Elections, Representation and the Americas

Road map

Seven lessons of 55 minutes, each timed for 52 minutes. In every lesson: timed steps, a stopping point at minute 44, an 8-minute emergency exit, and three visible outcomes: a written record, participation (every student speaks at least once) and homework for the next lesson.

  1. Lesson 1. Reading the Verdict 52 min

    Describe Bingham’s painting; separate observation, context and interpretation.

  2. Lesson 2. Why Midterms Matter 52 min

    Explain how Congress can limit presidential power; the passive voice with a reason.

  3. Lesson 3. Citizens, but Equally Represented? 52 min

    Compare Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C.; although, whereas, despite.

  4. Lesson 4. Images, Claims and Reported Speech 52 min

    Stump Speaking, claims and promises; reported speech.

  5. Lesson 5. Results: Establish, Date and Qualify 52 min

    Establish, date and qualify the 2026 results from real sources; past simple and present perfect.

  6. Lesson 6. Beyond US Borders 52 min

    Trade with Canada and Mexico; fact or hypothesis; conditionals.

  7. Lesson 7. The Cross-Border Radio Discussion 52 min

    The eight-minute radio discussion and the individual briefing note.

Also: the assessment page (oral and written grids /20), the flashcards page (method, model cards, blank template) and the teacher notes (level check against the Bulletin officiel, differentiation, checklist).

Level
Première générale et technologique, langue vivante A (LVA). Official end-of-year target: B1+. This sequence consolidates independent B1 performance and develops B2-level performance in selected activities, especially source comparison, supported interpretation and qualified argument. B2 is an extension, not a requirement for every student. In the programme, a “+” means that the next level is reached in at least one language activity.
Curriculum
Official programme: arrêté of 5 May 2025, Bulletin officiel n° 22 of 29 May 2025 (NOR MENE2504621A), annexe 4, Programme d’anglais pour les classes de lycée général et technologique (BO text). In force for Première and Terminale from the 2026 to 2027 school year. Cultural axes for Première: axe 6, « Les aires anglophones américaines » (objet d’étude « Porto Rico : le 51e état ? »); axe 3, « Art et pouvoir » (« L’art, les artistes et le pouvoir »); supporting link with axe 2, « Diversité et inclusion » (the role of political institutions in representation).
Driving question
How does the verdict of the people distribute power in the United States, and why does it matter beyond its borders?
Lessons
Seven lessons of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Minutes 52 to 55 stay free for collecting work and moving.
Final task
In groups of four, an eight-minute radio discussion in English for a Canadian student audience: “The Verdict of the People: What Can US Elections Change at Home and Across Borders?”, plus an individual briefing note of 140 to 170 words.
Assessment
An individual oral grid (/20) and a written briefing-note grid (/20). These are classroom assessments, not an automatic CEFR conversion.

Adapted version (dys), usable alone without an AESH: routes for SEGPA, CAP, CFA and Bac pro, with a worked example before each exercise and a self-check key after it.

About the 2026 results

The US midterm elections took place on 3 November 2026. This sequence deliberately gives no winner, seat total, gain or loss: in Lesson 5, students establish them from dated, identifiable sources, and say clearly what is not yet established. A media call, a reported count and a legal certification are not the same thing.

Your project

You are going to present a student radio programme for young Canadians who know little about US institutions. Starting from a nineteenth-century painting, you will explain how Congress shares power with the president, why Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C. are represented differently, how to report election results honestly, and why Canada and Mexico follow US elections closely.

How each lesson works

Participation routine. At the start of every lesson, Partner A speaks for about 20 seconds and Partner B answers for about 20 seconds; they swap when the prompt requires it. The teacher circulates with a participation roster. A quiet exchange with a partner counts; a student who needs support may use a prepared sentence. Whole-class volunteering does not replace this routine.

Learning log, five sections: cultural documents; corrected exercises; written records; source log; vocabulary and flashcard reviews.

Answer keys are printed straight after each exercise, so that students can check their work and catch up alone.

Related sequences on this site. The Capital Trap (Seconde and Première, B1 / B1+) works on the geography of US state capitals and the question “Is the biggest city always the capital?”: it does not cover Congress, elections or representation, which are taught only here. The King Reigns, Parliament Rules studies British institutions: students who know it can compare Parliament and Congress, but nothing from it is repeated in this sequence. Hijacking the Grid (Terminale) looks at New York City through its marathon.
