Lesson 6. Why Other Countries Care

Goal: I can tell a fact from a possible consequence.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 6 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Split the lesson into two blocks: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Say one sentence from the previous lesson (Lesson 1: what you already know about elections) 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Read the goal and the word box 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Read or listen to your route’s text 8 min (minutes 8 to 16)
  4. Exercise 1, then check it 8 min (minutes 16 to 24)
  5. Pause: look away from the text or move 2 min (minutes 24 to 26)
  6. Exercise 2, then check it 8 min (minutes 26 to 34)
  7. Exercise 3, then check it 6 min (minutes 34 to 40)
  8. Say your final answer aloud with the speaking frame 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  9. Written record 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  10. Progress record 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  11. Read and write down the homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. One sentence for your route (see below), copied or typed.
  • Participation. Say at least one answer aloud. In class, use a quiet voice; at home, say it to yourself. You do not need to record yourself.
  • Homework for next lesson. Prepare your final report. Choose one fact from three different lessons. Write the lesson number beside each fact.

Stopping point. Minute 44: stop the main exercises. Keep the written record and the homework, even if an exercise is unfinished.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: say the emergency sentence of the lesson aloud.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: complete the short written record.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: write down the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: check one answer with the key. Do not rush through all the unfinished exercises.

The USMCA trade agreement entered into force on 1 July 2020. The US Constitution gives Congress powers over trade with other countries. These facts do not make every predicted trade consequence certain.

Word box

trading partnera country that trades with another

Choose your route: Route S · SEGPA · Route C · CAP · Route P · Bac pro · CFA. Do only your route.

Route S · SEGPA

Short text

The United States trades with Canada and Mexico.

The three countries have a trade agreement.

Its name is USMCA.

Political decisions can affect trade.

Not every change happens immediately.

Exercise S1

Name

Worked example. “One country is the United States.”

Now you.
Name the other two.

Self-check

Canada and Mexico.

Exercise S2

Choose

Worked example. “Trade means buying and selling.”

Now you.
Choose:
A. Every political change immediately changes all trade.
B. A political change can affect trade.

Self-check

B.

Exercise S3

Complete

Worked example. “Canada is a trading partner.”

Now you.
Complete: “Political decisions can affect .”

Self-check

trade.

Route C · CAP

Short text

The United States, Canada and Mexico trade with each other.

USMCA is their trade agreement.

Congress has powers over trade.

A change in Congress may affect political debates.

However, an election does not immediately change every trade rule.

We must separate what happened from what may happen next.

Exercise C1

Fact or possibility?

Worked example. “USMCA is a trade agreement.” Fact.

Now you.
Classify: “A change in Congress may affect a trade debate.”

Self-check

A possibility.

Exercise C2

Complete

Worked example. “The word may shows possibility.”

Now you.
Complete: “An election affect trade debates.”

Self-check

may.

Exercise C3

Explain

Worked example. “The election has happened. Its future effects may still be uncertain.”

Now you.
Write two sentences with: One fact is… / One possible effect is…

Self-check

The first sentence uses information from the text. The second uses may or might and does not claim certainty.

Route P · Bac pro

Short text

Neighbouring countries may follow US congressional elections because Congress has powers over trade.

However, a result does not automatically decide future policy.

Existing laws, the president and decisions by other countries also matter.

A useful report explains how a political change could lead to a decision.

It then says what is still uncertain.

This is more careful than predicting an outcome without explaining the connection.

Exercise P1

Find limits

Worked example. “Congress is not the only actor.”

Now you.
Name two other factors from the text.

Self-check

Existing laws, the president, decisions by other countries.

Exercise P2

Conditional

Worked example. “If a proposal changes, the debate may change.”

Now you.
Complete with changes and may: “If Congress the law, trade conditions change.”

Self-check

“If Congress changes the law, trade conditions may change.”

Exercise P3

Write for a visitor

Worked example. Opening: “US elections can matter beyond US borders, but…”

Now you.
Write 60 to 80 words. Include a fact, a possible consequence and a limit.

Self-check

Your answer must separate established information from prediction. No invented 2026 outcome is needed.

CFA pathway

CAP apprentice: use Route C. Bac pro apprentice: use Route P. Do Exercises 1 and 2 of your route, then do this task instead of Exercise 3. Keep the same written record and homework.

CFA task

Separate fact and prediction

Worked example. “Canada and the United States trade with each other.” That is a fact.

Now you.
Write a careful sentence about a possible business consequence of political change. Starter: “A change may affect , but we need .”

Self-check

Accept a clearly labelled possibility. Do not accept a claim that a specific price or delivery outcome is certain.

Written record

S: “The United States trades with Canada and Mexico.”

C: “An election may affect trade, but its effects are not automatic.”

P: “Explain the possible connection between a political change and a consequence.”

Speaking frame. “One fact is . One possible consequence is .”

Emergency sentence. “A possible consequence is not a certain result.”

Homework

Prepare your final report. Choose one fact from three different lessons. Write the lesson number beside each fact.
