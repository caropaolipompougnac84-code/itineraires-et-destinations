Lesson 8. My Evidence and My Request

Goal: I can explain an issue and make a supported request.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 8 of 8. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Pause when necessary: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Read the goal and find your place. 0 to 4
  2. Read the short text and word meanings. 4 to 12
  3. Complete reading and vocabulary work. 12 to 20
  4. Complete listening or the labelled no-video route. 20 to 30
  5. Complete language practice. 30 to 37
  6. Prepare and say your answer. 37 to 44
  7. Keep the written record. 44 to 49
  8. Check answers and note homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the short written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: say your route sentences aloud, alone or to a partner.
  • Homework for next lesson. The homework at the end of this page, with its worked example and self-check.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: say your subject and request.
  • 46 to 49: complete the written record.
  • 49 to 51: note the revision homework.
  • 51 to 52: check your source.
Short text

A good public message explains its subject.

It gives facts.

It names sources.

It does not claim more than the evidence shows.

It ends with a clear request.

You do not need a group to complete this task.

Building your vocabulary

  • claim: a statement someone makes;
  • evidence: information supporting a claim;
  • request: something you ask someone to do;
  • source: where information comes from;
  • limitation: what the evidence does not establish.
Exercise 1

Choose a case

Worked example.
My case is drinking water.
My source is Indigenous Services Canada.

Choose one:

  • White Paper and Red Paper;
  • James Bay;
  • Muskrat Falls or Site C;
  • a pipeline;
  • drinking water.

Complete:

  1. My case is .
  2. My lesson source is .
  3. One fact I can explain is .

Self-check

All three fields must be complete. Use the earlier lesson text if necessary.

Exercise 2

Sort the message

Worked example.
“Please publish a dated update.”
This is a request.

Label:

  1. “The snapshot is dated 10 September 2026.” .
  2. “Please explain the remaining work.” .
  3. “The figures do not describe every water problem.” .

Choose: fact / request / limitation.

Self-check

Fact; request; limitation.

Exercise 3

Complete the language

Worked example.
I am writing about water.

Complete:

  1. I am writing the project.
    Choose: about / at.
  2. According the source, .
    Choose: to / for.
  3. Please the next steps.
    Choose: explain / explains.

General extension:

  1. Correct: “Although progress was made, but the task was unfinished.”

Self-check

About; to; explain; “Although progress was made, the task was unfinished.”

Exercise 4

Prepare your oral message

Worked example.
I am speaking about drinking water. The official snapshot records progress, but active advisories remained. I ask for clear dated updates.

Write:

  1. My subject is .
  2. According to , .
  3. One limit is .
  4. I ask for .

Say your four sentences aloud.

Then answer this question aloud:

Why is your request useful?

Self-check

Your answer should give a reason linked to the case.

Speaking: your route

Route S · SEGPA

Use the Route C model below. Choose two sentences. Copy them. Say them aloud.

Worked example.
I am writing about drinking water.
Please give communities clear information about the remaining work.

Route C · CAP or CFA

Write 40 to 60 words.

Worked model.
Dear Member of Parliament,
I am writing about drinking water. The official September 2026 snapshot shows progress, but some advisories were still active. The figures come from Indigenous Services Canada.
Please give communities clear information about the remaining work.
Yours sincerely,
A student

Route P · Bac pro or CFA

Write a briefing email of approximately 100 to 150 words.

Subject: Evidence and next steps

Dear ,

This message concerns .

The main fact is .

The source and date are .

A remaining concern is .

Before the next decision, please .

This would help because .

Kind regards,

For LVA development, add a second fact and qualify the recommendation.

Route G · Lycée général, LVA

Write approximately 150 to 200 words.

Include:

  • the issue;
  • two sourced facts;
  • a different viewpoint or limitation;
  • a reasoned request.

Route G · Lycée général, LVB

Write approximately 100 to 140 words.

Use the frame:

Dear Member of Parliament,

I am writing about .

According to , .

Another important fact is .

However, .

Please because .

Yours sincerely,

Self-check for the final message

Check each item:

  • I named the subject.
  • I used information from the lesson.
  • I named the source.
  • I dated any changing figure.
  • I made a clear request.
  • I did not invent evidence.
  • I said the message aloud.
