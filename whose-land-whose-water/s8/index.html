Lesson 8. A Citizens’ Hearing and an Open Letter

Goal: I can present sourced evidence, respond to a question and make a proportionate recommendation.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 8 of 8. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure.

  1. Identify the chosen case and available evidence. 0 to 4
  2. Complete the individual evidence card. 4 to 10
  3. Prepare a protected spoken contribution. 10 to 16
  4. Hold parallel hearings in groups of four. 16 to 30
  5. Check sources and revise one claim. 30 to 36
  6. Draft the open letter. 36 to 44
  7. Written record and self-assessment. 44 to 49
  8. Homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Complete the four-part written record on your case, evidence, limitation and recommendation.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student makes a statement of up to 90 seconds in the hearing, then asks and answers one short question.
  • Homework for next lesson. Finish the open letter and complete the four-line self-review.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: each partner states a recommendation and one reason.
  • 46 to 49: complete the four-sentence written record.
  • 49 to 51: record the homework.
  • 51 to 52: check that every figure has a source and date.

Teacher note. Parallel hearings make the timing workable in a large class. Each student receives an individual speaking turn and asks a question. The teacher samples groups or uses permitted school recording arrangements. A single teacher cannot reliably assess every simultaneous contribution live.

Dossier choices

A. The White Paper and Red Paper: a proposed policy and its rejection.

B. James Bay or one later dam: negotiated development, benefits and losses.

C. One pipeline: representation, consultation and disagreement.

D. Drinking water: a dated pledge, progress and an unmet deadline.

Do not force every dossier into an identical “promise broken” story. Where the evidence concerns a proposal or disputed commitment, say so.

Building your vocabulary

  • evidence: information supporting a claim;
  • recommendation: a proposed course of action;
  • limitation: something the evidence does not establish;
  • accountability: being required to explain decisions and results;
  • proportionate: appropriate to the evidence and problem.

Hearing procedure

Each group of four follows this order:

  1. Two minutes: silent preparation.
  2. Six minutes: four statements of up to 90 seconds.
  3. Four minutes: every student asks and answers one short question.
  4. Two minutes: agree on a recommendation and identify any remaining disagreement.

No student has to impersonate an Indigenous person or disclose a personal political position.

Model letter

Dear Member of Parliament,

I am writing about the promise to end long-term drinking-water advisories on First Nations reserves.

The deadline was March 2021. The Indigenous Services Canada page retrieved for our class displays a snapshot dated 10 September 2026. It records 158 advisories lifted since November 2015, but 38 active advisories in 36 communities.

These figures show progress, but they do not show that the promise was fully completed. They also concern a specific category of public systems on reserves, rather than every water problem affecting Indigenous people.

I therefore ask you to support clear, regularly dated reporting and the work needed to maintain reliable water services. Reports should distinguish completed construction, testing and the official lifting of an advisory.

Communities should be able to understand what remains to be done and how decisions are made. A fair assessment must recognise improvements without treating an unfinished commitment as a completed one.

Yours sincerely,
A student researcher

The figures and scope in this model follow the retrieved official page.

Answer key

Grammar:

  1. “We recommend that the government provide updates.”
    Also acceptable: “The government ought to provide updates.”
  2. “Although the deadline was missed, progress was recorded.”
  3. “The report proves…” However, this still requires evidence. A safer version is: “The report shows that some representatives agreed.”
  4. “The government promised to end the advisories.”

Vocabulary: limitation; recommendation; evidence.

For B1, accept connected simple sentences and brief reasons. For B1+, expect a more developed explanation and clearer qualification. Do not turn the rubric into a checklist requiring every advanced grammar structure.
