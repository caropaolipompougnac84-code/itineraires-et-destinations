Assessment

Final task, graded out of 20, LVA target B1+. Two options: a podcast or an article.

The article (option B) can be written as a lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. The oral (option A: 3 minutes plus 2 minutes of questions per student) is organised separately; it does not fit a whole class inside a single 52-minute lesson.

Option A: oral presentation (3 minutes + 2 minutes of questions)

You are invited to a podcast for English learners called Capital Stories. Compare one US capital (Washington, D.C., or a state capital) and one Commonwealth capital: when and why it was chosen, who decided, what it says about the country. End with your answer to the question: Is the biggest city always the capital, and should it be?

  • Speak from a card with five key words only.
  • Use at least two complex comparatives and two verbs in the passive.
  • Pronounce the names correctly.

Sentence starters. Today I’m going to compare… and… · … was chosen in… by…, mainly because… · Unlike…, … was built from scratch. · To sum up, I believe that…

Option B: article for a school web magazine (180 to 220 words)

Write an article titled Is the biggest city always the capital? Compare one US capital and one Commonwealth capital, explain the reasons for each choice and give your opinion. Use paragraphs and a range of connectors.

Model article

Is the biggest city always the capital?

If you ask people to name the capital of Australia, many will say Sydney. They are wrong, and the mistake tells a story. In 1901, Sydney and Melbourne were rivals, and neither accepted that the other one should win. As a result, a brand-new capital, Canberra, was chosen in 1908 and Parliament moved there in 1927.

The United States made a similar choice more than a century earlier. In 1790, the North and the South agreed on a deal: the capital would be built on the Potomac River. Today Washington, D.C. is nowhere near as big as New York City: with 8.8 million inhabitants, New York City is more than twelve times as big.

Moreover, these capitals were not chosen by chance. Their location was a political message: a compromise between rivals and a place that belonged to nobody in particular.

In my opinion, a capital does not need to be the biggest city. Whereas big cities attract business, a smaller capital can be fairer for all the regions. However, a new capital costs a fortune, so the choice must be made carefully.

About 210 words. Figures: 2020 United States census.

Student checklist

  • I compare one US capital and one Commonwealth capital.
  • I give dates, the people who decided and the reasons.
  • I use complex comparatives, the passive and connectors.
  • I give and justify my opinion.
  • For the oral: I speak from five key words and I pronounce the names correctly.
