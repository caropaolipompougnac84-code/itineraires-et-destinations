Lesson 3. Rivers into Reservoirs

Goal: I can explain why an agreement may bring benefits and losses at the same time.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 3 of 8. The remaining three minutes allow for arrival, equipment and departure.

  1. Recall “Citizens Plus”. 0 to 4
  2. Read the context and word box. 4 to 9
  3. Listen twice to the main CBC report, with note-taking pauses. 9 to 21
  4. Complete the cause-and-effect worksheet. 21 to 27
  5. Listen once to the second report and compare viewpoints. 27 to 33
  6. Grammar and vocabulary practice. 33 to 39
  7. Protected paired restitution. 39 to 44
  8. Written record. 44 to 49
  9. Homework. 49 to 52
  • Written record. Copy the written record into your notebook.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student gives a 40-second report in the third person, then answers “Whose viewpoint are you reporting?”
  • Homework for next lesson. A 90 to 110-word museum information panel on James Bay, and two flashcards.

Stopping point. Minute 44. If necessary, the emergency exit replaces everything remaining from minute 44.

Emergency exit · 8 min. This eight-minute ending replaces unfinished work inside the 52 minutes; it is not added to them.
  • 44 to 46: each student states one benefit and one loss.
  • 46 to 49: copy the written record’s first three sentences.
  • 49 to 51: record homework.
  • 51 to 52: explain what discharge measures.

Student text

Read

Quebec announced the James Bay hydroelectric project in 1971. Cree and Inuit opposition, legal action and negotiations helped lead to the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement in 1975.

The project produced electricity, but reservoirs also changed hunting grounds and access to land. The teaching dossier describes the relocation of Fort George residents to Chisasibi and concerns about mercury in fish.

The agreement also supported institutions and public services. These outcomes should not be reduced to “everyone benefited” or “nothing improved”. Different communities and speakers may assess the agreement differently.

Building your vocabulary

  • dam: a structure that holds back river water;
  • reservoir: an artificial lake storing water;
  • relocate: move to another place;
  • compensation: money or another response to a loss;
  • autonomy: the ability to make decisions;
  • discharge: the volume of water passing a point over time.

Listening documents

Listening protocol. 1. Read the listening purpose. 2. Listen once for the main message. 3. Note keywords, not complete sentences. 4. Listen again for the named details. 5. Prepare a short restitution. 6. Only then consult the support text and the answer key. Label your notes: H I heard it; R I read it afterwards; I I inferred it; ? I am unsure.

Grammar exercise

Choose used to, had started or was signed.

  1. The agreement in 1975.
  2. Families travel along routes later affected by the project.
  3. Work before the legal challenge described in the dossier.

Extension:

  1. Rewrite using would as a repeated action:
    “During regular gatherings, families met on the island.”

Expanding your vocabulary

Protected spoken turn

Each student gives a 40-second report in the third person:

One benefit was…
One loss was…
These outcomes can exist together because…

The listener asks:

Whose viewpoint are you reporting?

  • □ I gave a complete spoken answer.
  • □ I listened without interrupting.
  • □ I asked or answered a question.

Answer key

Worksheet A: Robert Bourassa; 1971; Cree and Inuit; legal action, opposition or negotiation; compensation, institutions or greater autonomy; flooded land, disrupted activities or relocation.

Worksheet B: answers depend on the details students identify. Credit a faithful contrast and an uncertainty label. Do not award credit for invented names.

Cause and effect: land and routes; relocation; traditional food practices; institutions and services; discharge.

Grammar:

  1. was signed;
  2. used to;
  3. had started;
  4. “During regular gatherings, families would meet on the island.”

Vocabulary: 1d, 2b, 3c, 4a.

Written record

The 1975 James Bay agreement followed conflict and negotiation over hydroelectric development. It brought compensation and institutions, while the project also transformed land and community life. We must identify whose assessment we are reporting. Discharge is measured in cubic metres per second; it is not the same as current speed.

Homework

Write a 90 to 110-word museum information panel:

James Bay: electricity, agreement and lasting change.

Include one benefit, one loss and a named source. Do not invent first-person testimony.

Replay the second video and add one accurately attributed viewpoint.

Flashcards. Make two cards from today’s word box, for example reservoir, discharge: the word on the front, the meaning and one example sentence on the back. See the flashcard method.
