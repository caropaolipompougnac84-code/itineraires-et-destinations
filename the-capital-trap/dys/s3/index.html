Lesson 3. Big cities vs capitals

Los Angeles is bigger, but Sacramento is the capital. Build the comparative and the superlative, and watch the silent letters.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. 7 lessons, plus a written and an oral assessment.

  1. Which one is the capital? Two photo pairs, a vote, then the reveal 10 min
  2. Memory game with cards A: state and capital 14 min
  3. Grammar: comparatives and superlatives, exercises 1 and 2 18 min
  4. Phonology: silent letters and unexpected sounds 10 min
  • Written record. Los Angeles is bigger than Sacramento, but Sacramento is the capital of California. Houston is bigger than Austin, but Austin is the capital of Texas.
  • Participation. Everyone speaks at least once: each student says one pair aloud: “The capital of Texas is Austin.”
  • Homework for next lesson. Write five sentences on the model “The capital of X is Y.” Learn the pronunciation of Illinois, Arkansas, Houston and Albany.

Stopping point. After the grammar exercises, before a long phonology drill if time is short. Keep at least a short pronunciation round.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If time runs short at this stopping point, condense the end of the lesson inside the 52 minutes (this is not 52 plus 8). Dictate a short written record (two sentences). A quick round so that everyone speaks at least once (one sentence each). Write the homework on the board.

Which one is the capital? · 10 min

Show the two photographs side by side. Students vote with a show of hands, then the teacher reveals the answer. Do the same with California.

Model sentences

Los Angeles is bigger, but Sacramento is the capital.

Houston is bigger than Austin, but Austin is the capital of Texas.

Memory game · 14 min

Use cards A (state / capital). A possible variant is to add the “biggest city” cards as traps. Each time a pair is found, it is said aloud:

The capital of Texas is Austin.

The fifteen pairs are printed in full on the cards page, so the game can be played without a screen.

Comparatives and superlatives · 18 min

Start from the surprise cards and from the two model sentences above, then build the rule.

ComparativeSuperlative
Short adjective: bigbigger thanthe biggest
Long adjective: famousmore famous thanthe most famous
Irregular: goodbetter thanthe best
Irregular: badworse thanthe worst

Observation: Montpelier is the smallest state capital in the USA. Los Angeles is bigger than Sacramento, but Sacramento is the capital.

Figures below are approximate (city proper). They are given so that students can compare; they are not a census table.

Exercise 1. Big city vs capital

Model: Houston is bigger than Austin, but Austin is the capital of Texas.

Answer key

  • Los Angeles is bigger than Sacramento, but Sacramento is the capital of California.
  • Houston is bigger than Austin, but Austin is the capital of Texas.
  • New York City is bigger than Albany, but Albany is the capital of New York State.
  • Chicago is bigger than Springfield, but Springfield is the capital of Illinois.

Exercise 2. USA records

  1. Montpelier is ______ (small) state capital.
  2. Alaska is ______ (large) state.
  3. Key West is ______ (southern) city in the continental USA.
  4. New York City is ______ (populated) city in the USA.
  5. Juneau is ______ (difficult) capital to reach.

Answer key

1. the smallest · 2. the largest · 3. the southernmost · 4. the most populated · 5. the most difficult

Not every state is a trap. Salt Lake City is both the capital and the biggest city of Utah. Oklahoma City is both the capital and the biggest city of Oklahoma. Students should notice the exception, not only the trick.

Phonology · 10 min

Silent letters. Illinois, Arkansas, Connecticut, Des Moines.

Unexpected sounds. Houston (the beginning sounds like “hew”), Albany (the beginning sounds like “all”), Juneau (the beginning sounds like “jew”, the ending like “no”), Phoenix (the beginning sounds like “fee”).

Each student says one name after the teacher, then a full sentence: “Springfield is the capital of Illinois.”
