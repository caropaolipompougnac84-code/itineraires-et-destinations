Lesson 7. My Short News Report

Goal: I can give a short, accurate report.

Road map

Lesson of 55 minutes, timed for 52 minutes. Lesson 7 of 7. Minutes 52 to 55: no new task. Working at home and need longer? Split the lesson into two blocks: this is a classroom structure, not a speed test.

  1. Say one sentence from the previous lesson (Lesson 1: what you already know about elections) 4 min (minutes 0 to 4)
  2. Read the goal and the word box 4 min (minutes 4 to 8)
  3. Read or listen to your route’s text 8 min (minutes 8 to 16)
  4. Exercise 1, then check it 8 min (minutes 16 to 24)
  5. Pause: look away from the text or move 2 min (minutes 24 to 26)
  6. Exercise 2, then check it 8 min (minutes 26 to 34)
  7. Exercise 3, then check it 6 min (minutes 34 to 40)
  8. Say your final answer aloud with the speaking frame 4 min (minutes 40 to 44)
  9. Written record 4 min (minutes 44 to 48)
  10. Progress record 2 min (minutes 48 to 50)
  11. Read and write down the homework 2 min (minutes 50 to 52)
  • Written record. One sentence for your route (see below), copied or typed.
  • Participation. Say at least one answer aloud. In class, use a quiet voice; at home, say it to yourself. You do not need to record yourself.
  • Homework for next lesson. Revise one sentence. Write: “Before: .” “After: .” “I changed it because .”

Stopping point. Minute 44: stop the main exercises. Keep the written record and the homework, even if an exercise is unfinished.

Emergency exit · 8 min. If only eight working minutes remain, this replaces the unfinished plan; it is not added to it.
  1. Minutes 0 to 2: say the emergency sentence of the lesson aloud.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: complete the short written record.
  3. Minutes 5 to 7: write down the homework.
  4. Minute 7 to 8: check one answer with the key. Do not rush through all the unfinished exercises.

You can complete this alone. You do not need a group debate. You do not need to publish or record your voice.

Choose your route: Route S · SEGPA · Route C · CAP · Route P · Bac pro · CFA. Do only your route.

Route S · SEGPA

Short text

Model report:

“This sequence is about US elections.

Bingham painted an election crowd.

Congress has the House and the Senate.

A promise is not a result.

I check the source and the date.”

Exercise S1

Choose facts

Worked example. “Congress has two chambers.” This is an accurate fact.

Now you.
Choose two more facts from your written records.

Self-check

Check each sentence against the lesson record. Do not use a fact you cannot find.

Exercise S2

Build your report

Worked example. Opening: “My report is about US elections.”

Now you.
Complete: “Bingham painted .” “Congress has .” “I check .”

Self-check

Possible answers: an election crowd; the House and the Senate; the source and the date.

Exercise S3

Speak

Worked example. Read one sentence. Pause. Read the next sentence.

Now you.
Say your report aloud.

Self-check

Did you say at least three accurate sentences? If yes, the task is complete.

Route C · CAP

Short text

Model report:

“Bingham’s painting shows different reactions to election results.

Today, US institutions divide power.

Congress has the House and the Senate.

The president does not decide everything alone.

A news report needs a clear source and date.

We should not confuse a promise with an achieved result.”

Exercise C1

Select

Worked example. Choose “Congress has two chambers” rather than “The president decides everything”.

Now you.
Choose three accurate facts from three lessons.

Self-check

Underline the matching sentence in each lesson.

Exercise C2

Organise

Worked example. Order: topic, fact, explanation, caution.

Now you.
Write a 50 to 70-word report. Use: My report explains… / One important fact is… / This matters because… / We should check…

Self-check

Your report needs three accurate facts, one explanation and one checking rule. The word range is guidance, not a speed or spelling test.

Exercise C3

Speak and answer

Worked example. Question: “Why should we check the date?” Model answer: “Because a report may be old.”

Now you.
Say your report aloud. Then answer: “Why should we not guess a missing result?”

Self-check

“Because we do not have evidence.” Other answers with the same meaning are accepted.

Route P · Bac pro

Short text

Model report:

“Bingham’s painting introduces a question about participation.

Modern US institutions also need careful explanation.

Congress can limit presidential action, while Puerto Rico shows that representation takes different forms.

Election information needs a source, a date and a status.

Possible consequences for trade should be labelled as predictions rather than established results.”

Exercise P1

Plan

Worked example. Fact: Congress has two chambers. Meaning: the president does not act alone in lawmaking.

Now you.
Choose three facts and explain why one of them matters.

Self-check

Each fact must match a lesson text. Its importance must follow logically.

Exercise P2

Write

Worked example. Structure: introduction, facts, implication, limit.

Now you.
Write 100 to 130 words with one cultural reference, two institutional or source-checking facts, one explanation and one qualification.

Self-check

Do not include an election winner or total unless a verified source has been supplied.

Exercise P3

Speak and clarify

Worked example. Question: “Does that prove every future outcome?” Answer: “No. It identifies a possible consequence, not a certainty.”

Now you.
Give a 60 to 90-second report. Then answer: “What is one thing your evidence does not establish?”

Self-check

A good answer names a real limit, for example: “The text does not establish a future trade outcome.”

CFA pathway

CAP apprentice: use Route C. Bac pro apprentice: use Route P. Do Exercises 1 and 2 of your route, then do this task instead of Exercise 3. Keep the same written record and homework.

CFA task

Give a short briefing

Worked example. Opening: “Here is what we can establish.”

Now you.
Give a 45-second briefing (CAP) or a 60 to 90-second briefing (Bac pro). Include one established fact, one explanation, one uncertainty and one checking question.

Self-check

Ask yourself: “Did I clearly separate what I know from what I need to check?”

Written record

S: “One accurate fact I used was .”

C: “One checking rule I used was .”

P: “My next target is .”

Speaking frame. Use your model report.

Emergency sentence. “My report uses facts I can check.”

Homework

Revise one sentence. Write: “Before: .” “After: .” “I changed it because .”

My progress record

  • I read or listened to the text.
  • I checked my answers.
  • I said at least one answer aloud.
  • I kept my written record.
  • I know the homework.
